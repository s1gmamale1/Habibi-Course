**Date:** 2026-07-19

## 1. What "Noorani Qaida" is

Noorani Qaida (نورانی قاعدہ) is a South-Asian (Pakistani/Indian, Deobandi-tradition) primer for teaching absolute beginners — traditionally children — to read the Quran in Arabic script with basic tajweed, before starting the Quran itself. It predates and heavily influenced almost every "Qaida" product sold today (Noorani, Yassarnal Quran, Qaida Nooraniya, Madani Qaida, various English/Urdu "kids Qaida" apps). It is **not** a single canonical copyrighted text controlled by one publisher — many publishers (Idara Ishaat-e-Diniyat, Darul-Uloom presses, Markaz al-Furqan, "Al Qaidah An-Nooraniyah" by Shaykh Nur Muhammad Haqqani et al.) have printed their own editions with the same ~17-lesson skeleton but different page layouts, colors, and added transliteration/English glosses.

Consequence for us: there is no single "the" Noorani Qaida to license. We are using it as a **pedagogical sequence/reference**, not as a text or asset we redistribute. That matches the spec's constraint (never re-host; only structure/inspiration is borrowed, and all actual Quran text and audio must come from Tanzil/QUL/everyayah).

## 2. Canonical Noorani Qaida progression — chapter by chapter

Collated and cross-checked across multiple independent write-ups (Bayan-ul-Quran Academy's "Lessons 1–16" page, Islamic Quran Center's table of contents, eQuranSchool's page-by-page index, QuranSheikh's per-lesson blog series, and app-store copy for the "Noorani Qaida – Learn Quran" apps). Lesson numbering varies slightly by edition (16, 17, or 18 "lessons" depending on whether revision/exercise pages are counted separately), but the **content order is consistent** across all sources:

| # | Chapter (English name) | Arabic/Urdu term | Content | Drill style |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Isolated letters | Huroof-e-Mufradaat | All 28(-29, incl. hamza) Arabic letters shown in isolated form, one per box, with correct makhraj/pronunciation. Goal: recognize shape + produce correct sound, no vowels yet. | Flashcard-style grid, recite left-to-right (Arabic reading direction), teacher models, student repeats each letter in isolation. |
| 2 | Joined/compound letters | Huroof-e-Murakkabaat | Two-and-three-letter combinations showing how letter shape changes by position (initial/medial/final) when letters connect in a word, still unvoweled. | Same grid drill, now reading letter-clusters (not full words) to train shape recognition in context. |
| 3 | Mysterious/disjointed letters | Huroof-e-Muqatta'at | The 14 unique letters/combinations that open 29 Surahs (e.g., الم، حم، كهيعص), each recited as isolated letter names, not sounded as a word. | Recitation drill only — read each muqatta'ah string letter-by-letter per the standard recited names. |
| 4 | Short vowels (harakat) | Harakaat: Fatha/Kasra/Damma | Introduces the three short-vowel diacritics and how each changes a letter's sound (a/i/u). | The classic "every letter × every vowel" grid — one of the two drills our spec explicitly borrows for Unit 1.3. |
| 5 | Tanwin | Tanween: fathatain/kasratain/dammatain | Double-vowel marks (ً ٍ ٌ) producing the final an/in/un nasalized sounds. | Grid drill, letter × each tanwin form. |
| 6 | Harakat + Tanwin exercises | — | Mixed practice combining lessons 4–5: reading strings of letters with mixed short vowels and tanwin. | Longer drill lines/words (2–3 letter combos), no longer single-letter grid — first "reading" style practice. |
< strong>7</strong> | Standing vowels (khari harakat) | Khari Harakaat (vertical fatha/damma/kasra used as small alif/waw/ya) | Quranic-script-specific marks: small superscript alif, inverted damma, etc., that indicate an implied long vowel not written as a full letter. | Recognition + recitation drill of words containing these marks. |
| 8 | Madd & Leen letters | Huroof-e-Maddah wa Leen | Distinguishes the three madd (elongation) letters — alif preceded by fatha, waw preceded by damma, ya preceded by kasra — from the two leen (soft/diphthong) letters — waw/ya preceded by fatha. Long-vowel vs. diphthong contrast. | Drill contrasts short vs. long vs. leen forms side by side; qari-style elongation counted in beats (harakah counts). |
| 9 | Combined exercise | — | Integrates tanwin + madd + leen letters into full practice words — a consolidation checkpoint before sukoon. | Word-list reading drill, increasing difficulty; some editions label this "Exercise of Movements, Maddah, Leen and Tanween." |
| 10 | Sukoon (jazm) | Sukoon/Jazm | The "no-vowel" mark (ه ), making a letter a closed consonant; introduces consonant clusters within a syllable. | Grid + word drills pairing a voweled letter with a following sakin (silent) letter — first real "syllable" reading. |
| 11 | Sukoon exercises | — | Practice reading words with multiple sukoon letters; some editions fold in Huroof-e-Qalqalah (the 5 "bouncing" letters ق ط ب ج د) here as a preview, though full qalqalah *rules* belong to tajweed proper (our Phase 3). | Word-list drills, increasing consonant-cluster complexity. |
| 12 | Shadda (tashdeed) | Tashdeed | The doubling/gemination mark (ّ ), teaching that a shadda-marked letter is pronounced twice (once sakin, once voweled) — "an emphasis/doubling sign." | Grid drill: letter+shadda+each harakah, then real words. |
| 13 | Shadda exercises | — | Practical word drills on shadda, since it can change word meaning (tajweed-adjacent point about precision). | Word-list drills. |
| 14 | Shadda + sukoon exercises | — | Combined drills: words containing both a shadda and a separate sukoon letter. | Word-list drills, harder combinatorics. |
| 15 | Double shadda exercises | — | Words with two shadda letters in the same word — timing/duration practice. | Word-list drills. |
| 16 | Shadda + madd combination | — | Shows how sukoon/shadda can extend madd duration (interaction between long-vowel letters and doubling) — sets up "madd lazim"-type situations without naming the tajweed rule yet. | Word-list drills, qari-modeled elongation timing. |
| 17 | Final comprehensive exercises / revision | — | Full mixed review: all previous marks combined; some editions frame this as "special situations for noon sakinah, meem sakinah, and tanwin" — i.e., a soft preview pointing toward Phase-3-style tajweed rules (izhar/idgham/iqlab/ikhfa) without formally teaching them. | Continuous reading passages / sentence-level drills — bridge into real mushaf reading. |

## 3. Alternative Qaida systems surveyed

### 3.1 Al-Qaidah An-Nooraniyah — Furqan Group / Shaykh Nur Muhammad Haqqani edition

- This is the specific edition authored by **Shaykh Nur Muhammad Haqqani**, published/associated with **Markaz al-Furqan Taleem-ul-Quran**. It is essentially the same Noorani lesson skeleton (letters → joins → muqatta'at → harakat → tanwin → madd/leen → sukoon → shadda → exercises) but is the edition most commonly sold in the West as "Al-Qaidah An-Nooraniyah" (Furqaan Bookstore USA, Al Barakah Books).
- Modern printings from **Al Barakah Books** and **Furqaan Bookstore USA** add **QR codes** linking each page to a recitation video/audio — useful precedent for our "tap-to-hear" UX, though their audio is not necessarily openly licensed (their commercial product, sold as a physical book).
- A companion volume applies the same Qaida rules directly to **Surah al-Fatihah and Juz 'Amma**, i.e., the qaida's own "graduation" reading practice is the same texts our Phase 3 hifz strand uses (al-Fatiha + short surahs) — a nice validation that our course's transition point (qaida rules → al-Fatiha/short surahs) matches traditional pedagogy.

### 3.2 Madani Qaida

- Another South Asian qaida, structurally near-identical to Noorani: Lesson 1 single letters (Huroof), Lesson 2 compound letters (Murakkabaat), Lesson 4 Harakaat, Lesson 5 Tanween, Lesson 6 practice of Harakaat+Tanween, continuing through Huroof Maddah, Khari Harakaat, Huroof Leen, Sukoon-Jazm, then **explicitly includes Noon Sakinah/Tanwin rules (izhar, ikhfa, idgham, iqlab)** and **Meem Sakinah rules** as later lessons — i.e., Madani Qaida pushes further into tajweed-proper territory (our Phase 3 rules) than Noorani Qaida does within the primer itself, rather than leaving those for a separate tajweed book.
- Page-numbered breakdown available at eQuranSchool (parallel structure to their Noorani index): https://www.equranschool.com/qaida/ (index), e.g. page 10 = "Lesson 05, Exercise of Tanween and Harakaat" (https://www.equranschool.com/qaida/10.htm), page 18 = "Lesson 10, Practise of Harakaat and Huroof e Maddah" (https://www.equranschool.com/qaida/18.htm).
- Free PDFs found at: https://shaykhsaeedridah.com/admin_res/books/english/Quraan/madani-qaida.pdf and https://jamiatulmadinahusa.com/526-1.pdf and https://www.equranschool.com/Madani-Qaida.pdf — **licensing unclear/unverified**, treat as reference only, not a source to link as "free resource" for students without checking each publisher's rights.
- Commercial print edition (English/Arabic) at Sunnibooks: https://www.sunnibooks.uk/product/madani-qaida/

**Takeaway:** Madani Qaida ≈ Noorani Qaida in the letters/vowels/joining stage (matches our Phase 1–2 scope), but extends further into noon/meem-sakinah tajweed rules that we deliberately reserve for Phase 3. Confirms our phase boundary (Qaida mechanics stop before formal tajweed-rule-naming) is a reasonable, precedented split point — we're cutting the qaida tradition at the same place Noorani (not Madani) cuts it.

### 3.3 Baghdadi Qaida

- Older, more traditional method (name derived from Baghdad), historically dominant across the Arab world (Egypt, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Somalia) and among Western Arab diaspora communities, contrasted with Noorani/Madani's South-Asian dominance.
- Structurally: alphabet → joining/word-building → sentences, generally **fewer, less granular lessons** than Noorani (some editions ~12, others ~15) — i.e., less step-by-step scaffolding of harakat/tanwin/sukoon/shadda as separate discrete lessons; more example-word-driven, with each lesson's drills built from real words that occur directly in Quranic text rather than abstract letter-grids.
- A commonly available modern English adaptation: "Easy Qur'an Reading with Baghdadi Primer" by Moustafa Elgindy (parts 1 & 2 combined), used in Western contexts:
  - https://hanibookstore.com/products/easy-quran-reading-with-baghdadi-primer-combined
  - https://noorart.com/products/easy-quran-reading-with-baghdadi-primer-for-teaching-the-arabic-alphabet-diacritics-and-reading-to-beginners-part-1-and-2-combined
  - Amazon (Arabic edition): https://www.amazon.com/Easy-Quran-Reading-Baghdadi-Arabic/dp/0990334228
  - Darussalam's "Qaida Baghdadi": https://www.darussalam.us/products/qaida-baghdadi
- **License: commercial products, no open license found.** Reference for method comparison only.
- **Comparison point for us:** Baghdadi's "drill with real Quranic words from lesson 1 onward" style is closer to what our spec wants at the Phase 2 "real words → phrases → fully-voweled sentences" stage than Noorani's abstract-grid style — validates blending: Noorani-style isolated-grid drills for Phase 1 (letters, joining, harakat), shifting toward Baghdadi-style real-word/sentence drills for Phase 2 (tanwin onward), which is effectively already what our spec's Phase 2 progression describes.

### 3.4 Popular Qaida apps — how they split lessons

Surveyed via app-store listings (could not install/run apps in this environment, so structure comes from store descriptions):

- **"Noorani Qaida – Learn Quran" (multiple iOS/Android apps published under this name, e.g. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/noorani-qaida-learn-quran-with-lessons-tajweed/id918383017 , https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.quranreading.nooraniqaida&hl=en_US , https://apps.apple.com/us/app/noorani-qaidah/id493718985):**
  - Follow the traditional 17-lesson structure closely: Ch.1 = 30 Arabic letters (some apps count 30 incl. hamza/lam-alif variants vs. our spec's 28), Ch.2 = common joined words, Ch.3 = Huroof-e-Muqatta'at, Ch.4–12 = "Arabic Phonics" (harakat, tanwin, madd/leen, sukoon, shadda drills).
  - Tap-to-hear audio is a core feature: apps advertise "2,000+ offline audio files," recordings by a hafiz/qari, color-coded tajweed highlighting, and multi-language (14 languages) support — i.e., the same tap-audio + color-coding UX pattern our platform spec is already committed to, independently validated as the standard UX for this content type.
  - These are commercial closed apps; **not usable as an asset source** (their audio files are proprietary), but strongly validate our planned UX (tap-to-hear + color spans).
- **"Qurani Qaida" (Darussalam-published book, app by Al-Huda):** https://apps.apple.com/us/app/qurani-qaida/id1510779231 , https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.alhuda.apps.learn.qaida&hl=en-US — app follows the printed Darussalam "Qurani Qaida" book lesson-by-lesson with practice exercises per lesson, mirroring the same paradigm (primer chapters → drill sets).
- **Understand Al-Quran Academy** (https://understandquran.com/, mobile apps overview: https://understandquran.com/our-mobile-apps/): different product line — word-frequency-based vocabulary course (232 highest-frequency words, 9 hours / 19 sessions), not a qaida/letters course; not directly relevant to Phase 1–2 mapping, more relevant to a future "understand meaning" enrichment layer, out of scope for v1 per the spec.

**General app-survey takeaway:** All commercial qaida apps converge on the same ~17-chapter skeleton described in §2; none have a materially different pedagogical order. This confirms the canonical order (isolated letters → joined letters → muqatta'at → harakat → tanwin → combined exercises → madd/leen → sukoon → shadda [single, then with sukoon, then double, then with madd] → final mixed exercises) is the de facto industry standard we should follow, not an idiosyncratic choice of one publisher.

### Phase 1 — Letters & Sounds (12 lessons)

### Net assessment

- Our Phase 1+2 = 26 lessons maps cleanly onto Noorani Qaida's ~17 chapters plus the extra scaffolding the spec already adds (shape-family grouping, real-word/phrase practice modeled on the Uzbek reference channel, first mushaf exposure). We have *more* lessons than the qaida has chapters, which is appropriate: our 26 lessons are live 45–60 min sessions for one adult student with homework between, not self-paced booklet pages, so 1 qaida chapter frequently maps to 1–2 of our lessons (chapter content + a dedicated drill/practice lesson), not a strict 1:1.
- The one clear content gap versus all surveyed qaida systems is **hamzat-ul-wasl** — worth a dedicated small research note before finalizing the Phase 2 lesson list, since no qaida tradition hands us a ready-made sequence for it.
- Muqatta'at (Ch.3) is the one qaida chapter that doesn't cleanly need a dedicated lesson slot in our system — recommend treating it as a short enrichment aside rather than forcing a 12th Phase-1-lesson slot for it, freeing a lesson for extra harakat/joining practice instead if the checkpoint reveals weak spots.
