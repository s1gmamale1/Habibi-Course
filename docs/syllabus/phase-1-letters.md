# Phase 1 — Letters & Sounds (Syllabus)

**Course:** Tajweed Course (English-language, absolute-beginner, one Uzbek-speaking adult student)
**Phase scope:** 12 lessons, ~4 weeks, live 3x/week (Mon/Wed/Sat), 45–60 min each + 15–20 min daily homework.
**Scope discipline:** recognition + pronunciation only. **No writing/handwriting requirement anywhere in Phase 1** (per spec, out of scope for v1). Correct makhraj (articulation point) is taught from day one on every letter, not deferred to Phase 3.
**Gate:** Checkpoint 1 (live, kit at the end of this file). Failing the checkpoint never means restarting Phase 1 — it triggers targeted revision of the specific weak unit(s), then a retest.

## Unit 1.1 — The 28 letters (Lessons 1.1–1.6)

### Lesson 1.1 — Orientation + Alif & the ب ت ث family

- **Objectives:**
  1. Student can explain that Arabic is read right-to-left and that letters are learned by shape-family, not strict alphabetical order.
  2. Student can identify and correctly pronounce ا (alif), ب, ت, ث in isolation, on sight, in random order.
  3. Student can correctly locate the makhraj (articulation point) for each of these 4 letters when asked "where does this sound come from?"
- **New content:** The concept of an isolated letter glyph; the "same body, different dots" pattern (ب = 1 dot below, ت = 2 dots above, ث = 3 dots above — the spec's own named example group); alif as the simplest letter (a vertical stroke, glottal/chest-cavity sound, foreshadowing its Phase 2 role as a madd/long-vowel letter). Note the 28-vs-29-letter debate as a one-slide aside (hamza ء is a glottal-stop mark, not counted as a separate one of "the 28" in this course's scope).
- **Slide outline:**
  1. Title slide: "Lesson 1.1 — Meet the Alphabet" (Arabic decorative header)
  2. Welcome + how this course works: live lesson + slide deck + tap-to-hear + daily homework
  3. Concept: Arabic reads right-to-left; letters are always taught in shape-families here, not strict abjad order
  4. Concept: makhraj — "where in your mouth/throat a sound is born" — introduce the 5 broad zones (chest, throat, tongue, lips, nose) at a plain-English level
  5. Letter: ا (alif) — shape, name, sound (a glottal/open-throat sound), makhraj = jawf (chest/oral cavity — the "empty space" articulation)
  6. Letter: ب (ba) — shape, one dot below, sound, makhraj = the two lips
  7. Letter: ت (ta) — shape, two dots above, sound, makhraj = tip of tongue against upper front teeth/gum ridge
  8. Letter: ث (tha) — shape, three dots above, sound (unvoiced "th" as in "think" — NOT "t" or "s"), makhraj = tip of tongue between the teeth
  9. Contrast slide: ب / ت / ث side by side, same body shape, only dots differ — "spot the dots" recognition drill
  10. Guided drill: teacher says a letter name aloud, student points/names it back, all 4 letters, randomized order ×3 rounds
  11. Sound drill: teacher models each sound in isolation, student imitates, teacher corrects makhraj live
  12. Aside slide: "28 or 29 letters?" — hamza note (optional, 1 minute)
  13. Wrap-up: today's 4 letters recap
  14. Homework assignment slide
- **Drills:**
  - Flashcard grid (4 large isolated glyphs): ا ب ت ث — student names each + produces the sound, teacher shuffles order every round.
  - Odd-one-out drill: teacher shows 3 letters, 2 match by dot-pattern-adjacent family, student identifies which one is not ب/ت/ث family (mixed with ا as the "different" one every time at this stage).
  - Minimal-pair listening drill: teacher says either "ت" or "ث", student points to which glyph was said (tests the plain-t vs interdental-th distinction early, before it can calcify as a merged sound).
- **Audio:** No open-licensed per-letter audio exists (confirmed gap, see Resource key above). Primary = live teacher voice, modeled and self-corrected against Video cross-check B below before the lesson.
- **Video:**
  - Arabic101 general alphabet orientation: `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j9BwXWpzB1Y` and `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c-7SVieC_04`
  - Arabic101 — "Is the Arabic alphabet 28 or 29 letters?": `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JLee5VQvdIo` (for the aside slide)
  - Uzbek channel (Muallimi Soniy) compilation — alif/long-vowel review section: `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=1117` ("the letters that make A, I, U")
  - (ب and ت/ث do not have dedicated Arabic101 makharij episodes — teacher voice + general alphabet videos above are primary for this lesson.)
- **Teacher listen-for:**
  - ث flattened toward plain ت or toward س — Uzbek/Turkic phonology has no interdental fricative, so this is the single highest-risk error of the lesson (source: `teaching-mistakes-assessment.md` A.4, ث vs ذ/ت confusion pattern, generalized to first exposure).
  - ب produced without full lip closure (turning it into a weak "v"-like glide) — watch especially if student's L1 orthography habits carry over.
  - ا (alif) given an added glottal catch/hamza sound that isn't there — alif in isolation should be an open, unobstructed sound, not a stop.
- **Homework (15–20 min/day):** Print take-home flashcard sheet (ا ب ت ث, large isolated glyphs, Amiri font, one per card/box). Student says each letter name + sound aloud 10× per letter in mixed order (tally boxes provided, no writing of the letters themselves). Re-watch the two linked Arabic101 orientation videos once.

### Lesson 1.2 — The throat-and-dot family: ج ح خ / د ذ

- **Objectives:**
  1. Student can identify and pronounce ج, ح, خ, د, ذ in isolation on sight.
  2. Student can consistently distinguish ح (heavy, mid-throat) from خ (upper-throat) and from ه (light — taught later, flagged now as "the sound you'll meet later that's NOT this").
  3. Student can distinguish د (plain "d") from ذ (voiced "th" as in "this").
- **New content:** ج ح خ shape-family (same body, dot-below / no-dot / dot-above pattern); د ذ shape-family (same body, no-dot / dot-above). Introduce the throat as a genuine 3-zone region (deep/mid/upper) since two of today's five letters are throat letters — this is the single most error-prone family in the whole alphabet for this student's likely L1 background.
- **Slide outline:**
  1. Title + recap of ا ب ت ث (quick cold-call, 4 letters, random order)
  2. Concept: the throat has three zones — deep (hamza/ه, met later), mid (ع/ح, ع met later), upper (غ/خ, غ met later) — today we meet the mid-zone ح and the upper-zone خ
  3. Letter: ج (jeem) — shape, sound (soft "j" affricate), makhraj = middle of tongue against the roof of the mouth
  4. Letter: ح (heavy ha) — shape, sound (a strong, unvoiced, breathy throat friction — "like fogging a mirror from mid-throat"), makhraj = middle of the throat
  5. Letter: خ (kha) — shape, sound (like the "kh" in loanwords the student's L1 already has), makhraj = upper throat/back of tongue against soft palate
  6. Contrast drill: ح vs خ vs (preview) ه — teacher demonstrates all three back to back, explaining this is the #1 mistake in the whole alphabet for non-Arab learners
  7. Letter: د (dal) — shape, sound (plain "d"), makhraj = tip of tongue against upper gum ridge
  8. Letter: ذ (dhal) — shape, sound (voiced "th" as in "this," NOT a "z"), makhraj = tip of tongue between the teeth (same point as ث, but voiced)
  9. Contrast drill: د vs ذ — minimal-pair listening check
  10. Full 5-letter shuffle drill (ج ح خ د ذ), cumulative with lesson 1.1's four
  11. Guided sound-imitation round, teacher corrects makhraj live
  12. Wrap-up recap
  13. Homework assignment slide
- **Drills:**
  - Flashcard grid: ج ح خ د ذ, named + sounded, shuffled.
  - Minimal-pair listening drill #1: ح / خ / ه(preview) — student points to which was said (ه itself isn't formally taught yet, used only as a contrast foil the teacher voices).
  - Minimal-pair listening drill #2: د / ذ — student points to which was said.
  - Cumulative 9-letter shuffle (all of 1.1 + 1.2 together): ا ب ت ث ج ح خ د ذ.
- **Audio:** Confirmed gap (Resource key). Primary = live teacher voice, self-checked against Video cross-check A/B below beforehand — this is the single lesson where the teacher should audition hardest, since ح/خ is the most-cited non-Arab error in the sourced research.
- **Video:**
  - Arabic101 Makharij Lesson 3 (ع + ح): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-oA0HfNUezI`
  - Arabic101 Makharij Lesson 4 (غ + خ): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FzDyCoJCgIg`
  - Arabic101 Makharij Lesson 6 (ج): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X-iZ4yZrltA`
  - Uzbek channel timestamps: ج `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=10344`, خ `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=11056`, ح `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=11593`, د `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=13525`, ذ `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=14598`
- **Teacher listen-for:**
  - **ح substituted with خ or with the light ه** — flagged in the sourced research as "the single most-cited non-Arab error" (`teaching-mistakes-assessment.md` A.1); no equivalent phoneme exists in English, Turkish, Uzbek, Persian, or Urdu. Give this the most correction time in the lesson.
  - خ losing its heaviness, flattened toward the student's L1 "x" sound (A.1) — watch that it stays a genuine back-of-throat fricative, not a softened palatal one.
  - ج over- or under-affricated (some traditions say "zh" or hard "g" — this course follows the Husary/Muallim standard soft-j articulation; watch for L1-transfer toward a hard "g").
  - **ذ collapsing toward a plain "z"** — explicitly documented as a Persian/Turkish/Urdu-speaker pattern (A.3: "the emphatic sounds become just another way to say 'Z' in these languages") and directly relevant to an Uzbek-background student; confirm this specific substitution against the student's actual speech in this lesson, don't assume.
  - د/ذ merged into a single sound generally.
- **Homework (15–20 min/day):** Flashcard sheet ج ح خ د ذ (tally-box practice, 10 reps each). Daily 2-minute "throat warm-up": ح then خ then ه(anticipatory) said slowly 5× each, self-recorded on phone if available and compared informally against the linked Arabic101 videos. Cumulative shuffle drill of all 9 letters learned so far.

### Lesson 1.3 — ر ز and the sibilants س ش

- **Objectives:**
  1. Student can identify and pronounce ر, ز, س, ش in isolation on sight.
  2. Student can produce ر with the correct light tongue-tap quality (not a static English/Uzbek "r").
  3. Student can distinguish ز from ذ (already taught) without collapsing them.
- **New content:** ر ز shape-family (same body, no-dot / dot-above); س ش shape-family (same body, no dots / three dots above). ر gets dedicated correction time since Arabic101's own research flags it as unusually error-prone even among fluent readers.
- **Slide outline:**
  1. Title + cumulative recap (all 9 letters so far, quick shuffle)
  2. Letter: ر (ra) — shape, sound (a light single tongue-flap/trill, NOT the English or Uzbek "r"), makhraj = tip of tongue curled slightly, touching the gum ridge
  3. Guided ر drill: teacher models slowly, student imitates, isolate the tongue-tap motion
  4. Letter: ز (zay) — shape, sound (plain "z"), makhraj = tip of tongue near lower teeth, sibilant
  5. Contrast drill: ز vs ذ (from lesson 1.2) — minimal-pair listening check, this is the exact collapse risk flagged in the research
  6. Letter: س (seen) — shape, sound (plain "s"), makhraj = tip of tongue near lower teeth, thin hiss
  7. Letter: ش (sheen) — shape, sound ("sh"), makhraj = middle of tongue against the roof of the mouth
  8. Contrast drill: س vs ش
  9. Full 4-letter shuffle: ر ز س ش
  10. Cumulative 13-letter shuffle (everything so far)
  11. Guided sound-imitation round with teacher correction
  12. Wrap-up recap
  13. Homework assignment slide
- **Drills:**
  - Flashcard grid: ر ز س ش, named + sounded, shuffled.
  - ر isolation drill: student repeats ر 10× focusing only on the tongue-tap motion, teacher gives a thumbs up/down each repetition.
  - Minimal-pair listening drill: ز / ذ — the exact D→Z-family collapse risk from the previous lesson, re-tested here with ز specifically.
  - Cumulative 13-letter shuffle: ا ب ت ث ج ح خ د ذ ر ز س ش.
- **Audio:** Confirmed gap (Resource key). Primary = live teacher voice.
- **Video:**
  - Arabic101 Makharij Lesson 10 (ر): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObYKEtceQbU`
  - Arabic101 Sifaat Lesson 6 ("90% of RA mistakes happen HERE"): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TBBMuGVy4mc`
  - Uzbek channel timestamps: ر `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=1497`, ز `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=1819`, ش `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=7914`, س `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=8227`
- **Teacher listen-for:**
  - ر produced as a static, untapped English/Uzbek-style "r" instead of a light single flap — per Arabic101's own framing ("90% of RA mistakes happen here").
  - ز drifting toward ذ or vice versa — re-confirm the D→Z-family collapse pattern flagged in lesson 1.2 is (or isn't) present for this specific student (A.3).
  - س and ش losing crispness when said quickly back to back (rushed, blurred sibilants).
- **Homework (15–20 min/day):** Flashcard sheet ر ز س ش. Daily ر-only drill: 15 slow repetitions focusing purely on the tongue-tap. Cumulative 13-letter shuffle, timed (just for the student's own sense of growing fluency, not graded).

### Lesson 1.4 — The heavy four: ص ض ط ظ

- **Objectives:**
  1. Student can identify and pronounce ص, ض, ط, ظ in isolation on sight.
  2. Student can explain, in plain English, what "heavy" (tafkheem) vs "light" (tarqeeq) means and demonstrate the difference against each letter's light counterpart (س/ت/ذ already taught, د already taught).
  3. Student is aware that ض is documented as the single hardest letter in the alphabet for non-native speakers and does not feel discouraged by early imperfection on it.
- **New content:** The four emphatic ("heavy") letters, each paired against a light counterpart the student already knows: ص↔س, ض↔د, ط↔ت, ظ↔ذ. First formal introduction of tafkheem/tarqeeq as a concept (full rule-naming is Phase 3, but the physical sensation — back of tongue raised, mouth cavity fuller/rounder — is taught now, since correct makhraj is a day-one commitment per the spec).
- **Slide outline:**
  1. Title + cumulative recap (13 letters, quick shuffle)
  2. Concept: "heavy vs light" letters — mouth cavity fills up, back of the tongue rises, sound gets a fuller/darker quality; contrast a light letter you already know against its heavy twin
  3. Letter: ص (saad) — heavy twin of س; drill: say س then ص back to back, feel the difference
  4. Letter: ض (daad) — no light twin in this set (closest reference: د); explicit note that this is the hardest letter in Arabic for virtually all non-native speakers, including many whose L1 is Persian/Turkish/Urdu (same family risk as Uzbek) — sound comes from the side of the tongue against the upper molars, a lateral release unique to this letter
  5. Guided ض drill: slow, exaggerated repetitions, no time pressure
  6. Letter: ط (heavy taa) — heavy twin of ت; drill: say ت then ط back to back
  7. Letter: ظ (heavy dhaal/zha) — heavy twin of ذ; drill: say ذ then ظ back to back
  8. Concept: "the heaviness rides through the whole syllable" — a heavy letter also darkens any vowel sound that follows it (light forward-preview of Phase 3's tafkheem/tarqeeq rule, kept purely sensory here)
  9. Full 4-letter shuffle: ص ض ط ظ
  10. Cumulative 17-letter shuffle
  11. Guided sound-imitation round with teacher correction, extra time budgeted for ض
  12. Wrap-up recap
  13. Homework assignment slide
- **Drills:**
  - Flashcard grid: ص ض ط ظ, named + sounded, shuffled.
  - Paired-contrast drill: س→ص, د→ض(closest), ت→ط, ذ→ظ — say the light one then its heavy twin, 5× each pair.
  - ض isolation drill: 10 slow repetitions, teacher demonstrates the side-of-tongue placement explicitly, no grading pressure given its documented difficulty.
  - Cumulative 17-letter shuffle: everything through lesson 1.4.
- **Audio:** Confirmed gap (Resource key). Primary = live teacher voice — this lesson especially benefits from the teacher pre-auditioning the linked videos, since tafkheem quality is subtle and easy to under-teach from memory alone.
- **Video:**
  - Arabic101 Makharij Lesson 7 (ض): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gEBzZLbuo-4`
  - Arabic101 bonus — "Follow these SEVEN STEPS to pronounce ض correctly": `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nz7BUoOpcXA`
  - Arabic101 Makharij Lesson 11 (ط): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XeQ5cxhkzlE`
  - Arabic101 Makharij Lesson 12 (ص): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yhIFJiPqVqA`
  - Arabic101 Makharij Lesson 13 (ظ): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6_Qfe0g6uks`
  - Arabic101 Sifaat Lesson 4 ("Why some HEAVY letters should be HEAVIER than others"): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I1BEoHQzK8E`
  - Uzbek channel timestamps: ص `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=9113`, ط `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=9693`, ض `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=13986`, ظ `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=15072`
- **Teacher listen-for:**
  - **ص pronounced as plain س** — loses the "thick hiss"/full-mouth quality (`teaching-mistakes-assessment.md` A.2).
  - **ض** — weak or absent lateral release, produced as a plain د or a "z"-like sound instead; explicitly documented as *the* hardest letter for all non-native speakers and specifically named for Persian/Turkish/Urdu-background learners (A.2, A.3) — do not expect mastery in one lesson, but do flag every instance for homework focus.
  - **ط pronounced like plain ت** — loses the held, forceful, full-mouth-echo release (A.2).
  - **ظ pronounced like plain ذ** — loses heaviness (A.2); note even some native Arabic dialects merge ظ/ض, so approximate imperfection here is normal, not alarming.
  - Watch for tafkheem "bleeding into the vowel" starting to show up even at this pre-vowel stage — if the student adds any vowel-like release after a heavy letter, check that it already sounds "backed"/rounded, not flat (A.2 — this becomes fully testable once Unit 1.3 adds real vowels).
- **Homework (15–20 min/day):** Flashcard sheet ص ض ط ظ with their light twins alongside for contrast. Daily paired-contrast drill (light→heavy, 5× each pair). Extra 3 minutes on ض alone, no pressure, just repetition. Cumulative shuffle of all 17 letters.

### Lesson 1.5 — Deep throat & back-of-tongue: ع غ ف ق

- **Objectives:**
  1. Student can identify and pronounce ع, غ, ف, ق in isolation on sight.
  2. Student can produce ع without collapsing it into ء (hamza/glottal stop).
  3. Student can distinguish ق from ك (introduced next lesson, previewed today as the contrast target).
- **New content:** ع غ shape-family (same body, no dot / dot above); ف ق shape-family (same/similar body, one dot above / two dots above). Two deep-throat/back-of-tongue letters (ع, ق) that are both flagged in the sourced research as commonly flattened by non-Arab learners toward "easier" nearby sounds.
- **Slide outline:**
  1. Title + cumulative recap (17 letters, quick shuffle)
  2. Letter: ع (ayn) — shape, sound (a mid-throat muscular squeeze/constriction, voiced — genuinely has no equivalent in English or Turkic languages), makhraj = middle of the throat
  3. Guided ع drill: teacher demonstrates the throat-squeeze explicitly, contrasts against a plain glottal stop (which is what ء sounds like) so the student hears the difference is a real muscular squeeze, not just "starting a vowel abruptly"
  4. Letter: غ (ghayn) — shape, sound (a uvular fricative, described as close to a French uvular "r" but must stay a fricative not a stop), makhraj = upper throat, same zone as خ but voiced
  5. Contrast drill: غ vs خ (from lesson 1.2) — same origin point, only voicing differs
  6. Letter: ف (fa) — shape, sound (plain "f"), makhraj = inner lower lip against the edge of the upper front teeth
  7. Letter: ق (qaaf) — shape, sound (a deep, uvular "cough-like" stop from the very back of the tongue against the soft palate — heavier and further back than ك), makhraj = back of tongue against the soft palate
  8. Preview-contrast slide: ق vs ك (ك not formally taught until next lesson, but flagged here as "the sound ق must NOT be flattened into") — cite that قال (a real word meaning "he said") vs a ك-flattened version is a genuine meaning-changing mix-up
  9. Full 4-letter shuffle: ع غ ف ق
  10. Cumulative 21-letter shuffle
  11. Guided sound-imitation round with teacher correction
  12. Wrap-up recap
  13. Homework assignment slide
- **Drills:**
  - Flashcard grid: ع غ ف ق, named + sounded, shuffled.
  - Minimal-pair listening drill: ع / ء(anticipatory, teacher voices only) — student points to which was said, tests the "is this a real throat squeeze or just a glottal stop" distinction early.
  - Minimal-pair listening drill: غ / خ — tests the voiced/unvoiced distinction at the same throat zone.
  - Cumulative 21-letter shuffle: everything through lesson 1.5.
- **Audio:** Confirmed gap (Resource key). Primary = live teacher voice.
- **Video:**
  - Arabic101 Makharij Lesson 2 (ء + هـ, useful contrast reference for ع): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bzz_wo6skWA`
  - Arabic101 Makharij Lesson 3 (ع + ح): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-oA0HfNUezI`
  - Arabic101 Makharij Lesson 5 (ق): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wwHmytGNi2M`
  - Arabic101 Makharij Lesson 14 (ف و م ب — ف covered here, و م ب covered next lesson): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sM27vvgq_os`
  - Uzbek channel timestamps: غ `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=12280`, ع `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=12720`, ف `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=6955`, ق `https://youtu.be/VhRHKdPcNPA?t=7275`
- **Teacher listen-for:**
  - **ع substituted with ء** — flattening the muscular throat-squeeze into a plain glottal stop, described in the sourced research as happening because "no equivalent phoneme exists in English or Turkic languages" (`teaching-mistakes-assessment.md` A.1). This is the single highest-priority correction in this lesson.
  - **غ confused with خ**, or blended toward ق — same-zone voicing confusion (A.1).

  - ف — usually low-risk, but do not let "easy-looking" letters get sloppy articulation just because they're comfortable (per Arabic101's own framing of lesson 14's "easy letters with tricky mistakes").
- **Homework (15–20 min/day):** Flashcard sheet ع غ ف ق. Daily ع-isolation drill: 10 slow repetitions of the throat-squeeze, explicitly not a glottal stop. Minimal-pair self-check ق vs (anticipated) ك using the linked videos. Cumulative 21-letter shuffle.

## Unit 1.2 — Letter forms & joining (Lessons 1.7–1.9)

### Lesson 1.7 — Letters change shape: initial / medial / final / isolated forms

- **Objectives:**
  1. Student can explain that most Arabic letters take up to 4 different shapes depending on position in a word (isolated / initial / medial / final), while the sound stays the same.
  2. Student can recognize a letter's initial, medial, and final forms on sight for at least 6 high-frequency letters.

  10. Wrap-up recap
  11. Homework assignment slide

### Lesson 1.8 — The 6 letters that never connect forward: ا د ذ ر ز و

- **Objectives:**
  1. Student can name the 6 non-connecting letters (ا د ذ ر ز و) and state the one rule they share: they never join to the letter that follows them, even mid-word.
  2. Student can read a cluster containing a non-connector and correctly identify the resulting visual "break" rather than misreading it as a joined shape.
  3. Student can distinguish, on sight, whether a gap between two letters in a cluster is (a) a genuine word-break or (b) simply a non-connector letter doing what it always does.
- **New content:** The single most important structural exception in Arabic joining mechanics. All 6 of today's letters were already learned in Unit 1.1 — this lesson is pure mechanics, no new sounds.
- **Slide outline:**
  1. Title + recap: quick review cluster-reading from lesson 1.7
  2. Concept: 6 letters — ا د ذ ر ز و — never connect to the letter AFTER them (they still connect fine to the letter before them). This is a fixed, closed list — no other letters behave this way.
  3. Worked example: د in a 3-letter cluster — show how the letter following د stays in its own isolated/initial form rather than a connected medial form
  4. Worked example: ر behaving the same way
  5. Worked example: و behaving the same way
  6. Contrast drill: show 2 similar-looking clusters, one where a mid-cluster "gap" is just a non-connector doing its normal job, one where it's a genuine two-word boundary — student learns these look different once they know the rule (a non-connector's own shape doesn't change, whereas a true word-boundary is a full spacing gap)
  7. Guided recognition drill: teacher shows a cluster, student identifies any non-connector letters present and predicts where the "break" in joining will appear before seeing the answer
  8. Reading drill: read a set of clusters that deliberately mix connectors and non-connectors
  9. Cumulative check: re-read 2 of lesson 1.7's clusters, now correctly explaining why they joined the way they did
  10. Wrap-up recap
  11. Homework assignment slide
- **Drills:**
  - Concept flashcard: the 6 non-connectors listed together as a fixed set to memorize by recognition (ا د ذ ر ز و) — drilled as a group chant/list until automatic.
  - Cluster-reading drill (unvoweled): درس — ردا — وزن — ذرا — ازد — كتاد (mix of clusters, each containing at least one non-connector, positioned in different spots — first, middle, last — so the student sees the rule apply regardless of position within the cluster).
  - "Predict the break" drill: teacher shows an unvoweled cluster with a non-connector circled, student predicts (before reading aloud) where the joined/unjoined shape boundary will fall, then reads it to confirm.
- **Audio:** Confirmed gap (Resource key); not audio-new content, teacher voice models cluster reading live.
- **Video:**
  - Arabic101 — "Letter FUSION in the Holy Quran" (general joining-mechanics cross-check, Phase 2-oriented but conceptually relevant here): `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NOCKyEcgW7U`
  - Uzbek channel per-letter series (same playlist as lesson 1.7, useful as a "watch native-speaker reading" reference): `https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLgrueUfOSy6toR7sz27dmPK2WPBNVLwRi`
- **Teacher listen-for:**
  - Student trying to force a non-connector into a joined medial shape that doesn't exist for it — a very natural over-generalization error at this stage, gently correct by re-showing the fixed list.
  - Student misreading a non-connector's normal "gap" as a full word-break, splitting one word into two when reading aloud.
  - Regression on the 6 individual letters' own sounds (since these are "old" content from Unit 1.1, a quick sound-accuracy spot-check is worthwhile alongside the shape-mechanics focus).
- **Homework (15–20 min/day):** Daily recitation (aloud, from the printed sheet) of the 6 non-connector clusters from today's drill, 3× through. Student attempts the "predict the break" exercise on 3 new clusters the teacher provides for next lesson (recognition/prediction only, no writing). ## Checkpoint 1 Kit

**Gate purpose:** confirm instant recognition of any letter in any position, acceptable makhraj for every letter, and the ability to read any voweled syllable — exactly the three criteria named in the course spec. A failed checkpoint **never** means restarting Phase 1; it triggers targeted revision of the specific weak unit(s) named below, then a retest of only that material.

### Test script (live, ~30–40 minutes)

1. **Warm-up recognition pass (3–4 min).** Full 28-letter flashcard set, fully randomized order (not grouped by shape-family). Student names each letter and produces its sound. No scoring pressure communicated — this is a warm-up, but the teacher quietly notes any hesitation/error on the rubric sheet.
2. **Makhraj spot-check (8–10 min).** Teacher selects 10 letters at random, weighted toward the high-risk set accumulated across Phase 1 (ح خ ه ع ء ص ض ط ظ ذ ز ق ك ر — pick 10 of these 14 each sitting so no two checkpoints are identical). For each: student produces the sound AND states in plain English where it's articulated (e.g., "mid-throat," "tip of tongue between the teeth," "the two lips"). Tests self-aware correctness, not just rote muscle memory.
3. **Positional reading (5–7 min).** Teacher presents 6 unvoweled 2–3 letter clusters built to include at least 2 non-connector letters (ا د ذ ر ز و) and at least 2 letters shown in medial form. Student reads each aloud, correctly identifying every component letter regardless of shape.
4. **Vowel grid spot-check (8–10 min).** Teacher samples 20 cells at random from the full 84-cell letter × harakat grid (not a fixed pre-known set — genuinely randomized each sitting so the student cannot pre-memorize a specific sequence). Student reads each cell aloud.
5. **Syllable transfer test (5–7 min).** Teacher presents 5–6 abstract 2–3 syllable combinations the student has **not** seen in any prior lesson or homework sheet (freshly built from the same letter/vowel inventory). Student decodes each — this is the one genuinely unseen-material test in the checkpoint, mirroring Checkpoint 2's later "unseen" standard at a smaller scale.
6. **Wrap-up (2–3 min).** Teacher completes the rubric sheet with the student present, states the pass/revise result plainly, and — if revision is needed — names the specific unit(s) to revisit (see mapping table below) rather than "do Phase 1 again."

### Pass rubric

Adapted directly from the Lahn Jali / Lahn Khafi scoring convention documented in `docs/research/teaching-mistakes-assessment.md` Part B (a live qiraat-competition marking scheme, cross-checked against a teacher's-guide source and the ijazah-correction literature).

| Error tier | Definition for Phase 1 purposes | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Lahn Jali (major)** | Wrong letter identified entirely; wrong sound produced (not recognizably the target phoneme); wrong vowel read (e.g. kasra read as fatha); a non-connector's joining rule violated so badly the cluster is misread as different letters/words | Hard-fail signal for that item |
| **Lahn Khafi (minor)** | Correct letter and correct vowel, but a quality issue — heaviness (tafkheem) noticeably under- or over-applied, ر's tongue-tap too static/too trilled, minor timing wobble on vowel length that doesn't cross into a full madd | Flagged, not failed — tolerated up to the cap below |

**Pass thresholds:**
- **Zero Jali errors** across the 28-letter warm-up pass and the makhraj spot-check. Any letter that is unrecognizable or produced as the wrong phoneme is a hard-fail item regardless of how well everything else goes.
- **≤3 total Khafi flags** across the whole session → **pass, with a homework flag** naming the specific letters/qualities to keep practicing at home (no retest required, just noted).
- **4+ Khafi flags**, or **any single Jali error** on more than 2 distinct letters, or **2+ Jali errors** in the vowel-grid spot-check, or **failure to decode 3+ of the 5–6 syllable-transfer items** → **checkpoint does not pass**; targeted revision assigned per the mapping table below, retest of the failed area(s) only, typically after about 1 week of focused home practice.
- The syllable-transfer test (step 5) is scored more leniently on hesitation/self-correction (a student who stumbles but self-corrects without teacher intervention should not be penalized as harshly as one who cannot produce the sound at all) — mirrors the "self-correction after a prompt" vs. "examiner has to correct" distinction in the source rubric.

### Weak-spot → unit revision mapping

| If the checkpoint reveals a weakness in… | Revise this lesson (or lessons) before retesting |
|---|---|
| ح / خ / ه confusion (any direction) | Lesson 1.2 and Lesson 1.6 |
| ص / ض / ط / ظ tafkheem quality (heaviness under- or mis-applied) | Lesson 1.4 |
| ع substituted with ء, or غ/خ/ق confusion | Lesson 1.5 |
| ذ / ز / ض "D→Z" collapse pattern (any pair merging) | Lessons 1.2, 1.3, and 1.4 (the cluster is taught across these three — revise all three briefly rather than guessing which one letter is the culprit) |
| ر tongue-tap quality (too static/English-style) | Lesson 1.3 |
| ق flattened toward ك (or vice versa) | Lesson 1.5 and Lesson 1.6 |
| Positional-shape misreading (initial/medial/final confusion) | Lesson 1.7 |
| Non-connector rule not applied (ا د ذ ر ز و) | Lesson 1.8 |
| General cluster-reading hesitation/pace, no specific letter culprit | Lesson 1.9 |
| Fatha timing/quality (lengthened into madd, or heavy-letter darkening missing) | Lesson 1.10 |
| Kasra/damma timing/quality, or kasra-vs-damma mix-ups | Lesson 1.11 |
| Syllable-transfer decoding failures with no single letter/vowel culprit (a fluency issue, not an accuracy issue) | Lesson 1.12 — assign extra cold-read syllable sets, not a full grid re-drill |

**Retest principle (per spec):** only the named unit(s) are revisited and retested — never a full Phase 1 restart. A student who fails only the ض/tafkheem row, for example, gets a short focused re-run of Lesson 1.4's drills and a retest limited to steps 2 and 4 of the test script (makhraj spot-check + vowel grid spot-check), not the entire 30–40 minute session.
