# Glossary — Uzbek · Arabic · English

The course owner learned tajweed in Uzbek, from [[Muallimi-Soniy]]. **Where an Uzbek
term is surfaced anywhere in the course, use the forms in this table — not scholarly
ALA-LC transliteration.** A student of that channel recognises `ixfo`, not `ikhfāʾ`.

Every Uzbek term below was extracted verbatim from that channel's video titles. The
Arabic and English columns are mappings.

## The naturalisation pattern

Arabic **ā** consistently surfaces as Uzbek **o**. That single shift explains most of
the table: `ikhfāʾ → ixfo`, `iẓhār → izhor`, `iqlāb → iqlob`, `lāzim → lozim`,
`ʿāriḍ → oriz` — the same shift that gives *Qur'on*, *namoz*, *Alloh*.

Two further habits worth knowing:

- **Ghunnah is expressed with Uzbek suffixes, not the Arabic genitive.** `ғunnali` /
  `ғunnasiz` (with / without ghunnah) rather than *bi-ghunnah* / *bilā ghunnah*.
- **Some rules are numbered locally rather than named.** The channel teaches "alif-lam
  rule 1/2/3/4" and "waqf rule 1/2/4/5". A student of it may not recognise
  [[Lam-Shamsiyyah]] or [[Lam-Qamariyyah]] by name — **introduce those as new
  vocabulary, not as review.**

## Where the Uzbek course diverges

Recorded so the difference is a deliberate teaching moment rather than a surprise.

1. **Its noon rules run idghām → iqlāb → iẓhār → ikhfāʾ.** Nearly every other syllabus,
   including [[Tuhfat-al-Atfal]], runs iẓhār first. This course follows the standard
   order — flag the difference when introducing the family.
2. **Madd is taught twice** — practically at its lessons 2–4, then as a full taxonomy at
   49–56. This course does the same thing across Unit 2 and Unit 3.6.
3. **Makhārij is absent from its tajweed series entirely**; articulation lives in that
   channel's separate 35-lesson alphabet course. This course treats makhārij as tajweed,
   following [[Muqaddimah-Jazariyyah]].
4. **Its lessons 70–77 are the six Kalimas** — Hanafi/Central Asian catechism, not
   tajweed. This course keeps them, as Unit 4, by the owner's decision.
