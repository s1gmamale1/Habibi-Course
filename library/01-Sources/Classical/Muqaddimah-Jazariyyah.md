# Al-Muqaddimah al-Jazariyyah

The comprehensive intermediate *matn*, by Ibn al-Jazari (d. 833 AH / 1429 CE),
the single most authoritative figure in the science of qira'at. Where
[[Tuhfat-al-Atfal]] is narrow by design, this covers what Tuhfah omits:
makharij, sifat, the definition and obligation of tajweed, tafkhim/tarqiq, the
rulings of ra' and lam, the ض/ظ distinction, waqf and ibtida', and mushaf
orthography.

**This course uses Jazariyyah's ordering as its spine.** See "Why this ordering"
below.

**On the 107 / 109 discrepancy.** This is not a transcription error, and it is
worth understanding rather than papering over. The matn's own closing couplet
counts itself at 107 by abjad. The retrieved edition prints 109 — the extra two
are the final doxological verses 108–109, which some recensions treat as the
poet's and others as a later addition. This is exactly the "~107–109 verses,
editions vary" situation the literature describes. **The verse numbering below
is the retrieved edition's own**, so any cross-reference to a printed edition
numbered to 107 will drift by up to two in the closing verses only; verses
1–107 are unaffected.

**Two sources, one much better.** The Arabic Wikisource copy of this matn is
**entirely unvocalised** (zero harakat in the whole file) and runs to 108
verses under 16 headings. An unvocalised matn is close to useless for a text
meant to be memorised and recited. The primary source above is fully
vocalised, verse-numbered, and chapter-numbered, and its verse text agrees with
Wikisource's throughout where the two were spot-compared. The vocalised copy
is used here; Wikisource is retained only as a cross-check on the wording.

**`status: needs-review`** because the English rendering is unreviewed and the

course maps against.

The edition printed below numbers **17** chapters, not 15. The difference is
purely one of splitting and sequence, not of content — no verse is present in
one scheme and absent from the other. Recorded because anyone collating the
text below against the table above will hit it immediately:

| 9 — باب التَّحْذِيرَاتِ (v.60–61) | **split out**; absorbed into ch. 8 in the 15-bab scheme |
| 13 — باب معرفة الوقف والابتداء (v.73–78) | ch. 12 |
| 14 — باب المَقْطُوعِ وَالمَوْصُولِ (v.79–93) | ch. 13 |

The two real ordering divergences are: **(a)** the miscellaneous chapter and the
ض/ظ chapter are swapped, and **(b)** rawm/ishmam are pulled out of the waqf
chapter into a chapter of their own at the very end.

## Why this ordering is the course spine

Jazariyyah opens with **makharij** (ch. 1) and **sifat** (ch. 2), then defines
tajweed itself (ch. 3) as *"giving the letters their due (ḥaqq) of the
characteristics belonging to them, and what they merit (mustaḥaqq)"* — verse 30.
That definition is the poem's own architecture made explicit:

> **ḥaqq al-ḥarf** — what a letter is owed *always*, independent of context: its
> articulation point and its intrinsic characteristics. Chapters 1–2.
>
> **mustaḥaqq al-ḥarf** — what a letter is owed *because of its neighbours*:
> tafkhim/tarqiq, the ra' and lam rulings, ghunnah, the noon and meem rules,
> madd, waqf. Chapters 4 onward.

Every contextual rule presupposes that the student can already produce the
letter correctly in isolation. Tuhfah does not enforce this — it starts at
noon sakinah with no articulation content at all, because it assumes a teacher
has already drilled the letters orally. A written course cannot assume that.

> **Decision: this course follows Jazariyyah's haqq-before-mustahaqq ordering,
> and uses [[Tuhfat-al-Atfal]] for drill content inside it.** Tuhfah's
> mnemonics and its far more granular madd taxonomy (10 verses on madd lazim
> against Jazariyyah's four-verse treatment of madd entire) are the memorisable
> payload; Jazariyyah supplies the skeleton and everything Tuhfah leaves out.

One adjustment is still needed: Jazariyyah places **madd** at chapter 11, near
the end, after all the noon and meem rules. That is late relative to how the
rule is actually used — it is unavoidable from the first ayah a learner reads.
Where the course departs from the spine here, it is recorded in the curriculum
notes rather than treated as following the matn.

# The matn

Arabic verbatim from the retrieved source. English is a working prose rendering
aimed at technical accuracy per hemistich, **not** a published or poetic
translation. Mnemonic strings are given in upper case because they are
letter-carriers, not sentences.

**Chapters 8, 14 and 15 are enumerations, not exposition.** Verses 53–59, 80–93
and 94–100 are dense lists of individual Qur'anic words and the surahs they
occur in, compressed to fit the metre. They are given here with a **gloss of
what the list is doing**, not a word-by-word translation: rendering them
literally produces English that is both unreadable and easy to get wrong, and
their content is only usable as a lookup table anyway. Anyone who needs those
chapters operationally should work from the Arabic with a commentary.

| 38 | فِيهَا وَفِي الجِيمِ؛ كَ «حُبِّ، الصَّبْرِ … رَبْوَةٍ، اجْتُثَّتْ، وَحَجِّ، الفَجْرِ» | in them and in the **jim** — as in ḤUBBI, AṢ-ṢABRI, RABWATIN, IJTUTHTHAT, WA-ḤAJJI, AL-FAJRI. |
| 39 | وَبَيِّنَنْ مُقَلْقَلاً إِنْ سَكَنَا … وَإِنْ يَكُنْ فِي الوَقْفِ كَانَ أَبْيَنَا | And make a **qalqalah** letter distinct when it carries sukun — and when it falls at a **stop**, it is more distinct still. |
| 40 | وَحَاءَ: «حَصْحَصَ، أَحَطْتُ، الحَقُّ» … وَسِينَ: «مُسْتَقِيمِ، يَسْطُو، يَسْقُو» | And the **ha'** of ḤAṢḤAṢA, AḤAṬTU, AL-ḤAQQU; and the **sin** of MUSTAQĪMI, YASṬŪ, YASQŪ. |

## 5 — بَابُ الرَّاءَات — The rulings of ra'

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 41 | وَرَقِّقِ الرَّاءَ إِذَا مَا كُسِرَتْ … كَذَاكَ بَعْدَ الكَسْرِ حَيْثُ سَكَنَتْ | **Thin the ra'** when it carries a kasra; likewise after a kasra, wherever it carries sukun — |
| 42 | إِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ مِنْ قَبْلِ حَرْفِ اسْتِعْلَا … أَوْ كَانَتِ الكَسْرَةُ لَيْسَتْ أَصْلَا | provided it is **not before a letter of elevation**, and the kasra is **original**, not incidental. |
| 43 | وَالخُلْفُ فِي «فِرْقٍ»؛ لِكَسْرٍ يُوجَدُ … وَأَخْفِ تَكْرِيراً إِذَا تُشَدَّدُ | And there is **disagreement over FIRQIN**, on account of a kasra found there. And **conceal the trilling** (*takrir*) when the ra' is doubled. |

## 6 — بَابُ اللَّامَات — The rulings of lam

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 44 | وَفَخِّمِ اللَّامَ مِنِ اسْمِ «اللَّهِ» … عَنْ فَتْحٍ اَوْ ضَمٍّ كَ «عَبْدُ اللَّهِ» | **Thicken the lam** of the name ALLĀH after a **fatha or a damma** — as in ʿABDU-LLĀH. |

Note how short this is. Jazariyyah gives the lam **one verse**, covering only
the lam of *lafz al-jalalah*; the shamsiyyah/qamariyyah distinction is not
treated here at all. [[Tuhfat-al-Atfal]] verses 24–29 supply it.

## 7 — بَابُ أَحْكَامٍ مُتَفَرِّقَة — Miscellaneous rulings

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|

| 46 | وَبَيِّنِ الإِطْبَاقَ مِنْ «أَحَطْتُ» مَعْ … «بَسَطْتَ»، وَالخُلْفُ بِ «نَخْلُقْكُمْ» وَقَعْ | And make the **covering** (*itbaq*) distinct in AḤAṬTU and BASAṬTA; and disagreement falls on NAKHLUQKUM. |
| 47 | وَاحْرِصْ عَلَى السُّكُونِ فِي «جَعَلْنَا» … «أَنْعَمْتَ» وَ «المَغْضُوبِ» مَعْ «ضَلَلْنَا» | Be keen on the **sukun** in JAʿALNĀ, ANʿAMTA, AL-MAGHḌŪBI, and ḌALALNĀ. |

| 49 | وَرَاعِ شِدَّةً بِكَافٍ وَبِتَا … كَ «شِرْكِكُمْ» وَ «تَتَوَفَّى، فِتْنَتَا» | Observe the **plosiveness** in the kaf and the ta — as in SHIRKIKUM, TATAWAFFĀ, FITNATĀ. |
| 50 | وَأَوَّلَيْ مِثْلٍ وَجِنْسٍ إِنْ سَكَنْ … أَدْغِمْ؛ كَ «قُلْ رَبِّ» وَ «بَلْ لَا»، وَأَبِنْ | The first of two **alike** or of the **same kind**, when it carries sukun — **assimilate** it; as in QUL RABBI and BAL LĀ. But make distinct: |
| 51 | «فِي يَوْمِ» مَعْ «قَالُوا وَهُمْ» وَ «قُلْ نَعَمْ» … «سَبِّحْهُ، لَا تُزِغْ قُلُوبَ، فَالْتَقَمْ» | FĪ YAWMI, QĀLŪ WA-HUM, QUL NAʿAM, SABBIḤHU, LĀ TUZIGH QULŪBA, FALTAQAM. |

## 8 — بَابُ الضَّادِ وَالظَّاء — Distinguishing ض from ظ

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 52 | وَالضَّادَ بِاسْتِطَالَةٍ وَمَخْرَجِ … مَيِّزْ مِنَ الظَّاءِ، وَكُلُّهَا تَجِي | Distinguish the **dad** from the **za'** by its **elongation** and its **articulation point**. And all of [the za's occurrences] come |
| 53 | فِي «الظَّعْنِ، ظِلُّ، الظُّهْرِ، عُظْمُ، الحِفْظِ … أَيْقِظْ، وَأَنْظِرْ، عَظْمَ، ظَهْرِ، اللَّفْظِ | *[List, 1 of 7.]* An exhaustive enumeration of every root spelled with ظ in the Qur'an, so a reciter can be certain which of the two letters a given word takes. |
| 54 | ظَاهِرْ، لَظَى، شُوَاظُ، كَظْمٍ، ظَلَمَا … أُغْلُظْ، ظَلَامَ، ظُفْرٍ، انْتَظِرْ، ظَمَا | *[List, 2 of 7.]* Continues the ظ inventory. |
| 55 | أَظْفَرَ، ظَنّاً كَيْفَ جَا، وَعِظْ» سِوَى … «عِضِينَ»، «ظَلَّ»: النَّحْلِ، زُخْرُفٍ؛ سَوَا | *[List, 3 of 7.]* Continues, and begins flagging the near-minimal pairs that must **not** be confused (`عِضِين` with ض against the ظ words). |
| 56 | وَ «ظَلْتَ، ظَلْتُمْ» وَبِرُومٍ: «ظَلُّوا» … كَالحِجْرِ: «ظَلَّتْ» شُعَرَا: «نَظَلُّ» | *[List, 4 of 7.]* Forms of ظلّ, located by surah (ar-Rum, al-Hijr, ash-Shu'ara'). |
| 57 | «يَظْلَلْنَ، مَحْظُوراً» مَعَ «المُحْتَظِرِ» … وَ «كُنْتَ فَظّاً» وَجَمِيعَ «النَّظَرِ» | *[List, 5 of 7.]* Continues, including all forms of the root نظر. |
| 58 | إِلَّا بِ «وَيْلٌ» «هَلْ» وَأُولَى «نَاضِرَهْ» … وَ «الغَيْظُ» لَا الرَّعْدُ وَهُودٌ؛ قَاصِرَهْ | *[List, 6 of 7.]* The **exceptions**: places where the similar-looking word takes ض instead (`نَاضِرَة`), and surah-specific restrictions. |
| 59 | وَ «الحَظُّ» لَا «الحَضُّ عَلَى الطَّعَامِ» … وَفِي «ظَنِينٍ»: الخِلَافُ سَامِي | *[List, 7 of 7.]* The pair الحَظّ (ظ) against الحَضّ (ض); and notes that **ظَنِين is disputed** between the readings. |

## 9 — بَابُ التَّحْذِيرَات — Warnings (ض/ظ in contact)

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 60 | وَإِنْ تَلَاقَيَا: البَيَانُ لَازِمُ … «أَنْقَضَ ظَهْرَكَ، يَعَضُّ الظَّالِمُ» | And when the two [ض and ظ] **meet**, keeping them distinct is **obligatory** — as in ANQAḌA ẒAHRAK, YAʿAḌḌU-Ẓ-ẒĀLIM. |
| 61 | وَ «اضْطُرَّ» مَعْ «وَعَظْتَ» مَعْ «أَفَضْتُمُ» … وَصَفِّ «هَا»: «جِبَاهُهُمْ، عَلَيْهِمُ» | And IḌṬURRA, WAʿAẒTA, AFAḌTUM. And keep the **ha' pure** in JIBĀHUHUM, ʿALAYHIMU. |

## 10 — بَابُ مِيمٍ وَنُونٍ مُشَدَّدَيْنِ وَالْمِيمِ السَّاكِنَة — Doubled meem/noon and meem sakinah

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 62 | وأَظْهِرِ الغُنَّةَ مِنْ: نُونٍ، وَمِنْ … مِيمٍ؛ إِذَا مَا شُدِّدَا، وَأَخْفِيَنْ | **Make the ghunnah manifest** in a noon and in a meem whenever they are **doubled**. And **conceal** |
| 63 | المِيمَ إِنْ تَسْكُنْ بِغُنَّةٍ لَدَى … بَاءٍ عَلَى المُخْتَارِ مِنْ أَهْلِ الأَدَا | the meem, when it carries sukun, **with ghunnah, before a ba'** — this being the preferred view of the people of transmission. |
| 64 | وَأَظْهِرَنْهَا عِنْدَ: بَاقِي الأَحْرُفِ … وَاحْذَرْ لَدَى وَاوٍ وَفَا: أَنْ تَخْتَفِي | And **make it clear** before the remaining letters — and beware, before **waw and fa'**, of letting it be concealed. |

Note that Jazariyyah, unlike [[Tuhfat-al-Atfal]], puts **ghunnah first** (v.62)
and derives the meem-sakinah rules from it in the same chapter. Its ordering
needs no correction; Tuhfah's does.

## 11 — بَابُ النُّونِ وَالتَّنْوِين — Noon sakinah and tanween

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 65 | وَحُكْمُ تَنْوِينٍ وَنُونٍ يُلْفَى … إِظْهَارٌ، ادْغَامٌ، وَقَلْبٌ، إِخْفَا | The ruling of a tanween and a noon is found to be: **izhar, idgham, qalb, ikhfa**. |
| 66 | فَعِنْدَ حَرْفِ الحَلْقِ: أَظْهِرْ، وَادَّغِمْ … فِي اللَّامِ وَالرَّا لَا بِغُنَّةٍ لَزِمْ | So before a **throat letter**: pronounce clearly. And **assimilate into lam and ra'** — without ghunnah — as required. |
| 67 | وَأَدْغِمَنْ بِغُنَّةٍ فِي: «يُومِنُ» … إِلَّا بِكِلْمَةٍ؛ كَ «دُنْيَا، عَنْوَنُوا» | And **assimilate with ghunnah** into YŪMIN [ي و م ن] — except within a single word, as in DUNYĀ, ʿANWANŪ. |
| 68 | وَالقَلْبُ عِنْدَ: البَا بِغُنَّةٍ، كَذَا … لِاخْفَا لَدَى: بَاقِي الحُرُوفِ؛ أُخِذَا | And **conversion** (*qalb*) before the ba', with ghunnah; likewise **concealment** before the remaining letters — so it has been received. |

## 12 — بَابُ الْمَدِّ وَالْقَصْر — Madd and shortening

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 69 | وَالمَدُّ: لَازِمٌ، وَوَاجِبٌ أَتَى … وَجَائِزٌ، وَهْوَ وَقَصْرٌ ثَبَتَا | Madd is: **necessary** (*lazim*), **obligatory** (*wajib*), and **permissible** (*ja'iz*) — in which both it and shortening are established. |
| 70 | فَلَازِمٌ: إِنْ جَاءَ بَعْدَ حَرْفِ مَدْ … سَاكِنُ حَالَيْنِ، وَبِالطُّولِ يُمَدْ | **Necessary**: if after a madd letter there comes a sukun **in both states** [continuing and stopping] — and it is lengthened to the **long** measure. |
| 71 | وَوَاجِبٌ: إِنْ جَاءَ قَبْلَ هَمْزَةِ … مُتَّصِلاً؛ إِنْ جُمِعَا بِكِلْمَةِ | **Obligatory**: if it comes before a hamza, **joined** — when the two are gathered in a single word. |
| 72 | وَجَائِزٌ: إِذَا أَتَى مُنْفَصِلَا … أَوْ عَرَضَ السُّكُونُ وَقْفاً مُسْجَلَا | **Permissible**: when it comes **separated**, or when the sukun is **accidental**, arising at a stop, as recorded. |

Four verses for the whole of madd. [[Tuhfat-al-Atfal]] spends 23 (verses
35–57) on the same material, including the badal and lin categories that
Jazariyyah does not name here at all — which is precisely why Tuhfah is the
drill text for this topic.

## 13 — بَابُ مَعْرِفَةِ الْوَقْفِ وَالاِبْتِدَاء — Waqf and ibtida'

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 73 | وَبَعْدَ تَجْوِيدِكَ لِلْحُرُوفِ … لَا بُدَّ مِنْ مَعْرِفَةِ الوُقُوفِ | And **after** your perfecting of the letters, there is no escaping knowledge of the **stops** |
| 74 | وَالِابْتِدَاءِ، وَهْيَ تُقْسَمُ إِذَنْ … ثَلَاثَةً: تَامٌ، وَكَافٍ، وَحَسَنْ | and the **starts**. And it divides, then, into three: **complete** (*tam*), **sufficient** (*kafi*), and **good** (*hasan*). |
| 75 | وَهْيَ لِمَا تَمَّ؛ فَإِنْ لَمْ يُوجَدِ … تَعَلُّقٌ، أَوْ كَانَ مَعْنىً؛ فَابْتَدِي | These apply where the sense is complete; so if **no connection** is found — or only a connection of meaning — then begin [there]. |
| 76 | فَالتَّامُ، فَالكَافِي، وَلَفْظاً: فَامْنَعَنْ … إِلَّا رُؤُوسَ الآيِ جَوِّزْ، فَالحَسَنْ | So: the complete, then the sufficient; but where the connection is **grammatical**, forbid [starting] — except permit it at **verse-ends**; that is the good. |
| 77 | وَغَيْرُ مَا تَمَّ قَبِيحٌ، وَلَهُ … الوَقْفُ مُضْطَرّاً؛ وَيَبْدَا قَبْلَهُ | And what is other than complete is **ugly** (*qabih*) — one may stop there only **under necessity**, and must resume from before it. |
| 78 | وَلَيْسَ فِي القُرْآنِ مِنْ وَقْفٍ وَجَبْ … وَلَا حَرَامٌ؛ غَيْرُ مَا لَهُ سَبَبْ | And there is in the Qur'an **no stop that is obligatory, and none that is forbidden** — except where a particular cause makes it so. |

Verse 78 is the line that matters most for a beginner course: the mushaf's waqf
symbols are **guidance, not law**. Verse 73 places the whole topic explicitly
*after* mastery of the letters — another confirmation of the haqq-first spine.

## 14 — بَابُ الْمَقْطُوعِ وَالْمَوْصُول — Separated and joined spellings

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 79 | وَاعْرِفْ لِمَقْطُوعٍ وَمَوْصُولٍ وَ «تَا» … فِي المُصْحَفِ الإِمَامِ فِيمَا قَدْ أَتَى | And **know** the separated, the joined, and the "ta" — as they occur in the **master mushaf**. |
| 80–93 | *(fourteen verses of enumeration)* | *[Gloss.]* A complete list of the places where a particle that would normally be written joined to the following word (`أن لا`, `إن ما`, `عن ما`, `من ما`, `أم من`, `حيث ما`, `في ما`, `أين ما`, `كي لا`, `مال هذا` …) is instead **written separated** in the Uthmanic mushaf — or the reverse — located by surah, with the disputed cases flagged. ## 15 — بَابُ التَّاءَات — The ta's

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 94–100 | *(seven verses of enumeration)* | *[Gloss.]* A complete list of the words where a feminine ending is written with an **open ta' (ت)** rather than the expected **closed ta' marbuta (ة)** — `رحمت`, `نعمت`, `لعنت`, `امرأت`, `معصيت`, `شجرت`, `سنت`, `قرت`, `جنت`, `فطرت`, `بقيت`, `ابنت`, `كلمت` — each located by surah. ## 16 — بَابُ هَمْزِ الْوَصْل — The connecting hamzah

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 101 | وَابْدَأْ بِهَمْزِ الوَصْلِ مِنْ فِعْلٍ بِضَمْ … إِنْ كَانَ ثَالِثٌ مِنَ الفِعْلِ يُضَمْ | **Begin** the connecting hamza of a **verb with a damma**, if the verb's **third letter** takes a damma; |

## 17 — بَابُ الْوَقْفِ عَلَى أَوَاخِرِ الْكَلِم — Stopping on word-endings (rawm and ishmam)

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 104 | وَحَاذِرِ الوَقْفَ بِكُلِّ الحَرَكَهْ … إِلَّا إِذَا رُمْتَ فَبَعْضُ الحَرَكَهْ | **Beware of stopping on a full vowel** — except when you perform **rawm**, in which case [you sound] *part* of the vowel; |

| 107 | أَبْيَاتُهَا قَافٌ وَزَاىٌ فِي الْعَدَدْ … مَنْ يُحْسِنِ التَّجْوِيدَ يَظْفَرْ بِالرَّشَدْ | **Its verses are qaf and zay in number** [100 + 7 = **107**]. Whoever perfects tajweed attains right guidance. |
| 108 | وَالحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ لَهَا خِتَامُ … ثُمَّ الصَّلَاةُ بَعْدُ وَالسَّلَامُ | And praise be to God — that is its conclusion; then blessings thereafter, and peace, |
| 109 | عَلَى النَّبِىِّ المُصْطَفى وَآلِهِ … وَصَحْبِهِ وتابعِ منوالهِ | upon the Prophet, the Chosen One, and his family, his companions, and whoever follows his way. |

Verses 108–109 are the two that carry the edition past the author's stated count
of 107. Note also that verse 109 is the only verse in the retrieved text whose
Arabic is visibly less carefully set than the rest (`وتابعِ منوالهِ` is
under-vocalised where every other hemistich is fully marked) — consistent with
it being appended from a different exemplar.
