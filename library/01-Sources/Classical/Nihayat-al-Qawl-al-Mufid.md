# Nihayat al-Qawl al-Mufid

A **prose encyclopedia** of tajwīd, not a *matn*. Al-Juraysī compiled it from twenty-four
earlier works in the field, and it is discursive where the three poems in this vault are
compressed — which is exactly why it can settle questions they cannot. Where a *matn* names a
rule in half a line of verse and leaves the rest to a teacher, this book defines, exemplifies
and classifies.

**It is here for one reason: the secondary madds.** [[Tuhfat-al-Atfal]] scopes itself in its
own v. 3 to *"the noon, the tanwīn, and the madds"* but treats only the main taxonomy;
[[Muqaddimah-Jazariyyah]] gives madd four verses; [[Shatibiyyah]] enumerates where the seven
readers differ. None of them names *madd at-tamkīn* or the compensating alif at all. This book
carries **a twenty-item enumerated madd taxonomy**, each type named, defined and exemplified.

## Why it is public domain

The author died **c. 1322 AH / 1902 CE** — over a century ago, so the work is out of copyright
by any life-plus-70 reckoning. The Internet Archive copy is a scan of a printed edition with
no rights statement attached. **The text itself is what is public domain**; the vendored
excerpts below are from the classical text, not from any modern editor's apparatus.

## Excerpt 1 — the alif compensates for tanwīn at waqf

**Printed page ٩٤ (94)**, *al-Faṣl al-Khāmis*, quoting *"some of the commentators on
al-Jazariyyah"* — al-Juraysī is relaying here, not asserting in his own voice.

> قال بعض شراح الجزرية: اعلم أنّ الألف الساكنة المفتوح ما قبلها انفردت بأحوال ليست في غيرها.
> منها أنها تقع زائدة إذا لم تنقلب عن حرف آخر، فإن انقلبت كانت أصلية فتنقلب عن واو نحو قال
> وعن ياء نحو جاء وعن همزة نحو سال، **وتكون عوضا عن التنوين المنصوب في حال الوقف**، وتكون
> تابعة للحرف الذي قبلها.

*"…and it stands **as a compensation for the tanwīn manṣūb in the state of waqf**, and it
follows the letter before it."*

**What this settles and what it does not.** It attests the *mechanism* of [[Madd-Iwad]]
directly: stopping on tanwīn fatḥ yields an alif, and that alif is a *ʿiwaḍ*, a compensation.
It does **not** state a count. The 2 ḥarakāt follow from the alif being *sākinah with a fatḥah
before it* — the definition of a madd letter, and so of [[Madd-Tabii]], which **is** in the
vendored matns. The substitution is transmitted; the length is derived from a transmitted
definition.

The same sentence also settles a second thing in passing: the alif is **tābiʿah li'l-ḥarf
alladhī qablahā**, it follows the letter before it in tafkhīm and tarqīq. That is the rule
[[Alif]] teaches.

## Excerpt 2 — madd al-farq is lāzim kalimī

**Printed page ١٩٣ (193)**, item 11 of the enumeration.

> والحادي عشر: مدّ الفرق نحو قوله آلذكرين وآلله وآلسحر وآلآن في قراءة من مدّ. سمي بذلك للفرق
> بين الاستفهام والخبر **وهو من أقسام المدّ اللازم الكلمي المثقل أو المخفف كما تقدم**.

*"…it is so named for the distinction it draws between a question and a statement, **and it is
one of the divisions of madd lāzim kalimī, muthaqqal or mukhaffaf**, as has preceded."*

**This is an upgrade, not a confirmation.** [[Madd-Farq]] was verified from [[Shatibiyyah]]
v. 192, which gives the rule but **no number** — this vault recorded its 6 ḥarakāt as *derived
rather than transmitted*. Classing it under *lāzim kalimī* transmits the length, because that
category is 6 by definition in both vendored matns. The derivation can be retired.

Item 10 on the same page classes مد العدل (`وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ`) the same way —
*"ويسمى أيضًا باللازم الكلمي المثقل"* — which is the ordinary madd lāzim this course already
teaches, under a name it does not use.

## Excerpt 3 — madd at-tamkīn, defined

**Printed page ١٩٤ (194)**, item 15.

> والخامس عشر: مد التمكين وهو إذا اجتمعت الواو الساكنة المضموم ما قبلها مع واو أخرى نحو آمنوا
> وعملوا أو الياء الساكنة المكسور ما قبلها مع ياء أخرى نحو في يومين فيجب الفصل بين الواوين أو
> الياءين بمدة لطيفة بمقدار المد الطبيعي حذرا من الإدغام أو الإسقاط.

*"…when a wāw sākinah with a ḍammah before it meets another wāw, as in ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟,
or a yāʾ sākinah with a kasrah before it meets another yāʾ, as in فِى يَوْمَيْنِ — then the two
wāws or the two yāʾs **must be separated by a light madd of the measure of madd ṭabīʿī**,
guarding against assimilation or elision."*

**The book states the rule a second time, from the other direction** — at **printed page ١٤٩
(149)**, inside *al-Faṣl ath-Thālith fī bayān al-idghām aṣ-ṣaghīr*, as the **second condition
for idghām** rather than as a madd:

> الشرط الثاني: أن لا يكون حرف مدّ نحو آمنوا وعملوا والذي يوسوس ونحو إلا بإذنه يعلم وسبحانه أن
> يكون له ولد لئلا يذهب المدّ بالإدغام، **وهذا النوع هو المسمى عندهم بمدّ التمكين**، ومعنى
> التمكين أنه يجب على القارئ أن يفصل بين الواوين أو اليائين بمدّة لطيفة بمقدار المدّ الطبيعي
> حذرًا من الإدغام أو الإسقاط.

*"…that it not be a madd letter — as in ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟, ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ … — lest the
madd be lost to the assimilation. **And this type is what they call madd at-tamkīn**; the
meaning of tamkīn is that the reciter must separate the two wāws or the two yāʾs by a light
madd of the measure of madd ṭabīʿī, guarding against assimilation or elision."*

This is the better of the two statements, because it gives the rule's **purpose**: tamkīn
exists to stop idghām from swallowing a madd. It also supplies [[Madd-Tamkeen]]'s own worked
example `ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ` verbatim.

**Both of these cover only [[Madd-Tamkeen]]'s Form 2** — a madd letter meeting its own kind
across a word boundary. Form 1 is attested separately, below.

## Excerpt 5 — tamkīn's Form 1, attested under the requirement of *bayān*

**Printed page ١٠٤ (104)**, in the chapter on the yāʾ. Two passages, and the distinction
between them matters.

First, in al-Juraysī's own voice, the cross-word case — note the verb **تمكّن**, the root the
rule is named from:

> وإذا سكنت بعد كسر وأتى بعدها مثلها **وجب بيان كل منهما خشية الإدغام** لأنه غير جائز
> **وتمكّن الأولى لمدها** وليِّنها، وذلك نحو قوله – في يوسف – والذي يوسوس.

Then, quoting **al-Marʿashī**, the case with a doubled yāʾ — this is Form 1:

> وقال المرعشي: إذا تكررت الياء في كلمة أو كلمتين وجب بيانهما نحو وأحيينا وأن يحيىَ الموتى…
> **خصوصًا إذا كانت إحداهما مشدَّدة مكسورة** نحو إن وليي الله وأنت وليي في الدنيا **وإذا
> حييتم**… فإن لم يتحفظ أسقط إحداهما في التلاوة.

*"When the yāʾ is repeated within a word or across two, **both must be made distinct** …
**especially when one of them carries shaddah and kasrah**, as in … وَإِذَا حُيِّيتُم. If the
reciter is not careful, one of the two drops out of the recitation."*

**Record the naming precisely.** Form 2 is *called* madd at-tamkīn (pp. 149, 194). Form 1 is
required here under **bayān** — making the two distinct — with the identical mechanism, the
identical purpose, and the identical failure it guards against, but **not under that label**.
The rule is attested; the modern habit of filing both forms under one name is not.

## Excerpt 4 — the name collision

**Printed page ١٩٥ (195)**, item 20. **This is not the rule this course calls madd al-ʿiwaḍ.**

> العشرون: مد العوض وهو في كل هاء كناية قبلها فعل مجزوم آخره ياء حذفت لأجل الجازم وعوضت عنها
> هاء الضمير، وقد اختلف القراء في إسكان تلك الهاء وتحريكها مع القصر والمد نحو يؤده إليك ونوله
> ما تولى.

*"The twentieth: **madd al-ʿiwaḍ** — in every hāʾ al-kināyah preceded by a jussive verb whose
final yāʾ was dropped for the jussive, the pronoun-hāʾ standing **in compensation** for it…
as in يُؤَدِّهِۦٓ إِلَيْكَ and نُوَلِّهِۦ مَا تَوَلَّىٰ."*

Here *ʿiwaḍ* names a **compensation for a deleted yāʾ**, not for a tanwīn. The term carries two
distinct referents in the tradition. What this course teaches under that name is the modern
usage, and [[Madd-Iwad]] now says so.

A second collision sits directly above it: item 19, **مد الصلة**, is defined here as the madd
of **mīm al-jamʿ** before hamzat al-qaṭʿ (`عَلَيْهِمْ ءَأَنذَرْتَهُمْ`) in the reading of Warsh
and Qālūn — **not** the hāʾ aḍ-ḍamīr ṣilah that [[Madd-Silah]] teaches. Both notes flag this.

## What this book does *not* cover

Checked directly against the text, not assumed:

- **The two muṣḥaf zeros.** `الصفر`, `المستدير` do not occur in the book at all; the two hits
  for `المستطيل` are about the *istiṭālah* of ḍād, an unrelated sense. The round and oblong
  zeros belong to the **rasm and ḍabṭ** tradition, not the tajwīd one — see [[Silent-Letters]].
- **The Madinah waqf-sign set.** Nothing on صلى or قلى as printed marks. See [[Waqf-Signs]].
- **يَبْصُۜطُ, not resolved.** `بَسْطَة` / `بَصْطَة` occurs once in the OCR layer, in the
  chapter on the compilation of the muṣḥaf, as a worked example of canonical variation *in the
  letters, differing in form but not in meaning*. **This one was NOT read off the scan** — the
  page estimate landed at printed ٢٤٨, which is past it, and it was not worth further hunting
  because even if confirmed it only shows both readings are transmitted rather than a printing
  artefact. It does not say which Ḥafṣ reads. Treat it as a lead, not a citation. See
  [[Hafs-Special-Words]].
