# As-Sajawandi — the origin of the waqf signs

**The letters printed above the line to mark stopping places come from this book.**
Al-Sajāwandī set out degrees of stopping and assigned each a letter, so a reciter could
carry the judgement on the page instead of in memory. The system he devised is the one
still in use, in adapted form, in every printed muṣḥaf today.

## Why this note exists — it corrects an error in this vault

[[Waqf-Signs]] and lesson `3-34` both attributed the sign set to a scholar who **died in
1357 AH**, and this log then reasoned from that attribution to the conclusion that the
signs were devised *roughly five centuries after Ibn al-Jazarī*.

**That is backwards.** Al-Sajāwandī died in **560 AH**, which is **273 years *before*** Ibn
al-Jazarī (d. 833 AH). The sign system is older than al-Jazariyyah, not newer.

## The symbols as al-Sajawandi assigned them

| Sign | Arabic | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **م** | الوقف اللازم | Stopping is necessary |
| **ط** | الوقف المطلق | Absolute stop |
| **ج** | الوقف الجائز | Permissible |
| **ز** | الوقف المجوَّز | Allowed |
| **ص** | الوقف المرخَّص | Licensed, for necessity |
| **لا** | لا وقف عليه | No stop here |

**Note which set this is.** ط, ز and ص are the signs lesson `3-34` currently presents as the
*Indo-Pak variant*. They are not a variant — **they are the original.** The Madinah set,
which uses صلى and قلى in their place, is the later adaptation. The lesson has the
relationship the wrong way round and says so on the board.

## What is *not* established here

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Al-Sajāwandī devised the sign system, d. 560 AH | **Verified** in two independent sources |
| The six symbols above, and their meanings | **Verified** — consistent across sources |
| Which signs the **Madinah (King Fahd) muṣḥaf** uses, and on whose authority | **NOT verified here.** Its documentation says the stopping marks were set by a **review committee** for that muṣḥaf, not inherited unchanged |
| The role of Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī Khalaf al-Ḥusaynī (**al-Ḥaddād**, 1865–1939 CE) | **Partly clarified.** He wrote out the 1924 Cairo muṣḥaf by hand in Uthmānī orthography, which Egypt adopted as its reference — so he **standardised modern printed usage**. He did not devise the symbols. The name "al-Hamadhānī" used elsewhere in this vault may be a garbled form of his; **treat that attribution as unverified** |
| The two zeros — الصفر المستدير and المستطيل | **Still unsourced.** See [[Silent-Letters]]. They are Uthmānī *printing* convention and are not in this book |

## Vendoring

**Citation only — no text is vendored.** The work is a prose treatise on the stopping places
of the whole Qurʾān, not a matn, and this course needs one table from it rather than its
argument. Al-Sajāwandī died 560 AH so the work is public domain and *may* be vendored if a
future rule needs its reasoning; nothing here requires it.

A full edition is available at [al-Maktaba al-Shamila](https://shamela.ws/book/30007).
