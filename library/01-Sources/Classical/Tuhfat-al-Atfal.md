# Tuhfat al-Atfal

The standard beginner's *matn* (didactic poem) of tajweed, memorised worldwide
before any other. Composed 1198 AH / 1784 CE by al-Jamzuri, who names his own
teacher al-Mayhi in verse 4.

**Scope is deliberately narrow.** Verse 3 states it outright: *"this poem … on
the noon, the tanween, and the madds."* It covers the noon/meem rules, the lam
of *al*, the three kinds of assimilation, and the full madd taxonomy — and
**nothing else**. It has no makharij, no sifat, no ra' rules, no
tafkhim/tarqiq, no waqf, no mushaf orthography. Those come from
[[Muqaddimah-Jazariyyah]].

Verses 1–60 agree between the two copies apart from minor vocalisation. The
61-verse reading is used here.

## The pedagogical fix this course applies

**Tuhfah teaches ghunnah too late.** Chapter 1 (verses 6–16) spends eleven
verses on the four rulings of noon sakinah — and three of those four rulings
(idgham bi-ghunnah, iqlab, ikhfa) are *defined in terms of ghunnah*. Verse 13
tells the student to convert the noon to a meem `بِغُنَّةٍ` before ghunnah has
been named. Ghunnah itself is not introduced until chapter 2, verse 17, as a
single line about doubled meem and noon.

The poem can get away with this: it is a mnemonic for material a teacher has
already delivered orally, not a self-contained syllabus. A course read in
sequence cannot.

> **This course teaches chapter 2 before chapter 1.** Ghunnah is a prerequisite
> skill — a two-count nasal hold produced at the khayshum — and the student
> drills it on the doubled meem and noon, where it is audible in isolation,
> before meeting it buried inside a conditional rule about noon sakinah.

This is the standard fix, applied by most teachers in practice; it is recorded
here so the deviation from the matn's own order is deliberate and documented
rather than accidental.

## Relation to the course spine

[[Muqaddimah-Jazariyyah]] supplies the ordering skeleton (haqq al-harf before
mustahaqq al-harf). Tuhfah supplies the drill content inside it: its mnemonics
(`يَرْمَلُون`, `يَنْمُو`, the fifteen-letter ikhfa line, `ابْغِ حَجَّكَ وَخَفْ عَقِيمَه`,
`كَمْ عَسَلْ نَقَصْ`) are the memorisable payload the lessons hang on, and its madd
taxonomy is more granular than Jazariyyah's four-line treatment.

# The matn

Arabic verbatim from the retrieved source (tatweel stripped). English is a
working prose rendering, **not** a published translation and not a poetic one —
it aims at technical accuracy per hemistich. Transliterated mnemonics are set
in SMALL CAPS-style upper case because they are meaningless as sentences: they
are letter-carriers, and only their initial letters matter.

|---|---|---|
| 42 | لِلْمَدِّ أَحْكَامٌ ثَلاَثَةٌ تَدُومْ … وَهْيَ الْوُجُوبُ وَالْجَوَازُ وَاللُّزُومْ | Madd has three abiding rulings: **obligation**, **permissibility**, and **necessity**. |
| 43 | فَوَاجِبٌ إِنْ جَاءَ هَمْزٌ بَعْدَ مَدْ … فِي كِلْمَةٍ وَذَا بِمُتَّصْلْ يُعَدْ | **Obligatory** (*wajib*), if a hamza comes after a madd **within one word** — and this is reckoned as *muttasil* (joined). |
| 44 | وَجَائزٌ مَدٌ وَقَصْرٌ إِنْ فُصِل … كُلٌّ بِكِلْمَةٍ وَهَذَا المُنْفَصِلْ | **Permissible** (*ja'iz*) — lengthening or shortening both — if each is separated into its own word: this is *munfasil*. |
| 45 | وَمِثْلُ ذَا إِنْ عَرَضَ السُّكُونُ … وَقْفَاً كَتَعْلَمُونَ نَسْتَعِينُ | And the same [permissibility] applies if the sukun is **accidental**, arising at a stop, as in *ta'lamoon*, *nasta'een*. |
| 46 | أَوْ قُدِّمَ الْهَمْزُ عَلَى المَدِّ وَذَا … بَدَلْ كَآمَنُوا وَإِيَماناً خُذَا | Or if the hamza **precedes** the madd — and that is *badal* (substitute), as in *aamanoo* and *eemaanan*; take it. |
| 47 | وَلاَزِمٌ إِنِ السُّكُونُ أُصِّلاَ … وَصْلاَ وَوَقْفاً بَعْدَ مَدٍّ طُوّلاَ | And **necessary** (*lazim*), if the sukun is **intrinsic** — both in continuation and at a stop — after a madd, lengthened long. |

## بَابُ أَقْسَامِ الْمَدِّ اللَّازِم — Categories of madd lazim

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 48 | أَقْسَامُ لاَزِمٍ لَدَيْهِمْ أَرْبَعَةْ … وَتِلْكَ كِلْمِيٌّ وَحَرْفِيٌّ مَعَهْ | The categories of *lazim*, with them, are **four**: word-type (*kalimi*), and letter-type (*harfi*) with it — |
| 49 | كِلاَهُمَا مُخَفَّفٌ مُثَقَّلُ … فَهَذِهِ أَرْبَعَةٌ تُفَصَّلُ | each of the two being either **light** (*mukhaffaf*) or **heavy** (*muthaqqal*). So these are the four, set out in detail. |
| 50 | فَإِنْ بِكِلْمَةٍ سُكُونٌ اجْتَمَعْ … مَعْ حَرْفِ مَدٍّ فَهْوَ كِلْمِيٌّ وَقَعْ | If, **within a word**, a sukun coincides with a madd letter, then word-type it is. |
| 51 | أَوْ في ثُلاَثِيِّ الْحُروفِ وُجِدَا … وَالْمَدُّ وَسْطُهُ فَحَرْفِيٌّ بَدَا | Or if it is found in a **three-lettered letter-name** with the madd in its middle, then letter-type it plainly is. |
| 52 | كِلاَهُمَا مُثَقَّلٌ إِنْ أُدْغِمَا … مُخَفَفٌ كُلُّ إِذَا لَمْ يُدْغَمَا | Both are **heavy** if assimilated; each is **light** when not assimilated. |
| 53 | وَاللاَّزِمُ الْحَرْفِيُّ أَوَّلَ السُّوَرْ … وُجُودُهُ وَفِي ثَمَانٍ انحَصَرْ | Letter-type *lazim* occurs at the **openings of the surahs**, and is confined to **eight** letters, |
| 54 | يَجْمَعُهَا حُرُوفُ كَمْ عَسَلْ نَقَصْ … وَعَيْنُ ذُو وَجْهَيْنِ والطُّولُ أَخَصْ | gathered by the letters of KAM 'ASAL NAQAS. And the 'ayn admits **two lengths**, the long being the more particular. |
| 55 | وَمَا سِوَى الْحَرْفِ الثُّلاَثِي لاَ أَلِفْ … فَمَدُّهُ مَدٌّ طَبِيعِيٌّ أُلِفْ | And whatever is not a three-lettered letter-name — the alif excepted — its madd is a **natural** madd, as is familiar. |
| 56 | وَذَاكَ أَيْضاً فِي فَوَاتِحِ السُّوَرْ … فِي لَفْظِ حَيٌّ طَاهِرٌ قَدِ انْحَصَرْ | That too is in the openings of the surahs, confined to the words HAYYUN TAHIRUN. |
| 57 | وَيَجْمَعُ الْفَوَاتِحَ الأَرْبَعْ عَشَرْ … صِلْهُ سُحَيْراً مَنْ قَطَعْكَ ذَا اشْتَهَرْ | And the **fourteen** opening letters are gathered by SILHU SUHAYRAN MAN QATA'AKA — this is well known. |

## الْخَاتِمَة — Conclusion

| # | Arabic | English |
|---|---|---|
| 58 | وَتَمَّ ذَا النَّظْمُ بِحَمْدِ اللَّهِ … عَلَى تَمَامِهِ بِلاَ تَنَاهِى | This poem is complete, with praise to God for its completion — praise without end. |
| 59 | أَبْيَاتُهُ نَدٌّ بَداَ لِذِى النُّهَى … تَارِيخُها بُشْرَى لِمَنْ يُتْقِنُهَا | Its verses are NADD, evident to the person of intellect; its date is BUSHRA, for whoever masters it. |
| 60 | ثُمَّ الصَّلاَةُ وَالسَّلاَمُ أَبَداَ … عِلى خِتَامِ الأَنْبِيَاءِ أَحْمَدَا | Then blessings and peace, forever, upon the seal of the prophets, Ahmad — |
| 61 | وَالآلِ وَالصَّحْبِ وَكُلِّ تَابِعِ … وَكُلِّ قَارِئٍ وكُلِّ سَامِعِ | and upon the family, the companions, every follower, every reciter, and every listener. |

Verse 59 encodes the verse count (`نَدّ`) and the composition date (`بُشْرَى`) as
*abjad* numerals. **The arithmetic has not been checked here** and the two
retrieved copies disagree on the total (60 vs 61), so the encoded count should
not be cited as independent confirmation of either.
