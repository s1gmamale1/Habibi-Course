# Al-Ghunnah as a sifah — الغنة

> This note describes ghunnah as a **characteristic of the letters ن and م**.
> For the *rule* — where ghunnah is applied, for how long, and in which of the noon and
> meem chapters — see [[Ghunnah]].

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *ghunnah* is a nasal twang in the voice.

**Technically**, ghunnah is a **nasal resonance issuing from the khayshum** — the nasal
passage — which is **inseparable** from ن and م. It is not something added to these two
letters in certain positions; it is part of what they are. Their consonantal makhraj is in
the mouth (the tongue tip for ن, the two lips for م), but the *sound* comes out through
the nose.

Because the khayshum is the source, ghunnah is the only sifah whose makhraj lies outside
the mouth and throat entirely.

## Its opposite

None. Ghunnah is one of the sifat without an opposite.

## Letters

Two letters: **ن** and **م**.

Every ن and every م carries ghunnah in every position. What changes from position to
position is not whether the ghunnah is present but **how complete it is**.

## The five levels of completeness

From most complete (*akmal*) to least (*anqas*):

| # | State | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mushaddad** — carrying a shaddah | إِنَّ · ثُمَّ |
| 2 | **Mudgham** — assimilated into a following letter | مِّن مَّآءٍ |
| 3 | **Mukhfa** — hidden before an ikhfa letter | مِن قَبْلُ |
| 4 | **Sakin with izhar** — clear, unassimilated | مِنْ هَادٍ |
| 5 | **Mutaharrik** — carrying its own vowel | نَعْبُدُ · مَالِكِ |

Levels 1–3 are held for the full two counts. **Levels 4 and 5 are not held at all** —
the ghunnah is present as the natural nasality of the letter and passes at ordinary
speaking speed.

## What it sounds like

The teacher's instrument is the **nose-pinch test**: have the student pinch the nostrils
closed and pronounce إِنَّ. If a genuine ghunnah is being produced, the sound is blocked
and the student feels the pressure. If nothing changes, the "ghunnah" is being made in
the mouth as a hum, which is the single most common ghunnah error.

Two failure modes:

- **Oral hum instead of nasal resonance** — audible as a flat "nnn" with no ring. Caught
  by the pinch test.
- **Over-application** — giving a full two-count ghunnah to levels 4 and 5. A student who
  has just learned the rule will hold every ن they see; the result sounds droning and is
  as wrong as having no ghunnah at all.

## Why it matters

Ghunnah as a sifah is the raw material for a whole family of rules, all of which are
downstream of it:

- The noon sakinah and tanween rules — ikhfa, idgham with ghunnah, iqlab.
- The meem sakinah rules — ikhfa shafawi, idgham shafawi.
- The obligatory ghunnah of every mushaddad ن and م.

All of these are treated in [[Ghunnah]] and the chapters it links to. What belongs here is
only the fact that the nasal resonance is intrinsic: the rules govern its *duration*, never
its existence.
