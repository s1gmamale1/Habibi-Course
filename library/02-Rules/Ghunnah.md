# Al-Ghunnah — الغُنَّة

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *ghunnah* is a sound resonating in the nose.

**Technically**, it is a sound that issues from the **khayshum** (الخَيْشُوم — the nasal
cavity), has **no makhraj on the tongue or lips of its own**, and is an inseparable
attribute (*sifah lazimah*) of the two letters ن and م. You cannot remove the ghunnah
from a ن or a م; you can only make it longer or shorter.

Its **most complete** form (*akmal al-ghunnah*) is on a **ن or م carrying a shaddah**.
That is the rule taught here: **any نّ or مّ, anywhere, in any word, in wasl or waqf,
is held for 2 counts of ghunnah.**

Tuhfat al-Atfal names them for exactly this reason:

> وَغُنَّ مِيمًا ثُمَّ نُونًا شُدِّدَا ⁕ وَسَمِّ كُلًّا حَرْفَ غُنَّةٍ بَدَا
> *"Nasalise a meem, then a noon, that are doubled — and call each one 'the letter of
> ghunnah', plainly."*

## Why this course teaches ghunnah FIRST

**This is a deliberate departure from the classical order, and it matters.**

Tuhfat al-Atfal places the rulings of the noon sakinah and tanween in **chapter 1**, and
only reaches *an-noon wal-meem al-mushaddadatan* in **chapter 2**. So the classical
sequence teaches idghām bi-ghunnah, iqlāb and ikhfāʾ — three rules whose entire content
is *"hold a ghunnah for 2 counts"* — **before it ever defines what a ghunnah is or how
long 2 counts are.**

That order works for a student sitting in front of a shaykh who simply imitates the
sound. It does not work for a self-paced learner. So **this course inverts it**:

1. **Ghunnah** — what the nasal sound is, where it comes from, how long 2 counts are.
2. Then every rule that *uses* it: [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]], [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]], [[Iqlab]],
   [[Ikhfa-Shafawi]], [[Idgham-Shafawi]].

Ghunnah is the **prerequisite**, not the appendix. Every one of those five rules lists
`prerequisites: [ghunnah]`, and none of them should be attempted before the learner can
produce and hold a 2-count nasal on `إِنَّ` and `ٱلنَّاسِ`.

## Where the sound comes from

The khayshum is the **fifth makhraj zone**, alongside the jawf, the halq, the lisān and
the shafatān. It is the only zone with no moving articulator: the sound is shaped by
lowering the soft palate so air escapes through the nose instead of the mouth.

**The pinch test.** Recite `إِنَّا` (108:1) and, while holding the نّ, pinch your nostrils
shut. If the sound stops dead, the ghunnah is genuine. If the sound continues unchanged,
you are producing an oral "n" and the ghunnah is missing.

## Duration — 2 harakāt

Two counts, at the tempo you are already reciting in. The classical measure is *the time
taken to fold two fingers at a moderate pace*.

A harakah has **no fixed length in seconds** — scholars differ on whether the finger
convention, a measured ~0.5–1 second, or a purely relative definition is correct. This
course teaches the **finger convention as a learning aid** and the relative definition as
the truth: 2 counts means *twice your own short-vowel duration*, so a slow tartīl ghunnah
is objectively longer than a fast ḥadr ghunnah, and both are correct.

## The levels of ghunnah

Not all ghunnah is equal. From strongest to weakest:

| Rank | Where | Length |
|---|---|---|
| 1. *Akmal* — most complete | **نّ / مّ mushaddad** (this rule) | 2 counts |
| 2. Merged | [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]], [[Idgham-Shafawi]] | 2 counts |
| 3. Concealed | [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]], [[Ikhfa-Shafawi]], [[Iqlab]] | 2 counts |
| 4. Sākin, plain | ن / م sākinah with no rule acting on it | inherent only |
| 5. Mutaḥarrik | ن / م carrying a vowel | inherent only |

Ranks 4 and 5 still *have* ghunnah — it is inseparable — but it is **not prolonged**.
This is exactly why [[Izhar-Halqi]] and [[Izhar-Shafawi]] are not "ghunnah-less": they are
*prolongation-less*. Learners who are told "izhār has no ghunnah" often over-correct into
a hard, denasalised "n".

Ghunnah as a *sifah of the letters ن and م* — the attribute rather than the timed rule —
is covered separately in [[Ghunnah-sifah]].

## Heaviness

The ghunnah of a mushaddad ن/م is generally **light** (*muraqqaq*). It picks up slight
heaviness when an adjacent letter is heavy — compare the ghunnah in `إِنَّ` with the
ghunnah in a word where an isti'lā letter neighbours the shaddah. The systematic version
of this behaviour appears in [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]], where the ghunnah takes its heaviness
directly from the letter that follows.

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping it entirely on the high-frequency words** — `إِنَّ`, `ثُمَّ`, `أَنَّ`. These
  appear so often that the error becomes invisible to the reciter.
- **Over-lengthening** to 4 or 6 counts, turning the ghunnah into a madd.
- **Producing it in the mouth** rather than the nose — fails the pinch test.
- **Dropping it at waqf.** Stopping on `ٱلنَّاسِ` still requires the full 2 counts on the
  نّ; the shaddah does not disappear because the word-final vowel does.
- **Denasalising after learning izhār** — see the levels table above.
