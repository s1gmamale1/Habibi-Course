# The Special Words of Ḥafṣ — الكلمات الخاصة بحفص

> ### ⚠ Status: needs-review — يَبْصُۜطُ / بَصْۜطَةً, sīn or ṣād?
>
> **This whole note is held at `needs-review` because of two words.**
>
> Sources conflict on **يَبْصُۜطُ** (al-Baqarah 2:245) and **بَصْۜطَةً**
> (al-Aʿrāf 7:69). The predominant position for Ḥafṣ via ash-Shāṭibiyyah is
> **ṣād** for both, with the small superscript **س** marking the *alternate*
> reading of other qurrāʾ — and **sīn** for **ٱلْمُصَۣيْطِرُونَ** (aṭ-Ṭūr 52:37),
> where the small sīn is written **below** the ṣād rather than above.
> **Some regional teaching traditions reverse this for 2:245.**
>
> Getting this wrong means publishing a lesson that teaches a learner to recite
> a letter of the Qurʾān incorrectly. **Verify against a physical Madinah
> mushaf print and a licensed teacher with an ijāzah in Ḥafṣ before this note
> leaves `needs-review`.** Do not resolve it from secondary web sources.
>
> Everything else in this note is stable; the status reflects those two words.

## What these are

Places where **Ḥafṣ departs from the general rules**. Each is unique or
near-unique in the whole Qurʾān, which means **a learner cannot derive them** —
every one has to be taught explicitly and memorised.

## The four "only ones" — the headline set

| Phenomenon | Word | Ref | What you do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ishmām** — the only one | لَا تَأْمَ۫نَّا | Yūsuf 12:11 | Merge the two nūns while **rounding the lips silently** — visible to a watcher, inaudible. *Rawm/ikhtilās* (about ⅓ of the ḍammah) is the valid alternative; ishmām is more common. |
| **Imālah kubrā** — the only one | مَجْر۪ىٰهَا | Hūd 11:41 | The rāʾ inclines toward kasrah and the alif toward yāʾ: `majrēhā`. |
| **Tas-hīl** — the only one | ءَا۬عْجَمِىٌّ | Fuṣṣilat 41:44 | The second hamzah is softened, pronounced **between** a hamzah and an alif. Taḥqīq is also transmitted by some. |
| **Naql** — the only one | بِئْسَ ٱلِٱسْمُ | al-Ḥujurāt 49:11 | The hamzah's kasrah **transfers** onto the lām: `bi'sa-lismu`. |

Teach these four as a set. "There is exactly one X in the whole Qurʾān, and here
it is" is memorable in a way that a list of thirty exceptions is not.

## The sīn / ṣād words

| Word | Ref | Reading | Marking |
|---|---|---|---|
| صِرَٰط / ٱلصِّرَٰط | 1:6, 1:7 and throughout | **ṣād** | written with ṣād; sīn is the reading of other qurrāʾ |
| يَبْصُۜطُ | 2:245 | ṣād *(see review note)* | small sīn **above** |
| بَصْۜطَةً | 7:69 | ṣād *(see review note)* | small sīn **above** |
| ٱلْمُصَۣيْطِرُونَ | 52:37 | **sīn** | small sīn **below** — the mirror case |
| بِمُصَيْطِرٍ | 88:22 | ṣād; sīn also transmitted | |

**The convention to teach:** a small sīn written **above** marks an alternate
reading you do *not* take; written **below**, it marks the letter you *do*
recite. That rule of thumb is exactly what the review is meant to confirm.

## Hāʾ aḍ-ḍamīr irregularities

Four places break [[Madd-Silah]]:

- **يَرْضَهُ لَكُمْ** (39:7) — conditions met, **no ṣilah**.
- **فِيهِۦ مُهَانًا** (25:69) — preceded by a sākin yāʾ, yet **ṣilah given**.
- **وَمَآ أَنسَىٰنِيهُ** (18:63) — ḍammah on the hāʾ, no ṣilah.
- **عَلَيْهُ ٱللَّهَ** (48:10) — ḍammah on the hāʾ, no ṣilah.

## Idghām irregularities

| Place | Ref | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| يَلْهَث ذَّٰلِكَ | 7:176 | idghām mutajānisayn, thāʾ into dhāl |
| ٱرْكَب مَّعَنَا | 11:42 | idghām of bāʾ into mīm |
| أَلَمْ نَخْلُقكُّم | 77:20 | idghām of qāf into kāf — **nāqiṣ** (the qāf's istiʿlāʾ is retained) per most; kāmil also transmitted |
| مَكَّنِّى | 18:95 | idghām kabīr — مَكَّنَنِي becomes مَكَّنِّي |
| نٓ وَٱلْقَلَمِ · يسٓ وَٱلْقُرْءَانِ | 68:1 · 36:1–2 | **iẓhār** of the spelt letter's final nūn before wāw, against the general idghām rule |

## Other transmitted differences

- **ضَعْف / ضُعْف** (Rūm 30:54, three times) — Ḥafṣ reads the **ḍammah**;
  the fatḥah is also transmitted via ash-Shāṭibiyyah. Both valid; teach ḍammah.
- **Double hamzah** (ءَأَنذَرْتَهُمْ and its like, throughout) — Ḥafṣ uses
  **taḥqīq**, both hamzahs fully pronounced, **everywhere except** ءَا۬عْجَمِىٌّ.
- **ءَآلذَّكَرَيْنِ · ءَآللَّهُ · ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ** — 6 counts or tas-hīl; see
  [[Madd-Farq]].
- The four obligatory and two permissible **sakt** places are Ḥafṣ specifics
  too; they have their own note, [[Sakt]].
