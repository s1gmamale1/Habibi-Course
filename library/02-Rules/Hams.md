# Al-Hams — الهمس

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *hams* is a whisper: speech kept faint, under the breath.

**Technically**, hams is the continued flow of breath (*nafas*) alongside the letter,
caused by the weakness of the tongue's reliance (*i'timad*) on the makhraj. Because
the articulators press only lightly, they never seal the airstream, so breath keeps
escaping while the letter is being produced.

Hams is a property of the *breath*. Do not confuse it with [[Rakhawah]], which is a
property of the *sound*. A letter can be both (ف, ح, ث, ه, ش, خ, ص, س), or hams
without rakhawah (ك and ت, which are plosives that still leak breath on release).

## Its opposite

[[Jahr]] — the breath is stopped because reliance on the makhraj is strong.
Every letter is either hams or jahr; there is no third option and no overlap.

## Letters

Ten letters, gathered in the classical mnemonic:

> **فَحَثَّهُ شَخْصٌ سَكَتْ** — *fa-hathhahu shakhsun sakat*

ف · ح · ث · ه · ش · خ · ص · س · ك · ت

The mnemonic is a sentence ("a person who had fallen silent urged him on"), so it is
memorised as language rather than as a letter list.

## What it sounds like

The teacher listens for **audible breath continuing past the letter**. Two bedside tests:

- **Palm test** — pronounce the letter sakin against the palm held a few centimetres
  from the mouth. On a hams letter you feel a puff of air; on a jahr letter you do not.
- **Candle test** — a flame flickers on ت, ك, ص; it stays steady on د, ج, ز.

A student who *has* hams sounds slightly airy on these ten letters. A student who has
lost it sounds clipped and hard — most often on word-final ت and ك, which English and
Turkic speakers tend to swallow, and on ص, which collapses toward a bare س stop.

## Why it matters

- **Qalqalah requires jahr + [[Shiddah|shiddah]] together.** Because hams letters are by definition
  not jahr, no hams letter can ever carry qalqalah — this is why ت and ك, despite being
  plosives, are excluded from قُطْبُ جَدٍّ. See [[Qalqalah-sifah]].
- **[[Waqf-Types|Waqf]] on a hams letter** is the commonest place for the breath to be cut, producing a
  clipped stop that sounds like a different letter.
- Hams is one half of the pair that distinguishes letters sharing a makhraj: [[Ta|ت]] (hams)
  against [[Dal|د]] (jahr), and [[Sad|ص]] (hams) against [[Dad|ض]] (jahr) at the tongue's edge.

## How many pairs? — scholars differ

This course teaches the **[[Sifat|five-pair scheme]]** (ten sifat with opposites). Some scholars
add a sixth pair by opposing *al-khafa'* (الخفاء, faintness — ه and the three madd
letters ا و ي) to *az-zuhur* (الظهور, clarity); others treat khafa' as a sifah without
an opposite. The disagreement is one of **arrangement, not of substance** — the same
sounds are described either way. Learn the five pairs; recognise the sixth when a book
uses it.
