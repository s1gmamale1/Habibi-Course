# Hamzat al-Waṣl — همزة الوصل

## The two hamzahs

| | **Hamzat al-qaṭʿ** (القطع) | **Hamzat al-waṣl** (الوصل) |
|---|---|---|
| Written | أ إ ؤ ئ ء | **ٱ** — an alif with a small **ص** (for صِلَة) |
| Pronounced when **starting** on the word | yes | yes |
| Pronounced when **joining** from the previous word | **yes** | **no — dropped entirely** |
| Examples | أَنْعَمْتَ · إِيَّاكَ · أَحَدٌ | ٱلْحَمْدُ · ٱدْخُلُوا · ٱبْن |

The waṣl hamzah exists only to give a sākin letter something to start on. Once
there is a previous word to lean on, it is not needed and disappears:
**وَٱسْتَغْفِرْ** is `wastaghfir`, never `wa-istaghfir`.

## Starting on a hamzat al-waṣl: which vowel?

This is the whole rule. It has three branches.

### A. Verbs — أفعال

| Case | Vowel | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Past tense of 5- and 6-letter verbs | **kasrah** | اِنْطَلَقَ · اِسْتَغْفَرَ · اِقْتَرَبَ |
| Command of 5- and 6-letter verbs | **kasrah** | اِنْطَلِقْ · اِسْتَغْفِرْ · اِسْتَقِمْ |
| Command of 3-letter verbs — **look at the third letter of the present tense**; if it has an **original ḍammah** | **ḍammah** | اُدْخُلُوا (يَدْخُلُ) · اُنظُرْ (يَنظُرُ) · اُعْبُدُوا (يَعْبُدُ) |
| Command of 3-letter verbs — third letter has **fatḥah or kasrah** | **kasrah** | اِضْرِبْ (يَضْرِبُ) · اِذْهَبْ (يَذْهَبُ) · اِجْعَلْ (يَجْعَلُ) |
| **Exception** — the ḍammah is **ʿāriḍ** (temporary, arising from a deleted weak letter) | **kasrah** | اِمْشُوا (38:6) · اِبْنُوا (18:21) · اِقْضُوا (10:71) |

The trick to teach: *say the present tense in your head first.* يَدْخُلُ has a
ḍammah on the khāʾ → start `udkhulū`. يَذْهَبُ has a fatḥah on the hāʾ → start
`idhhab`.

### B. Nouns — أسماء

**The seven samāʿī ("heard") nouns that occur in the Qurʾān all take kasrah:**

اِسْم · اِبْن · اِبْنَة · اِمْرُؤ · اِمْرَأَة · اِثْنَان · اِثْنَتَان

Classical Arabic lists ten; اِسْت, اِيْمُن and اِبْنُم do not occur in the
Qurʾān, so seven is the working number.

### C. Particles — حروف

**The definite article ال always takes fatḥah:** اَلْحَمْدُ · اَلرَّحْمَٰن ·
اَلنَّاس · اَلَّذِينَ.

## D. Waṣl hamzah followed by a sākin hamzah

When you **start** on a word whose waṣl hamzah is immediately followed by a
**sākin hamzah**, the second hamzah turns into a madd letter matching the first
vowel:

| Written | Started as | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| ٱئْتُوا | اِيتُوا | 2:23 |
| ٱئْتِ | اِيتِ | 10:15 |
| ٱؤْتُمِنَ | اُوتُمِنَ | 2:283 |
| ٱئْذَن | اِيذَن | 9:49 |

## E. Waṣl hamzah after an interrogative hamzah

See [[Madd-Farq]]: ءَآلذَّكَرَيْنِ · ءَآللَّهُ · ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ — 6 counts, or
tas-hīl.

## Why this is taught with stopping

You only need this rule when you **start** somewhere — which is exactly what
happens every time you stop and resume. [[Waqf-Types]] and hamzat al-waṣl are
one practical skill, and Unit 2 teaches them together.

## Common mistakes

1. Starting ٱلْحَمْدُ with a kasrah — "il-ḥamd".
2. Starting ٱدْخُلُوا with a kasrah.
3. **Pronouncing the waṣl hamzah when joining** — an audible glottal stop
   mid-phrase. Common in learners with Persian or Urdu speech habits.
4. Failing to convert ٱئْتُوا when starting on it.
