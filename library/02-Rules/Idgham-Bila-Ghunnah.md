# Al-Idghām bi-ghayr Ghunnah — الإِدْغَام بِغَيْرِ غُنَّة

## Definition

**Complete merging** of the noon sākinah or tanwīn into a following **ل** or **ر**, with
the ghunnah **entirely dropped**.

The noon is gone. There is no nasal residue at all. What you hear is a doubled ل or ر held
for the ordinary duration of a shaddah — nothing more.

## Condition

Noon sākinah at the **end of a word**, or a tanwīn, followed by **ل** or **ر** at the start
of the next word.

Like [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]], this rule needs the word boundary. Unlike it, there is no
in-word exception to memorise — the sequence نْ + ل / ر simply does not arise inside a
single Qurʾānic word.

## The two letters

**ل · ر**

## Always kāmil

This idghām is **always complete**. The noon's makhraj *and* its ghunnah both go. There is
no nāqiṣ variant and no scholarly disagreement about it.

That makes it the clean contrast case for the يَنْمُو rule next door:

| | Letters | Ghunnah? | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]] | ن · م | **Yes**, 2 counts | Kāmil |
| [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]] | ي · و | **Yes**, 2 counts | **Nāqiṣ** — the noon's ghunnah survives |
| **This rule** | ل · ر | **No** | Kāmil |

**⚠ Scholars differ on the ي/و row** — a minority read it as kāmil. This course teaches
**nāqiṣ**, following the jumhūr and the muṣḥaf's own orthography (ي and و carry no shaddah
after a merged noon; ن and م do). The disagreement is discussed in full in
[[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]]. It does **not** touch ل and ر, which are kāmil on every account.

## Duration

**No ghunnah at all — 0 counts.** The doubled ل or ر is held exactly as long as any other
shaddah.

This is the one merge in the nasal syllabus where the answer to "how long is the ghunnah?"
is "there isn't one", and learners who have just drilled [[Ghunnah]] and
[[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]] will import a nasal here out of pure habit.

## Why these two letters lose the ghunnah

ل and ر are the tongue's two closest neighbours to the noon — all three are articulated at
the gum ridge with the tip of the tongue. The makhārij are so close that the merge is
total: there is no residual position for a nasal to sound from. The general theory of this
kind of pairing is [[Idgham-Mutaqaribayn]], where **ن → ل / ر** appears as a listed case;
this rule is that theory as it applies specifically to the noon sākinah.

[cpfair/quran-tajweed](https://github.com/cpfair/quran-tajweed) key is
**`idghaam_no_ghunnah`** — see [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]].

Note that Family B gives this rule a **green almost indistinguishable from the `#169777`
of [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]]** at small type sizes. That is a deliberate signal that the two
are the same operation, but it is a genuine accessibility hazard: a learner who cannot
separate the hues cannot tell "hold a 2-count nasal" from "hold none". Do not rely on
colour alone to teach this pair — teach it on the letter.

## Inside the memorised sūrahs

Both examples a beginner needs are already in their ḥifẓ:

- `وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ` — al-Ikhlāṣ 112:4
- `فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ` — al-Māʿūn 107:4

One with a noon sākinah, one with a tanwīn, one sūrah apart. This is unusually convenient;
most nasal rules need a reference example from outside the ḥifẓ set.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding a ghunnah**, carried over from يَنْمُو. The commonest error by a wide margin.
- **Not fully doubling** the ل or ر — the shaddah must be audible.
- **Pronouncing the noon first** — `min-rabbihim` instead of `mir-rabbihim`.
- **Forgetting tafkhīm/tarqīq on the resulting ر.** The merged ر is still a rāʾ and still
  takes the rāʾ rules: `مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ` has a heavy rāʾ because of the fatḥah on it.
