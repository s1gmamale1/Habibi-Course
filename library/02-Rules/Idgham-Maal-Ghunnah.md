# Al-Idghām bi-Ghunnah — الإِدْغَام بِغُنَّة

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *idghām* is inserting one thing into another.

**Technically**, it is **merging the noon sākinah or tanwīn into the following letter**,
which becomes doubled, **while a 2-count ghunnah is retained**.

The noon does not disappear silently. It is absorbed into the next letter and its nasal
resonance is what you hear for those 2 counts.

## Condition

Noon sākinah or tanwīn at the **end of a word**, followed by one of four letters at the
**start of the next word**.

**The word boundary is part of the rule, not an accident of examples.** When these same
four letters follow a noon sākinah *inside* one word, the rule is cancelled — see
[[#The four-word exception — iẓhār muṭlaq]] below.

## The four letters — يَنْمُو

**[[Ya]] ي · ن · م · و** — gathered in the word **يَنْمُو** (*yanmū*, "it grows").

Tanwīn always sits at the end of a word, so for tanwīn the boundary condition is
automatically satisfied.

## Kāmil and nāqiṣ — the merge is not always complete

This is the most important technical point in the rule, and it is the reason the four
letters do **not** behave alike:

| Letters | Type | What happens | Shaddah in the muṣḥaf? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ن · م** | **Kāmil** (complete) | Letter *and* ghunnah both merge; the noon is entirely gone | **Yes** |
| **ي · و** | **Nāqiṣ** (incomplete) | The noon's *body* merges, but its **ghunnah remains** | **No** |

In `مِن نِّعْمَةٍ` (16:53) the ن carries a shaddah: two noons became one doubled noon, and
the ghunnah you hear belongs to that doubled noon.

In `مَن يَقُولُ` (2:8) the ي carries **no shaddah**: the ghunnah you hear is still the
*departed noon's* — it is a leftover, not a property of the yāʾ. This is why a يَنْمُو
idghām on ي and و is called *incomplete*.

**⚠ Scholars differ.** A minority hold that the idghām of ي and و is *kāmil* like the
others. **This course teaches nāqiṣ**, following the jumhūr, and because the Madinah
muṣḥaf's own orthography reflects it — the missing shaddah on ي and و is not a printing
convention, it is a ruling written into the page. Note the kāmil position once; do not
drill it.

## The four-word exception — iẓhār muṭlaq

الإِظْهَار المُطْلَق (also الإِظْهَار الوَاجِب, "obligatory clarification").

When a noon sākinah meets ي ن م و **inside the same word**, there is **no idghām**. The
noon is pronounced clearly, exactly as in [[Izhar-Halqi]] — but it is called *muṭlaq*
("absolute", i.e. unqualified by a makhraj zone) because the trigger is position, not the
throat.

**The rationale:** merging inside a word would erase the boundary between a genuine root
letter and an assimilated one. `الدُّنْيَا` would become *ad-duyyā*, and the word would no
longer be recoverable from its sound.

**There are exactly four such words in the entire Qur'an:**

| Word | Verified reference |
|---|---|
| **ٱلدُّنْيَا** | 2:85 — and ~115 further places |
| **بُنْيَٰن** | 61:4 `بُنْيَٰنٌ مَّرْصُوصٌ` |
| **صِنْوَان** | 13:4 `صِنْوَانٌ وَغَيْرُ صِنْوَانٍ` |
| **قِنْوَان** | 6:99 `قِنْوَانٌ دَانِيَةٌ` |

All four are corpus-verified against the pinned Tanzil text.

`بُنْيَٰنٌ مَّرْصُوصٌ` (61:4) is the best single drill in the set, because it contains
**both** behaviours four letters apart: the نْ + ي inside `بُنْيَٰن` gets **no** idghām,
and the tanwīn + م across the boundary gets a **full** one.

**ٱلدُّنْيَا is where this error actually lives.** It is high-frequency, it is learned
early by ear, and *ad-duyyā* is an easy slip. Teach the exception on this word first.

## Inside the memorised sūrahs

`حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ` (111:5) is the richest line available: **two idghāms back to back**,
both kāmil, both with م. `بِحِجَارَةٍ مِّن سِجِّيلٍ` (105:4) gives a third. None of the four
iẓhār muṭlaq words occurs in al-Fātiḥa or sūrahs 105–114, so those must be taught from
reference examples — label them as such.

## Common mistakes

- **Pronouncing the noon before merging it** — `min-nyaqūl`. This defeats the idghām
  entirely and is the single most common fault.
- **Dropping the ghunnah on ي and و** because there is no shaddah to remind you. The
  shaddah's absence means the merge is incomplete, *not* that the nasal is gone.
- **Failing to make the shaddah audible** on ن and م — a doubled letter must be held.
- **Merging inside a word** — *ad-duyyā*, *buyyān*.
- Applying the rule to `الم` and other muqaṭṭaʿāt letter-names without checking whether a
  word boundary is genuinely present.
