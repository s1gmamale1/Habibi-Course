# Idghām al-Mutajānisayn — إِدْغَام المُتَجَانِسَيْن

## Definition

Two letters share the **same makhraj** but differ in their **ṣifāt**. The first, being
sākin, merges into the second.

*Mutajānisān* means "the two of one kind" — same articulation point, different character.

Compare [[Idgham-Mutamathilayn]] (same makhraj *and* same ṣifāt) and
[[Idgham-Mutaqaribayn]] (makhārij merely close).

## The seven pairings in Ḥafṣ

Mutajānisayn is a **closed list**. These are the only pairings that produce idghām in this
reading; every other same-makhraj sequence is iẓhār.

| Pair | Direction | Type | Verified examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **د → ت** | د merges into ت | Kāmil | `قَد تَّبَيَّنَ` 2:256 · `لَّقَد تَّابَ` 9:117 · `عَبَدتُّمْ` 109:4 |
| **ت → د** | ت merges into د | Kāmil | `أَثْقَلَت دَّعَوَا` 7:189 |
| **ت → ط** | ت merges into ط | Kāmil | `وَقَالَت طَّآئِفَةٌ` 3:72 |
| **ط → ت** | ط merges into ت | **Nāqiṣ** | `أَحَطتُ` 27:22 · `بَسَطتَ` 5:28 · `فَرَّطتُمْ` 12:80 |
| **ذ → ظ** | ذ merges into ظ | Kāmil | `إِذ ظَّلَمُوٓا۟` 4:64 · `إِذ ظَّلَمْتُمْ` 43:39 |
| **ث → ذ** | ث merges into ذ | Kāmil | `يَلْهَث ذَّٰلِكَ` 7:176 — the only occurrence |
| **ب → م** | ب merges into م | Kāmil | `ٱرْكَب مَّعَنَا` 11:42 — the only occurrence |

Three makhraj groups account for all seven: the gum-ridge plosives (د ت ط), the
tongue-tip-and-teeth fricatives (ذ ظ ث), and the lips (ب م).

## ط → ت is nāqiṣ — and this is the rule's real content

Every pairing above is complete **except** ط → ت.

When a sākin **ط** merges into a **ت**, the ط's **iṭbāq** (إطباق — the tongue's rise to the
roof) and **isti'lā** (see [[Istila]]) **survive**. The letter becomes a ت; its *heaviness*
does not go with it.

So `أَحَطتُ` is **not** `aḥattu`. It is a ت pronounced with the tongue still raised — a
heavy, hollow ت that sounds unlike any other ت in the Qurʾān. The same in `بَسَطتَ` and
`فَرَّطتُمْ`.

**This is the classic error in the whole idghām syllabus.** A learner who has been told
"the ط merges" flattens it, and `أَحَطتُ` comes out light. The word appears once; the
mistake, once made, is rarely corrected.

Note also the orthography: the ت in `أَحَطتُ` carries **no shaddah**, exactly as ي and و
carry none after a merged noon in [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]]. **A missing shaddah on a merged
letter is the muṣḥaf's standing signal for nāqiṣ.**

## Scholars differ — ب → م

`ٱرْكَب مَّعَنَا` (11:42) is classified as:

- **mutajānisayn** by some — ب and م are both labial, so same makhraj, different ṣifāt;
- **[[Idgham-Mutaqaribayn|mutaqāribayn]]** by others — within the lips, ب (both lips, with
  the inner surface) and م (both lips, with the outer) are argued to be distinct points.

**The disagreement is about the label, not the sound.** Both camps merge the ب into the م
and hold the resulting shaddah with ghunnah. This course files it under **mutajānisayn**
and notes the alternative — pick one and be consistent, because a learner who meets both
labels in two books will assume they are two different rules.

## Colour

Family B paints mutajānisayn **`#A1A1A1`** (neutral grey) — the same grey as
[[Idgham-Mutaqaribayn]]. The [cpfair/quran-tajweed](https://github.com/cpfair/quran-tajweed)
key is **`idghaam_mutajanisayn`**; see [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]].

The shared grey is deliberate: these two rules are rare, structurally similar, and are not
worth two colours a learner has to distinguish under pressure. Grey reads as *"something
merged here — look it up"*, which is the correct instruction for a rule with fewer than
thirty occurrences in the whole text.

## Inside the memorised sūrahs

**`عَبَدتُّمْ` — al-Kāfirūn 109:4.** A sākin د merging into a shaddah'd ت, in a sūrah every
learner already has. It is the only mutajānisayn in the ḥifẓ set, and it is worth pointing
at precisely because nobody notices it: `mā ʿabattum`, not `mā ʿabad-tum`.

Everything else on this page is a **reference example — outside your memorised sūrahs.**

## Common mistakes

- **Losing the ط's heaviness in `أَحَطتُ`, `بَسَطتَ`, `فَرَّطتُمْ`** — the signature error.
- **Pronouncing the merged letter separately** — `qad tabayyana` instead of `qat-tabayyana`.
- **Reading the missing shaddah on a nāqiṣ merge as "no idghām"**.
- **Not holding the shaddah** on the kāmil pairings.
