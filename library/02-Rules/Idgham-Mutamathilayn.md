# Idghām al-Mutamāthilayn — إِدْغَام المُتَمَاثِلَيْن

## Definition

**Two identical letters meet** — same makhraj, same ṣifāt — and the first, being sākin,
merges completely into the second. One doubled letter results.

*Mutamāthilān* means "the two alike".

This is the **simplest** of the four makhraj relationships, and the one every learner has
already met without being told: [[Idgham-Shafawi]] (م into م) and the ن-into-ن half of
[[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]] are both instances of it, taught earlier because they are
high-frequency.

## Where it sits in the theory

Idghām is classified twice over — by **how related** the two letters are, and by **their
vowel state**.

### By relationship (makhraj and ṣifāt)

| Type | Makhraj | Ṣifāt | Occurs in Ḥafṣ? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mutamāthilān** الْمُتَمَاثِلَان | Same | Same | **Yes — always merged** |
| **Mutajānisān** الْمُتَجَانِسَان — [[Idgham-Mutajanisayn]] | Same | Different | Yes — 7 pairings |
| **Mutaqāribān** الْمُتَقَارِبَان — [[Idgham-Mutaqaribayn]] | Close | Close | Yes — specific pairings |
| **Mutabāʿidān** الْمُتَبَاعِدَان | Far apart | Far apart | **No — always iẓhār** |

Mutabāʿidān is the residual category and the reason most letter sequences in the Qurʾān
have no rule at all: two distant letters simply cannot merge.

### By vowel state

| Type | Definition | Ruling in Ḥafṣ |
|---|---|---|
| **Ṣaghīr** الصَّغِير | First **sākin**, second **mutaḥarrik** | Idghām wherever the pair permits |
| **Kabīr** الْكَبِير | **Both** mutaḥarrik | Almost none — see below |
| **Muṭlaq** الْمُطْلَق | First **mutaḥarrik**, second **sākin** | **Never** idghām |

Everything taught in this course is **ṣaghīr**. That is not a limitation of the syllabus;
it is what Ḥafṣ actually transmits.

## Ruling

**Idghām kāmil, always.** Two identical letters have nothing to distinguish them, so
nothing can survive the merge. The second letter takes a shaddah and is held.

## Examples

- `ٱضْرِب بِّعَصَاكَ` (26:63) — ب into ب
- `قَد دَّخَلُوا` (5:61) — د into د
- `بَل لَّا يَخَافُونَ` (74:53) — ل into ل
- `أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن` (106:4) — م into م, i.e. [[Idgham-Shafawi]]
- `مِّن نِّعْمَةٍ` (16:53) — ن into ن, i.e. [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]] kāmil

## The exception — madd and leen letters do not merge

If the first of the two identical letters is a **madd letter** (ا و ي in their madd
configuration) or a **leen letter**, there is **no idghām**. The letter is a vowel-carrier,
not a consonant to be absorbed; merging it would destroy the prolongation.

- **`ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ`** — an-Nās **114:5**. The ى of `ٱلَّذِى` is a madd letter and the
  next word begins with ي. They are identical letters in sequence, and **nothing happens**.
- `قَالُوا۟ وَهُمْ` — the و of `قَالُوا۟` is a madd letter before a و.
- `فِى يَوْمٍ` — the ى is a madd letter before a ي.

114:5 is inside the ḥifẓ set, which makes the exception teachable from memory rather than
from a reference. Point at it directly: *"two yāʾs, no merge — because the first one is a
madd."*

## Idghām kabīr — only two places in Ḥafṣ

When **both** letters carry a vowel, Ḥafṣ merges in exactly two words:

1. **`تَأْمَ۫نَّا`** — Yūsuf **12:11** (originally تَأْمَنُنَا). Read with **ishmām** (lips
   round silently on the merged ن) or **rawm / ikhtilāṣ** (a fraction of the vowel is
   sounded). Both are transmitted.
2. **`مَكَّنِّى`** — al-Kahf 18:95 (originally مَكَّنَنِى).

**Idghām kabīr as a *system* belongs to Abū ʿAmr al-Baṣrī, not to Ḥafṣ.** Flag this
explicitly: a learner who listens to Abū ʿAmr recitations will hear merges that are simply
not available in the reading they are being taught, and will conclude they have been taught
wrongly.

## No colour, no cpfair key

**Mutamāthilayn has no colour in this course and no key in
[cpfair/quran-tajweed](https://github.com/cpfair/quran-tajweed).**

Its two high-frequency cases are already annotated under their own names — `idghaam_shafawi`
for م + م and `idghaam_ghunnah` for ن + ن — so cpfair has no separate category for the
general rule. The remaining cases (ب+ب, د+د, ل+ل) are left unmarked.

Practically: on a coloured page, `ٱضْرِب بِّعَصَاكَ` shows nothing but the shaddah. **The
shaddah is the annotation.** This is worth saying out loud, because it teaches the more
general habit — a shaddah on the first letter of a word, where the previous word ended in
the same letter, *is* an idghām mark, regardless of colour.

## Common mistakes

- **Pronouncing both letters separately** — `iḍrib biʿaṣāka` instead of `iḍribbiʿaṣāka`.
- **Failing to hold the resulting shaddah** for its full duration.
- **Merging after a madd letter** — `ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ` is the case to drill.
- **Importing Abū ʿAmr's kabīr merges** into a Ḥafṣ recitation.
