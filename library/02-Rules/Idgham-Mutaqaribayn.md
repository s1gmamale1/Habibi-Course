# Idghām al-Mutaqāribayn — إِدْغَام المُتَقَارِبَيْن

## Definition

Two letters whose **makhārij and/or ṣifāt are close but not identical**. The first, being
sākin, merges into the second.

*Mutaqāribān* means "the two that are near each other".

This is the loosest of the three productive relationships — see [[Idgham-Mutamathilayn]]
for the full classification — and correspondingly the one with the fewest cases, because
"close" is not close enough to force a merge except where the tradition transmits one.

## The pairings in Ḥafṣ

| Pair | Type | Verified examples |
|---|---|---|
| **ل → ر** | Kāmil | `قُل رَّبِّ` 23:118 · `بَل رَّفَعَهُ` 4:158 |
| **ق → ك** | **Nāqiṣ** per most; kāmil per some | `نَخْلُقكُّم` 77:20 — the only occurrence |
| **ن → ل, ر** | Kāmil | `مِّن لَّدُنْهُ` 18:2 · `مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ` 2:5 |

The third row is not a new rule. It is [[Idgham-Bila-Ghunnah]] — the noon sākinah merging
into ل or ر — viewed from the theory side rather than the noon-rules side. Learners meet it
long before they meet this page; the point here is that **it was never a special case**. It
is what happens when three letters share the gum ridge and two of them are near enough to
absorb the third.

**ل → ر is the mirror of it.** The same two letters, merging the other way.

## أَلَمْ نَخْلُقكُّم — one word, one disagreement

Al-Mursalāt **77:20** is the **only** place in the Qurʾān where a sākin **ق** meets a **ك**.
Ḥafṣ transmits **both wajh**:

- **Idghām nāqiṣ** — the ق's body merges into the ك, but its **isti'lā** (see [[Istila]])
  remains, so the ك is pronounced heavy. **This is the more common teaching.**
- **Idghām kāmil** — the ق disappears entirely and a plain doubled ك is heard.

Both are valid; neither is an error. Teach **nāqiṣ**, permit kāmil, and say clearly that
the choice is between two transmitted readings rather than between right and wrong — this
is one of the few places where a learner will hear two reciters differ and needs to know it
is not a mistake.

The pattern is identical to **ط → ت** in [[Idgham-Mutajanisayn]]: a heavy letter merges, and
the argument is over whether its heaviness travels with it. In both cases the muṣḥaf's
missing shaddah on the merged letter is the orthographic tell.

## Colour

Family B paints mutaqāribayn **`#A1A1A1`** (neutral grey) — the same grey as
[[Idgham-Mutajanisayn]]. The [cpfair/quran-tajweed](https://github.com/cpfair/quran-tajweed)
key is **`idghaam_mutaqaribayn`**; see [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]].

Note a real inconsistency in the colour scheme, and do not hide it from learners: **ن → ل /
ر is coloured `#169200` as [[Idgham-Bila-Ghunnah]], not grey**, even though it is a
mutaqāribayn merge. The palette classifies by *where the rule is taught* (the noon-sākinah
chapter), not by *what the phonetics are*. Both classifications are correct; they answer
different questions.

## Not in the memorised sūrahs

There is no ل → ر or ق → ك in al-Fātiḥa or sūrahs 105–114. Every example on this page is a
**reference example — outside your memorised sūrahs.**

The ن → ل / ر row, however, **is** in the ḥifẓ set — `وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ` (112:4) and
`فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ` (107:4). Teach the relationship from those, then generalise to
`قُل رَّبِّ`.

## Common mistakes

- **Flattening the ق in `نَخْلُقكُّم`** when reading it as nāqiṣ — the heaviness is the
  entire content of that wajh.
- **Treating the two wujūh as an error** in whichever reciter you did not learn from.
- **Pronouncing the ل before the ر** — `qul rabbi` instead of `qur-rabbi`.
- **Forgetting the rāʾ rules on the merged ر** — it is still a rāʾ and still takes tafkhīm
  or tarqīq from its own vowel.
