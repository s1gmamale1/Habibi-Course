# Al-Idhlāq — الإذلاق

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *idhlāq* is sharpness and fluency of speech — a *dhaliq al-lisan* is
someone with a quick, ready tongue. The word also names the **tip/edge of the tongue**
(*dhalq*).

**Technically**, idhlāq is the **ease and speed with which the letter is pronounced**,
because these letters issue either from the edge of the tongue (ر, ن, ل) or from the lips
(ف, م, ب) — the two most mobile and least effortful articulators in the mouth.

## Its opposite

[[Ismat]] — heaviness and slowness of articulation, from makharij that are harder to reach.

## Letters

Six letters:

> **فِرَّ مِنْ لُبِّ** — *firra min lubb*

[[Fa|ف]] · [[Ra|ر]] · [[Meem|م]] · [[Noon|ن]] · [[Lam|ل]] · [[Ba|ب]]

Three from the tongue's edge (ر, ن, ل), three from the lips (ف, م, ب) — the mnemonic is
worth teaching alongside that split, since the split is the actual reason for the grouping.

## What it sounds like

Idhlāq is the one sifah a teacher does not usually diagnose by ear on a single letter: it
describes facility rather than a distinguishing sound. What *is* audible is its
consequence — these six letters are the ones a rushing student slurs, because they are
easy enough to be produced carelessly. Listen for ن and م that lose their
[[Ghunnah-sifah]], and for ر that softens into an English approximant.

## Why it matters

- **Morphological test.** Classical grammarians observed that a genuine Arabic root of
  four or five letters is never free of at least one idhlāq letter. A quadriliteral or
  quinquiliteral word made entirely of [[Ismat]] letters is, on this test, a borrowing
  rather than native Arabic. This is why the pair is included in tajweed manuals at all:
  the practical payoff is lexical, not phonetic.
- Four of the six (ر, ن, ل, م) are also [[Tawassut]] letters, and ر and ل carry
  [[Inhiraf]] — the same mobility that makes them fluent is what lets their sound deviate
  around the obstruction.
- Because these letters are effortless, they are the ones that survive when a reciter
  tires. Endurance drills should target them for *precision*, not for effort.
