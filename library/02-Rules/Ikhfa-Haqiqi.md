# Al-Ikhfāʾ al-Ḥaqīqī — الإِخْفَاء الحَقِيقِيّ

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *ikhfāʾ* is concealment.

**Technically**, it is pronouncing the noon sākinah or tanwīn in a state **between
[[Izhar-Halqi|iẓhār]] and [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah|idghām]]**, **without a shaddah**, with a
**2-count ghunnah**.

Mechanically: **the tongue never touches the noon's makhraj.** It leaves the gum ridge
alone and instead moves into position for the letter that is coming, while the nasal
sound runs for 2 counts. The noon is neither said nor deleted — it is *hidden*.

It is called **ḥaqīqī** ("true") because the noon is genuinely concealed, in contrast to
[[Ikhfa-Shafawi|ikhfāʾ shafawī]], where a meem is concealed at the lips.

## Condition

Noon sākinah (نْ) or tanwīn followed by any of the **15 remaining letters** — everything
that is not one of the 6 throat letters, the 4 of يَنْمُو, the 2 of ل ر, or ب.

This makes ikhfāʾ **by far the most frequent** of the noon rules: 15 letters out of 28.

## The 15 letters

**ص · ذ · ث · ك · ج · ش · ق · س · د · ط · ز · ف · ت · ض · ظ**

Al-Jamzūrī's mnemonic in Tuhfat al-Atfal is a **15-word poem**, and the **first letter of
each word** is one of the 15:

> صِفْ ذَا ثَنَا كَمْ جَادَ شَخْصٌ قَدْ سَمَا ⁕ دُمْ طَيِّبًا زِدْ فِي تُقًى ضَعْ ظَالِمَا

*"Describe one of praise — how generously a person of standing gave! Remain good, increase
in piety, put aside one who wrongs."*

Learn the couplet as language, not as a letter list. Recovering the letters from the words
is fast; recovering 15 unordered letters from memory is not.

## Duration and shape

**2 counts of ghunnah — and no shaddah.**

The absence of the shaddah is the whole difference between ikhfāʾ and idghām, and the
muṣḥaf shows it: in `مِن جُوعٍ` the ج carries **no** shaddah, while in `مِن نِّعْمَةٍ`
(16:53) the ن does. Learners who double the following letter have produced idghām by
mistake; learners who touch the gum ridge have produced iẓhār by mistake.

## Heavy or light? — the ghunnah takes the colour of what follows

The ghunnah of ikhfāʾ is **not fixed in weight**. It adopts the heaviness of the letter it
is running into:

- **Heavy (mufakhkham)** when the next letter is one of the isti'lā letters — see
  [[Istila]]. Among the 15 these are **ص ض ط ظ ق**:
  `عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ` (107:5) · `مِن قَبْلُ` (2:25)
- **Light (muraqqaq)** before the other ten:
  `وَلَآ أَنتُمْ` (109:3) · `كُنتُمْ` (2:23) · `أَنفُسَهُمْ` (2:9)

This is the earliest place in the syllabus where tafkhīm and tarqīq stop being a property
of *letters* and become a property of a *sound in context*. It is worth slowing down for.

## Three degrees of concealment

The nearer the following letter's makhraj is to the noon's, the closer the ikhfāʾ sits to
idghām:

| Degree | Letters | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Aʿlā** (أعلى) — closest to idghām | ط · د · ت | Makhraj almost on the noon's own |
| **Awsaṭ** (أوسط) — middle | the remaining 11 | The default sound |
| **Adnā** (أدنى) — closest to iẓhār | [[Qaf]] ق · [[Kaf]] ك | Furthest makhraj; the noon is barely veiled |

This is a **descriptive** classification, not three different rulings — all three are
2-count ghunnah with no shaddah. Its practical use is diagnostic: a student whose `مِن
قَبْلُ` sounds like iẓhār and whose `أَنتُمْ` sounds like idghām is not making two errors,
they are exaggerating a real gradient.

## The Quraysh anchor — 106:4

> ٱلَّذِىٓ أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ

`مِّن جُوعٍ` (ikhfāʾ, [[Jeem]] ج) against `مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ` ([[Izhar-Halqi|iẓhār]], [[Kha]] خ)
— the same word `مِن`, two lines apart, treated in opposite ways. Drill the pair together;
neither rule is properly learned in isolation. Full discussion in [[Izhar-Halqi]].

## Common mistakes

- **Letting the tongue touch the noon's makhraj** — produces iẓhār.
- **Doubling the following letter** — produces idghām.
- **Omitting the tafkhīm** before ص ض ط ظ ق, so `مِن قَبْلُ` comes out thin.
- **Dropping the ghunnah entirely on ف and ك**, which are the two the ear forgives most
  easily and therefore the two that decay first.
- **Adding a shaddah** to the following letter in writing or in recitation.
