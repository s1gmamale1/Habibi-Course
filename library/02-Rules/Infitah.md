# Al-Infitāḥ — الانفتاح

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *infitāḥ* is opening, being open, separation.

**Technically**, infitāḥ is the **remaining of a gap between the tongue and the palate**
during the letter, so that air passes out between them instead of being trapped. It is
the ordinary, unclamped configuration of the mouth.

## Its opposite

[[Itbaq]] — the tongue clamps flat against the palate and seals the airspace.

## Letters

Twenty-five letters — everything except ص ض ط ظ:

ء · ا · ب · ت · ث · ج · ح · خ · د · ذ · ر · ز · س · ش · ع · غ · ف · ق · ك · ل · م · ن ·
ه · و · ي

Note the three letters that are **istiʿlāʾ *and* infitāḥ**: خ, غ, ق. They are heavy
letters that are not clamped, which places them at a lower degree of heaviness than
ص ض ط ظ. Every other infitāḥ letter is also [[Istifal]] and therefore light.

## What it sounds like

Infitāḥ is heard as **openness** — the sound is not boxed. The useful teaching move is
minimal-pair contrast rather than isolated drilling:

- س (infitāḥ) against ص (iṭbāq)
- ت (infitāḥ) against ط (iṭbāq)
- ذ (infitāḥ) against ظ (iṭbāq)
- د (infitāḥ) against ض (iṭbāq)

A student who cannot hear the difference in the pair cannot yet produce either member of
it reliably.

## Why it matters

- Infitāḥ is what makes the light letters light and keeps them light: heaviness bleeding
  from a neighbouring heavy letter is, mechanically, an unwanted clamp. See [[Tafkhim]].
- The distinction between infitāḥ istiʿlāʾ letters (خ غ ق) and iṭbāq letters (ص ض ط ظ) is
  the reason tafkhim has *degrees* rather than being a switch.
- It is also the reason the قَظْ-type heavy letters can sit next to light letters without
  contaminating them: nothing was ever sealed.
