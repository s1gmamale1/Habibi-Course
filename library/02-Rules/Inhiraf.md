# Al-Inḥirāf — الانحراف

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *inḥirāf* is turning aside, deviating, going off at an angle.

**Technically**, inḥirāf is the **deviation of the sound away from its own makhraj toward
another point** in the mouth. The tongue makes contact, the airstream cannot pass through
that contact, and the sound bends around it instead of stopping.

## Its opposite

None. Inḥirāf is one of the sifat without an opposite.

## Letters

Two letters, deviating in opposite directions:

- **ل** — the tongue tip touches the gum ridge, and the sound deviates **around the sides**
  of the tongue toward the tip. (This is what phoneticians call a lateral.)
- **ر** — the tongue tip touches slightly further back, and the sound deviates **toward the
  back** of the tongue.

Both are [[Tawassut]] letters, and inḥirāf is the mechanism *behind* their tawassut: the
sound is neither blocked nor freely flowing precisely because it is detouring.

## What it sounds like

The teacher listens for a **held sound that still has a point of contact**. Ask for a
sustained sakin ل: the tip must stay planted on the ridge for the whole duration while
the sound continues around it. If the tip lifts, the letter turns into a vowel; if the
sound stops, it turns into a د.

Failure modes:

- **ل produced without contact** — a dark, English-style "l" made with the back of the
  tongue, common for English and Russian speakers.
- **ر produced as an approximant** — the English "r", with no contact at all. There is no
  deviation because there is no obstruction to deviate around.
- **ر over-deviated** — the tongue slides back far enough to darken every ر into a heavy
  one. Weight is decided by ر's own rules, not by inḥirāf. See [[Ra]].

## Why it matters

- Inḥirāf is the reason ل and ر can be held without hissing, and the reason they are the
  two letters most often mispronounced by *substitution from the first language* rather
  than by omission — every language has some sort of l and r, and the student imports theirs.
- ر's deviation toward the back of the tongue is what makes it capable of heaviness at
  all, and is bound up with its other unique sifah, [[Takrir]].
- ل is the letter whose weight is governed by a dedicated rule (the ل of ٱللَّه) — see
  [[Lam]] — and by the sun/moon letter assimilation of ٱل.
