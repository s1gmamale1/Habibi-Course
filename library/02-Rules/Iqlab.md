# Al-Iqlāb — الإِقْلَاب

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *iqlāb* is turning a thing over — converting it.

**Technically**, it is **converting the noon sākinah or tanwīn into a hidden meem**
(*mīm mukhfāh*), pronounced with a **2-count ghunnah**, when the letter **ب** follows.

The noon is not said, and the meem is not fully said either. What is produced is a nasal
held at the lips, on its way into the ب.

## Condition

Noon sākinah or tanwīn followed by **ب** — inside a word or across a word boundary.

## The trigger — one letter only

**ب** — and nothing else. Iqlāb is the narrowest of the four noon rules: a single letter
against ikhfāʾ's fifteen.

**Why a meem?** The ب is a lip letter. Reaching it from the noon's gum-ridge position and
still holding a nasal is mechanically awkward; the nearest nasal sound the lips *can* make
is a meem. So the tongue gives up its position and the nasal moves to the lips. The rule is
a shortcut the mouth takes on its own.

## Articulation — the light touch

This is a rule that is almost always taught correctly and almost always executed wrongly.

**The lips come together lightly, without pressure.** They must not:

- **press shut** — that produces a clear, fully-formed meem, which is [[Izhar-Shafawi|iẓhār]]
  of a meem, not iqlāb;
- **stay apart** — that leaves the original noon audible.

They rest against each other just enough to close the mouth, the nasal runs for 2 counts,
and only then is the ب released. If you can feel your lips tense, you are pressing.

This is the same lip posture as [[Ikhfa-Shafawi]] — and the sounds are near-identical. The
two rules differ only in **what the letter was before the rule applied**: a noon (iqlāb) or
a meem (ikhfāʾ shafawī). Teach them adjacent, and teach the *cause*, not the sound.

## How the muṣḥaf marks it

A **small standing meem** — `ـۢ` (U+06E2) — is printed **above the noon**, or in place of
one stroke of the tanwīn:

- `أَنۢبِئْهُم` — above the noon
- `مِّنۢ بَعْدِهِۦ` — above the noon
- `خَوْفٍۭ` `أَحَدٌۢ` `مَّسَدٍۭ` — replacing a stroke of the tanwīn

**The small meem is not a vowel and is never pronounced as a full meem.** It is a
pronunciation instruction: *"here, the noon has become a nasal at the lips."* Learners who
read it as a written meem produce `an-m-baʾ`.

The Uthmānī orthography also **omits the sukūn** on an iqlāb noon (`أَنۢبِئْهُم`, not
`أَنْۢبِئْهُم`), for the same reason it omits it on an ikhfāʾ noon: the noon is not being
pronounced in its written place. Compare `مِّنْ` in [[Izhar-Halqi]], which keeps its sukūn.

## Duration

**2 counts of ghunnah**, held at the lips before the ب is released. See [[Ghunnah]].

## Where it occurs in the memorised sūrahs — an unexpected answer

Course research assumed iqlāb had **no clean example** in the ḥifẓ set (al-Fātiḥa +
105–114). Checked against the pinned corpus, that is **half right**:

- **There is no ayah-internal iqlāb** anywhere in al-Fātiḥa or sūrahs 105–114. Confirmed by
  a full scan. The nearest candidate, `تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ` (105:4), is a *meem* before
  ب — [[Ikhfa-Shafawi]], not iqlāb.
- **But five ayāt in the ḥifẓ set carry the iqlāb sign** anyway, and it is real:

| Ref | Word | Why the sign is there |
|---|---|---|
| 105:5 | `كَعَصْفٍ مَّأْكُولٍۭ` | joins into `بِسْمِ` of Quraysh |
| 106:4 | `خَوْفٍۭ` | joins into `بِسْمِ` of al-Māʿūn |
| 110:3 | `تَوَّابًۢا` | joins into `بِسْمِ` of al-Masad |
| 111:5 | `مَّسَدٍۭ` | joins into `بِسْمِ` of al-Ikhlāṣ |
| 112:4 | `أَحَدٌۢ` | joins into `بِسْمِ` of al-Falaq |

When a reciter runs one sūrah into the next through the basmalah, the **final tanwīn meets
the ب of بِسْمِ** — and iqlāb applies. The muṣḥaf prints the small meem there precisely
because reciting the sūrahs consecutively is the normal case.

**Pedagogically this is the best possible teaching example**, because the learner already
recites these joins in ṣalāh every day, usually without noticing what they are doing. It
also explains a mark they have been looking at for years.

Note the condition: **the rule only fires on continuation.** If you stop (waqf) at the end
of the sūrah, the tanwīn is dropped and there is no iqlāb — which is itself a useful lesson
about tanwīn.

## Reference examples — outside your memorised sūrahs

For iqlāb **within** a single ayah, use:

- `أَنۢبِئْهُم` — al-Baqarah 2:33 (within one word)
- `مِّنۢ بَعْدِهِۦ` — Āl ʿImrān 3:160 (across a word boundary)
- `لَيُنۢبَذَنَّ` — al-Humazah 104:4 (one sūrah before the ḥifẓ set, so easy to add)

**Correction to the course brief:** the candidate ref `2:97` for `أَنۢبِئْهُم` is **wrong** —
2:97 does not contain the word. The correct reference is **2:33** (`قَالَ يَـٰٓـَٔادَمُ
أَنۢبِئْهُم بِأَسْمَآئِهِمْ`), which is corpus-verified. As a bonus, that phrase contains an
iqlāb and an [[Ikhfa-Shafawi|ikhfāʾ shafawī]] four letters apart — `أَنۢبِئْهُم بِ` — making
it the ideal drill for the pair.

## Common mistakes

- **Pressing the lips fully shut** — produces a plain meem.
- **Omitting the ghunnah** — produces `am-baʾ` with no nasal hold.
- **Pronouncing the original noon** — `an-baʾ`.
- **Reading the small meem as a written letter**, inserting a meem *and* a noon.
- **Applying it at waqf**, when the tanwīn has been dropped and there is nothing to convert.
