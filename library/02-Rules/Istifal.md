# Al-Istifāl — الاستفال

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *istifāl* is going low, sinking, descending.

**Technically**, istifāl is the **staying-down of the tongue** during the letter: the
tongue body remains on the floor of the mouth instead of rising toward the palate, so the
resonance stays forward and bright. The letter is heard as *muraqqaq*, light.

Istifāl is the default state of the Arabic letter. It is not a thing the student adds;
it is what remains when nothing heavy is being done.

## Its opposite

[[Istila]] — the rear of the tongue rises and the letter is heavy. The two sets divide
the 29 letters exhaustively: 7 istiʿlāʾ, 22 istifāl.

## Letters

Twenty-two letters — every letter outside خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ:

ء · ا · ب · ت · ث · ج · ح · د · ذ · ر · ز · س · ش · ع · ف · ك · ل · م · ن · ه · و · ي

**Three of these are light by default but heavy under stated conditions**, and each has
its own rule note:

- **ل** — light everywhere except the ل of the name ٱللَّه when preceded by fathah or
  dammah. See [[Lam]].
- **ر** — governed by its own set of conditions. See [[Ra-Tafkhim]] and [[Ra-Tarqiq]].
- **ا** — has no independent ruling at all; alif inherits the weight of the letter before it.

These are *exceptions to the lightness*, not exceptions to istifāl: the letters remain
istifāl letters, and the heaviness is imported by a separate rule.

## What it sounds like

The teacher listens for **brightness that does not darken near a heavy neighbour**. The
telling test is a light letter sitting immediately beside a heavy one — ٱلصِّرَٰطَ has a
light ر after a heavy ص, and أَنْعَمْتَ has a light ن and م among throat letters.

The characteristic error is **heaviness bleeding backwards or forwards** onto ل, ر, ا,
م, ن or ب adjacent to an istiʿlāʾ letter. The student is not mispronouncing the heavy
letter; they are failing to switch back. Isolating the two letters and alternating them
slowly is the standard correction.

The mirror error is **over-lightening**, in which a student who has been drilled on
tarqiq thins the letters into a nasal English-sounding register. Light is not thin.

## Why it matters

- Istifāl is the input to tarqiq: the reason the great majority of letters are light in
  every position is exactly this sifah. See [[Tafkhim]] for the paired system.
- The ghunnah of [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]] is light when the following letter is istifāl and heavy when
  it is istiʿlāʾ — the light case is the more common one, and is the one students lose
  once they learn the heavy one.
- Because istifāl is the default, a lesson on heaviness should always end by returning
  to a light word: the skill being taught is the *switch*, not the heaviness.
