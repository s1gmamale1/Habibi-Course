# Al-Istiʿlāʾ — الاستعلاء

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *istiʿlāʾ* is rising, ascending, seeking elevation.

**Technically**, istiʿlāʾ is the **rising of the rear of the tongue toward the roof of
the mouth** during the letter. The raised tongue body enlarges the resonating space
behind it and darkens the sound: the letter is heard as *mufakhkham*, heavy.

This is the single most load-bearing sifah in the whole tajweed syllabus, because
heaviness in Qur'anic recitation is defined by it rather than described case by case.

## Its opposite

[[Istifal]] — the tongue stays low and the letter is light (*muraqqaq*). All 22 remaining
letters are istifal.

## Letters

Seven letters:

> **خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ** — *khussa daghtin qiz*

[[Kha|خ]] · [[Sad|ص]] · [[Dad|ض]] · [[Ghayn|غ]] · [[Ta-heavy|ط]] · [[Qaf|ق]] · [[Za-heavy|ظ]]

Four of the seven — ص ض ط ظ — additionally carry [[Itbaq]], which is a *stronger* form of
the same elevation. The remaining three — خ غ ق — are istiʿlāʾ with [[Infitah]], and are
therefore audibly lighter than the itbaq four while still being heavy letters.

## What it sounds like

The teacher listens for a **dark, hollow, "back" quality** that fills the mouth, and
watches the jaw and tongue: on a heavy letter the tongue's rear visibly bunches upward.

The diagnostic test is the **kasrah test**. These seven letters are heavy in *every*
position and with *every* vowel; the vowel changes only the degree, never the fact. Ask
the student to read ٱلصِّرَٰطَ: the ص carries a kasrah, and the overwhelming instinct is
to flatten it into a light س-like sound. If the ص goes light under kasrah, istiʿlāʾ has
not been internalised. The same trap sits on قِ, طِ, ضِ.

The second failure is **bleeding**: heaviness that starts on the heavy letter and then
leaks onto the following light letters, or heaviness that is applied only to the
consonant and drops on its vowel. Heaviness must fill the letter and stop at its edge.

## Why it matters

Istiʿlāʾ is not a descriptive footnote — downstream rules are *defined* in terms of its
seven letters:

- **[[Tafkhim]] and tarqiq.** The rule "these letters are always heavy" is simply
  istiʿlāʾ restated. The whole heaviness/lightness system, including the degrees of
  tafkhim by vowel, takes خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ as its input set.
- **ر heaviness.** Whether a ر is heavy or light is decided partly by whether a
  neighbouring letter is one of the istiʿlāʾ seven. See [[Ra-Tafkhim]] and [[Ra-Tarqiq]].
- **Heavy ghunnah in [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]].** Five of the fifteen ikhfa letters — ص ض ط ظ ق —
  are istiʿlāʾ letters. When one of them follows a noon sakinah or tanween, the two-count
  ghunnah of the ikhfa is itself pronounced **heavy**; before the other ten it is
  pronounced light. (The remaining two istiʿlāʾ letters, خ and غ, are throat letters and
  take izhar instead.) A student who has not internalised istiʿlāʾ cannot execute ikhfa
  correctly, because they will produce one flat ghunnah everywhere.
- **Alif** has no heaviness of its own: it inherits from the letter before it. That rule
  only works if the istiʿlāʾ set is known.
