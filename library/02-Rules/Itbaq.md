# Al-Iṭbāq — الإطباق

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *iṭbāq* is covering one thing with another, clamping a lid onto a pot.

**Technically**, iṭbāq is the **clamping of the body of the tongue up against the roof of
the mouth**, sealing the airspace between them. Where [[Istila]] merely raises the rear of
the tongue, iṭbāq brings a whole stretch of the tongue flat against the palate and traps
the sound in the pocket underneath. This is the strongest form of heaviness in the
language.

## Its opposite

[[Infitah]] — a gap remains between tongue and palate and the air escapes freely.

## Letters

Four letters:

ص · ض · ط · ظ

All four are also [[Istila]] letters. **Iṭbāq is a strict subset of istiʿlāʾ**: every
iṭbāq letter is heavy, but not every heavy letter is clamped. This is exactly why ص is
heavier than خ, and ط heavier than ق — خ, غ, ق have istiʿlāʾ with infitah, while ص ض ط ظ
have istiʿlāʾ *and* iṭbāq stacked together.

Each of the four has a light counterpart at or near the same makhraj, which is what makes
them so error-prone:

| Clamped | Collapses to |
|---|---|
| ص | س |
| ض | د (or "z") |
| ط | ت |
| ظ | ذ (or "z") |

## What it sounds like

The teacher listens for **a closed, boxed-in resonance** — the sound seems to come from
inside a covered space rather than from an open mouth. A student with iṭbāq produces a ص
that is unmistakably not a س even when whispered.

The characteristic failure is the **half-heavy letter**: the student raises the back of
the tongue (istiʿlāʾ achieved) but never flattens it against the palate (iṭbāq missing).
The result sounds "sort of heavy" and is the single most common intermediate plateau.
The correction is tactile, not auditory — have the student feel the width of tongue
contact on ط, then transfer that contact to ص and ظ.

The ذ/ز/ظ/ض **collapse-to-"z" cluster** is explicitly documented for Persian, Turkish and
Urdu speakers, and is the highest-priority error to catch early.

## Why it matters

- Iṭbāq letters take the **strongest degree of tafkhim** in every position; they are the
  reference point against which the lighter heavy letters are calibrated. See [[Tafkhim]].
- A noon sakinah before ص ض ط ظ takes [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]] with a **heavy** ghunnah — the ghunnah is
  coloured by the clamped letter that follows it.
- ط is both an iṭbāq letter and a qalqalah letter, which is why a correct sakin ط is the
  hardest single sound to produce cleanly: it must be clamped, heavy, and bounced without
  adding a vowel. See [[Qalqalah-sifah]].
- ض's iṭbāq combines with [[Istitalah]] to make it the letter Arabic itself is named after.
