# Al-Iẓhār al-Ḥalqī — الإِظْهَار الحَلْقِيّ

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *iẓhār* is making a thing plain.

**Technically**, it is pronouncing the **noon sākinah or tanwīn clearly from its own
makhraj**, with no merging into what follows and **no prolongation of the ghunnah**.

The noon is simply itself. Nothing happens to it.

## Condition

A noon sākinah (نْ) or a tanwīn (ـً ـٌ ـٍ) is followed by one of the **six throat
letters** — whether inside one word or across a word boundary.

## The six letters

**ء · [[Ha-soft]] ه · [[Ayn]] ع · [[Ha-throat]] ح · [[Ghayn]] غ · [[Kha]] خ**

Tuhfat al-Atfal lists them in makhraj order, deepest throat outward:

> هَمْزٌ فَهَاءٌ ثُمَّ عَيْنٌ حَاءُ ⁕ مُهْمَلَتَانِ ثُمَّ غَيْنٌ خَاءُ

The common teaching mnemonic is the sentence
**أَخِي هَاكَ عِلْمًا حَازَهُ غَيْرُ خَاسِرِ** — *"My brother, here is knowledge that a
non-loser has acquired."* Its first letters spell ء ه ع ح غ خ.

**The reason is mechanical, not arbitrary.** The noon's makhraj is the tip of the tongue
against the gum ridge; the throat letters are as far away from it as a letter can get.
There is nothing for the noon to assimilate *toward*, so it stays whole. Every one of
the four noon rules is a consequence of distance:

| Distance from the noon | What happens | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Furthest (throat) | Nothing | **Iẓhār ḥalqī** |
| Nearest / identical | Full merge | [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]], [[Idgham-Bila-Ghunnah]] |
| Lips (ب) | Becomes a meem | [[Iqlab]] |
| Everything in between | Half-way state | [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]] |

## Duration

**No prolongation.** The noon's own inherent ghunnah is still there — it is a *sifah
lāzimah* and cannot be removed — but it is **not extended**. See the levels table in
[[Ghunnah]]: this is level 4, not level 0.

## No colour — and that is the point

**Iẓhār has no colour in this course, and no key in
[cpfair/quran-tajweed](https://github.com/cpfair/quran-tajweed).**

This is not an oversight in the data. Colour-coded muṣḥafs mark **modifications** —
places where the letter on the page is not what comes out of the mouth. Iẓhār is the
*absence* of a modification, so there is nothing to mark.

Teach this explicitly: **the absence of colour is itself a signal.** A learner scanning a
coloured page should read black-on-white as an instruction — *"pronounce exactly what is
written"* — rather than as "no rule here". Half of tajwīd literacy is knowing when to do
nothing.

The same holds for [[Izhar-Shafawi]] on the meem side.

## The Quraysh anchor — 106:4

Sūrat Quraysh ayah 4 puts the two most-confused noon rules **side by side in one line**:

> ٱلَّذِىٓ أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ

- **مِّن جُوعٍ** — noon sākinah + ج → [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]], concealed, 2-count ghunnah,
  coloured `#9400A8`.
- **مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ** — noon sākinah + خ → **iẓhār ḥalqī**, plain, no colour.

Same word, `مِن`. Same position. Opposite treatment, decided entirely by the next letter.
Note also that the muṣḥaf prints the sukūn **only on the iẓhār noon** (`مِّنْ`) and leaves
it bare on the ikhfāʾ noon (`مِّن`) — the orthography is already telling you which is
which, before any colour is applied.

This ayah is the single best drill in the whole nasal syllabus, and it sits inside the
memorised sūrahs.

## Common mistakes

- **Lengthening the ghunnah** — turning iẓhār into ikhfāʾ. The most frequent error, and it
  happens because the learner has just spent a lesson practising 2-count nasals.
- **Inserting a vowel** between the noon and the throat letter (`min-a-khawf`), usually
  because the throat letter is hard to reach from a sākin position.
- **Weakening or swallowing the noon** so it barely sounds.
- **Over-correcting into denasalisation** — a hard, dry "n" with no nasal resonance at
  all. The ghunnah is not prolonged; it is not deleted.
