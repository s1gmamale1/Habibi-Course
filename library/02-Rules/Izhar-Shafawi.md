# Al-Iẓhār ash-Shafawī — الإِظْهَار الشَّفَوِيّ

## Definition

**Pronouncing the meem sākinah clearly** — lips **fully closed**, meem fully formed, **no
prolonged ghunnah** — before any letter other than ب and م.

## Condition

Meem sākinah (مْ) followed by any of the **remaining 26 letters**: everything except **ب**
(which gives [[Ikhfa-Shafawi]]) and **م** (which gives [[Idgham-Shafawi]]).

Two exceptions, twenty-six defaults. Iẓhār shafawī is the meem's normal state, and by a
wide margin the most frequent of the three meem rules.

## The lips close — properly

This rule is the mirror image of [[Ikhfa-Shafawi]] in a way that trips learners who study
the two in the wrong order:

| | Lips | Ghunnah |
|---|---|---|
| **Iẓhār shafawī** (26 letters) | **Fully closed** | Not prolonged |
| [[Ikhfa-Shafawi]] (ب) | **Lightly touching** | 2 counts |
| [[Idgham-Shafawi]] (م) | Fully closed, doubled | 2 counts |

"No prolongation" does **not** mean "no nasal". A meem cannot exist without ghunnah — it is
an inseparable *sifah*. What iẓhār removes is the **hold**, not the resonance. See the
levels table in [[Ghunnah]]: this is level 4.

## Special care before و and ف

Tuhfat al-Atfal singles these two out by name:

> وَٱحْذَرْ لَدَى وَاوٍ وَفَا أَنْ تَخْتَفِي
> *"And beware, at a wāw or a fāʾ, of letting it be concealed."*

**Why these two.** ب and م are not the meem's only lip neighbours:

- **و** is articulated at the **lips**, exactly like the meem. The mouth is already there;
  closing for the meem and then rounding for the wāw feels redundant, so the meem gets
  swallowed.
- **ف** is articulated at the **lower lip against the upper teeth** — immediately adjacent.
  Same pull, slightly weaker.

The result in both cases is a half-concealed meem that sounds like [[Ikhfa-Shafawi]] applied
to the wrong letter. **The iẓhār must be strongest here, not weakest.**

Both danger cases are inside the memorised sūrahs:

- **`عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا`** — al-Fātiḥa 1:7, recited seventeen times a day
- **`كَيْدَهُمْ فِى تَضْلِيلٍ`** — al-Fīl 105:2

Drill them by exaggerating: close the lips fully, pause a fraction, then release into the
و / ف. Once the lips learn the closure, remove the pause.

## No colour — and that is the point

**Iẓhār shafawī has no colour in this course, and no key in
[cpfair/quran-tajweed](https://github.com/cpfair/quran-tajweed).**

Colour-coded muṣḥafs mark **modifications** — the places where what you say differs from
what is written. Iẓhār is the *absence* of a modification: the meem on the page is exactly
the meem in the mouth, so there is nothing to annotate.

Teach this explicitly. **On a coloured page, the absence of colour is itself an
instruction** — *"say precisely what is written"* — and not an indication that no rule
applies. A student who learns to read black meems as "unmarked, therefore ignore" will
swallow every one of them before و.

The same reasoning covers [[Izhar-Halqi]] on the noon side.

## Common mistakes

- **Concealing the meem before و and ف** — the classic error, named in the matn itself.
- **Adding a 2-count ghunnah**, imported from the two meem rules that do take one.
- **Opening the lips before the meem is complete**, producing a clipped half-meem.
- **Denasalising** — over-correcting "no prolonged ghunnah" into "no nasal at all".
- Merging into a following **ن** (`لَهُمْ نَصِيبٌ`) — meem and noon are both nasals, but
  they are not identical letters and no idghām applies.
