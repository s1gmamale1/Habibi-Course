# Al-Jahr — الجهر

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *jahr* means to announce openly, to make plain, the opposite of
whispering.

**Technically**, jahr is the **stopping of the breath flow** with the letter, caused by
the strength of the tongue's reliance (*i'timad*) on the makhraj. The articulators press
firmly enough to block the airstream, so the sound carries on the voice rather than on
escaping breath.

## Its opposite

[[Hams]] — the breath flows because reliance on the makhraj is weak. The two divide the
29 letters exhaustively: 10 hams, 19 jahr.

## Letters

Nineteen letters — everything not in فَحَثَّهُ شَخْصٌ سَكَتْ. The classical mnemonic
gathers them positively:

> **عَظُمَ وَزْنُ قَارِئٍ ذِي غَضٍّ جَدَّ طَلَبْ** — *ʿazuma waznu qāriʾin dhī ghaddin jadda talab*

ء · ا · ب · ج · د · ذ · ر · ز · ض · ط · ظ · ع · غ · ق · ل · م · ن · و · ي

In practice most students learn the ten hams letters and take the rest by subtraction;
the mnemonic is worth memorising anyway because it is what the classical commentaries cite.

## What it sounds like

The teacher listens for **no breath escaping** — the letter rides on voice alone. Held
against the palm, ز, ذ, د, ط give no puff.

The diagnostic failures are all **devoicing**, where a jahr letter is replaced by its
hams neighbour at the same or a nearby makhraj:

| Should be (jahr) | Collapses to (hams) |
|---|---|
| ز | س |
| ذ | ث |
| د | ت |
| ط | ت |
| ض | ص |

This is the single most common consonant error for speakers whose first language
devoices final stops. Ask the student to place a hand on the throat: on a jahr letter
they should feel the vocal folds vibrating right through it.

## Why it matters

- **Qalqalah** is produced by jahr and [[Shiddah]] occurring together: the sound cannot
  flow (shiddah) and cannot be released silently (jahr), so it escapes as a bounce. See
  [[Qalqalah-sifah]].
- Devoicing a jahr letter usually changes the word, not just its texture — ظَلَمَ with a
  devoiced ظ becomes a different root entirely. This class of error is *lahn jali*.
- ا, و, ي are counted as jahr in their consonantal reading; as madd letters they carry
  no reliance at all, which is part of why some scholars group them under khafa'
  (see [[Hams]] for that disagreement).
