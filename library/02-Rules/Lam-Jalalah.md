# Lām Lafẓ al-Jalālah — لَامُ لَفْظِ الْجَلَالَة

## Definition

The ل in the word **ٱللَّه** is the **only lām in the language that alternates
between heavy and light**. Every other lām in the Qur'an — in صَلَاة, in طَلَبَ, in
ٱلْحَمْدُ, anywhere at all — is **always light**, without exception.

Together with [[Ra]], this is one of only two letters whose heaviness is
conditional. It is by far the simpler of the two: one condition, no exception
list, no waṣl/waqf split.

Requires [[Tafkhim]] and [[Tarqiq]] — the learner must already be able to produce
both qualities before this rule means anything.

## Trigger condition

Look at the vowel on the letter **immediately before** the word ٱللَّه:

| Preceding vowel | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **fatḥa** — قَالَ ٱللَّهُ | **Heavy** (tafkhīm) |
| **ḍamma** — نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ | **Heavy** |
| **kasra** — بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ | **Light** (tarqīq) |
| **nothing — you begin on the word** | **Heavy** |

The kasra makes it light whether it is **original** (دِينِ ٱللَّهِ), part of a
**preposition** fused into the word (لِلَّهِ, بِٱللَّهِ), or produced by a
**connecting vowel** (قُلِ ٱللَّهُمَّ). Unlike the rā' rules, no distinction is made
between original and temporary kasras here.

Ibn al-Jazarī states it in one line:

> وَفَخِّمِ اللَّامَ مِنِ اسْمِ اللَّهِ ⁕ عَنْ فَتْحٍ أَوْ ضَمٍّ كَعَبْدِ اللَّهِ
>
> *Make heavy the lām of the name of Allāh — after a fatḥa or a ḍamma, as in
> ʿabdu-llāh.*

## How to perform it

**Heavy:** the tongue tip stays exactly where a lām is made — against the gum
ridge behind the upper teeth — while the **back** of the tongue rises toward the
soft palate. The vowel darkens: *Allōh*, with the vowel of English *law*. The
shaddah is held for its full measure, heavy throughout.

**Light:** the ordinary clear Arabic lām, tongue flat at the back. The vowel is
forward: *Allah*, with the vowel of English *cat*. Nothing rises.

Drill the pair back to back until the difference is unmistakable:

> **بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ** (light) → **نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ** (heavy) → **دِينِ ٱللَّهِ** (light)

| 110:2 | دِينِ ٱللَّهِ | kasra on دِينِ | **Light** |

بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ is the highest-value drill item in this entire family: it is the
first phrase of the Qur'an, it is said dozens of times a day, and it is read
heavy by most untrained readers.

## Common mistakes

- **Heavying it after a kasra**, above all in **بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ**.
  If a learner fixes one thing in this lesson, fix this.
- **A uniformly heavy lām** — usually carried over from a language where the
  Divine Name is always pronounced *Allōh*.
- **A uniformly light lām**, which flattens قَالَ ٱللَّهُ.
- **Using an English dark "l" everywhere else.** The velarised /ɫ/ of English
  *feel* is close to the heavy lām, and English speakers install it in every لام.
  Outside ٱللَّه, the lām is always the clear /l/ of *leaf*.
- **Looking at the wrong letter.** The rule reads the vowel on the letter *before*
  the word, not the vowel on the lām itself.
- **Applying it to ٱللَّهُمَّ but forgetting the connecting kasra** — قُلِ ٱللَّهُمَّ
  is light.

## Contrast with

- **[[Lam-Shamsiyyah]]** and **[[Lam-Qamariyyah]]** — the other two lām rules, and
  both are about the definite article, not about heaviness. Their lām is always
  light when it is pronounced at all. This is the standard distractor set: given a
  lām, is the question *heavy or light*, *pronounced or merged*?
- **[[Ra-Tafkhim]]** / **[[Ra-Tarqiq]]** — the same heavy/light axis, with eight
  conditions instead of one. Teach lām jalālah first; it is the same idea at a
  scale a beginner can hold.
- **[[Tafkhim]]** — the seven letters that are always heavy. Lām is not one of
  them, and this word is its only exception.

## Scholars differ

The rule itself is not contested. There is a minor descriptive difference over how
heavy the heavy lām should be: some place it at the same level as a ط or ص, others
describe it as a lighter grade of tafkhīm sitting between the light lām and the
isti'lā' letters. This bears on polish rather than on correctness; see the note on
the "medium band" in [[Tafkhim]].

## Colour and dataset coverage

**This rule has no `cpfair_key` and therefore no colour in Family B.** The 18
rules in [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]] include `lam_shamsiyyah` but nothing for the lām
of the Divine Name — the dataset has no concept of heaviness at all. This is a
**known gap**, and a conspicuous one: the single most common tajweed error in the
most frequently recited phrase in the Qur'an cannot be highlighted from the
corpus. It needs hand-authored drill data.
