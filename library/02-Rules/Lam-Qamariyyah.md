# Al-Lām al-Qamariyyah — اللَّامُ الْقَمَرِيَّة

## Definition

When the definite article **ٱلـ** is followed by one of the other fourteen
letters, the **ل is pronounced clearly**, with a sukūn, and nothing merges. Its
technical name is **إِظْهَارٌ قَمَرِيّ** — lunar clarity.

"Lunar" is again just a mnemonic: **ٱلْقَمَر** (*the moon*) begins with one of these
letters and shows the clear ل, exactly as **ٱلشَّمْس** shows the merged one. The
pair ٱلْقَمَر / ٱلشَّمْس is the whole lesson in two words.

The complement is [[Lam-Shamsiyyah]].

## Trigger condition

The definite article **ٱلـ** immediately followed by one of the **14 moon
letters**:

> **ا · ب · غ · ح · ج · ك · و · خ · ف · ع · ق · ي · م · هـ**

The mnemonic is a single short phrase whose fourteen letters *are* the list:

> **ابْغِ حَجَّكَ وَخَفْ عَقِيمَهُ**
> *"Seek your ḥajj, and fear its barrenness."*

Because it is one phrase rather than fourteen words, this is much easier to
memorise than the solar list — so most teachers have learners memorise **this**
one and treat everything else as solar by elimination.

**The muṣḥaf tells you outright:**

| Marking | Rule |
|---|---|
| The ل carries a **sukūn** ( ـْ ) | Qamariyyah — pronounce it |
| No sukūn on the ل, **shaddah on the next letter** | Shamsiyyah — merge it |

## How to perform it

1. Say the **hamzat waṣl** if you are starting on the word; it drops when you join
   from the previous word.
2. **Sound the ل fully** — tongue tip on the gum ridge behind the upper teeth,
   clear and **light** (see [[Tarqiq]]; every lām outside the Divine Name is
   light).
3. **Release it cleanly into the next letter.** No vowel after it, no pause, and
   **no bounce** — the ل is sākin, but it is not a qalqalah letter.

> ٱلْحَمْدُ → *al-ḥamd*
> ٱلْقَمَر → *al-qamar*

Āyah 89:3 — **وَٱلشَّفْعِ وَٱلْوَتْرِ** — is the ideal drill: one solar and one lunar
lām, side by side, in five syllables.

## Common mistakes

- **Merging it** — *aq-qāri'ah* for ٱلْقَارِعَةُ, usually by over-applying the solar
  rule once it has been learned.
- **Inserting a vowel after the ل** — *ala-ḥamd*, *ala-falaq*.
- **Bouncing the sākin ل** as if it were a qalqalah letter. Only ق ط ب ج د bounce
  — see [[Qalqalah-Sughra]].
- **A dark English "l".** ٱلْحَمْدُ takes the clear /l/ of *leaf*, not the velarised
  /ɫ/ of *feel*.
- **Heavying the ل before a heavy letter** — ٱلْقَارِعَةُ, ٱلطَّامَّة. The ق and ط are
  heavy; the ل before them is not.
- **Dropping the hamzat waṣl when starting** on the word.

## Contrast with

- **[[Lam-Shamsiyyah]]** — the exact complement. Same article, opposite outcome,
  and the muṣḥaf's sukūn-vs-shaddah marking decides it every time. Always taught
  and quizzed as a pair; the classic quiz item is ٱلْقَمَر vs ٱلشَّمْس.
- **[[Lam-Jalalah]]** — a different question about a different lām: *heavy or
  light*, not *clear or merged*. The lām here is always light.
- **[[Qalqalah-Sughra]]** — the other rule about a sākin letter mid-word, and the
  contrast that stops learners bouncing the ل: a sākin ل is released cleanly,
  a sākin د is released with an echo.

## Scholars differ

Nothing substantive. Some works count the alif among the fourteen moon letters (as
here, following the ابْغِ حَجَّكَ mnemonic), others treat the article's own hamzah
separately and give thirteen — a bookkeeping difference with no effect on
recitation.

## Colour and dataset coverage

**This rule has no `cpfair_key` and therefore no colour in Family B.** The dataset
annotates `lam_shamsiyyah` — the merged case — but has no key for the lunar lām,
because there is nothing special to mark: the ل is simply read as written. This is
a **known gap** in the strict sense that the two rules cannot be highlighted
symmetrically, but a benign one. In practice the app can present the lunar lām as
"unmarked" and let the grey [[Lam-Shamsiyyah]] highlight carry the contrast.
