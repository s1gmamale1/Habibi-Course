# Al-Lām ash-Shamsiyyah — اللَّامُ الشَّمْسِيَّة

## Definition

When the definite article **ٱلـ** is followed by one of fourteen letters, the **ل
is not pronounced at all**. It merges completely into the following letter, which
doubles and carries a **shaddah**. Its technical name is **إِدْغَامٌ شَمْسِيّ** —
solar assimilation.

"Solar" is not a description of the letters; it is a mnemonic. The word for *sun*,
**ٱلشَّمْس**, happens to begin with one, and demonstrates the rule while naming it.
Its partner is **ٱلْقَمَر** (*the moon*) — see [[Lam-Qamariyyah]].

## Trigger condition

The definite article **ٱلـ** immediately followed by one of the **14 sun letters**:

> **ط · ث · ص · ر · ت · ض · ذ · ن · د · س · ظ · ز · ش · ل**

The mnemonic from Tuḥfat al-Aṭfāl encodes them as the first letter of each of
fourteen words:

> طِبْ ثُمَّ صِلْ رُحْمًا تَفُزْ ضِفْ ذَا نِعَمْ ⁕
> دَعْ سُوءَ ظَنٍّ زُرْ شَرِيفًا لِلْكَرَمْ

Faster still: the sun letters are the ones made **at or near the tongue** — which
is where the ل is made too. Assimilation happens because the two articulations are
already neighbours.

**The muṣḥaf tells you outright.** You never have to recall the list while
reading:

| Marking | Rule |
|---|---|
| The ل carries **no sukūn**, and the **next letter carries a shaddah** | Shamsiyyah — merge |
| The ل carries a **sukūn** ( ـْ ) | Qamariyyah — pronounce it |

## How to perform it

1. Say the **hamzat waṣl** — the *a* of *al* — if you are starting on the word.
   If you are joining from the previous word, it drops.
2. **Skip the ل entirely.** Do not touch the gum ridge; do not let a trace of an
   *l* sound out.
3. Go straight to the following letter and hold it **doubled**, for the full
   measure of a shaddah.

> ٱلشَّمْس → *ash-shams*, never *al-shams*
> ٱلرَّحْمَٰن → *ar-raḥmān*, never *al-raḥmān*

The assimilation is **idghām kāmil bilā ghunnah** — complete, with no nasal hum —
in thirteen of the fourteen cases. The exception is **ن**: ٱلنَّاسِ carries a
ghunnah, but that ghunnah belongs to the doubled ن itself, not to any surviving
trace of the ل.

Sūrat al-Fātiḥah alone contains four of these, which makes it the natural first
drill — and Sūrat Quraysh 106:2 puts two in a single breath.

## Common mistakes

- **Pronouncing the ل** — *al-shams*, *al-raḥmān*. The defining error, and the one
  that identifies a reader as untrained within three words.
- **Not holding the shaddah**, so ٱلرَّحْمَٰن comes out with a single ر. The merged
  ل has to be *heard*, as extra length on the following letter.
- **Inserting a vowel or a pause** between the article and the letter.
- **Reading the ل before ل as a stutter** — ٱلَّذِينَ is one held ل, not two.
- **Adding a ghunnah to the wrong sun letters.** Only ن hums.
- **Making ٱلرَّحْمَٰن's ر light.** The merge and the heaviness are independent
  rules; that ر is mushaddad with a fatḥa, so it is heavy — see [[Ra-Tafkhim]].

## Contrast with

- **[[Lam-Qamariyyah]]** — the exact complement, same article, opposite outcome.
  These two are always taught and quizzed together; the muṣḥaf's sukūn/shaddah
  marking is the discriminator.
- **[[Lam-Jalalah]]** — a completely different axis. That rule asks *heavy or
  light*; this one asks *pronounced or merged*. Learners conflate them because
  both are called "a lām rule".
- **[[Tarqiq]]** — the surviving lām sound in this family is always light; only the
  Divine Name's lām is ever heavy.

## Scholars differ

Nothing substantive. Works vary only in whether they classify solar assimilation
as a species of idghām mutamāthilayn (for ل into ل) or list it as its own category,
and in whether they present the fourteen letters via the Tuḥfah verse or via a
plain list. The recitation is identical.

## Colour

**Family B (the course default, matching the Quranly app): `#AAAAAA` — grey**, the
same grey used for silent letters. That is deliberate and pedagogically apt: the
solar lām **is** a silent letter. Grey says "written but not sounded", which is
exactly the instruction.
