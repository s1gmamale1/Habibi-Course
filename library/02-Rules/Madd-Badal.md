# Madd al-Badal — مد البدل

## Definition

A madd letter **preceded** by a hamzah, with no hamzah or sukūn after it.

"Badal" means *substitute*, because historically the madd letter replaced a
second hamzah: أَأْمَنَ became آمَنَ, أُأْتُوا became أُوتُوا. The madd is the
substitute for the hamzah that was dropped.

Note the order carefully — this is the mirror image of [[Madd-Muttasil]]:

| Pattern | Rule | Counts |
|---|---|---|
| **ء** then madd letter | badal | 2 |
| madd letter then **ء** (same word) | [[Madd-Muttasil]] | 4–5 |
| madd letter then **ء** (next word) | [[Madd-Munfasil]] | 4–5 |

## Length

**2 ḥarakāt in Ḥafṣ** — that is, badal is recited exactly like
[[Madd-Tabii]]. Ḥafṣ gives it nothing extra.

> **⚠ The flagship riwāyah difference.** In the riwāyah of **Warsh** ʿan Nāfiʿ,
> badal may be given **2, 4 or 6** counts. It is the single most audible marker
> distinguishing a Warsh recitation from a Ḥafṣ one, and learners who have heard
> North or West African recitation will have this length in their ear. Name it,
> then set it aside: **this course teaches Ḥafṣ, so badal is 2.**

## How to spot it

The alif-maddah `آ` at the start of a word (ءَا in the Tanzīl orthography) is
almost always badal: آمَنَ · آدَمَ · آتَىٰ · الْقُرْآن. A hamzah followed by a
small wāw or yāʾ madd is the same rule with a different madd letter.

## Common mistakes

1. **Applying Warsh's lengthening while reciting Ḥafṣ** — the most common
   confusion, especially among learners who switch teachers.
2. Failing to realise the hamzah fully before the madd, so ءَامَنُوا opens as a
   soft "aa" with no glottal onset.
3. Treating word-initial آ as if it were a 4-count madd because it carries a
   maddah-like sign.
