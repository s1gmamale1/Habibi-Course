# Madd al-Farq — مد الفرق

## Definition

When the **interrogative [[Hamza|hamzah]]** (همزة الاستفهام) enters upon a word beginning
with a **hamzat al-waṣl** — in practice, the definite article — the two hamzahs
would collide. The second is converted into a **6-count [[Alif|alif]]** to keep the
question audibly distinct from the statement.

*Farq* means **distinction**. That is the entire purpose:

| Read as | Means |
|---|---|
| ءَآللَّهُ (6 counts) | "**Is it** Allāh…?" |
| ٱللَّهُ (no madd) | "Allāh…" |

Drop the madd and a question becomes an assertion. This is a rule about
**meaning**, not decoration.

## All six places in the Qurʾān — three words, six occurrences

| Word | References |
|---|---|
| **ءَآلذَّكَرَيْنِ** | al-Anʿām **6:143**, **6:144** |
| **ءَآللَّهُ** | Yūnus **10:59**, an-Naml **27:59** |
| **ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ** | Yūnus **10:51**, **10:91** |

All six verified verbatim against the pinned [[Tanzil]] corpus. That is the
complete list — the rule is finite and memorisable in one sitting.

## Length

**6 ḥarakāt.**

> **The 6 is transmitted after all.** *(Upgraded 2026-08-11.)*
> [[Shatibiyyah]] v. 192 gives the condition and the instruction —
> *"if a hamzah of connection lies between a sākin lām and the interrogative hamzah,
> then **lengthen it, substituting**"* — but **no count**, so this note previously
> recorded the 6 as *derived*: substitute the hamzah, and the resulting alif in
> ءَآللَّهُ stands before a lām carrying shaddah, a permanent sukūn, hence
> [[Madd-Lazim]] at six counts by [[Tuhfat-al-Atfal]] v. 47.
>
> **[[Nihayat-al-Qawl-al-Mufid]] states the classification outright**, printed page
> ١٩٣, item 11 of its madd taxonomy: madd al-farq *"is one of the divisions of **madd
> lāzim kalimī**, muthaqqal or mukhaffaf."* Lāzim kalimī is six by definition in both
> vendored matns, so the count is carried by the category rather than reconstructed.
> The derivation above is kept because it is a good check — it and the transmitted
> classification agree — but the rule no longer rests on it.

**Or tas-hīl.** Ḥafṣ also permits *tas-hīl* of the second hamzah — pronouncing it
between a hamzah and an alif, softened rather than lengthened. Both are
transmitted; 6 counts is the reading almost every learner will hear, so teach
that and mention tas-hīl so the alternative is recognised.

## The double classification of ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ

The two occurrences of ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ are **simultaneously** madd al-farq (by cause:
interrogative hamzah on a waṣl hamzah) and **madd lāzim kalimī mukhaffaf** (by
form: a madd letter followed by an original sukūn in the same word). See
[[Madd-Lazim]]. Both labels are correct; the recitation — 6 counts — is
identical. Present the two names together rather than letting the learner
discover the clash later and assume one of their teachers was wrong.

## Prerequisite

This rule cannot be understood without [[Hamzat-Wasl]]: the whole mechanism is a
question-hamzah landing on a connecting hamzah that would otherwise be silent.
Teach the two together.

## Common mistakes

1. Reading ءَآللَّهُ as a plain definite article — the meaning-changing error.
2. Giving 4 counts instead of 6.
3. Not recognising ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ because it is spelt unlike anything else.
