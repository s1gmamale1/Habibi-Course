# Madd al-Līn — مد اللين

Full name: **مد اللين العارض للسكون**, *al-Līn al-ʿĀriḍ li's-Sukūn*.

## The līn letters are not madd letters

**و** and **ي** carrying a **sukūn** and preceded by a **fatḥah** are *[[Leen|līn]]*
(soft) letters, not madd letters:

| | Preceded by | Example |
|---|---|---|
| madd wāw | **ḍammah** | يَقُولُ |
| **līn wāw** | **fatḥah** | خَوْف |
| madd yāʾ | **kasrah** | قِيلَ |
| **līn yāʾ** | **fatḥah** | بَيْت |

A līn letter has **no madd of its own**. In wasl, خَوْفٍ and قُرَيْشٍ carry no
prolongation whatsoever.

## Condition

`fatḥah + وْ/يْ + one letter + waqf`. Only when you **[[Waqf-Types|stop]]** does the līn letter
gain length — by exactly the mechanism of [[Madd-Arid-Lissukun]], applied to a
soft letter instead of a true madd letter.

## Length

**2, 4 or 6 ḥarakāt** — the same three options as ʿāriḍ, held consistently.

Some teachers restrict līn to 2 or 4; others to 4 or 6. The jumhūr permit all
three, and that is what this course teaches. Practically: pick the same count you
picked for ʿāriḍ, so a single decision governs both.

## The ʿayn of the muqaṭṭaʿāt — līn with a *permanent* sukūn

In **كٓهيعٓصٓ** (Maryam 19:1) and **حمٓ عٓسٓقٓ** (ash-Shūrā), the letter **ع** is
spelt `ʿayn-yāʾ-nūn`: a līn yāʾ followed by a sukūn that never goes away.

Because the sukūn is permanent, this is a **lāzim** līn madd — **4 or 6 counts,
never 2** — even though its neighbours in نَقَصَ عَسَلُكُمْ take a flat 6. Both
4 and 6 are transmitted for Ḥafṣ; state both, pick one. See [[Madd-Lazim]] for
the full three-tier muqaṭṭaʿāt rule.

## Teaching route

Sūrat Quraysh is the perfect drill: قُرَيْشٍ · ٱلشِّتَآءِ · وَٱلصَّيْفِ ·
ٱلْبَيْتِ · خَوْفٍ — four līn stops and one muttaṣil in four short āyāt. Read it
stopping at every āyah end, then read it joined, so the learner hears the madd
appear and disappear.

## Common mistakes

1. Applying it in wasl — there is no madd at all when joining.
2. Treating līn wāw/yāʾ as full madd letters and giving them 2 counts everywhere.
3. Giving the ع of كٓهيعٓصٓ only 2 counts.
4. Switching between 2/4/6 within a passage.
