# Madd Munfaṣil — المد الجائز المنفصل

## Definition

A madd letter at the **end of one word** followed by a [[Hamza|hamzah]] at the **start of
the next**. The two are "separated" (munfaṣil) by a word boundary — which is
exactly why the ruling is **jāʾiz**: the reciters differ, so more than one
length is genuinely correct.

## Condition

`madd letter (word-final) + ء (word-initial)` — **and only when joining**.
If you stop on the first word, the cause disappears and the madd reverts to a
plain 2-count [[Madd-Tabii]].

## Length — and the disagreement you must name

| Ṭarīq | Length | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **ash-Shāṭibiyyah** | **4 or 5 ḥarakāt** | what this course teaches |
| aṭ-Ṭayyibah ("Ḥafṣ bi-qaṣr al-munfaṣil") | **2 ḥarakāt** | equally valid Ḥafṣ |

> **Teach 4–5. Name the 2-count option out loud.** A learner who has only ever
> heard 4–5 will assume a reciter using qaṣr al-munfaṣil is making a mistake.
> They are not — they are reading a different ṭarīq. Many widely-heard reciters
> use the 2-count option, so the learner *will* meet it.

**Consistency rule:** your munfaṣil count must be **less than or equal to** your
[[Madd-Muttasil]] count, and must not change within a session.

## Why learners miss it

The words are printed as two separate words, so the eye reads a boundary and the
tongue stops lengthening. The maddah `~` over the final madd letter is the cue —
train the eye to look one word ahead.

## Common mistakes

1. Mixing 2-count and 4-count munfaṣil in the same session.
2. Applying it at [[Waqf-Types|waqf]] — stop on بِمَآ and it is simply 2 counts.
3. Reading munfaṣil *longer* than muttaṣil.
4. Missing it in يَٰٓأَيُّهَا, which looks like one word but is two.

## Related

[[Madd-Silah]] describes *madd ṣilah kubrā*, which is a munfaṣil created by the
implied wāw/yāʾ of the pronoun hāʾ — it takes the same 4–5 count.
