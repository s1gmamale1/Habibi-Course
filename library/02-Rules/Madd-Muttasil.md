# Madd Muttaṣil — المد الواجب المتصل

## Definition

A **madd letter followed by a hamzah inside the same word**. Because the cause
and the madd letter live in one word, they can never be separated — hence
"connected" (muttaṣil), and hence **wājib**: every single qāriʾ lengthens it
beyond 2 counts. Nobody shortens it.

## Condition

`madd letter + ء`, with **no word boundary** between them.

## Length

**4 or 5 ḥarakāt** via ṭarīq ash-Shāṭibiyyah — 4 in ḥadr and tadwīr, 5 in
tartīl. **This course pins 4–5**, matching the Ḥuṣarī recitation used
throughout.

Whichever you choose, hold it constant for the whole session, and keep it
**at or above** your [[Madd-Munfasil]] count.

**At waqf on the hamzah itself** (e.g. stopping on ٱلسَّمَآءِ) a sixth count is
also permitted — the sukūn of stopping stacks onto the hamzah. This is sometimes
named *madd muttaṣil ʿāriḍ*; see [[Madd-Arid-Lissukun]] for the same mechanism.

## How to spot it in the mushaf

The **maddah** sign `~` printed over the madd letter (آ / ـَٰٓ / ـُوٓ / ـِيٓ)
marks every madd farʿī. If the hamzah that caused it is in the same word, it is
muttaṣil; if the hamzah begins the *next* word, it is munfaṣil.

## Common mistakes

1. Giving only 2 counts — this is a *lahn* by consensus, since no reading
   shortens muttaṣil.
2. Being inconsistent: 4 in one āyah, 5 in the next.
3. Swallowing the hamzah after the long madd, so جَآءَ comes out as "jaa".
4. Letting the madd sag in pitch or volume before the hamzah lands.
