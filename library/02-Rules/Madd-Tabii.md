# Madd Ṭabīʿī — المد الطبيعي

Also called **al-Madd al-Aṣlī** (المد الأصلي, "the original madd"). This is the
madd on which the identity of a madd letter depends: no madd letter is complete
without it, and it needs no external cause.

## The three madd letters

| Letter | Condition |
|---|---|
| ا | always sākinah, always preceded by a fatḥah |
| و | sākinah, preceded by a ḍammah |
| ي | sākinah, preceded by a kasrah |

Mnemonic: they are gathered in **وَاي**, or in the single word **نُوحِيهَا**
(Hūd 11:49), which contains all three.

## Condition

A madd letter **not** followed by a hamzah and **not** followed by a sukūn.
The moment either of those appears, the madd becomes a *madd farʿī* — see
[[Madd-Muttasil]], [[Madd-Munfasil]], [[Madd-Arid-Lissukun]], [[Madd-Lazim]].

## Length

**Exactly 2 ḥarakāt.** Neither more nor less. This is the baseline against which
every other madd is measured.

### What a ḥarakah actually is

A ḥarakah (حَرَكَة) is the time taken to pronounce one vowelled letter at a
moderate pace.

> **Scholars differ on how to define it.** Three positions are current: (a) the
> time to fold or extend one finger; (b) a measured ~0.5–1 second; (c) purely
> relative to the reciter's own tempo, with no absolute value.
>
> **This course teaches the finger convention as a learning aid only.** The
> relative definition is the true one: a ḥarakah is a proportion of your own
> recitation speed, so the same reciter's 2 counts are shorter in ḥadr than in
> tartīl. What must never change *within* a session is the *ratio* between 2,
> 4 and 6.

## Madd ṭabīʿī ḥarfī

In the disconnected letters (الحروف المقطعة) at the head of certain sūrahs,
a letter whose spelt name is **two letters long**, the second being a madd
letter with no sukūn after it, gets a plain 2-count madd.

**The five letters: ح ي ط ه ر — mnemonic حَيٌّ طَهُرْ.**

- طه (20:1) — "Ṭā-Hā"
- يسٓ (36:1) — the yāʾ is 2 counts, the sīn is 6
- الٓر (10:1) — "Alif Lām Rā", the rā is 2 counts
- حمٓ (40:1) — the ḥāʾ is 2 counts, the mīm is 6

**ا (alif) has no madd at all** — its name is spelt with three letters but ends
in a non-madd letter.

## Common mistakes

1. **Shortening to 1 count.** The single most common error in the whole of
   tajwīd. It changes قَالَ ("he said") into قَلَّ ("it became little").
2. **Over-correcting to 4 counts.** A natural madd that is stretched is no
   longer natural.
3. Ignoring the dagger alif in ذَٰلِكَ, ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ, هَٰذَا — it is a full
   2-count madd even though it is not written on the line.
4. Giving ح or ط six counts in the muqaṭṭaʿāt; giving alif a madd.
