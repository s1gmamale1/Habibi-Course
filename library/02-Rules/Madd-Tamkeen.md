# Madd at-Tamkīn — مد التمكين

## Definition

A natural madd given **extra care so it is not swallowed**, occurring when a madd
letter meets a letter of its own kind. *Tamkīn* means establishing, making firm:
the madd is "established" so the two identical letters stay audible as two.

## The two forms

**Form 1 — two [[Ya|yāʾs]] inside a word.** The first carries **shaddah + kasrah**, the
second is **sākinah** (a madd yāʾ):

حُيِّيتُم · ٱلنَّبِيِّۦنَ · عِلِّيِّينَ · نُبَيِّئُ

**Form 2 — a madd letter meeting the same letter across a word boundary.** A [[Waw|wāw]]
madd followed by a wāw, or a yāʾ madd followed by a yāʾ:

ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ · ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ

## Length

**2 ḥarakāt.** Tamkīn is not longer than [[Madd-Tabii]] — it is the *same*
length, executed with more deliberation. Nothing is added except clarity.

## Is it really a separate rule?

> **Classificatory disagreement, flagged.** Some scholars list tamkīn as a
> distinct madd; others say it is simply madd ṭabīʿī that requires care, and
> decline to name it at all. Nothing in the recitation changes either way.
>
> **This course teaches it as a named case** — because giving the phenomenon a
> name is precisely what stops the learner from collapsing the two yāʾs, which is
> the whole point of the rule. But say plainly that the disagreement is about
> labelling, not sound.

> ### ⚠ Status: needs-review — and the search above settles which side is which
>
> **No matn in this vault names it.** *tamkīn* occurs zero times in
> [[Tuhfat-al-Atfal]], [[Muqaddimah-Jazariyyah]] and [[Shatibiyyah]] alike, and unlike
> [[Madd-Iwad]] there is no indirect attestation either — nothing in the three texts
> presupposes it.
>
> That is evidence *for* the second position recorded above: the scholars who decline
> to name tamkīn are the ones the classical matns agree with. **It remains a useful
> teaching label and the recitation is not in question** — a madd ṭabīʿī given care so
> two identical letters stay audible — but it should be presented as pedagogy rather
> than as transmitted doctrine. Verifying it needs a tajwīd manual that states it as a
> rule. *(Found 2026-08-11.)*

## Common mistakes

1. Merging the two yāʾs into one long one, so حُيِّيتُم becomes `ḥuyītum`.
2. Dropping the madd altogether under speed.
3. Inserting a pause between the two letters to keep them separate — the fix is
   a clean 2-count madd, not a gap.
