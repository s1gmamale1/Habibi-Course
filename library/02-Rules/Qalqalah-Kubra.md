# Al-Qalqalah al-Kubrā — الْقَلْقَلَةُ الْكُبْرَى

## Definition

The stronger degree of the bounce: a qalqalah letter at the **end of a word that
you stop on**. The sukūn here is **'āriḍ** — accidental, produced by the waqf
itself rather than written into the word. Because nothing follows to carry the
sound, the release must be clearly audible or the letter disappears entirely.

The five letters are the same as in [[Qalqalah-Sughra]]: **ق ط ب ج د**, mnemonic
**قُطْبُ جَدٍّ**.

## Trigger condition

You stop (waqf) on a word whose **final letter** is one of the five.

This includes the case where the letter carries a vowel or tanwīn in waṣl — at
waqf that ending drops and the letter becomes sākin:

| In waṣl | At waqf | Result |
|---|---|---|
| أَحَدٌ | أَحَدْ | Bounce the د |
| ٱلْفَلَقِ | ٱلْفَلَقْ | Bounce the ق |
| وَقَبَ | وَقَبْ | Bounce the ب |
| يُولَدْ | يُولَدْ | Already sākin; bounce it |

**Kubrā is therefore a decision, not a property of the word.** The same word is
ṣughrā if you read on and kubrā if you stop. لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ contains both:
the د of يَلِدْ is ṣughrā (you continue), the د of يُولَدْ is kubrā (you stop).

## How to perform it

1. Close the makhraj fully and hold it a fraction longer than for ṣughrā.
2. **Release with a clear, firm bounce** — noticeably stronger than the mid-word
   version, because it is the last thing the listener hears.
3. Still **no vowel colour**, and still no added duration. Stronger, not longer,
   and not vowelled.
4. If the letter carries a **shaddah**, hold the doubled consonant for its full
   measure first, *then* release the bounce once at the end — وَتَبّْ, not
   وَتَبْ‑بْ as two separate bounces.

Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ is the ideal first drill: every one of its four āyahs ends on a
qalqalah letter or on a stop that produces one.

## Common mistakes

- **Stopping with no bounce at all**, so the final letter is simply inaudible —
  *aḥa* instead of *aḥad*. This is the defining kubrā error.
- **Adding a full vowel** on the way out: *aḥada*, *al-falaqa*.
- **Lengthening** the bounce into a hummed extra beat.
- **Two bounces on a shaddah** — وَتَبّْ takes one release after the held ب, not
  one per half.
- **Bouncing a non-qalqalah final letter** by analogy — stopping on ٱلنَّاسْ or
  نَسْتَعِينْ with a bounced س or ن. Only the five letters bounce.

## Contrast with

- **[[Qalqalah-Sughra]]** — same letters, permanent sukūn, weaker bounce, and it
  does not depend on where you choose to stop. The pair is the standard quiz
  distractor set: given a word, is the sukūn lāzim or 'āriḍ?
- **[[Ra-Tafkhim]]** and **[[Ra-Tarqiq]]** — the other family whose ruling changes
  between waṣl and waqf. Worth teaching alongside, because the underlying idea is
  identical: *stopping changes the word*.
- **[[Tafkhim]]** — ق and ط keep their heaviness through the bounce; the two
  qualities stack.

## Scholars differ

**Two degrees or three.** Some scholars add a third, strongest degree —
**qalqalah akbar** (الْقَلْقَلَةُ الْأَكْبَر) — for stopping on a qalqalah letter that
carries a **shaddah**: وَتَبّْ (111:1), بِٱلْحَقّْ (103:3), ٱلْحَقّْ. Others fold this
straight into kubrā and hold that there are only **two** degrees.

**This course teaches two degrees**, with akbar named and demonstrated as a note
rather than as a category to be examined on. The practical instruction is the same
either way: hold the shaddah, then release one firm bounce.

**Vowel colour.** As in [[Qalqalah-Sughra]], the jumhūr hold the bounce carries no
vowel colour; a minority hold it inclines toward the preceding vowel. This course
teaches the jumhūr position.
