# Al-Qalqalah aṣ-Ṣughrā — الْقَلْقَلَةُ الصُّغْرَى

## Definition

Qalqalah is a slight **bounce** or echo released at the point of articulation when
one of five letters carries a sukūn. Because those five are both *shadīdah*
(the sound is completely blocked) and *majhūrah* (the vocal folds are engaged),
stopping on them with no vowel would make them inaudible — the bounce is what
lets the ear hear the letter at all.

**Ṣughrā** ("minor") is the weaker of the two degrees: the bounce on a sukūn that
is **lāzim** — original, permanent, part of how the word is written — and not at a
place where you stop.

## Trigger condition

Both conditions must hold:

1. The letter is one of the **five qalqalah letters**:

   > **ق · ط · ب · ج · د**
   >
   > Mnemonic: **قُطْبُ جَدٍّ** — *"the pole of a grandfather"*
   > (also given as قَطْبُ جَدٍّ; the letters, not the phrase, are the point)

2. It carries an **original (lāzim) sukūn** — either in the middle of a word, or
   at the end of a word that you continue past in waṣl.

If instead the sukūn appears only because you **stopped** on the word, the rule is
[[Qalqalah-Kubra]].

| Situation | Degree |
|---|---|
| Sukūn written mid-word — يَجْعَلْ | **Ṣughrā** |
| Word-final sukūn, read on through — لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ… | **Ṣughrā** |
| Word-final, and you stop there — …وَلَمْ يُولَدْ | Kubrā |

## How to perform it

1. Close the makhraj completely, as the letter requires — lips for ب, tongue tip
   at the gum for د and ط, mid-tongue for ج, deep tongue for ق.
2. **Release it sharply**, letting the trapped voice escape as a short click of
   sound. That released puff *is* the qalqalah.
3. Keep it **brief**. It is a release, not a syllable. It has no measurable
   duration in ḥarakāt.
4. **Add no vowel colour.** يَجْعَلْ is *yaj-'al*, never *yaja-'al*.

| 2:17 | يُبْصِرُونَ | ب | Sākin ب, light, still bounces |

| 97:5 | مَطْلَعِ | ط | Sākin ط mid-word |
| 112:3 | يَلِدْ | د | Word-final sukūn read in waṣl — ṣughrā, not kubrā |

Note that 105:2 and 112:3 sit in the two shortest surahs most learners already
have memorised, which makes them the right first drill.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding a vowel.** *yaqa-ṭa'ūn* instead of *yaq-ṭa'ūn* — by far the most
  common. The bounce is a release, not a ḥarakah.
- **Omitting it entirely**, so يَجْعَلْ has a dead, swallowed ج.
- **Over-bouncing** until the echo becomes an audible extra syllable.
- **Treating ṣughrā like kubrā** — giving a full, strong bounce mid-word. Ṣughrā
  is deliberately the *lighter* of the two.
- **Adding qalqalah to a mushaddad letter mid-word**, e.g. bouncing the first half
  of the shaddah in تَبَّتْ (111:1). The first half of a shaddah is sākin, but
  qalqalah is not applied to it in waṣl.

## Contrast with

- **[[Qalqalah-Kubra]]** — same five letters, but the sukūn is 'āriḍ (caused by
  stopping) and the bounce is stronger. The sibling that learners must be able to
  tell apart on sight.
- **[[Tafkhim]]** — ق and ط are both qalqalah letters *and* isti'lā' letters.
  These are two independent properties applying at once, not one rule. ب, ج and د
  bounce while staying light.
- **[[Lam-Qamariyyah]]** — another clearly-articulated sākin letter, and a useful
  contrast: a sākin ل is released cleanly but **does not** bounce.

## Scholars differ

**On the number of degrees.** Most divide qalqalah into **two** — ṣughrā and
kubrā. Others count **three**, adding *qalqalah akbar* (الْقَلْقَلَةُ الْأَكْبَر) for
stopping on a qalqalah letter that carries a shaddah. **This course teaches two
degrees** and names akbar as a note; see [[Qalqalah-Kubra]] for the detail.

**On vowel colour.** The majority (jumhūr) hold that the bounce carries **no vowel
colour at all** — it is neither fatḥa nor kasra nor ḍamma, inclining at most very
slightly toward fatḥa in every case. A minority, associated with some Egyptian
schools, hold that it should incline toward the vowel that **precedes** it, so the
bounce in يُبْصِرُونَ would carry a kasra tint. **This course teaches the jumhūr
position: no vowel colour.** It is also the easier position to teach, because it
makes the commonest error (adding a vowel) unambiguously an error.
