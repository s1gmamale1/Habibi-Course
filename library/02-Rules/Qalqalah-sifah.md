# Al-Qalqalah as a sifah — القلقلة

> This note describes qalqalah as a **characteristic of five letters**.
> The applied rules — where the bounce is small and where it is large — are separate
> notes: [[Qalqalah-Sughra]] and [[Qalqalah-Kubra]].

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *qalqalah* is disturbance, shaking, agitation.

**Technically**, qalqalah is a **disturbance or echo in the makhraj when the letter is
sakin**, heard as a small bounce at the moment of release.

## Why exactly these five letters

Qalqalah is not an extra property bolted onto five arbitrary letters. It is the
**necessary consequence of two other sifat occurring together**:

- **[[Shiddah]]** — the sound is completely blocked, so it cannot flow out.
- **[[Jahr]]** — the breath is stopped, so it cannot leak out either.

A sakin letter that is both blocked and voiced has nowhere to go. When the articulators
part, the trapped sound escapes as an audible bounce. That is qalqalah.

This also explains the exclusions. Of the eight [[Shiddah]] letters, ك and ت are
[[Hams]] letters — their breath escapes, so nothing is trapped and no bounce occurs.
Hamzah is shiddah and jahr but is excluded by transmission: it is released as a clean
glottal stop. What remains is five.

## Letters

Five letters:

> **قُطْبُ جَدٍّ** — *qutbu jaddin*

ق · ط · ب · ج · د

Two of them (ق and ط) are also [[Istila]] letters, so their bounce is **heavy**; ب, ج, د
are [[Istifal]], so their bounce is **light**. The bounce carries the weight of its letter.

## What it sounds like

The teacher listens for a **short, clean, vowel-less echo**. The bounce should be:

- **Audible** — a student with no qalqalah produces a dead stop, and the sakin letter
  disappears into silence.
- **Vowel-free** — the commonest error is a bounce that lands on a full vowel, so that
  وَٱلْفَجْرِ becomes "wal-faj**a**r". The jumhur position is that the bounce carries **no
  vowel colour**, inclining very slightly toward fathah; a minority hold that it inclines
  toward the preceding vowel. Teach the jumhur position.
- **Not doubled** — one bounce, not a stutter.

Diagnostic words: أَبْصَٰرِهِمْ (light ب mid-word), وَٱلْفَجْرِ (light ج mid-word),
ٱلْفَلَقِ stopped upon (heavy ق at the end), لَمْ يَلِدْ stopped upon (light د at the end).

## Why it matters

- Qalqalah is the most immediately audible marker of trained recitation, and its absence
  is what makes a beginner sound like they are reading rather than reciting.
- It is the clearest demonstration in the syllabus that sifat **compose**: a student who
  understands why shiddah + jahr produces a bounce does not need to memorise قُطْبُ جَدٍّ
  at all, and can derive it.
- The applied rules split by position — a sakin qalqalah letter in the middle of a word or
  ayah takes the smaller bounce, and one that is sakin because of stopping takes the
  larger. See [[Qalqalah-Sughra]] and [[Qalqalah-Kubra]].
