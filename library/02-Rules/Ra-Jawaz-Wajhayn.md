# Jawāz al-Wajhayn — جَوَازُ الْوَجْهَيْنِ

## Definition

A small set of words where **both** a heavy and a light [[Ra]] are transmitted for
Ḥafṣ, and either is correct. "Jawāz al-wajhayn" means literally *the permissibility
of the two faces*.

This note exists so that learners stop treating these as mistakes when they hear
them read the other way, and so that the quiz engine never asks "heavy or light?"
about them. There are only three cases.

## Trigger condition

### Case 1 — فِرْقٍ (26:63)

The ر is sākinah after an **original kasra**, and a ḥarf isti'lā' (ق) does follow
in the same word — which is exactly the shape of the five heavy exception words in
[[Ra-Tafkhim]]. But here the **ق itself carries a kasra**, and a kasra'd isti'lā'
letter has its own downward pull, so it does not drag the ر heavy with any force.

Both readings are transmitted. **Tarqīq is the more commonly preferred wajh.**

This is why the exception list in [[Ra-Tafkhim]] is exactly five words and not six:
فِرْقٍ has the same structure but a different answer.

### Case 2 — مِصْرَ and ٱلْقِطْرِ, at waqf only

In waṣl both are unambiguous: مِصْرَ has a fatḥa on the ر (heavy) and ٱلْقِطْرِ has a
kasra (light). The ambiguity appears **only when you stop**, because the ر then
becomes sākin and the letter before it is a sākin isti'lā' letter (ص, ط).

| Word | Refs | Preferred at waqf | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| مِصْرَ | 10:87 · 12:21 · 12:99 · 43:51 | **Tafkhīm** | The ر's original vowel was a fatḥa |
| ٱلْقِطْرِ | 34:12 | **Tarqīq** | The ر's original vowel was a kasra |

Both wajh are permitted for both words; the table records the convention, not a
ruling.

### Case 3 — words with a deleted yā', at waqf

Where the muṣḥaf's orthography has dropped a final yā', you may treat that yā' as
**present** — in which case the ر follows a yā' and is **light** — or as **absent**,
in which case it is heavy. **Tarqīq is generally preferred.**

| Word | Refs |
|---|---|
| يَسْرِ | 89:4 |
| فَأَسْرِ / أَنْ أَسْرِ | 11:81 · 15:65 · 44:23 |
| نُذُرِ | 54:16 · 54:18 · 54:21 · 54:30 · 54:37 · 54:39 |

## How to perform it

1. **Pick one wajh and hold it** for the whole recitation. Alternating between
   heavy and light for the same word within one sitting is not "both permitted" —
   it is inconsistency.
2. Where a preference is recorded above, teach that one as the default. Introduce
   the alternative as recognition only.
3. For cases 2 and 3, remember the ambiguity **only exists at waqf**. If you read
   on through مِصْرَ or يَسْرِ there is nothing to choose.

## Common mistakes

- **Correcting someone who chose the other wajh.** Both are transmitted. This is
  the mistake this note exists to prevent.
- **Generalising فِرْقٍ** into a rule that any kasra'd isti'lā' letter licenses both
  readings. It is this word.
- **Applying case 2 in waṣl.** مِصْرَ read with a light ر mid-sentence is simply
  wrong; the ر has a fatḥa there.
- **Switching wajh mid-recitation** for the same word.

## Contrast with

- **[[Ra-Tafkhim]]** — especially its five-word exception list, which فِرْقٍ
  resembles exactly and belongs to not at all.
- **[[Ra-Tarqiq]]** — condition 2, whose "no isti'lā' follows" qualifier is what
  فِرْقٍ sits on the boundary of.
- **[[Qalqalah-Kubra]]** — another rule that only materialises at waqf. Teaching
  the two together makes the waṣl/waqf distinction concrete.

## Scholars differ

This note **is** the disagreement, and it is a settled one: the difference is in
transmission, not in anyone being wrong. What varies between works is only which
wajh is named as *preferred* — the preferences recorded above (tarqīq for فِرْقٍ,
tafkhīm for مِصْرْ, tarqīq for ٱلْقِطْرْ, tarqīq for the deleted-yā' words) are the
mainstream conventions, not unanimous ones.

Some works also list further ja'iz cases beyond these three. This course teaches
the three above and treats anything further as advanced material.
