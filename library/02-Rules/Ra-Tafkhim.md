# Tafkhīm ar-Rāʾ — تَفْخِيمُ الرَّاء

## Definition

[[Ra]] is **the busiest letter in tajweed**. Together with the ل of the Divine
Name (see [[Lam-Jalalah]]) it is one of only two letters that switch between heavy
and light — but unlike that ل, which has one rule, the rā' has a dozen
conditions, differs between waṣl and waqf, and has a five-word exception list.

This note covers the conditions under which ر is **heavy**. Its counterpart is
[[Ra-Tarqiq]]; the three words where both readings are transmitted are in
[[Ra-Jawaz-Wajhayn]].

## Trigger condition

### The baseline, in one line

> **fatḥa or ḍamma → heavy · kasra → light.**
> **A sākin rā' takes its ruling from the vowel BEFORE it.**

Nearly everything else is a refinement of those two sentences. Learn them first.

### The full list

| # | Condition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ر carries a **fatḥa** | تَرَ (105:1) |
| 2 | ر carries a **ḍamma** | نَصْرُ (110:1) |
| 3 | ر is **sākinah** after a **fatḥa** | تَرْمِيهِم (105:4) · وَأَرْسَلَ (105:3) |
| 4 | ر is **sākinah** after a **ḍamma** | ٱلْقُرْءَانُ (2:185) |
| 5 | ر is **sākinah** after a **temporary kasra** — the kasra of a hamzat waṣl, which vanishes when you join | ٱرْجِعِىٓ (89:28) |
| 6 | ر is **sākinah** after an **original kasra**, but a **ḥarf isti'lā'** follows in the same word | the five words below |
| 7 | ر is **sākinah at waqf**, preceded by a sākin letter, which is itself preceded by a **fatḥa or ḍamma** | ٱلْقَدْرْ (97:1) · وَٱلْعَصْرْ (103:1) |
| 8 | ر carries a **shaddah** with fatḥa or ḍamma | ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ (1:3) |

### ⚠ The five isti'lā' words — reference only

Condition 6 has exactly **five** occurrences in the whole Qur'an. Nobody needs to
derive it; learners need to recognise these five words and move on.

| Word | Ref | The isti'lā' letter |
|---|---|---|
| قِرْطَاسٍ | 6:7 | ط |
| إِرْصَادًا | 9:107 | ص |
| فِرْقَةٍ | 9:122 | ق |
| مِرْصَادًا | 78:21 | ص |
| لَبِٱلْمِرْصَادِ | 89:14 | ص |

In each, the ر is sākinah after an **original** kasra — which would normally make
it light — but a heavy letter follows inside the same word and pulls it heavy.
The pull only works when the isti'lā' letter does **not** itself carry a kasra; if
it does, the rule breaks down and both readings become permissible. That is the
case of فِرْقٍ, in [[Ra-Jawaz-Wajhayn]].

All five refs above were checked against the corpus and are correct as cited.

## How to perform it

1. Make the ر at the **tip of the tongue against the gum ridge**, the same place
   as ن and ل — the makhraj does not change between heavy and light.
2. Raise the **back** of the tongue toward the soft palate. The vowel around the
   ر darkens: *ra* moves toward *rɑ*, as in English *rob* rather than *rib*.
3. **One tap, not a trill.** The single most damaging habit when reaching for
   heaviness is over-rolling — *takrīr*. Ibn al-Jazarī explicitly warns against
   letting the takrīr become audible. Press the tongue firmly against the ridge to
   suppress the extra taps.
4. An alif after a heavy ر is heavy too: **قِرْطَاسٍ**, **مِرْصَادًا**. The alif has
   no ruling of its own — see [[Tafkhim]].

## Common mistakes

- **Reading every ر heavy by default.** The single commonest error in tajweed.
  A heavy ر is the *English* default, so English and Urdu speakers arrive with it
  already installed.
- **Trilling (takrīr)** while reaching for the heaviness — *rrrabb* instead of
  *rabb*. Heaviness comes from the tongue's rear, not from extra taps.
- **Applying the waṣl ruling at waqf.** ٱلْقَدْرِ is light while you read on and
  heavy the moment you stop on it. The word did not change; your stop did.
- **Treating the hamzat-waṣl kasra as a real kasra**, and reading ٱرْجِعِىٓ light.
  That kasra disappears the instant you join to the previous word — it was never
  the ر's own.
- **Extending the five-word list by analogy** to any ر followed by a heavy letter.
  It is five words, closed, and فِرْقٍ is deliberately not one of them.

## Contrast with

- **[[Ra-Tarqiq]]** — the mirror note. Every quiz item in this family is really
  "heavy or light?", so the two notes must be taught as one pair.
- **[[Ra-Jawaz-Wajhayn]]** — the handful of words where the answer is "either".
- **[[Lam-Jalalah]]** — the other switching letter, and a far simpler rule: one
  condition instead of eight. Teach lam jalālah first as the gentle version of the
  same idea.
- **[[Tafkhim]]** — the seven letters that are *always* heavy. ر is not one of
  them; it is a guest.

## Scholars differ

The eight conditions above are the standard Ḥafṣ presentation and are not
themselves contested. What varies between works is **how they are grouped** —
some collapse the shaddah cases into conditions 1 and 2, giving six conditions;
some split condition 7 by vowel, giving nine. The recitation is identical. Where a
genuine difference of transmission exists, it is confined to the cases in
[[Ra-Jawaz-Wajhayn]].

## Colour and dataset coverage

**This rule has no `cpfair_key` and therefore no colour in Family B.** None of the
rā' rules appear among the 18 annotated in [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]] — the dataset
covers madd, ghunnah, idghām, ikhfā', iqlāb, qalqalah, hamzat waṣl, silent letters
and lām shamsiyyah, and nothing about heaviness. This is a **known gap**: rā'
heaviness cannot be auto-highlighted and needs hand-authored drill data.

Given that ر is the busiest letter in tajweed and completely uncovered by the
dataset, this is the largest single gap in the annotation pipeline.
