# Ar-Rakhawah — الرخاوة

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *rakhawah* is softness, slackness, looseness.

**Technically**, rakhawah is the **continuous flow of the sound (*sawt*) with the letter**.
The articulators narrow the channel but never seal it, so the letter can be held for as
long as the breath lasts. These are the fricatives.

## Its opposite

[[Shiddah]] — the sound is completely blocked. [[Tawassut]] occupies the middle of the
same scale.

## Letters

Sixteen letters — everything outside أَجِدْ قَطٍ بَكَتْ and لِنْ عُمَرْ:

ث · ح · خ · ذ · ز · س · ش · ص · ض · ظ · غ · ف · ه · و · ي · ا

There is no single classical mnemonic in wide use; the set is normally reached by
subtraction from the shiddah eight and the tawassut five.

## What it sounds like

The teacher's test is simply: **can the student hold the letter?** Ask for a sustained
sakin ص, ش, or ذ for two or three seconds. A correct rakhawah letter continues
unchanged; a wrong one either stops dead or drifts into a vowel.

The characteristic failure is **cutting the flow short**, especially on ص, ض, ظ, where
learners who are concentrating on heaviness clamp down and turn the letter into a stop —
[[Dad|ض]] becomes a heavy [[Dal|د]], [[Za-heavy|ظ]] becomes a heavy د or [[Ta-heavy|ط]]. The heaviness is right; the continuity is
missing.

## Why it matters

- Rakhawah is what stops the heavy letters from collapsing into their plosive neighbours:
  ض held long is the entire basis of [[Istitalah]], the sifah that makes ض unique.
- No rakhawah letter carries qalqalah, because qalqalah needs a blocked sound to bounce
  out of — see [[Qalqalah-sifah]].
- The continuous letters are where madd-like over-lengthening creeps in: rakhawah means
  the sound *can* be held, not that it *should* be. Length belongs to the madd rules,
  not to the consonant.
