# Aṣ-Ṣafīr — الصفير

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *ṣafīr* is a whistle — the sound made to call a bird or a horse.

**Technically**, ṣafīr is a **sharp, whistle-like sound emerging alongside the letter**,
produced by the very narrow channel between the tip of the tongue and the back of the
lower front teeth. The air is forced through a slit rather than an opening, and the
resulting high-frequency hiss is part of the letter, not an artefact of it.

## Its opposite

None. Ṣafīr belongs to the sifat **without** an opposite: a letter either has this
whistle or the description simply does not apply to it.

The five-pair scheme this course teaches treats ṣafīr as one of the unopposed sifat.
Some works add *al-khafa'* to this group and others pair khafa' with *az-zuhur*, making
six opposed pairs — a difference of arrangement only. See [[Hams]] for that note.

## Letters

Three letters:

ص · ز · س

They differ from each other by their other sifat, not by the whistle:

| Letter | Breath | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| س | [[Hams]] | [[Istifal]] — light |
| ز | [[Jahr]] | [[Istifal]] — light |
| ص | [[Hams]] | [[Istila]] + [[Itbaq]] — heavy |

All three are [[Rakhawah]] letters, so the whistle can be held.

## What it sounds like

The teacher listens for a **thin, high, sustained whistle** riding on the letter. The
test is to hold a sakin س for two seconds: the pitch should be clearly higher than the
breath of ث or ش.

Two failure modes, in opposite directions:

- **Exaggeration** — the student turns the whistle into a piercing hiss, usually while
  trying to make ص "sound Arabic". The whistle of ص is *lower* than that of س, because
  the clamped tongue enlarges the cavity; a shrill ص is a ص that has lost its [[Itbaq]].
- **Suppression** — the student produces a dull, tongue-back "s" with no whistle at all,
  common where the first language has a retracted /s/.

## Why it matters

- Ṣafīr is what keeps ص distinguishable from ط: both are heavy and clamped, but only ص
  whistles.
- It also keeps ز distinguishable from ذ and ظ — the collapse of ذ, ز, ظ and ض into a
  single "z" is a documented error cluster for Persian, Turkish and Urdu speakers, and
  ṣafīr is the sifah that separates ز from the other three.
- ص and س are the two letters at issue in the disputed reading of يَبْصُۜطُ / بَصْۜطَةً,
  where the mushaf itself records the alternative; the whistle is the same, the weight is not.
