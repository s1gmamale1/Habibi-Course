# As-Sakt — السكت

## Definition

**Cutting off the sound for a very short moment — about 2 ḥarakāt — without
taking a breath**, then continuing.

| | **Sakt** | **Waqf** |
|---|---|---|
| Breath taken | **no** | yes |
| Phrase ended | no | yes |
| Duration | ~2 ḥarakāt | as long as you like |

The whole point is that the breath is *not* broken: the two sides of a sakt
belong to one continuous utterance, separated only by silence.

**Mushaf marking:** a small **س** or the word **سكتة** above the line — one of
the six signs in [[Waqf-Signs]].

## The four obligatory (wājib) sakt in Ḥafṣ via ash-Shāṭibiyyah

| # | Place | Text | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **al-Kahf 18:1–2** | عِوَجَا ۜ قَيِّمًا | Prevents عِوَجًا قَيِّمًا reading as one phrase, which would suggest the Book *has* crookedness. |
| 2 | **Yā-Sīn 36:52** | مِن مَّرْقَدِنَا ۜ هَٰذَا | Separates the disbelievers' speech from the angels' reply. |
| 3 | **al-Qiyāmah 75:27** | مَنْ ۜ رَاقٍ | **Blocks the idghām** of ن into ر, which would produce `marrāq`. |
| 4 | **al-Muṭaffifīn 83:14** | بَلْ ۜ رَانَ | **Blocks the idghām** of ل into ر, which would produce `barrāna`. |

Note the pattern: **two are about meaning, two are about phonetics.** In 75:27
and 83:14 the sakt exists purely to stop a merger that the general rules would
otherwise force.

## The two permissible (jāʾiz) sakt

| # | Place | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | **Between al-Anfāl (8) and at-Tawbah (9)** | Three ways are permitted: **sakt**, **waqf**, or **waṣl** — but **never with a basmalah**. |
| 6 | **al-Ḥāqqah 69:28–29** | مَالِيَهْ ۜ هَلَكَ — two ways: **sakt** (showing the hāʾ clearly) or **idghām** of the two hāʾs. Sakt is preferred by most. |

## ⚠ The count is disputed

> Sources give the number of sakt in Ḥafṣ as **4**, **6**, or **7**. The
> difference is bookkeeping, not recitation: some count only the obligatory
> places, some add the Anfāl/Tawbah junction and al-Ḥāqqah, some import places
> from other ṭuruq.
>
> **This course teaches 4 obligatory + 2 permissible**, and says out loud that
> the learner will meet other counts. A learner told "there are four" who then
> reads "there are seven" elsewhere concludes that someone is wrong; nobody is.

## Common mistakes

1. **Breathing during the sakt** — that converts it into a [[Waqf-Types|waqf]] and defeats the
   purpose.
2. Making it too long, so it sounds like a stop.
3. Making it too short, so the idghām it was blocking happens anyway (75:27,
   83:14).
4. Reciting the basmalah between al-Anfāl and at-Tawbah.
