# Ash-Shiddah — الشدة

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *shiddah* is strength, firmness, tightness.

**Technically**, shiddah is the **complete blockage of the sound (*sawt*) at the makhraj**,
followed by a release. The articulators close fully; nothing at all comes out for the
duration of the closure, and the letter is heard in the burst of the release. These are
what phoneticians call plosives or stops.

Shiddah concerns the **sound**; [[Hams]] and [[Jahr]] concern the **breath**. A letter can
be shiddah and hams (ك, ت), or shiddah and jahr (ء, ج, د, ق, ط, ب).

## Its opposite

[[Rakhawah]] — the sound flows continuously. Between the two sits [[Tawassut]], which is
neither a full block nor a free flow: the three together form one scale, not a clean binary.

## Letters

Eight letters:

> **أَجِدْ قَطٍ بَكَتْ** — *ajid qatin bakat*

ء · ج · د · ق · ط · ب · ك · ت

## What it sounds like

The teacher listens for **a real moment of silence inside the letter**. Say a sakin ب, ت,
ق: there should be a closure you can hold, then a clean release. Two failure modes:

- **Leaking** — ج turns into a fricative "zh"/"j" that hums continuously instead of
  stopping. This is the commonest shiddah error and it is audible immediately in وَٱلْفَجْرِ.
- **Over-releasing** — the student adds a small vowel after the release ("qa-", "ba-"),
  turning a sakin letter into a syllable. This corrupts the rhythm of the ayah.

## Why it matters

- **Qalqalah** exists only where shiddah and jahr coincide. The five qalqalah letters
  قُطْبُ جَدٍّ are exactly the shiddah letters minus the hams ones (ك, ت) and minus
  hamzah. See [[Qalqalah-sifah]].
- Shiddah is what separates ط from ص and ض at the same articulation zone, and د from ذ.
- Sakin shiddah letters are where sloppy recitation inserts phantom vowels; getting
  shiddah right is what keeps sukūn genuinely silent.
