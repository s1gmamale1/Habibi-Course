# Ṣifāt al-Ḥurūf — the characteristics of the letters

Where **makhraj** answers *where* a letter is produced, **ṣifah** answers *how*. Two
letters can share a makhraj and be told apart only by their ṣifāt — ت and د and ط all
come from the same place; what separates them is voicing, heaviness and bounce.

Ibn al-Jazari places ṣifāt second in [[Muqaddimah-Jazariyyah]], immediately after the
makharij and before any contextual rule. This course follows that order: a letter's
intrinsic properties (*ḥaqq al-ḥarf*) before what it becomes in context
(*mustaḥaqq al-ḥarf*).

## With opposites — five pairs

Every letter carries exactly one of each pair.

| | |
|---|---|
| [[Hams]] whispered ↔ [[Jahr]] voiced | is the breath running or stopped? |
| [[Shiddah]] plosive ↔ [[Rakhawah]] continuant | (with [[Tawassut]] between them) |
| [[Istila]] elevated ↔ [[Istifal]] lowered | **the heaviness switch** |
| [[Itbaq]] covered ↔ [[Infitah]] open | how much tongue meets palate |
| [[Idhlaq]] fluent ↔ [[Ismat]] restrained | ease on the tongue |

## Without opposites

A letter may carry any number of these, or none.

[[Safir]] whistling · [[Leen]] softness · [[Inhiraf]] deviation · [[Takrir]] repetition ·
[[Tafashshi]] spreading · [[Istitalah]] elongation · [[Ghunnah-sifah]] nasality ·
[[Qalqalah-sifah]] bounce

## Why this matters downstream

Three later rule families are unteachable without the ṣifāt:

- **[[Istila]] drives [[Tafkhim]]** — its seven letters **خص ضغط قظ** are the heavy ones,
  and everything in the rā'/lām stage rests on that set.
- **The heavy variant of [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]]'s ghunnah** depends on whether the following
  letter carries isti'lā.
- **[[Qalqalah-sifah]]** is the characteristic; [[Qalqalah-Sughra]] and
  [[Qalqalah-Kubra]] are the rules built on it.

This is the reason the course teaches the ṣifāt in Unit 3.1, before the nasal rules —
the earlier syllabus taught tafkhīm *after* ikhfā', which inverted the dependency.

## Two cautions

**Takrīr is a ṣifah to suppress, not to perform.** ر carries it, but audibly trilling
the rā' is a documented error — the ṣifah describes a tendency the reciter controls.

**Scholars differ on khafā'.** Some count it among the unopposed ṣifāt; others oppose it
to ẓuhūr, giving six pairs rather than five. This course teaches the five-pair scheme and
names the alternative once.
