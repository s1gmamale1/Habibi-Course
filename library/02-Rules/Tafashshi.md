# At-Tafashshī — التفشي

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *tafashshī* is spreading out, diffusing, becoming widespread — the
word is used of news that gets around.

**Technically**, tafashshī is the **spreading of the breath widely inside the mouth**
when the letter is pronounced. The air is not channelled through a narrow slit as it is
in the [[Safir]] letters, but released across the full width of the tongue and palate, so
it fills the mouth before it leaves.

## Its opposite

None. Tafashshī is one of the sifat without an opposite.

## Letters

One letter: **[[Sheen|ش]]**.

ش is also a [[Hams]] letter (breath flows) and a [[Rakhawah]] letter (sound flows), and it
is [[Istifal]] — light. Tafashshī is what distinguishes it from س, which shares the breath
and the flow but concentrates them into a whistle.

## What it sounds like

The teacher listens for a **broad, diffuse, low-pitched "sh"** that seems to fill the
whole mouth, and holds. Compared side by side:

- **س** — narrow, high, focused: a whistle.
- **ش** — wide, low, spread: a rush of air.

The characteristic error is the **narrow English "sh"**, produced with rounded lips and a
tightly grooved tongue as in *ship*. It is recognisably in the right family, which is
precisely why it goes uncorrected: the student is not making a wrong letter, they are
making a thin one. Ask for the lips to relax out of the rounding and the tongue to flatten
and widen; the pitch should drop audibly.

The mirror error is over-correction into a خ-like scrape at the back of the mouth. The
spreading is forward and flat, not deep.

## Why it matters

- ش is one of the fifteen [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]] letters, and the ikhfa before it is a **light**
  ghunnah — the sound of the ghunnah must be shaped by a spread, light ش, not by a
  clenched one.
- ش is a **sun letter**: the ل of ٱل assimilates fully into it, as in وَٱلشَّمْسِ, so the
  letter is very often doubled and its width is exposed for a full two-count hold.
- Tafashshī is the clearest example in the whole chapter of a sifah that is purely
  descriptive of *one* letter and yet indispensable — without it, nothing in the system
  distinguishes ش from a heavy س.
