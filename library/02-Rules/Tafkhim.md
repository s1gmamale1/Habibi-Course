# At-Tafkhīm — التَّفْخِيم

## Definition

Tafkhīm is "thickening": the back of the tongue rises toward the soft palate, the
mouth fills with the sound, and the letter comes out fat and rounded. The
articulatory quality behind it is **isti'lā'** (اِسْتِعْلَاء, "elevation") — see
[[Istila]] for isti'lā' as a permanent attribute of a letter, as opposed to
tafkhīm as the audible result.

Its opposite is [[Tarqiq]].

## Trigger condition

**Seven letters are always heavy** — the ḥurūf al-isti'lā':

> **خ ص ض غ ط ق ظ**
>
> Mnemonic: **خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ**

These are never light in any position. Everything else in the alphabet is light
by default (the 22 ḥurūf al-istifāl — see [[Tarqiq]]) **except**:

| Letter | Where it turns heavy |
|---|---|
| [[Ra]] — ر | Depending on its vowel and the vowel before it — see [[Ra-Tafkhim]] |
| [[Lam]] — ل of لفظ الجلالة | After a fatḥa or a ḍamma — see [[Lam-Jalalah]] |
| ا alif | Whenever the letter before it is heavy (see below) |
| The ghunnah of a concealed ن / م | Whenever the letter after it is heavy |

## How to perform it

1. Say the letter light first (e.g. a plain English "s"), then raise the **back**
   of the tongue toward the roof of the mouth without moving the tip. The vowel
   colour darkens: "si" becomes closer to "sˤu".
2. The mouth **rounds and fills** — تفخيم is felt as volume, not as loudness.
   Getting louder is not getting heavier.
3. Do not move the makhraj. The tongue tip stays exactly where the letter is
   articulated; only the rear rises. Sliding the tip backwards produces a
   different letter.

### The five levels (marātib at-tafkhīm)

Every heavy letter is heavy, but not equally heavy. From heaviest down:

| Level | Condition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | fatḥa **followed by an alif** | قَالَ |
| 2 | fatḥa alone | خَلَقَ |
| 3 | ḍamma | صُدُورِ |
| 4 | sukūn | وَٱلْعَصْرِ |
| 5 | kasra | ٱلصِّرَٰطَ |

A useful drill: run one letter through all five — قَالَ · قَدْ · قُلْ · يَقْطَعُ · قِيلَ.

### ⚠ The alif has no ruling of its own

**الْأَلِفُ تَتْبَعُ مَا قَبْلَهَا** — *the alif follows what precedes it.*

The alif is never listed among the heavy letters and never among the light ones.
It has **no independent heaviness at all**: it simply prolongs whatever colour the
preceding letter gave it. In غَاسِقٍ the alif is heavy; in كَانَ the identical
written alif is light. Learners who memorise "alif is a light letter" will read
قَالَ wrong every time, and learners who over-correct will read كَانَ wrong every
time. The diagnostic pair is **قَالَ / كَانَ** — say them back to back until the two
alifs sound audibly different.

This is the single highest-frequency error in this whole family, so it is stated
in both [[Tafkhim]] and [[Tarqiq]].

## Common mistakes

- **Heavying the alif everywhere, or nowhere.** See the box above.
- **Confusing heavy with loud.** Tafkhīm is resonance, not volume. Shouting a
  ق does not make it heavy.
- **Dragging the makhraj back.** Trying to "deepen" ص by pulling the tongue tip
  backward turns it into something between ص and a retroflex — a real
  mispronunciation, not a stylistic one.
- **Contaminating the neighbours.** A heavy letter should not heavy the letters
  around it. In طَعَامِ the ط is heavy, but the ع, the ا and the م stay light.
- **Flattening the five levels** into one uniform "heavy". قِيلَ and قَالَ should
  not sound equally thick.

## Contrast with

- **[[Tarqiq]]** — the opposite quality, and the default state of 22 of the
  letters.
- **[[Ra-Tafkhim]]** / **[[Ra-Tarqiq]]** — ر is the one letter that switches
  between both sides on a per-word basis.
- **[[Lam-Jalalah]]** — the only ل that switches.
- **[[Qalqalah-Sughra]]** — a *different* axis entirely. Qalqalah is a bounce on a
  sākin letter; tafkhīm is a tongue posture. ق and ط are both heavy *and*
  qalqalah letters, which is why learners conflate them.

## Scholars differ

**On the number of levels.** Ibn al-Jazarī and most later works give **five**,
ordered fatḥa+alif > fatḥa > ḍamma > sukūn > kasra. Some works give **four**
(merging sukūn into a neighbouring level), and some — notably a number of
Egyptian teachers — **swap levels 3 and 4**, putting sukūn above ḍamma. The
audible difference is slight and the practical outcome is nearly identical.
**This course teaches five levels in Ibn al-Jazarī's order** and notes the
alternative.

There is also a descriptive nuance, not a rule: some authorities place ل (outside
the Divine Name), ر in its light state, and ا in a "medium" band rather than
squarely among the light letters. It matters only when a light letter sits
between two heavy ones. Treat it as an observation about performance, not a
sixth category.

## Colour and dataset coverage

**This rule has no `cpfair_key` and therefore no colour in Family B.** The
cpfair/quran-tajweed dataset (see [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]]) annotates 18 rules,
and tafkhīm/tarqīq is not among them — the dataset carries no isti'lā', no rā'
rules and no lam jalālah. This is a **known gap**: heaviness cannot be
auto-highlighted from the corpus and must be taught by ear, by rule, and by
hand-authored drills.
