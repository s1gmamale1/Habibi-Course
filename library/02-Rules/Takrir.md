# At-Takrīr — التكرير

## Definition

**Linguistically**, *takrīr* is repetition — doing a thing again and again.

**Technically**, takrīr is the **tendency of the tip of the tongue to vibrate repeatedly**
against the gum ridge while ر is being produced. The tip is held loosely enough that the
airstream can flap it more than once.

## Its opposite

None. Takrīr is one of the sifat without an opposite.

## Letters

One letter: **ر**.

## This sifah is identified in order to be avoided

Takrīr is the exception among the sifat. Every other characteristic is something the
student must *produce*; takrīr is something the student must **suppress**. The classical
formulation is explicit: the sifah is mentioned *so that it may be guarded against*.

**A correct ر has exactly one contact.** The tongue tip strikes the ridge once and
releases. Two or more strikes is a trill, and a trill is an error — one that multiplies
the letter, so that a single ر is heard as several.

The over-trill is documented as most common in learners whose first language has a rolled
r: **Spanish, Italian, Russian, Urdu and Persian** backgrounds. It is not a beginner's
error that disappears with practice; unchecked, it hardens into a habit that survives for
years, because the student is producing a sound that is *correct in their own language*.

The opposite error — the English approximant ر with no contact at all — is a failure of
[[Inhiraf]], not of takrīr.

## What it sounds like

The teacher listens at the two places where trilling surfaces:

- **Shaddah on ر** — شَرِّ. A doubled ر is *two* contacts, held; it is not a roll. This is
  the single most reliable diagnostic, because the shaddah gives the tongue permission to
  linger and the trill escapes.
- **Sakin ر at waqf** — بِٱلصَّبْرِ, وَٱلْفَجْرِ stopped upon. A stopped ر ends the breath,
  and a loose tip flaps as the air runs out.

Correction cue: have the student press the tip slightly *harder* and shorten the contact.
Trilling comes from a slack tongue, not a fast one.

## Why it matters

- Takrīr sits directly on the most frequent letter-level error in adult learners, and it
  is a *lahn khafi* — hidden error — meaning it does not change the word but does mark the
  recitation as untrained.
- Because it is corrective rather than productive, it belongs in the assessment rubric as
  something to listen *for*, not to score as a skill acquired.
- ر also carries [[Inhiraf]] and has its own heaviness rules; see [[Ra]] for the full
  treatment of the letter.
