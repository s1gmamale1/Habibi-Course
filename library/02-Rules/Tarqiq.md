# At-Tarqīq — التَّرْقِيق

## Definition

Tarqīq is "thinning": the sound comes out slim and forward, the back of the
tongue stays **low** ([[Istifal]], اِسْتِفَال — "lowering"), and the mouth does not
round. It is the opposite of [[Tafkhim]] and the **default state of the
alphabet** — 22 of the 29 letters are light and never anything else.

Tarqīq is not a rule you apply. It is what happens when you *don't* do anything.
The teaching problem is therefore never "how do I make it light" but "how do I
stop leaking heaviness into it."

## Trigger condition

A letter is light when it is **not** one of the seven ḥurūf al-isti'lā'
(خ ص ض غ ط ق ظ) and not one of the standing exceptions:

| Exception | Ruling |
|---|---|
| ر — see [[Ra-Tarqiq]] | Light with a kasra, and in several sākin positions |
| ل of لفظ الجلالة — see [[Lam-Jalalah]] | Light after a kasra; heavy after fatḥa or ḍamma |
| Every **other** ل | **Always light, without exception** |
| ا alif | No ruling of its own — inherits from the letter before it |
| ghunnah of a concealed ن / م | Inherits from the letter **after** it |

### The letters that need the most vigilance

Learners rarely heavy a ك or a ت by accident. They constantly heavy these,
because a heavy neighbour bleeds into them:

> **ا · ب · ل · م · ن · ه · ء**

Test words: **طَعَامِ** (ط heavy, ع ا م light) · **أَنقَضَ** (ق heavy, أ ن light)
· **نَصَرَ** (ص heavy, ن ر ... light).

## How to perform it

1. **Keep the tongue flat.** The back of the tongue rests on the floor of the
   mouth. Nothing rises.
2. **Keep the lips unrounded** unless the vowel itself is a ḍamma.
3. **Reset between letters.** The commonest failure is inertia: the tongue is
   still up from the previous heavy letter when the light letter arrives. Drill
   heavy→light transitions slowly (طَـ‑عَـ‑ا‑مِ) until the drop is deliberate.
4. Say the light letter with an English "ee" colour in your head — that forward,
   thin resonance is what tarqīq sounds like.

### ⚠ The alif has no ruling of its own

**الْأَلِفُ تَتْبَعُ مَا قَبْلَهَا** — *the alif follows what precedes it.*

The alif is **not** a light letter. It is not a heavy letter either. It carries no
independent heaviness whatsoever and simply extends the colour of the letter
before it. In كَانَ it is light; in قَالَ the identical written alif is heavy.

Memorising "alif is light" is the single most common error in this family — it is
why so many readers pronounce قَالَ, طَابَ and ٱلضَّآلِّينَ with a thin, front alif.
The correction drill is the minimal pair **قَالَ / كَانَ**, said back to back until
the two alifs are audibly different lengths of the same duration but different
colours.

Stated identically in [[Tafkhim]]; both notes carry it deliberately.

| 10:81 | قَالَ | Counter-example: the same alif shape, now heavy |

## Common mistakes

- **"Alif is a light letter."** It is not a letter with a ruling at all. See
  above.
- **Contamination from a heavy neighbour** — the ل in صَلَاة or طَلَبَ pulled
  heavy by the ص/ط before it. Every ل outside the Divine Name is light, always.
- **A dark English "l"** used for every ل. English speakers default to the velarised
  /ɫ/ of *feel*; Arabic wants the clear /l/ of *leaf*.
- **Persian/Urdu-influenced uniform vowel colour**, where every alif comes out the
  same regardless of what precedes it.
- **Over-correcting**, thinning the isti'lā' letters themselves so ص becomes س.

## Contrast with

- **[[Tafkhim]]** — the direct opposite; the two notes are one lesson.
- **[[Ra-Tarqiq]]** — the special case where a letter *becomes* light by rule
  rather than by default.
- **[[Lam-Jalalah]]** — the only ل that is ever heavy; everywhere else, ل is
  light and this note is the reason why.
- **[[Lam-Qamariyyah]]** — a clearly pronounced sākin ل, and a good place to
  drill a light ل in isolation.

## Scholars differ

The count of "always light" letters is stated as 22 here (29 letters minus the 7
of isti'lā'), with ر, the ل of the Divine Name, the alif and the ghunnah handled
separately as standing exceptions. Some works instead give the count as 21 and
list the alif as a light letter — which produces exactly the misreading warned
against above. Where sources disagree on the count, they agree on the practice:
**the alif takes the colour of what precedes it.**

On the "medium band" (ل, light ر, ا described as neither fully heavy nor fully
light), see the corresponding section of [[Tafkhim]]. It is descriptive nuance,
not a separate ruling.
