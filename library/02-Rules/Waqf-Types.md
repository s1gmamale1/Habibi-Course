# Types of Waqf — أنواع الوقف

Ibn al-Jazarī: **وَبَعْدَ تَجْوِيدِكَ لِلْحُرُوفِ ⁕ لَا بُدَّ مِنْ مَعْرِفَةِ
الْوُقُوفِ** — "after mastering the letters, knowledge of the stops is
indispensable."

> **Why this sits in Unit 2, not Unit 3.** Al-Jazariyyah treats madd (ch. 11)
> before waqf (ch. 12), and an earlier draft of this syllabus copied that order.
> But [[Madd-Arid-Lissukun]], [[Madd-Leen]] and [[Madd-Iwad]] are *defined by
> stopping* — they cannot be taught to someone who does not yet stop correctly.
> **This course teaches practical stopping early**, in Unit 2, so that the madd
> rules in Unit 3 have solid ground under them.

## A. Four types by **cause**

| Type | Arabic | What it is | Ruling |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ikhtiyārī** | الاختياري | Chosen deliberately | The one classified for quality below |
| **Iḍṭirārī** | الاضطراري | Compelled — breath, cough, sneeze | Permitted **anywhere**; you must **resume from before** the stopping point |
| **Ikhtibārī** | الاختباري | For testing/teaching — stopping on a word to show its waqf form | Teaching contexts only; resume from that word or earlier |
| **Intiẓārī** | الانتظاري | "Waiting" — gathering variant readings of a word | Specialist qirāʾāt study only |

The practical takeaway for a beginner: **running out of breath is not a sin.**
Stop, breathe, go back a word or two, and carry on.

## B. Four types by **quality** (Ibn al-Jazarī's scheme)

| Type | Arabic | Meaning | Restart from the next word? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tām** | التام | Complete; no grammatical or semantic link forward | **Excellent** |
| **Kāfī** | الكافي | Complete; linked in *meaning* but not grammar | **Good** |
| **Ḥasan** | الحسن | Understandable alone, but grammatically attached forward | Stopping is good; **restarting from the next word is not** — unless the stop was an āyah end |
| **Qabīḥ** | القبيح | Incomplete, or the stop **distorts** the meaning | **Impermissible** deliberately; if forced, go back |

**Stopping at the end of any āyah is always permitted**, by the Prophet's own
practice ﷺ, even where the meaning runs on. This is the single most useful rule
for a beginner: the rosette ۝ is always a safe stop.

### The qabīḥ trap

فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ ("so woe to those who pray") is complete as a sentence
and lands on an āyah end — but the meaning is completed only by the next āyah
(ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ سَاهُونَ). Stopping deliberately at a place that
*reverses* the sense is qabīḥ even at an āyah end.

## C. Al-Ibtidāʾ — where you **start**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Governing rule** | A start must **always** be at a place where the meaning is complete and correct. |
| **Why it is stricter than waqf** | There is **no such thing as a compelled start.** The reciter always chooses where to begin, so an ugly start is never excused. |
| **Practical rule** | If you stopped at a qabīḥ place, or were forced to stop, **go back** to a suitable earlier point and resume there. |

Starting on a word also invokes [[Hamzat-Wasl]] — you must know what vowel the
connecting hamzah takes when it becomes the first sound you utter. **Teach waqf
and ibtidāʾ together with hamzat al-waṣl; they are one skill.**
