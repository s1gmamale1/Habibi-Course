# ا — alif

## Makhraj

**Point 1 of 17** — *al-jawf* (الْجَوْف), the **empty space of the mouth and throat**. Zone 1, and the only makhraj with no contact point at all. Alif, madd [[Waw]] and madd yāʾ are the three *ḥurūf jawfiyyah* / *hawāʾiyyah* — the air letters. The sound simply resonates in the open cavity until the breath runs out or the next letter arrives.

**Which alif this note is about:** alif is *always* sākinah and *always* preceded by a fathah. There is no such thing as أَلِف with a vowel on it. When you see a written ا carrying a hamzah or a vowel, that is [[Hamza]] using the alif as a chair. The two are counted separately: tajweed counts **29 ḥurūf al-hijāʾ** = 28 letters + alif distinct from hamzah.

Teacher's script: *"Open your mouth, say `aa`, and change nothing — no tongue, no lips, no jaw. Two counts. The moment your jaw starts to close you have turned it into a schwa (`uh`), which is the most common way learners spoil the madd."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Jahr** (voiced) | hams | Voiced throughout; the breath does not escape unvoiced. |
| **Rakhāwah** (flowing) | shiddah | Pure flow — nothing blocks it anywhere. |
| **Istifāl** (lowered) | istiʿlāʾ | Nominally light — **but see the inheritance rule below, which overrides this in practice.** |
| **Infitāḥ** (open) | iṭbāq | Fully open. |
| **Iṣmāt** (prevented) | idhlāq | Not a "fluent" letter. |
| **Khafāʾ** (hiddenness) | (zuhūr, per some) | Faint and easily lost — grouped by Ibn al-Jazarī with [[Ha-soft]] and the other madd letters as the sounds that vanish under carelessness. |

## The inheritance rule — teach this explicitly

> **Alif al-madd has NO independent tafkhīm/tarqīq ruling. It takes its weight entirely from the letter immediately before it.**

This is a high-frequency error (course-research). ر and ل have their own tafkhīm rules; alif has none. It is a mirror.

- After a heavy letter (خ ص ض ط ظ غ ق, or a heavy ر, or the ل of lafẓ al-jalālah after fathah/dammah) → **heavy alif**: ٱلصِّرَٰطَ, قَالَ, طَالَ, ٱللَّهُ.
- After a light letter → **light alif**: وَلَا, ٱهْدِنَا, مَٰلِكِ.

The alif in ٱلصِّرَٰطَ and the alif in وَلَا are the same letter with two completely different mouth shapes. Say them back to back for the student — the difference is unmistakable and takes about ten seconds to demonstrate.

Correction cue: *"Don't decide anything about the alif. Look at the letter before it and copy that letter's mouth. The alif is a mirror — it has no opinion of its own."*

## Common mistakes

- **Flattening a heavy alif** — ٱلصِّرَٰطَ read with a thin, front `aa` as in English "cat". Correction cue: *"Your ص was heavy and then your mouth emptied. Keep the back of your tongue up right through the alif."*
- **Heavying a light alif** — usually contagion from a nearby heavy letter, or over-drilling. وَلَا must stay bright and forward.
- **Letting the jaw close mid-madd**, producing a schwa (taxonomy §2.1). Correction cue: *"Hold the jaw. If I put a finger under your chin, it must not move for the whole two counts."*
- **Adding a glottal catch at the end of the madd** (taxonomy §2.1) — نَا finished with a small stop. That is an inserted [[Hamza]]. Let the sound fade.
- **Turkic / Uzbek L1** — vowel harmony pulls the alif toward a front or back vowel depending on neighbours. Correction cue: *"One alif sound, decided only by the consonant before it — not by the rest of the word."*
- **Persian / Urdu L1** — the alif in these languages is a plain long `ā` with no heavy variant, so heavy alifs are consistently produced light.
- **Shortening the two counts** — the base madd ṭabīʿī is exactly two ḥarakāt, everywhere, unless a rule lengthens it.
- **Confusing ا with ء in writing and reading** — أَ / إِ / آ are hamzah on an alif chair, not alif.

## Confused with

- **[[Hamza]] (ء)** — the two share a written shape constantly. Alif = a silent stretch. Hamzah = a stop. Students must be able to say which one a written ا is in any given word.
- **[[Waw]] (و) and [[Ya]] (ي) as madd letters** — same makhraj (al-jawf), same two-count duration; the difference is lip rounding (و) and tongue raising (ي). Alif requires neither.
- **A schwa** — the most common accidental substitute.

## In tajweed

- **Madd ṭabīʿī** — two counts, the baseline for all madd rules.
- **Madd muttaṣil / munfaṣil** — an alif followed by [[Hamza]] in the same word (4–5 counts) or the next word (4–5 counts).
- **Madd lāzim** — an alif followed by a sukūn or shaddah: six counts (ٱلضَّآلِّينَ, ٱلْحَآقَّةُ).
- **Madd ʿiwaḍ** — at a waqf, tanwīn fatḥ is read as a two-count alif.
- **Madd badal** — hamzah then alif: ءَامَنُوا۟.
- **Hamzat waṣl (ٱ)** — an alif carrying the waṣl head; pronounced only when starting on it.
- **Alif fāriqah** — the silent alif written after a plural wāw (ءَامَنُوا۟, تَوَاصَوْا۟). It is written, never pronounced; the small circle above it marks it silent.
- **Tafkhīm / tarqīq** — no independent ruling; inherits from the preceding letter, as above.
