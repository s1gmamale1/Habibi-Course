# ع — ʿayn

## Makhraj

**Point 3 of 17** — *wasaṭ al-ḥalq* (وَسَطُ الْحَلْق), the **middle of the throat**. Zone 2, al-Ḥalq. Same point as [[Ha-throat]]; ع is voiced, ح is not.

Physically: the pharynx walls squeeze inward — the same constriction as ح — but the vocal folds are running, and the closure is **partial**. Air keeps moving, but not freely. The result is a thick, voiced, slightly strained sound that seems to come from behind the tongue. The tongue itself stays flat and does nothing.

Teacher's script: *"Make a long ح — the dry rasp. Hold it. Now, without moving anything, switch your voice on. That is ع. Fingers on your throat: ح is quiet machinery, ع buzzes."*

Second cue: *"Say `aaa` like a doctor looking at your throat. Now squeeze the middle of your throat while the sound keeps going, so the `aaa` gets pinched and strained. Do not close it — pinch it."*

**The word "pinch, don't close" is the whole lesson.** Closing gives you [[Hamza]].

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Jahr** (voiced) | hams | The vocal folds vibrate throughout. No breath escapes past them. |
| **Tawassuṭ** (between) | shiddah / rakhāwah | *The key sifah.* The sound is neither fully stopped nor fully flowing. It is one of the five لِنْ عُمَرْ letters. Stop it completely and you get a glottal stop; open it completely and you get a plain vowel. |
| **Istifāl** (lowered) | istiʿlāʾ | Light. ع stays light even in وَٱلْعَصْرِ, beside the heavy ص. |
| **Infitāḥ** (open) | iṭbāq | No clamping. |
| **Iṣmāt** (prevented) | idhlāq | Not a "fluent" letter. |

## Common mistakes

**ع substituted with [[Hamza]] (ء) is a priority error** (course-research): the throat squeeze is not habituated, so the learner reaches for the nearest thing they can already do — a glottal stop.

- **ع → ء (hamzah)** — نَعْبُدُ becomes `naʾbudu`. This is the default failure in Persian, Urdu and Turkish, where ع in loanwords is realised as a glottal catch or simply as a vowel lengthener. Correction cue: *"You closed your throat. Don't close it — squeeze it and keep the sound running. `aaa` … now pinch … `ʿaaa`. If the sound stops for even a moment, it is a hamzah."* Drill أَعُوذُ (113:1) slowly: the أ stops, the ع does not.
- **ع → plain vowel (deleted)** — عَ becomes a bare `a`; نَعْبُدُ becomes `naabudu`. Correction cue: *"Where did the letter go? I heard a vowel. Give me the strain."*
- **Turkic / Slavic L1** — no pharyngeal consonants at all; ع is heard as a vowel colouring and simply not produced.
- **Persian / Urdu L1** — ع is written but not pronounced (Persian علم = `elm`). The learner reads the letter fluently and produces nothing. Correction cue: *"In Farsi this letter is a spelling rule. Here it is a sound. Make it."*
- **Making ع heavy** — because it is throaty, learners often thicken it into something like a heavy vowel. ع is **istifāl**; it is a light letter. Correction cue: *"Keep the back of your tongue down. Deep is not the same as heavy."*
- **Sākin ع bounced like a qalqalah letter** — نَعْبُدُ gets an echo. ع has no qalqalah; the sound is held, not released with a pop.

**Drill sequence:** أَ / حَ / عَ — stop, breath, voice. Same neighbourhood, three different behaviours. Then نَعْبُدُ at half speed.

## Confused with

- **[[Hamza]] (ء)** — the priority substitution. Test: can you hold the sound? ع yes, ء no.
- **[[Ha-throat]] (ح)** — identical makhraj; ع voiced, ح voiceless. Losing the voice on ع gives ح.
- **[[Ghayn]] (غ)** — both are voiced throat letters. غ is higher and scrapes; ع is lower and strains. Learners who cannot do ع sometimes overshoot into غ.
- **A long vowel** — the most common "substitute" of all: nothing at all.

## In tajweed

- **[[Izhar-Halqi|Izhār ḥalqī]]** — ع is one of the six throat letters (أَخِي هَاكَ عِلْمًا حَازَهُ غَيْرُ خَاسِرِ); nūn sākinah and tanwīn before it are pronounced clearly with no ghunnah stretch.
- **[[Tawassut|Tawassuṭ]]** — ع must be given real duration when sākin. It is neither snapped nor stretched into a vowel.
- **[[Istifal|Istifāl]]** — always light; never let a neighbouring ص, ض, ط, ظ, خ, غ or ق drag it into [[Tafkhim|tafkhīm]].
- **Sūrah headings** — ع is the letter used in the muṣḥaf margin to mark rukūʿ divisions; worth naming so students do not read it as text.
- **No [[Qalqalah-sifah|qalqalah]].**
