# د — dāl

## Makhraj

The **8th of the ten tongue makharij** — the *nit'iyyah* group. The **tip of the tongue** against the **roots** of the two upper incisors: where the teeth meet the gum, on the inside.

Same point as [[Ta-heavy]] and [[Ta]]. د is the **light, voiced** member of that trio:

- **د** — voiced, light, bounces.
- **ط** — voiced, **heavy**, bounces.
- **ت** — **breathed**, light, puffs.

Cue: *"Tip of the tongue up on the gum where your top teeth start — not on the teeth themselves, and definitely not between them."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **jahr** (voiced) | hams | Voiced. Fingers on the throat: a buzz. |
| **shiddah** (plosive) | rakhawah | Full stop, then burst. |
| **istifal** (lowered) | isti'la | **Light.** Even next to a heavy letter. |
| **infitah** (open) | itbaq | No clamping. |
| **ismat** | idhlaq | Not a fluent letter. |
| **qalqalah** | — | shiddah + jahr ⇒ a sakin د bounces. |

## Common mistakes

- **A dental / interdental د.** English speakers, and Uzbek and Turkish speakers whose native "d" is dental, put the tongue on or between the teeth. Arabic د is on the **gum root**, further back. The tongue tip should not be visible.
- **Devoicing it into ت**, especially at word-end — أَحَدْ read as "ahat". Uzbek and Turkish devoice final stops as a matter of course, so this is systematic, not sloppy.
- **Dropping the qalqalah**: قَدْ, وَقَدْ, أَدْبَرَ all need the bounce.
- **Making it heavy** when it neighbours ص ض ط ظ, as in صَدَقَ.
- **Confusing it with ض** in the other direction — see below.

**Spoken correction cue:** *"Back off the teeth — up onto the gum. Voice it, and bounce it: qad, with a kick at the end."*

## Confused with

- [[Ta]] — the devoicing pair. د is voiced and bounces; ت is breathed and puffs. This is the same contrast as ط/ت one weight class down.
- [[Ta-heavy]] — د is exactly ط minus the heaviness. Students who over-darken د produce ط; students who flatten ط produce د.
- [[Dad]] — **the important one in the other direction.** ض is *not* a heavy د. When a student produces ض with the tongue **tip** and stops the sound, what comes out is a heavy د: rakhawah and istitalah are both destroyed. ض is made with the **side** of the tongue against the molars and the sound is dragged forward. If you hear a bounce, it is a د.
- [[Dhal]] — the dotted partner in the script; ذ is made further forward, on the *edges* of the upper teeth, and flows instead of stopping.

## In tajweed

- **[[Qalqalah-sifah|Qalqalah]].** د is the د of **قُطْبُ جَدٍّ** — the last letter of the mnemonic and the one students meet first, in قَدْ.
- **[[Ikhfa-Haqiqi|Ikhfa']].** د is one of the fifteen ikhfa' letters: noon sakinah before د is hidden with a **light** ghunnah, as in أَنْدَادًا.
- **[[Idgham-Mutajanisayn|Idgham mutajanisayn]]** — a **sakin د merges into a following ت**: قَد تَّبَيَّنَ. In the reverse direction, a sakin ت merges into a following د: أَثْقَلَت دَّعَوَا (7:189).
- **[[Idgham-Mutamathilayn|Idgham mutamathilayn]]** — sakin د into د: قَد دَّخَلُوا.
- **[[Istifal|Istifal]]** — د never makes a following ر heavy.
