# ذ — dhāl

## Makhraj

The **10th and last of the ten tongue makharij** — the *lithawiyyah* group. The **tip of the tongue** touches the **lower edges** of the two upper front incisors. The tip protrudes slightly and should be **visible** when the student says it.

Shared with [[Za-heavy]] and [[Tha]]:

- **ذ** — voiced, light, flowing.
- **ظ** — voiced, **heavy**, flowing. (ذ with the back of the tongue clamped.)
- **ث** — **breathed**, light, flowing. (ذ with the voice off.)

Cue: *"Put the tip of your tongue on the edge of your top teeth — let it stick out a little. Turn your voice on and let the sound flow over it. Don't stop the air."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **jahr** (voiced) | hams | Voiced throughout. Fingers on the throat: buzz. |
| **rakhawah** (flowing) | shiddah | Holdable. A sakin ذ is sustained, never bounced. |
| **istifal** (lowered) | isti'la | **Light.** Even beside a heavy letter. |
| **infitah** (open) | itbaq | No clamping — this is what separates it from ظ. |
| **ismat** | idhlaq | Not a fluent letter. |

## Common mistakes

- **ذ → ز.** The dominant error, and a first-language fact rather than a slip. **In Persian and Urdu, ذ ز ض ظ are all pronounced [z]** — a single sound written four ways in borrowed vocabulary. Turkish does the same for ذ and ز. So an Urdu-speaking student reading ٱلَّذِينَ produces "allazīna" and hears nothing missing. The tongue tip never comes forward to the teeth at all.

  The fix is visual before it is auditory: **make them watch their tongue in a mirror.** For ذ the tip must appear between the teeth. For [[Zay]] it must not.
- **ذ → د.** The tongue tip retreats to the *roots* of the teeth and the air stops. That is [[Dal]]'s makhraj plus shiddah — a different letter.
- **ذ ↔ ث.** Swapping voiced for breathed. English speakers who have both sounds (*this* vs *thin*) still mix them up, because in English the choice is lexical, not phonemic in the Arabic way. Test with the throat: ذ buzzes, ث does not.
- **Making it heavy** near ط ص ض ظ, e.g. in إِذْ ظَلَمُوا, where the ذ must stay light right up until it merges.
- **Insufficient protrusion** — a ذ made behind the teeth becomes a soft د or a ز.

**Spoken correction cue:** *"Tongue out onto the top teeth — I want to see it. Now buzz, and keep it flowing. If your tongue stays down, you're saying zāy."*

## Confused with

- [[Zay]] — the collapse to watch for, and the one that Persian, Turkish and Urdu speakers arrive with.
- [[Za-heavy]] — the heavy counterpart. Same tip position; ظ adds isti'la and itbaq.
- [[Tha]] — the voicing pair. Same tip position; ث has hams instead of jahr. **ث ↔ ذ swapping runs in both directions** and is one of the most common uncorrected errors in adult learners.
- [[Dal]] — what ذ becomes when the tip pulls back and the air stops.
- [[Dad]] — part of the same four-way z-collapse, though the makhraj is nowhere near.

## In tajweed

- **[[Idgham-Mutajanisayn|Idgham mutajanisayn]]** — a **sakin ذ merges into a following ظ**: إِذ ظَّلَمُوا (4:64). Also the merging of a sakin ذ into the ظ in وَإِذ ظَّلَمْتُمْ-type constructions.
- **[[Lam-Shamsiyyah|Idgham shamsiyyah]]** — ذ is one of the fourteen sun letters: the ل of ٱل is silent before it, ٱلذِّكْر is read "adh-dhikr".
- **[[Ikhfa-Haqiqi|Ikhfa']].** ذ is one of the fifteen ikhfa' letters: noon sakinah before ذ is hidden with a **light** ghunnah, as in مُنذِرٌ.
- **[[Istifal|Istifal]]** — ذ never makes a following ر heavy.
- ذ is not a [[Qalqalah-sifah|qalqalah]] letter — it has [[Rakhawah|rakhawah]], not [[Shiddah|shiddah]].
