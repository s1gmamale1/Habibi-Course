# ف — fāʾ

## Makhraj

**Point 15 of 17** — *ash-shafatān* (الشَّفَتَان), specifically the **inside of the lower lip against the edges of the two upper incisors**. Zone 4, the lips. It is the only Arabic letter made with lip-and-teeth contact.

Physically: the lower lip rises and its inner surface — not its outer edge — meets the biting edges of the upper front teeth. Breath is pushed through the gap between them. No voice.

Teacher's script: *"Bite gently on the inside of your bottom lip with your top teeth, then blow. That is ف. Look in a mirror: I should see your top teeth resting on your lip. If your two lips are touching each other instead, you are blowing a candle out, not saying ف."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Hams** (whispered) | jahr | Voiceless. Say ف sākin against your palm — a clear jet of air. Fingers on the throat: no buzz. |
| **Rakhāwah** (flowing) | shiddah | Continuous — `فففف` can be held. |
| **Istifāl** (lowered) | istiʿlāʾ | Light, always. |
| **Infitāḥ** (open) | iṭbāq | No clamping. |
| **Idhlāq** (fluency) | iṣmāt | One of the six easy, quick letters — فِرَّ مِنْ لُبِّ (ف ر م ن ل ب). It costs almost no effort, which is why it is one of the safest letters to teach early. |

## Common mistakes

ف is genuinely easy for Uzbek, Turkish, Persian and Urdu speakers — all four languages have `f`. The errors are therefore small and mechanical rather than substitutional.

- **Producing it with both lips** — a bilabial [ɸ], the sound of blowing out a candle (taxonomy §2.15). Common where the learner's dialect substitutes `p`/`f` loosely. Correction cue: *"Teeth on lip. Show me in the mirror — I want to see the top teeth."*
- **Insufficient contact**, so the ف comes out as a weak `h`-ish puff (taxonomy §2.15). Correction cue: *"Press a little harder and hold the air back before you release it."*
- **Uzbek / Turkic L1** — `f` entered these languages through loanwords, and colloquial speech still swaps it for `p` in some regions and registers. Under pressure the habit can resurface: ٱلْفَلَقِ → `al-palaq`. Correction cue: *"`p` stops the air. ف lets it flow. Hold the sound: fffff — you cannot hold a `p`."*
- **Swallowing the breath at a waqf** — stopping on a final ف without releasing the air. It is a hams letter; the breath must be heard.
- **Voicing it into [v]** — makes it the mirror image of the [[Waw]] error. Arabic has no `v`.

## Confused with

- **[[Waw]] (و)** — through `v`. If a learner's و has teeth in it, they are producing a voiced ف. Drill وَ / فَ with a finger on the lower lip.
- **[[Ba]] (ب)** — same zone; ب closes both lips and stops. If the ف stops instead of flowing, it has drifted toward ب.
- **پ (`p`)** — not an Arabic letter at all, but a live substitute for Persian, Urdu and Uzbek speakers.
- **[[Tha]] (ث)** — both are light voiceless fricatives with hams. Learners occasionally hear them as the same "soft th/f" and blur them; ث is made at the tongue tip and upper incisor **edges**, not at the lip.

## In tajweed

- **[[Hams|Hams]]** — one of the ten فَحَثَّهُ شَخْصٌ سَكَتْ letters; the breath must escape, especially on a sākin ف and at a waqf.
- **[[Idhlaq|Idhlāq]]** — one of the six فِرَّ مِنْ لُبِّ letters used classically to test whether a quadriliteral root is genuinely Arabic.
- **[[Ikhfa-Haqiqi|Ikhfāʾ ḥaqīqī]]** — ف is one of the fifteen ikhfāʾ letters. A nūn sākinah or tanwīn before ف is hidden with a two-count ghunnah and the mouth **already in ف position**: أَنفُسَكُمْ, يُنفِقُونَ. This is the most useful rule attached to this letter.
- **[[Izhar-Shafawi|Izhār shafawī]]** — a mīm sākinah before ف is pronounced clearly; do not let the mīm blur into the ف. Classical texts single out ف and [[Waw]] here precisely because they are so close to the mīm's own makhraj.
- **No [[Qalqalah-sifah|qalqalah]], no [[Tafkhim|tafkhīm]].**
