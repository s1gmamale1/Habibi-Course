# غ — ghayn

## Makhraj

**Point 4 of 17** — *adnā al-ḥalq* (أَدْنَى الْحَلْق), the **nearest part of the throat**, closest to the mouth. Zone 2, al-Ḥalq. Same point as [[Kha]]; غ is voiced, خ is not.

Physically: the back of the tongue rises toward the uvula, leaving a narrow gap, and voiced air is pushed through so that the uvula and the soft tissue behind it vibrate loosely. It is a **voiced gargle**.

Teacher's script: *"Gargle without water. Now add your voice — let it rumble. That loose flutter at the very back is غ. Fingers on the throat: غ buzzes, خ does not."*

The pairing drill that teaches both letters at once: `خ … غ … خ … غ`, holding each for two seconds, hand on the throat, switching only the voice. Nothing else in the mouth may move.

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Jahr** (voiced) | hams | The voice runs the whole time. |
| **Rakhāwah** (flowing) | shiddah | Continuous — `غغغغ` can be held. If it stops, you have made a ق-like closure. |
| **Istiʿlāʾ** (elevation) | istifāl | The back of the tongue is up; غ is **always heavy (mufakhkham)**, with every vowel. |
| **Infitāḥ** (open) | iṭbāq | Heavy without clamping — one of the lighter members of the heavy seven. |
| **Iṣmāt** (prevented) | idhlāq | Not a "fluent" letter. |

## Common mistakes

**غ ↔ خ confusion is a priority error** (course-research). The two share one makhraj and differ only in voicing, so any instability in voicing swaps them.

- **غ devoiced into خ** — most common at word-end and in sākin position: ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ becomes `al-makhḍūb`. Correction cue: *"Fingers on the throat. Hold غ for three seconds and feel the buzz never stop. Now say the word without letting the buzz drop out."*
- **خ voiced into غ** — the reverse, usually from over-drilling غ. Correction cue: *"Switch the motor off. Same place, breath only."*
- **Produced as a French/uvular trilling `r`** with heavy vibration (taxonomy §2.4). Uzbek ғ and Russian-influenced learners often over-trill. Correction cue: *"One smooth rumble, not a rattle. Let the back of your tongue hover, don't flap it."*
- **Turkish L1** — Turkish ğ is silent (it merely lengthens the preceding vowel), so غ is often **dropped entirely** or turned into a vowel stretch. Correction cue: *"Turkish ğ is a ghost. Arabic غ is a real sound with a rumble. Make me hear it."*
- **Persian / Urdu L1** — غ and ق have merged in both languages into a single sound, so غ is frequently produced as a ق (a hard stop) or the two are used interchangeably. Correction cue: *"ق stops the air completely; غ never stops. If you can hold it, it's غ."*
- **Uzbek L1** — Uzbek ғ is genuinely close to غ. Use it as the anchor and refine: keep it heavy, keep it smooth, don't trill.
- **Losing the heaviness with kasrah** — غِ still needs the raised tongue back and a full mouth.

## Confused with

- **[[Kha]] (خ)** — the priority pair; identical makhraj, voicing is the only difference. Minimal drill: غَ / خَ.
- **[[Qaf]] (ق)** — for Persian and Urdu speakers these are the same phoneme. غ flows; ق stops and bounces (qalqalah).
- **French / Parisian `r`** and Uzbek ғ — close relatives, but غ must not trill.
- **[[Ayn]] (ع)** — both voiced throat letters. ع is deeper, lighter, and strained; غ is higher, heavy, and rumbling.

## In tajweed

- **[[Izhar-Halqi|Izhār ḥalqī]]** — غ is one of the six throat letters; nūn sākinah and tanwīn before it are pronounced clearly.
- **[[Tafkhim|Tafkhīm]]** — غ is one of the seven [[Istila|istiʿlāʾ]] letters (خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ) and is **always** heavy; degree follows the vowel (fathah > dammah > kasrah).
- **[[Ra-Tafkhim|Rāʾ rules]]** — an istiʿlāʾ letter such as غ in the environment of a sākin rāʾ can prevent [[Ra-Tarqiq|tarqīq]].
- **Alif after غ** — the alif inherits غ's heaviness completely; see [[Alif]].
- **No [[Qalqalah-sifah|qalqalah]]** — sākin غ is a held rumble, never a bounce.
