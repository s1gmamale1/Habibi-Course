# ه — hāʾ

## Makhraj

**Point 2 of 17** — *aqṣā al-ḥalq* (أَقْصَى الْحَلْق), the **deepest part of the throat**, nearest the chest. Zone 2, al-Ḥalq. It shares this point with [[Hamza]]; ء is the same location closed, ه is the same location open.

Physically: the vocal folds are held **apart** and breath passes between them with no obstruction anywhere above. Nothing in the mouth moves for ه. The tongue is irrelevant, the lips are irrelevant. It is the sound of a sigh, produced as far down as you can produce anything.

Teacher's script: *"Sigh. Just sigh — هـاااه. That is the letter. The only work is making sure the breath actually leaves you, and that it comes from the bottom of your throat, not from your mouth."*

**The pair test that fixes the whole zone:** ء and ه come from the same place. Say `أَ` — feel the throat snap shut and pop open. Now say `هَ` — same place, but the door stays open the whole time. One point, two doors.

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Hams** (whispered) | jahr | Pure breath, no voice. Fingers on the throat: nothing. |
| **Rakhāwah** (flowing) | shiddah | Continuous. It can be held. |
| **Istifāl** (lowered) | istiʿlāʾ | Light. ه is never heavy, not even in لَهَبٍ next to heavy neighbours. |
| **Infitāḥ** (open) | iṭbāq | Nothing clamps. |
| **Iṣmāt** (prevented) | idhlāq | Not a "fluent" letter. |
| **Khafāʾ** (hiddenness) | (zuhūr, per some) | *The defining problem of this letter.* Its sound is faint and is easily lost — which is exactly why it must be given deliberate effort. Ibn al-Jazarī groups ه with the three madd letters ا و ي as the letters that disappear if you are not careful. |

## Common mistakes

**ح · خ · ه are the single most-cited error group for non-Arab learners** (course-research). For ه, the errors are not usually substitution *by* the learner's own sounds — every one of these languages has an `h` — but **deletion**.

- **Dropping ه entirely, especially at word-end and mid-word** (taxonomy §2.2). عَلَيْهِمْ becomes `ʿalayim`; لَهَبٍ becomes `laab`. Correction cue: *"Put your hand in front of your mouth. On every ه I must feel a puff on my palm. عَلَيْـ**هـ**ـِمْ — again, and let me feel it."*
- **Persian / Urdu L1** — in these languages word-final ه is often silent or a vowel marker (Persian خانه, Urdu کہ). The habit transfers directly and final ه goes silent in Arabic too. Correction cue: *"In Farsi this ه is a vowel. In the Qurʾān it is a consonant with breath. Say it, don't spell it."*
- **Turkish L1** — Turkish `h` is fine as a starting point, but Turkish also weakens `h` between vowels. Watch for ه vanishing in the middle of words.
- **Uzbek L1** — Uzbek ҳ maps well onto ه. The risk here is the reverse error: the learner uses ҳ for ح too. See [[Ha-throat]].
- **Making ه too shallow / too far forward** — a mouth-level `h`. Correction cue: *"Take it lower. Sigh from your chest, not from your cheeks."*
- **Substituting ح for ه when overcorrecting** — a learner drilled hard on ح starts squeezing every `h`. Correction cue: *"Relax the throat completely. ه is the easy one. Save the squeeze for ح."*
- **Ha' aḍ-ḍamīr treated as silent** — the pronoun ه at the end of words (ـهُ / ـهِ) is a real letter and often carries a two-count madd ṣilah. Dropping it changes grammar.

## Confused with

- **[[Ha-throat]] (ح)** — the priority pair. ه = breath, no squeeze; ح = breath, throat squeezed. Drill هَ / حَ with fingers on the throat.
- **[[Kha]] (خ)** — Turkish speakers merge all three; خ scrapes at the back of the mouth, ه does not scrape at all.
- **[[Hamza]] (ء)** — same makhraj, opposite door position. Beginners sometimes insert a glottal catch before ه.
- **Silence** — genuinely the most common "substitute" for ه.

## In tajweed

- **Izhār ḥalqī** — ه is one of the six throat letters; nūn sākinah and tanwīn before it are clear, with no ghunnah stretch.
- **Madd ṣilah ṣughrā / kubrā** — the pronoun ه between two voweled letters is stretched two counts (ṣughrā), or four–five counts when a hamzah follows (kubrā). This rule is impossible if the ه is being dropped, which is why the letter must be secured early.
- **Hams** — the breath must be completed, particularly at a waqf on a final ه.
- **Khafāʾ** — treat ه as a letter needing extra volume, not less.
- **No qalqalah, no tafkhīm** — ه is always light and never bounced.
