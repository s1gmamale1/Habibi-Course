# ح — ḥāʾ

## Makhraj

**Point 3 of 17** — *wasaṭ al-ḥalq* (وَسَطُ الْحَلْق), the **middle of the throat**. Zone 2, al-Ḥalq. It shares this exact point with [[Ayn]]; ع is its voiced twin.

Physically: the walls of the pharynx — the tube behind your tongue, level with roughly where your jaw hinges — squeeze inward toward each other. The tongue plays **no part**. It lies flat on the floor of the mouth and must not rise, arch, or pull back. The air is forced through the narrowed throat and comes out as a hot, dry rasp.

Teacher's script for finding it: *"Open your mouth wide, tongue flat, and breathe out as if you are fogging a cold window — but from much lower down. Now tighten the middle of your throat until the breath starts to scrape. Put two fingers flat across the front of your throat: you should feel the squeeze, but **no buzzing**. If you feel a buzz, you have made ع, not ح."*

Second cue, physical and reliable: *"Breathe on your glasses to clean them. Now do the same thing but narrow your throat so the breath hisses. That hiss is ح."*

The sound must be sustainable. You should be able to hold ح for three or four seconds without it turning into anything else — that is what *rakhāwah* means here.

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Hams** (whispered) | jahr | Breath flows freely all the way through. Say `ح` sākin against your palm — you must feel a steady stream of warm air. If your palm feels nothing, you voiced it. |
| **Rakhāwah** (flowing) | shiddah | The sound never stops or blocks. It can be stretched. If it starts and cuts off, you made a stop — probably [[Hamza]]. |
| **Istifāl** (lowered) | istiʿlāʾ | The back of the tongue stays **down**. ح is a light (muraqqaq) letter, even next to heavy letters. |
| **Infitāḥ** (open) | iṭbāq | Air escapes freely between tongue and palate; no clamping. |
| **Iṣmāt** (prevented) | idhlāq | Not one of the six "fluent" letters — it takes deliberate effort, which is exactly why learners avoid it. |

Note the combination **hams + rakhāwah + istifāl**: voiceless, continuous, light. Any one of those three failing produces a different letter.

## Common mistakes

**ح · خ · ه are the single most-cited error group for non-Arab learners** (course-research). None of these three exists as a distinct phoneme in English, Turkish, Uzbek, Persian, or Urdu, and the substitution pattern is predictable by first language:

- **Turkish L1** — Turkish has only one `h`. All three letters collapse into it, so ح, خ and ه come out identical. Correction cue: *"Turkish `h` is your ه. It is correct — for ه only. ح needs the throat squeezed shut around the breath; خ needs a scrape further up, at the back of the mouth. Three different places, three different letters."*
- **Uzbek L1** — Uzbek distinguishes ҳ [h] from х [x], so learners map **ح → ҳ** (producing ه) and **خ → х** (which is close enough to pass). The result: خ is usually fine, ح is almost always wrong. Correction cue: *"Your ҳ is too easy. Squeeze the middle of your throat until the breath rasps — you should feel your throat working."*
- **Persian / Urdu L1** — ح is written in these languages but pronounced [h], identical to ه. Learners read the letter correctly and pronounce it as ه without noticing. Correction cue: *"In Farsi/Urdu this letter is silent work. In the Qurʾān it is real work. Make me hear the difference between رَحِيم and رَهِيم."*
- **Overcorrecting into خ** — the learner knows ح is "harder than h", tries harder, and pulls the back of the tongue up. Now it is خ. Correction cue: *"Too high. Drop your tongue completely — lie it flat on the floor of your mouth — and keep the effort in your throat, not your mouth."*
- **Voicing it** — the throat squeeze arrives but the vocal folds are running, so ح becomes ع. Correction cue: *"Fingers on the throat. ع buzzes, ح does not. Say ع … ح … ع … ح and feel the buzz switch on and off."*

**Drill:** hold a single long `ححححح` for four seconds with fingers on the throat, then say ٱلرَّحِيمِ. The ح inside the word must have the same texture as the isolated one.

## Confused with

- **[[Kha]] (خ)** — the classic pair. خ is *higher* (back of the tongue against the soft palate area, near the mouth) and *scrapy*; ح is *lower* (pure throat) and *breathy*. Minimal pair to drill: حَلَقَ / خَلَقَ.
- **[[Ha-soft]] (ه)** — ه is the same breath with **no squeeze**, from deeper down. If your ح costs you no effort, it is a ه.
- **[[Ayn]] (ع)** — same makhraj, voiced. Losing hams turns ح into ع.
- English/Turkic **`h`** — the default substitute; too shallow, too weak.

## In tajweed

- **Izhār ḥalqī** — ح is one of the six throat letters (ء ه ع ح غ خ). A nūn sākinah or tanwīn before it is pronounced clearly, with no ghunnah stretch and no merging. Mnemonic line: أَخِي هَاكَ عِلْمًا حَازَهُ غَيْرُ خَاسِرِ.
- **Hams** — the breath must be released after a sākin ح, especially at a waqf. Do not swallow it.
- **Istifāl** — ح stays light next to isti'lāʾ letters. It never takes tafkhīm.
- **No qalqalah** — sākin ح is held and released as breath, never bounced.
