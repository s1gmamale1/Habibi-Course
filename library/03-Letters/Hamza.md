# ء — hamzah

## Makhraj

**Point 2 of 17** — *aqṣā al-ḥalq* (أَقْصَى الْحَلْق), the **deepest part of the throat**, nearest the chest. Zone 2, al-Ḥalq. Same point as [[Ha-soft]]; ء closes it, ه leaves it open.

Physically: the vocal folds slam **completely shut**, air pressure builds behind them, and they release. It is a full stop made at the very bottom of the throat. Nothing above the larynx participates.

Teacher's script: *"Say `uh-oh` and stop between the two halves. That catch in your throat is hamzah. Or: cough gently — the start of the cough is exactly this closure."*

**Counting note:** tajweed counts **29 ḥurūf al-hijāʾ** — the 28 letters plus [[Alif]] counted separately from hamzah. ا and ء are two different letters that happen to share a written shape in many positions. Phase 1 lesson `1-13` now teaches hamzah as a letter in its own right, alongside sukūn, rather than leaving it as an orthographic mark.

**Two hamzahs to distinguish for the student:**
- **Hamzat qaṭʿ (هَمْزَةُ الْقَطْع)** — a real letter, always pronounced: أَنْعَمْتَ, أَحَدٌ.
- **Hamzat waṣl (هَمْزَةُ الْوَصْل)** — a helper written ٱ, pronounced only when starting on it and **dropped** when joining from a previous word: ٱلْحَمْدُ read after a pause begins `al-`, but بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ joins straight through with no catch.

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Jahr** (voiced) | hams | No breath escapes — the folds are shut. Say `أْ` against your palm: you should feel *nothing*, then a small pop. |
| **Shiddah** (plosive) | rakhāwah | Complete blockage then release. One of the eight أَجِدْ قَطٍ بَكَتْ letters. It cannot be held or stretched. |
| **Istifāl** (lowered) | istiʿlāʾ | Light, always. |
| **Infitāḥ** (open) | iṭbāq | No clamping. |
| **Iṣmāt** (prevented) | idhlāq | Not a "fluent" letter. |

**Why there is no qalqalah on ء** even though it is shiddah + jahr: the classical five are ق ط ب ج د (قُطْبُ جَدٍّ). Hamzah is excluded — releasing it with an audible echo would generate a second hamzah. Say it cleanly and stop.

## Common mistakes

- **Produced too shallow, turning into a light [[Ayn]]** (taxonomy §2.2) — the closure happens higher in the pharynx instead of at the folds. Correction cue: *"Take it all the way down. Complete silence, then release. If any sound leaks through, you are making ع."*
- **The reverse and more common error: ع produced as ء** — see [[Ayn]]. Drill the pair أَ / عَ back to back.
- **Persian / Urdu L1** — medial and final hamzah are routinely dropped or smoothed into a vowel glide. Correction cue: *"There is a wall here. Stop, then start again."*
- **Turkic L1** — Turkish and Uzbek forbid word-initial consonant awkwardness by inserting vowels; learners may add a vowel before or after the hamzah, or run it into the previous word. Correction cue: *"Nothing before it. Just the stop, then the vowel."*
- **Confusing hamzat waṣl with hamzat qaṭʿ** — reading وَٱلْعَصْرِ as `wa-alʿaṣr` with an audible catch. Correction cue: *"The ٱ with the little head on it is a door you walk through, not a door you knock on. Slide straight in: `wal-ʿaṣr`."*
- **Over-forcing it into a glottal cough** at every occurrence, which makes recitation choppy. It is a clean stop, not a grunt.
- **Losing the hamzah at the end of a madd** — جَآءَ read as `jāā`. The hamzah is the reason for the four- to five-count madd; if it disappears, the madd has no cause.

## Confused with

- **[[Ayn]] (ع)** — the priority pair, in both directions. ء stops; ع strains and continues.
- **[[Ha-soft]] (ه)** — same makhraj, opposite gesture: ء shut, ه open. Learners sometimes soften ء into ه when tired.
- **[[Qaf]] (ق)** — for some learners a deep hamzah drifts forward into a ق. ق is made by the back of the tongue against the soft palate; ء involves no tongue at all.
- **Nothing** — dropping it, especially medially.

## In tajweed

- **Madd muttaṣil** — a madd letter followed by hamzah **in the same word**: 4–5 counts (جَآءَتِ, ٱلسَّمَآءِ).
- **Madd munfaṣil** — a madd letter at the end of a word followed by hamzah at the start of the next: 4–5 counts (يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا).
- **Madd badal** — hamzah *first*, then the madd letter: ءَامَنُوا۟ — two counts.
- **Hamzat waṣl** — the elision rule above; also determines whether a sūrah is begun with a full or reduced vowel.
- **Izhār ḥalqī** — ء is the first of the six throat letters; nūn sākinah and tanwīn before it are pronounced clearly.
- **Shiddah** — no leakage, no echo, no qalqalah.
- **Advanced (Ḥafṣ specifics)** — tas·hīl in ءَا۬عْجَمِىٌّ (41:44) and naql in بِئْسَ ٱلِٱسْمُ (49:11) are hamzah rules; flag them, teach them later.
