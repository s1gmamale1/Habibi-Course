# ك — kāf

## Makhraj

The **2nd of the ten tongue makharij**. The back of the tongue rises to the palate at a point **just forward of [[Qaf]]** — where the roof of the mouth is still bone, not yet soft. The closure is complete, then released with an audible escape of breath.

The distance between ق and ك is about the width of a fingertip. There is no third thing distinguishing them at the makhraj: **ق is further back and against soft tissue; ك is further forward and against hard tissue.** Have the student say قَ كَ قَ كَ slowly and feel the contact point walk forward and back.

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **hams** (whispered) | jahr | Breath keeps flowing on release. Hold your palm in front of your mouth on a sakin ك — you must feel a puff. ك is in the mnemonic فَحَثَّهُ شَخْصٌ سَكَتْ. |
| **shiddah** (plosive) | rakhawah | Full stop, then burst. |
| **istifal** (lowered) | isti'la | **ك is a light letter.** The tongue body stays down. This is the single fact students most often get wrong. |
| **infitah** (open) | itbaq | No clamping. |
| **ismat** | idhlaq | Not one of the six fluent letters. |

**ك has hams but no qalqalah.** This is the crucial pairing: a sakin ك releases with *breath*, a sakin ق releases with a *bounce*. أَلَمْ نَشْرَحْ … the sakin ك of وَٱذْكُر is breathed out, never bounced.

## Common mistakes

- **Making it heavy.** Because ك sits next to ق in the mouth, students carry ق's weight forward onto it — especially next to a heavy letter or an alif, as in كَانَ. ك must stay **light in every position**, including in ٱللَّهُ أَكْبَر.
- **Swallowing the hams at word-end.** English, Uzbek, and Turkish speakers cut the final stop dead. Sakin ك needs its escaping breath: مُلْكْ ends in a puff, not silence.
- **Adding a qalqalah bounce**, by analogy with ق. ك is not in قُطْبُ جَدٍّ.
- **Aspirating it into a Persian/Urdu "kh"-ish release**, or fronting it toward "ky" before a kasrah (Urdu speakers commonly palatalise before front vowels): كِتَاب must not become "kyitāb".

**Spoken correction cue:** *"Lighter, and bring it forward. Now breathe out after it — I should hear the air, not a bounce."*

## Confused with

- [[Qaf]] — the meaning-bearing contrast. قَالَ ("he said") vs كَالَ ("he measured"). If your ق sounds like a heavy ك, you are reading a different word.
- [[Kha]] — when the closure is not complete, ك leaks into a throat fricative.

## In tajweed

- **Hams**, and therefore full audible release on sukun.
- **Istifal / tarqeeq** — ك is always light; a ر after it follows the ordinary ر rules with no tafkheem carried over.
- **Ikhfa'.** ك is one of the fifteen ikhfa' letters: مَن كَانَ hides the noon with a *light* ghunnah, because ك is a light letter.
- ك is not a qalqalah letter and not an idgham letter.
- Sakin ك followed by ك (as across word boundaries) is a case of idgham mutamathilayn — two identical letters merging.
