# خ — khāʾ

## Makhraj

**Point 4 of 17** — *adnā al-ḥalq* (أَدْنَى الْحَلْق), the **nearest part of the throat**, the part closest to the mouth. Zone 2, al-Ḥalq. It shares this point with [[Ghayn]]; غ is its voiced twin.

Physically: the very back of the tongue rises toward the uvula and the soft palate behind it, leaving a narrow channel. Breath is pushed through and scrapes. This is the highest and most forward of the three throat points — it is the throat letter that is nearly a mouth letter.

Teacher's script: *"Pretend you are about to gargle water, but with no water and no voice. That dry scrape at the very back of your mouth is خ. Now feel where it happens — it is far higher than [[Ha-throat]]. If you point at your Adam's apple you are in the wrong place; point at the back of the roof of your mouth."*

Because خ is an **istiʿlāʾ** letter, the back of the tongue is raised on every خ, in every word, with every vowel. The mouth cavity behind the letter is full and round.

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Hams** (whispered) | jahr | Voiceless scrape. Fingers on the throat: no buzz. |
| **Rakhāwah** (flowing) | shiddah | Continuous — you can hold `خخخخ` for several seconds. |
| **Istiʿlāʾ** (elevation) | istifāl | The back of the tongue lifts; the sound is **always heavy (mufakhkham)**, even with kasrah. |
| **Infitāḥ** (open) | iṭbāq | Heavy, but with no clamping seal — lighter than ص ض ط ظ. It is the *weakest* of the seven heavy letters. |
| **Iṣmāt** (prevented) | idhlāq | Not a "fluent" letter. |

**Teaching point:** خ is heavy but *not* iṭbāq. Compare خُ with صُ — both heavy, but ص has the tongue clamped flat against the palate and feels much thicker. Do not let خ swell to ص's weight.

## Common mistakes

**ح · خ · ه are the single most-cited error group for non-Arab learners** (course-research); خ is the one most learners already have, which creates its own problem — it becomes the dumping ground for the other two.

- **ح pronounced as خ** — the most damaging direction. The learner knows ح is "a strong h", pushes harder, and lifts the tongue. Correction cue: *"You have moved into your mouth. Drop the back of your tongue flat and take the effort down into your throat. خ scrapes; ح only squeezes."* Minimal pair: خَلَقَ / حَلَقَ — "He created" vs "He shaved."
- **Producing خ from the tongue's back only, without throat involvement** — a purely velar [x], too far forward and thin (taxonomy §2.4). Correction cue: *"Move it back one step, toward the uvula — the little flap at the back. It should feel like the start of a gargle, not like a Scottish 'loch'."*
- **Voicing it into غ** — Uzbek and Persian speakers who have both х and ғ/غ sometimes let the voice bleed in. Correction cue: *"Fingers on the throat — خ is silent machinery, غ has the motor running."*
- **Losing the heaviness next to a kasrah** — خِ still has a raised tongue back. Correction cue: *"Keep the back of your tongue up even when the vowel pulls it down. Fill your mouth."*
- **Turkish L1** — no [x] at all; خ, ح and ه all surface as `h`. This learner needs خ built from scratch, and gargling is the fastest route in.
- **Uzbek / Persian / Urdu L1** — these have [x] natively (Uzbek х, Persian/Urdu خ) and produce خ well. Praise it, then use it as the reference point: *"That خ is correct. Now make ح — same air, but from much deeper, with a flat tongue."*

## Confused with

- **[[Ha-throat]] (ح)** — the priority pair. Drill حَ / خَ / حَ / خَ with a hand on the jaw: خ raises the tongue, ح does not.
- **[[Ghayn]] (غ)** — identical makhraj, differing only in voice. See [[Ghayn]] for the drill.
- **[[Ha-soft]] (ه)** — for Turkish speakers all three merge; ه is pure breath from the deepest throat with no scrape at all.
- **[[Qaf]] (ق) and [[Kaf]] (ك)** — learners sometimes stop the airflow and turn خ into a plosive. خ never stops; it flows.

## In tajweed

- **Izhār ḥalqī** — خ is one of the six throat letters; nūn sākinah and tanwīn before it are pronounced clearly.
- **Tafkhīm** — خ is one of the seven istiʿlāʾ letters (خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ) and is **always** heavy. Its degree of heaviness follows the vowel: fathah heaviest, then dammah, then kasrah.
- **Rāʾ rules** — a خ in the neighbourhood of a ر is one of the istiʿlāʾ conditions that can keep a sākin rāʾ heavy after a kasrah.
- **Lām of lafẓ al-jalālah** is unaffected by خ; do not let خ's weight spill onto neighbouring light letters such as ل and م.
- **No qalqalah** — sākin خ flows out as breath.
