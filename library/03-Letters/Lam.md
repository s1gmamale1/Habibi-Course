# ل — lām

## Makhraj

The **5th of the ten tongue makharij** — *adna hafat al-lisan*, the edge of the tongue **nearest the tip**. The tongue's edge, running from its rearmost side all the way forward to the tip, meets the **gums of the upper front teeth** (the flesh above the incisors, not the teeth themselves).

The contact is broad and shallow: the tip and the near edges touch the gum, while the sides stay open so the sound can slip out around them. That side-escape is the sifah of **inhiraf** — the sound *deviates* from its makhraj rather than exiting straight ahead.

Cue: *"Tip of the tongue up on the gum ridge, just behind your top front teeth — not on the teeth. Let the sound run out around the sides of your tongue."*

[[Lam]] sits **above** [[Noon]] in the mouth: ن is made a little lower on the same gum ridge, and [[Ra]] a little deeper still. These three neighbours are the reason al-Farra' counted them as one makhraj — teach them as a family.

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **jahr** (voiced) | hams | Fully voiced, no breath. |
| **tawassut** (in between) | shiddah / rakhawah | Neither fully stopped nor fully flowing. ل is one of the five letters of **لِنْ عُمَرْ** (ل ن ع م ر). |
| **istifal** (lowered) | isti'la | Light — with one famous exception, below. |
| **infitah** (open) | itbaq | No clamping. |
| **idhlaq** (fluency) | ismat | ل comes off the tongue easily; it is one of the six letters of **فِرَّ مِنْ لُبِّ**. |
| **inhiraf** (deviation) | — | Shared only with [[Ra]]. The sound turns aside and exits around the tongue rather than over it. |

## Common mistakes

- **A dark "l" everywhere.** English speakers — and anyone schooled in English — carry the "velarised l" of *ball*, *milk*, *full* into Arabic. Russian-influenced Uzbek speakers have the same hard *л*. Arabic ل is **light by default**: قُلْ, ٱلْحَمْدُ, لَهَبٍ all take a thin, forward, bright ل.
- **The mirror error: failing to darken it where it must be dark.** The ل of **lafẓ al-Jalālah** (ٱللَّه) is heavy after a **fathah** or a **dammah** — قَالَ ٱللَّهُ, نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ — and light after a **kasrah** — بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ, لِلَّهِ. This is the one ل rule every student must have automatic.
- **Touching the teeth instead of the gum**, producing a dental ل.
- **Dropping the ل of ٱل** without applying the shamsiyyah/qamariyyah rule properly, or pronouncing the silent ل of a shamsi word (ٱلشَّمْس read as "al-shams").
- **Persian and Urdu speakers** generally have a clean light ل and struggle mainly with the lafẓ al-Jalālah rule rather than the sound itself.

**Spoken correction cue:** *"Brighter, more forward — tip on the gum, not the teeth. Now say bismi-llāhi: thin. Now qāla-llāhu: fat. Hear the difference?"*

## Confused with

- [[Noon]] — the neighbouring makhraj, a little lower on the same ridge. A ل whose tip slides down and whose nose opens becomes ن.
- [[Ra]] — the other inhiraf letter, deeper on the ridge. ل contacts and holds; ر contacts once and flicks.

## In tajweed

- **[[Lam-Jalalah|Tafkheem of lafẓ al-Jalālah]]** — heavy after fathah/dammah, light after kasrah. A rule of its own.
- **[[Lam-Shamsiyyah|Idgham shamsiyyah]]** — the ل of ٱل is silent and merged into the following letter for the fourteen "sun letters" (ٱلشَّمْس, ٱلرَّحْمَٰن, ٱلدِّين). For the fourteen "[[Lam-Qamariyyah|moon letters]]" it is pronounced with sukun (ٱلْحَمْد, ٱلْقَمَر).
- **[[Idgham-Mutaqaribayn|Idgham mutaqaribayn]]** — a sakin ل merges into a following ر: قُل رَّبِّ.
- **Idgham for noon sakinah.** ل is one of the letters of **يَرْمَلُونَ** — noon sakinah or tanween before ل merges **[[Idgham-Bila-Ghunnah|without ghunnah]]**, completely: مِن لَّدُنْهُ.
- **[[Tawassut|Tawassut]]** — a sakin ل is held, not bounced; ل is not a [[Qalqalah-sifah|qalqalah]] letter.
