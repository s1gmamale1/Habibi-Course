# ق — qāf

## Makhraj

The **1st of the ten tongue makharij**, and the deepest point in the whole mouth. The very back of the tongue — the part you cannot see in a mirror — humps up and slams against the **soft palate**, the fleshy part of the roof of the mouth right where it stops being bone.

Tell the student: *"Put your finger flat on the roof of your mouth and slide it backwards until it goes soft. That is where ق lives. Now push the back of your tongue up to meet it and let go."*

The contact is complete — no air passes at all — and then it is released sharply.

**ق and [[Kaf]] differ only in how far back the contact is made.** They are neighbours: ق is at the very rear against the *soft* palate; ك is a fingertip's width forward, against the *hard* palate. Nothing else separates their makhraj. Everything else that separates them is sifat.

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **jahr** (voiced) | hams | The vocal cords are running. Put two fingers on your throat — you must feel a buzz on the release. |
| **shiddah** (plosive) | rakhawah | The sound is completely stopped, then burst. You cannot hold a ق the way you can hold a س. |
| **isti'la** (elevated) | istifal | The tongue body is already up — that is the makhraj itself. ق is heavy in every position, with every vowel. |
| **infitah** (open) | itbaq | The tongue is *not* clamped to the roof of the mouth along its length. So ق is heavy, but not as heavy as ص ض ط ظ. |
| **ismat** | idhlaq | Slow, effortful, heavy-bodied. |
| **qalqalah** | — | Because it is both shiddah *and* jahr, a sakin ق cannot escape without a small bounce. |

A note for the teacher: some classical works count ق among the letters of **shiddah + jahr** and this is what makes it a qalqalah letter. Later phoneticians argue modern Arabic ق is voiceless. Teach the transmitted position — jahr — and correct by ear: the release must have body, not just a puff.

## Common mistakes

- **Flattening ق into ك.** By far the most common. The student pulls the contact forward off the soft palate onto the hard palate and produces a heavy-ish ك. **This changes meaning:** قَالَ ("he said") becomes كَالَ ("he measured"). Say both to the student back to back and let them hear the collapse.
- **Turning it into a hard "g"** (Egyptian and Gulf colloquial), or into the Persian/Urdu **غ**-like uvular. Urdu and Persian speakers routinely read ق as غ or as a plain "k" because their spoken registers have merged them.
- **Turkic and Uzbek speakers** have a native back "q" (as in Uzbek *qish*) — this is a real advantage. Point at it: *"You already say this letter every day. Use your own q, then make it heavier."*
- **Dropping the qalqalah** on a sakin ق — reading يَقْطَعُونَ with a silent, swallowed ق.
- **Over-doing the qalqalah** into a full vowel: "qu-taluu" instead of a clean bounce.

**Spoken correction cue:** *"Further back. Not with the front of your tongue — with the root of it. Now let it go like a cork."*

## Confused with

- [[Kaf]] — the meaning-changing pair. ق back and heavy, ك forward and light.
- [[Ghayn]] and [[Kha]] — both are throat letters (adna al-halq), not tongue letters, and both are fricatives with no complete closure. Persian and Urdu speakers most often substitute these for ق.

## In tajweed

- **[[Qalqalah-sifah|Qalqalah]].** ق is the first letter of the mnemonic **قُطْبُ جَدٍّ** (ق ط ب ج د). Sakin ق bounces — mid-word ([[Qalqalah-Sughra|qalqalah sughra]]) and at a stop ([[Qalqalah-Kubra|qalqalah kubra]]).
- **[[Istila|Isti'la]] / [[Tafkhim|tafkheem]].** ق is one of the seven isti'la letters (خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ) and is therefore **always heavy**, even under kasrah.
- **[[Ra-Tafkhim|Tafkheem]] of a neighbouring ر.** A ر with kasrah after a sakin isti'la letter such as ق is the case behind فِرْقٍ.
- **[[Ikhfa-Haqiqi|Ikhfa']].** ق is one of the fifteen ikhfa' letters for noon sakinah and tanween — مِن قَبْلُ is hidden with ghunnah, and because ق is heavy the ghunnah is made heavy with it.
- ق is not an idgham letter and never merges into what follows.
