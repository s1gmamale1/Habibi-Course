# ر — rā'

> **ر is the busiest letter in tajweed.** It is the only letter whose heaviness changes from word to word, and its tafkheem/tarqeeq rules take **two full lessons** later in the course. This note covers the sound; the weight rules get their own treatment.

## Makhraj

The **7th of the ten tongue makharij**. The **upper surface of the tongue tip** — the back of the tip, not the very point — meets the **gums of the upper incisors**, at a spot **slightly deeper into the mouth than [[Noon]]**.

Order on the gum ridge, front to back: [[Lam]] highest and broadest, [[Noon]] just below it, **ر deeper still**.

The tongue tip makes **one contact** and releases. One.

Cue: *"Just behind where you make your ن. Tap it once — one flick, then stop."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **jahr** (voiced) | hams | Voiced. |
| **tawassut** (in between) | shiddah / rakhawah | Not stopped, not flowing — one of **لِنْ عُمَرْ**. |
| **istifal** (lowered) | isti'la | Light *by classification* — but ر has its own tafkheem rules and is heavy in many positions. This is why it is the exception every list mentions. |
| **infitah** (open) | itbaq | No clamping. |
| **idhlaq** (fluency) | ismat | One of **فِرَّ مِنْ لُبِّ** — and the ف and ر of that mnemonic are ر's own neighbours. |
| **inhiraf** (deviation) | — | Shared with [[Lam]]. The sound turns aside from the makhraj. |
| **takrir** (repetition) | — | **ر only.** And it exists to be *avoided*. |

**On takrir — read this twice.** Takrir is the tongue tip's natural tendency to vibrate repeatedly. Ibn al-Jazari's instruction is **وَحَرِّرِ التَّكْرِيرَ** — *restrain the takrir*. It is listed as a sifah so the reciter knows what to suppress. A correct ر strikes **once**. A trilled ر is a mistake, not a feature.

## Common mistakes

- **Trilling / rolling the ر.** The single biggest ر error, and it is a first-language problem: **Persian, Urdu, Uzbek, Turkish, and Russian** all have trilled or tapped r's, and Persian and Urdu speakers in particular roll freely. The student is not being dramatic — they are speaking their own language's r. Under a shaddah (ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ) the trill becomes very audible.
- **An English/American "r"** — no tongue contact at all, tongue bunched back. Anyone schooled in English does this.
- **Getting the weight wrong.** Heavy where it should be light, or flattening it under a kasrah. Learners overwhelmingly default to heavy everywhere because ر *sounds* heavy in ٱلرَّحْمَٰن.

**Spoken correction cue:** *"One tap. Not three. Bite the sound off — ra, not rrra."* For a student who cannot stop trilling, have them say a fast Arabic د first, then move the tongue back a few millimetres; the single-contact habit transfers.

## Confused with

- [[Lam]] — the neighbouring inhiraf letter. Both deviate, but ل holds contact and ر flicks.
- [[Noon]] — the neighbouring makhraj, slightly forward and with ghunnah.
- [[Ghayn]] — for students who substitute a French/uvular r, ر drifts back into the throat and lands there.

## In tajweed

- **Tafkheem and tarqeeq of ر** — the letter's own rule set, and the reason it gets two lessons:
  - **Heavy** with fathah or dammah (رَبِّ, رُسُل); sakin after fathah or dammah (ٱلْأَرْض, قُرْآن); sakin after an incidental kasrah (ٱرْجِعِي); sakin after a kasrah when followed in the same word by an isti'la letter (فِرْقٍ).
  - **Light** with kasrah (بِرَبِّ, رِجَال); sakin after an original kasrah with no isti'la letter after it (فِرْعَوْن); sakin after a ي sakinah (خَيْرْ at a stop).
  - **Either permitted** in a small set of cases, the classic being فِرْقٍ.
- **Idgham without ghunnah.** ر is one of the letters of **يَرْمَلُونَ**: noon sakinah or tanween before ر merges **completely and without ghunnah** — مِن رَّبِّهِمْ, غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ.
- **Idgham mutaqaribayn** — sakin ل merges into ر: قُل رَّبِّ.
- **Takrir suppression** — a graded item in its own right; a trilled ر is marked wrong even when everything else is correct.
- ر is not a qalqalah letter.
