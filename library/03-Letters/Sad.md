# ص — ṣād

## Makhraj

The **9th of the ten tongue makharij** — *al-huruf al-asaliyyah*, the whistling group. The **tip of the tongue** sits between the upper and lower incisors, **closer to the lower ones**, leaving a narrow groove down the middle of the tongue. Air is forced through that groove and whistles.

ص shares this exact point with [[Seen]] and [[Zay]]. All three have **safir** (whistling) and **rakhawah** (flow). They differ only here:

| | voice | weight |
|---|---|---|
| **ص** | **hams** (breathed) | **heavy** — isti'la + itbaq |
| **س** | hams (breathed) | light |
| **ز** | **jahr** (voiced) | light |

So: **ص is س made heavy. That is the whole difference.**

Cue: *"Start on a س. Keep the tip exactly where it is — don't move it. Now lift the back of your tongue up and clamp it against the roof of your mouth. Same whistle, but now the sound has a room behind it."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **hams** (whispered) | jahr | No voice at all — pure breath. Fingers on the throat: **no buzz**. |
| **rakhawah** (flowing) | shiddah | You can hold it as long as your breath lasts. |
| **isti'la** (elevated) | istifal | Heavy in every position, including under kasrah. |
| **itbaq** (clamped) | infitah | Tongue body seals against the palate. One of four itbaq letters (ص ض ط ظ). |
| **ismat** | idhlaq | Heavy and slow. |
| **safir** (whistling) | — | Shared with س and ز only. A sharp, high whistle down the tongue's groove. |

## Common mistakes

- **ص → س.** The commonest error, and the diagnostic is easy: the tip is already in the right place, so the *whistle* sounds fine and the student thinks they have it. What is missing is the **itbaq** — the back of the tongue never rises. Persian, Urdu, Turkish and Uzbek speakers all merge ص into س in their own languages (Persian *sabr* for صَبْر), so this is not carelessness; the contrast does not exist in their ears yet.
- **"Half-heavy" ص** — isti'la without itbaq. Sounds vaguely dark but hollow.
- **Flattening it under a kasrah.** ٱلصِّرَٰطَ is the standard test: that ص must stay fully heavy despite the kasrah. If the student's صِ sounds like سِ, start again.
- **Clipping it.** ص is rakhawah — it must be *held*, especially under shaddah.
- **Over-whistling** into a hiss.

**Spoken correction cue:** *"Same tongue tip, but lift the back. Say sīn… now ṣād. Feel the roof of your mouth close down. As-ṣirāṭ — fat, not thin."*

## Confused with

- [[Seen]] — the meaning-bearing pair and the default collapse. Identical tip position; ص has isti'la and itbaq, س has neither.
- [[Za-heavy]] — both heavy and flowing; ظ is **voiced** and made on the *edges of the upper teeth*, ص is **breathed** and made low near the bottom teeth.
- [[Ta-heavy]] — the other very heavy letter students confuse when over-darkening; ط stops the air, ص lets it flow.
- [[Zay]] — a voiced ص is a heavy ز, not a ص.

## In tajweed

- **[[Istila|Isti'la]] and [[Itbaq|itbaq]]** — always **mufakhkham**, at the strongest degree.
- **[[Ra-Tafkhim|Tafkheem]] of a neighbouring ر** — a sakin ص before ر creates the isti'la environment.
- **[[Ikhfa-Haqiqi|Ikhfa']].** ص is one of the fifteen ikhfa' letters: noon sakinah before ص is hidden with a **heavy** ghunnah, as in أَنصَارِي.
- **[[Idgham-Mutajanisayn|Idgham mutajanisayn]] is not applicable**, but a sakin ص before a ز gives the recognised case of **ishmam/ishmam-like** variation in a few readings — teach the Hafs default: read it as written.
- **Isti'la and [[Hams|hams]] together** make ص one of only two letters that are both heavy and breathed (with خ).
- ص is not a [[Qalqalah-sifah|qalqalah]] letter — [[Rakhawah|rakhawah]] rules it out.
