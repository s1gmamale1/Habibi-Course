# س — sīn

## Makhraj

The **9th of the ten tongue makharij** — the *asaliyyah* (whistling) group. The **tip of the tongue** between the upper and lower incisors, **closer to the lower ones**, with a narrow groove running down the middle of the tongue. The breath is squeezed through that groove and comes out as a thin, high whistle.

Shared with [[Sad]] and [[Zay]]. س is the **light, breathed** member: **ص is س with the back of the tongue raised and clamped; ز is س with the voice switched on.**

Cue: *"Tip of the tongue down near your bottom teeth, tongue flat and grooved, and blow a thin stream straight down the middle."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **hams** (whispered) | jahr | Pure breath, no voice. |
| **rakhawah** (flowing) | shiddah | Holdable for as long as the breath lasts. |
| **istifal** (lowered) | isti'la | **Light.** Even beside ط or ص. |
| **infitah** (open) | itbaq | No clamping. |
| **ismat** | idhlaq | Not a fluent letter. |
| **safir** (whistling) | — | The narrowest, highest whistle of the three. |

Contrast with [[Sheen]]: س has **safir**, a thin whistle down a narrow groove; ش has **tafashshi**, a broad fan spreading across the mouth. Same breath, opposite shape.

## Common mistakes

- **Darkening it toward ص** next to a heavy letter or a back vowel. س is light in every position — ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ stays thin throughout.
- **Voicing it into ز**, particularly between vowels, where English and Turkish habits turn "s" into "z". مُوسَىٰ must not become "Muza".
- **The reverse error: reading ز as س.** Devoicing is the far bigger problem — see [[Zay]].
- **Substituting س for ث.** This is the **ث → س** collapse, and it is close to universal outside Arabic: Persian, Urdu, Turkish and Uzbek all render Arabic ث as a plain "s" (Persian *sābit* for ثَابِت). The student hears no difference at all until you make them look at where the tongue is. See [[Tha]].
- **A lisped, interdental س** — the tongue tip pushed forward between the teeth, which is [[Tha]], not س.

**Spoken correction cue:** *"Thin, forward, no voice — and keep your tongue behind your teeth, not between them."*

## Confused with

- [[Sad]] — the heavy counterpart. Same tip, plus isti'la and itbaq.
- [[Zay]] — the voiced counterpart. Same tip, plus jahr.
- [[Tha]] — the direction students collapse *into* س. ث is made on the **edges** of the upper teeth with the tongue tip visible; س is made behind the teeth, tongue hidden.
- [[Sheen]] — narrow whistle vs broad spread.

## In tajweed

- **Ikhfa'.** س is one of the fifteen ikhfa' letters: noon sakinah before س is hidden with a **light** ghunnah, as in إِنسَان.
- **Istifal** — س never makes a following ر heavy; the ر of ٱلْمُسْتَقِيم-type words follows its own rules.
- **Idgham mutamathilayn** — sakin س into س.
- **Waqf and hams** — a sakin س at a stop must keep its full flowing whistle, not be cut short.
- **The four س / ص words in Hafs.** Four places are written with a ص carrying a small س (or the reverse) to mark that the root is a س root: يَبْصُۜطُ (2:245), بَصْۜطَةً (7:69), ٱلْمُصَۣيْطِرُونَ (52:37), بِمُصَيْطِرٍ (88:22). Hafs reads the first three with **ص**; the fourth admits both. Compare بَسْطَةً at 2:247, written plainly with س. These are transmission details — flag them, and handle them when the student reaches those ayat, not in the first pass.
- س is not a qalqalah letter.
