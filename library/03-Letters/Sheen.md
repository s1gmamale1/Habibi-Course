# ش — shīn

## Makhraj

The **3rd tongue makhraj** — **wasat al-lisan**, the same broad middle-of-the-tongue-against-the-palate point as [[Jeem]] and [[Ya]] (the شَجْرِيَّة group). But where ج closes completely, ش leaves a **wide, shallow channel**: the tongue comes close to the palate without touching, and the breath pours through and spreads.

That spreading is the letter's signature sifah, **tafashshi**.

Cue: *"Same place as ج — middle of the tongue. But don't touch. Leave a gap the width of a sheet of paper and blow through it, and let the air fan out sideways across the whole mouth."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **hams** (whispered) | jahr | Pure breath, no voice. Fingers on the throat: no buzz at all. |
| **rakhawah** (flowing) | shiddah | You can hold ش for as long as you have air. |
| **istifal** (lowered) | isti'la | Light. |
| **infitah** (open) | itbaq | No clamping. |
| **ismat** | idhlaq | Not a fluent letter. |
| **tafashshi** (spreading) | — | **ش is the only letter with tafashshi.** The breath spreads broadly inside the mouth rather than shooting down a narrow channel. This is what makes Arabic ش fuller and airier than English "sh". |

Contrast with [[Seen]]: س has **safir**, a thin whistle down a narrow groove. ش has **tafashshi**, a broad fan. Same family of sound, opposite geometry.

## Common mistakes

- **A narrow, tight English "sh"**, or the Russian-influenced *ш* many Uzbek and Central Asian students carry. It is produced too far forward, with the tongue tip, and with the lips rounded. Arabic ش is made with the *middle* of the tongue, lips neutral, and the air spread wide.
- **Rounding the lips.** English "sh" is lip-rounded; Arabic ش is not. Have the student hold a finger flat against their lips.
- **Shortening it.** ش is rakhawah — under shaddah, as in وَٱلشَّمْسِ, it must be held, not clipped.
- **Persian and Urdu speakers** generally have a solid ش and rarely struggle with it — this is one to praise and use as an anchor when teaching the harder letters.

**Spoken correction cue:** *"Pull it back to the middle of your tongue, unround your lips, and let the air spread out wide — like you're hushing a whole room, not one person."*

## Confused with

- [[Jeem]] — same makhraj, opposite flow.
- [[Seen]] — the other broad-breath sibilant. س whistles narrow; ش spreads wide.
- [[Sad]] — students who over-darken ش drift toward ص. ش is a **light** letter with no isti'la.

## In tajweed

- **Tafashshi** — the sifah exists to be *made*, not avoided, unlike takrir.
- **Idgham with ghunnah? No.** ش is not in يَرْمَلُونَ.
- **Ikhfa'.** ش is one of the fifteen ikhfa' letters: مِن شَرِّ (113:2) hides the noon with a light ghunnah — and it is a textbook ikhfa' example precisely because it appears three times in Surah al-Falaq.
- **Idgham mutajanisayn / mutaqaribayn** — a sakin ذ or ل before ش does not merge; but sakin ش before ش is idgham mutamathilayn.
- **Istifal** — ش never makes a following ر heavy.
