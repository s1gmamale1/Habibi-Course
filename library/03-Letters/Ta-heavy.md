# ط — ṭā'

## Makhraj

The **8th of the ten tongue makharij** — *al-huruf an-nit'iyyah*. The **tip of the tongue** presses against the **roots** (usul) of the two upper front incisors: the point where the teeth go into the gum, not the flat face of the teeth and not the biting edge.

ط shares this exact point with [[Dal]] and [[Ta]]. **All three are made in the same place.** They are told apart *only* by sifat — and this is the classic demonstration of why sifat exist:

| | makhraj | voice | weight | release |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ط** | roots of upper incisors | **jahr** (voiced) | **heavy** (isti'la + itbaq) | **qalqalah** — bounces |
| **د** | same | jahr (voiced) | light | qalqalah — bounces |
| **ت** | same | **hams** (breathed) | light | breath, no bounce |

Cue: *"Tip of the tongue up where the top teeth go into the gum. Now make the whole back of your tongue rise and clamp against the roof of your mouth at the same time. That clamp is what makes it ط and not د."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **jahr** (voiced) | hams | **Voiced.** No breath escapes. This is the sifah students lose first. |
| **shiddah** (plosive) | rakhawah | Complete stop, then burst. |
| **isti'la** (elevated) | istifal | Heavy in every position. |
| **itbaq** (clamped) | infitah | The tongue seals against the palate. One of only four itbaq letters (ص ض ط ظ) — and ط is usually called the **strongest letter in Arabic**, because it carries shiddah, jahr, isti'la, itbaq and qalqalah all at once. |
| **ismat** | idhlaq | Heavy and slow. |
| **qalqalah** | — | shiddah + jahr ⇒ the sakin ط must bounce out. |

## Common mistakes

- **ط → a heavy ت.** The single most common error. The student makes an emphatic-sounding "t" but **devoices it**, so jahr is gone — and with jahr gone, qalqalah is impossible too. Two sifat lost in one stroke. Persian, Urdu, Turkish and Uzbek speakers all do this by default, because in all four languages ط and ت are simply the same sound.
- **Dropping the qalqalah** on sakin ط: أَطْعَمَ read with the ط leaned on and swallowed instead of released with a bounce.
- **Making it heavy but not clamped** — isti'la without itbaq, giving a "half-heavy" letter.
- **Losing the heaviness before a kasrah**, as in طِبْت.

**Spoken correction cue:** *"Voice it — put your hand on your throat, I want to feel a buzz. And when it's sukun, let it bounce out: aṭ-ṭ, not at-t."*

## ط bounces, [[Za-heavy]] is held

This contrast is taught explicitly in lesson **1-14** and is worth drilling on its own:

- **ط** is **shiddah** — the air is completely stopped, so a sakin ط has nowhere to go but out, in a bounce. **قَطْ** ends with a kick.
- **ظ** is **rakhawah** — the air keeps flowing, so a sakin ظ is simply **held** and faded. **حَفِظْ** ends with a sustained hiss.

Both are heavy, both are voiced, both are itbaq. The difference the student hears is: **one pops, the other lasts.**

## Confused with

- [[Ta]] — same makhraj, and the direction the letter collapses in for almost every non-Arab student. ط is voiced, heavy and bouncing; ت is breathed, light and puffed.
- [[Dal]] — same makhraj, same voicing, same qalqalah; د is simply **light**. A ط with no isti'la is a د.
- [[Za-heavy]] — the bounce/hold pair, and the other voiced heavy letter.
- [[Sad]] — the other heavy letter that students flatten; ص is breathed and flowing, ط is voiced and stopped.

## In tajweed

- **[[Qalqalah-sifah|Qalqalah]].** ط is the ط of **قُطْبُ جَدٍّ** — [[Qalqalah-Sughra|sughra]] mid-word (أَطْعَمَ), [[Qalqalah-Kubra|kubra]] at a stop.
- **[[Istila|Isti'la]] and [[Itbaq|itbaq]]** — always **mufakhkham**, at the strongest degree.
- **[[Ra-Tafkhim|Tafkheem]] of a neighbouring ر** — a sakin ط before ر creates the isti'la environment.
- **[[Ikhfa-Haqiqi|Ikhfa']].** ط is one of the fifteen ikhfa' letters: noon sakinah before ط is hidden with a **heavy** ghunnah, as in يَنطِقُونَ.
- **[[Idgham-Mutajanisayn|Idgham mutajanisayn]]** — a **sakin ت merges into a following ط**: أَثْقَلَت دَّعَوَا-type cases, and canonically وَقَالَت طَّآئِفَةٌ. In the reverse direction — sakin **ط before ت** — the merge is **incomplete** (idgham naqis): the itbaq of the ط is retained even though the letter merges, as in أَحَطتُ and فَرَّطتُمْ. Do not let the student flatten the ط there.
