# ت — tā'

## Makhraj

The **8th of the ten tongue makharij** — the *nit'iyyah* group. **Tip of the tongue against the roots of the two upper incisors**, at the gum line.

Same point as [[Ta-heavy]] and [[Dal]]. ت is the **breathed, light** member:

- **ت** — **hams** (breath escapes), light, no bounce.
- **د** — voiced, light, bounces.
- **ط** — voiced, heavy, bounces.

Cue: *"Tip on the gum where the top teeth start. Now let the air out after it — I should feel the puff on my hand."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **hams** (whispered) | jahr | **Breath flows out with the letter.** ت is the last letter of the mnemonic فَحَثَّهُ شَخْصٌ سَكَتْ. Hold your palm in front of your mouth on a sakin ت — you must feel air. |
| **shiddah** (plosive) | rakhawah | Complete stop, then release. |
| **istifal** (lowered) | isti'la | Light. |
| **infitah** (open) | itbaq | No clamping. |
| **ismat** | idhlaq | Not a fluent letter. |

**hams + shiddah, with no jahr, is why ت has no qalqalah.** A sakin ت releases into *breath*, never into a bounce. This is the most-tested contrast in the whole nit'iyyah trio.

## Common mistakes

- **Swallowing the final ت.** تَبَّتْ, كَانَتْ, قَالَتْ read with the tongue clamped and the air trapped. English, Uzbek and Turkish speakers all cut final stops dead. The letter is not finished until the breath is out.
- **Bouncing it** by analogy with د and ط. ت is not in **قُطْبُ جَدٍّ**.
- **A dental or interdental ت** — tongue on the teeth rather than the gum root.
- **Over-aspirating it** into an English "t" with a heavy puff, or the Urdu aspirated تھ. The hams is a soft release of breath, not an explosion.
- **Reading ta' marbutah (ة) as ت at a stop.** At a waqf, ة becomes **ه**, not ت: رَحْمَةٌ stopped on is "rahmah". Persian and Urdu speakers, whose languages borrow Arabic words with an audible "t", get this wrong constantly.

**Spoken correction cue:** *"Let the air out. Hand in front of your mouth — I want to feel the puff. And no bounce: it breathes, it doesn't kick."*

## Confused with

- [[Ta-heavy]] — **ط → ت is the collapse to watch for.** ط is voiced, heavy, and bounces; ت is breathed, light, and puffs. In Persian, Urdu, Turkish and Uzbek the two are pronounced identically, so the student must be taught the distinction from zero.
- [[Dal]] — the voicing pair. Same makhraj, same weight; د has jahr and qalqalah, ت has hams and neither.
- [[Tha]] — the script neighbours (two dots vs three) are also sound neighbours: a ث made with the tongue behind the teeth instead of on their edges becomes ت.

## In tajweed

- **[[Hams|Hams]]** — audible breath on release, especially in waqf.
- **No [[Qalqalah-sifah|qalqalah]]** — a hard rule and a common exam item.
- **[[Ikhfa-Haqiqi|Ikhfa']].** ت is one of the fifteen ikhfa' letters: noon sakinah before ت is hidden with a **light** ghunnah, as in كُنتُمْ.
- **[[Idgham-Mutajanisayn|Idgham mutajanisayn]]** — a **sakin ت merges into a following ط or د**: وَقَالَت طَّآئِفَةٌ, أَثْقَلَت دَّعَوَا. And a sakin **د or ط** merges into a following **ت** — into ت from د completely (قَد تَّبَيَّنَ), from ط **incompletely**, with the ط's itbaq retained (أَحَطتُ).
- **[[Idgham-Mutamathilayn|Idgham mutamathilayn]]** — sakin ت into ت.
- **[[Istifal|Istifal]]** — ت never makes a following ر heavy.
