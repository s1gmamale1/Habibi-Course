# ث — thā'

## Makhraj

The **10th and last of the ten tongue makharij** — the *lithawiyyah* group. The **tip of the tongue** touches the **lower edges** of the two upper front incisors, protruding slightly so that it is **visible**. Breath — no voice — flows past it.

Shared with [[Dhal]] and [[Za-heavy]]:

- **ث** — **breathed**, light, flowing.
- **ذ** — voiced, light, flowing. (ث with the voice on.)
- **ظ** — voiced, heavy, flowing.

Cue: *"Tongue tip out onto the edges of your top teeth so I can see it, and just blow. No voice at all — hand on your throat, it must be silent."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **hams** (whispered) | jahr | Pure breath. ث is the ث of the mnemonic فَحَثَّهُ شَخْصٌ سَكَتْ. |
| **rakhawah** (flowing) | shiddah | Holdable. Under shaddah (فَحَثَّهُ) it is held, not clipped. |
| **istifal** (lowered) | isti'la | Light in every position. |
| **infitah** (open) | itbaq | No clamping. |
| **ismat** | idhlaq | Not a fluent letter. |

## Common mistakes

- **ث → س.** The dominant error worldwide. **Persian, Urdu, Turkish and Uzbek all render Arabic ث as a plain [s]** in borrowed words — Persian *sābit* for ثَابِت, Urdu *sawāb* for ثَوَاب. The student is reproducing their own language faithfully; they do not hear a difference. The tongue tip stays behind the teeth instead of coming out onto them.

  This is a **visual** correction before it is an auditory one. Mirror, tongue out, watch it. If the tongue is not visible, the letter is not being made.
- **ث → ذ.** Voicing a letter that must be breathless — and the reverse, ذ → ث. Both directions are common and both are diagnosed the same way: fingers on the throat. **ث must be silent there.**
- **ث → ت.** The tongue retreats to the *roots* of the teeth and the air stops. That is [[Ta]]'s makhraj with shiddah added.
- **Clipping it.** ث is rakhawah — مِثْقَالَ needs the sakin ث held and flowing, not cut off.
- **English speakers** have the sound (*thin*) and usually place it correctly; their error is the ث/ذ voicing swap, not the makhraj.

**Spoken correction cue:** *"Tongue out — on the teeth, not behind them. Now blow, no voice. Watch it in the mirror: if you can't see your tongue, you're saying sīn."*

## Confused with

- [[Seen]] — the collapse that Persian, Urdu, Turkish and Uzbek speakers arrive with. س is behind the teeth and whistles; ث is on the teeth and hisses broadly.
- [[Dhal]] — the voicing pair, confused in both directions.
- [[Ta]] — the script neighbours (two dots vs three) are also sound neighbours; a ث pulled back behind the teeth becomes ت.
- [[Za-heavy]] — same makhraj, but voiced and heavy.

## In tajweed

- **[[Lam-Shamsiyyah|Idgham shamsiyyah]]** — ث is one of the fourteen sun letters: the ل of ٱل is silent before it. ٱلثَّوَاب is read "ath-thawāb".
- **[[Ikhfa-Haqiqi|Ikhfa']].** ث is one of the fifteen ikhfa' letters: noon sakinah and tanween before ث are hidden with a **light** ghunnah, as in مَنثُورًا.
- **[[Idgham-Mutaqaribayn|Idgham mutaqaribayn]]** — a sakin ذ before a ث does not merge in Hafs; read each clearly.
- **[[Hams|Hams]] in [[Waqf-Types|waqf]]** — a sakin ث at a stop keeps its full flowing breath.
- **[[Istifal|Istifal]]** — ث never makes a following ر heavy.
- ث is not a [[Qalqalah-sifah|qalqalah]] letter.
