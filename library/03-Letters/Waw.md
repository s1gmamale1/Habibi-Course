# و — wāw

## Makhraj

و has **two** homes, and the student must be able to tell which one they are in:

1. **Consonantal wāw (wāw ghayr maddiyyah)** — **point 16 of 17**, *ash-shafatān* (الشَّفَتَان), **both lips**. Zone 4. This is the makhraj recorded in this note's frontmatter. The lips are **rounded but not closed** — a gap remains. Compare [[Ba]] and [[Meem]], which sit at the same makhraj with the lips fully shut.
2. **Madd wāw (wāw maddiyyah)** — sākinah preceded by a dammah, e.g. يُولَدْ. It leaves the lips and belongs to **al-jawf** (point 1), the open cavity, with the other madd letters [[Alif]] and yāʾ.

Teacher's script for the consonant: *"Round your lips as if you are about to whistle, but do not let them touch. Now push voice through the gap and open straight into the vowel. Both lips work; your teeth do nothing."*

**The teeth test — use this on every Turkic, Slavic, Persian and Urdu speaker:** *"Put a finger flat across your lips. When you say و, your lower lip must not go anywhere near your top teeth. If your teeth touch your lip, you have said [[Fa]] with voice — that is `v`, and it is not an Arabic letter."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Jahr** (voiced) | hams | Fully voiced; no breath escapes. |
| **Rakhāwah** (flowing) | shiddah | Continuous — never a stop. |
| **Istifāl** (lowered) | istiʿlāʾ | Light. و is never heavy. |
| **Infitāḥ** (open) | iṭbāq | No clamping. |
| **Iṣmāt** (prevented) | idhlāq | Not a "fluent" letter. |
| **Līn** (ease) | — | Applies to **و sākinah preceded by a fathah** (خَوْفٍ). The sound emerges with no strain on the makhraj. This is the basis of madd līn. |
| **Khafāʾ** (hiddenness) | (zuhūr, per some) | Applies to the madd wāw — faint and easily lost. |

## Common mistakes

**و produced as [v] rather than [w] is a priority error** (course-research) and is the single most reliable marker of a Turkic or Slavic first language.

- **و → [v]** — Uzbek `в`, Russian `в`, Turkish `v`, Persian و and Urdu و are all labiodental or near-labiodental. The learner's mouth reaches for the teeth automatically. وَٱلْعَصْرِ becomes `val-ʿaṣr`. Correction cue: *"Teeth off the lip. Make a kiss shape, then open it. `oo-a`, `oo-a`, faster — wa. Say وَ ten times with your finger held against your lips."*
- **Turkish L1** specifically — Turkish `v` is often already close to [w] between vowels, so use that: *"You already say it correctly in `tavuk`. Use that mouth here."*
- **Turning wāw līn into a full English diphthong** (taxonomy §3.14) — خَوْفٍ read as `khowf` with the lips gliding and rounding. In Arabic the fathah and the wāw stay distinct and the lips must **not** round on the līn wāw. Correction cue: *"Don't slide. Say the fathah, then let the wāw sit there without curling your lips. `kha-w-f`, not `khow-f`."*
- **Rounding the lips on the madd wāw** (taxonomy §2.1) — يُولَدْ read with the lips pursed through the whole madd. Madd wāw comes from al-jawf; the lips are already relaxed. Correction cue: *"Set the `u` shape once and then leave your mouth alone for two counts."*
- **Inserting a vowel before an initial wāw** — Turkic phonotactics. وَ is one unit, not `uwa`.
- **Merging wāw with the following letter when it shouldn't** — see the idghām note below.

## Confused with

- **[[Fa]] (ف)** — via `v`: `v` is a voiced ف, and that is exactly the wrong target. ف uses teeth; و must not.
- **[[Ba]] (ب)** — same makhraj zone, but ب closes the lips completely and stops the air. و leaves a gap and flows.
- **[[Meem]] (م)** — also both lips, closed, plus nasal resonance. و has neither closure nor ghunnah.
- **[[Ya]] (ي)** — the other līn/madd letter; ي raises the tongue, و rounds the lips. Learners sometimes neutralise both into a plain vowel.

## In tajweed

- **Madd ṭabīʿī** — madd wāw is two counts (يُولَدْ, نُوحِيهَا).
- **Madd līn** — wāw sākinah after a fathah, followed by a letter made sākin by the stop: خَوْفٍ read as خَوْفْ. Two, four or six counts, and only at a waqf; in waṣl it is plain līn with no stretch.
- **Madd muttaṣil / munfaṣil** — a madd wāw meeting a [[Hamza]] within or across a word.
- **Idghām with ghunnah** — و is one of the يَرْمَلُونَ letters. A nūn sākinah or tanwīn before و merges into it with a two-count ghunnah (مِن وَلِيٍّ). This idghām is **nāqiṣ** (incomplete) — the ghunnah remains.
- **Izhār shafawī** — a mīm sākinah before و is pronounced clearly, with the lips **not** touching. This is a favourite slip: عَلَيْهِمْ وَ… must not close into a hum.
- **The six non-connecting letters** — و never joins to the letter after it (lesson `1-08`).
- **No qalqalah, no tafkhīm.**
