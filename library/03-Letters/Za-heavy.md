# ظ — ẓā'

> Ibn al-Jazari devoted a whole chapter — **بَابُ الضَّادِ وَالظَّاء** — to keeping ظ and [[Dad]] apart. If a scholar of the 9th century AH thought this pair needed its own chapter for Arab reciters, expect to teach it explicitly.

## Makhraj

The **10th and last of the ten tongue makharij** — *al-huruf al-lithawiyyah*. The **tip of the tongue** touches the **lower edges** of the two upper front incisors — the biting edges, not the roots, not the flat face. The tongue tip protrudes slightly and is **visible**.

ظ shares this exact point with [[Dhal]] and [[Tha]]. All three are rakhawah — the air flows past the tongue tip. They differ only in voice and weight:

| | voice | weight |
|---|---|---|
| **ظ** | jahr (voiced) | **heavy** — isti'la + itbaq |
| **ذ** | jahr (voiced) | light |
| **ث** | **hams** (breathed) | light |

So **ظ is ذ made heavy**, exactly as ص is س made heavy.

Cue: *"Tip of the tongue out onto the edges of your top teeth — I should be able to see it. Now lift the back of your tongue and clamp it to the roof of your mouth, and let the sound flow out over the tip."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **jahr** (voiced) | hams | Voiced all the way through. |
| **rakhawah** (flowing) | shiddah | **The sound continues.** A sakin ظ is *held*, never bounced. |
| **isti'la** (elevated) | istifal | Heavy in every position. |
| **itbaq** (clamped) | infitah | One of four itbaq letters (ص ض ط ظ). |
| **ismat** | idhlaq | Heavy and slow. |

## ظ is held, [[Ta-heavy]] bounces

This contrast is taught explicitly in lesson **1-14**. Both letters are heavy, voiced, and itbaq. The difference is the fifth sifah:

- **ط** has **shiddah** — the air is completely stopped, so a sakin ط has to *pop* out. That pop is **qalqalah**. قَطْ ends with a kick.
- **ظ** has **rakhawah** — the air never stops, so a sakin ظ is simply **sustained and faded**. حَفِظْ ends with a long heavy hiss.

Say them back to back for the student: **aṭ — aẓẓẓẓ.** One pops, the other lasts.

## Common mistakes

- **ظ → ذ.** The heaviness is dropped: the tip is in the right place but the back of the tongue never rises, so isti'la and itbaq are lost. Extremely common, and the mirror of ص → س.
- **ظ → "z".** Part of the **ذ / ز / ظ / ض collapse to [z]** documented for **Persian, Turkish and Urdu** speakers. In Persian and Urdu all four of these letters are pronounced identically as a plain [z] — an Urdu speaker says *zulm* for ظُلْم and hears nothing missing, because in their own language nothing is. The tongue tip never leaves the floor of the mouth. See [[Dhal]], [[Zay]], [[Dad]].
- **ظ → ض, or ض → ظ.** The classical confusion. Both are heavy, voiced and flowing, so they *sound* interchangeable to an untrained ear — but the makharij are completely different parts of the tongue.
- **Bouncing a sakin ظ**, by analogy with ط.
- **Tongue tip not protruding** — a ظ made behind the teeth is a heavy ز.

**Spoken correction cue:** *"Tongue on the edge of your top teeth where I can see it — and lift the back. Now hold it: aẓẓẓẓ. Don't stop it, don't kick it."*

## Confused with

- [[Dad]] — **the pair Ibn al-Jazari gave its own chapter.** The test is purely positional: **ظ is the tongue TIP on the edges of the upper front teeth. ض is the SIDE of the tongue against the upper molars, dragged forward (istitalah).** Same weight, same voice, same flow — different part of the tongue entirely. Teach the two notes in one sitting.
- [[Dhal]] — the light counterpart. Same tip position; ذ has no isti'la and no itbaq.
- [[Zay]] — where both ظ and ذ end up for Persian/Urdu/Turkish speakers.
- [[Ta-heavy]] — the bounce/hold pair.
- [[Sad]] — the other heavy fricative; ص is breathed and made low, ظ is voiced and made on the upper teeth.

## In tajweed

- **Isti'la and itbaq** — always **mufakhkham**, at the strongest degree.
- **Tafkheem of a neighbouring ر** — a sakin ظ before ر creates the isti'la environment.
- **Ikhfa'.** ظ is one of the fifteen ikhfa' letters: noon sakinah before ظ is hidden with a **heavy** ghunnah, as in يَنظُرُونَ.
- **Idgham mutajanisayn** — a **sakin ذ merges into a following ظ**: إِذ ظَّلَمُوا (4:64). This is the one place the two letters legitimately become one, and students who cannot tell them apart cannot execute it.
- **No qalqalah** — rakhawah rules it out, and this is the graded contrast with ط.
- **The ظ words are a closed set.** Roughly a few dozen roots in the whole Qur'an use ظ. Some teachers hand students the list. It is a legitimate crutch for reading, but it does not fix the sound — drill the makhraj too.
