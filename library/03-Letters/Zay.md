# ز — zāy

## Makhraj

The **9th of the ten tongue makharij** — the *asaliyyah* (whistling) group. The **tip of the tongue** between the upper and lower incisors, **closer to the lower ones**, air forced down a narrow groove in the tongue.

Shared with [[Seen]] and [[Sad]]. ز is the **light, voiced** member: **ز is س with the voice switched on.**

Cue: *"Say a long sīn. Now, without moving anything, turn your voice on. That's zāy."*

## Sifat

| Sifah | Opposite | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **jahr** (voiced) | hams | **Voiced throughout.** Fingers on the throat: a continuous buzz. This is the sifah that separates ز from س, and the one students drop. |
| **rakhawah** (flowing) | shiddah | Holdable. |
| **istifal** (lowered) | isti'la | Light in every position. |
| **infitah** (open) | itbaq | No clamping. |
| **ismat** | idhlaq | Not a fluent letter. |
| **safir** (whistling) | — | A voiced whistle — buzzier and lower than س. |

## Common mistakes

- **Devoicing into س**, especially at word-end and before a voiceless letter. Turkish and Uzbek devoice final obstruents systematically, so a final ز is routinely read as س without the student noticing.
- **ز as the dumping ground for ذ, ظ and ض.** This is the big structural problem. **In Persian and Urdu, the four letters ذ ز ض ظ are all pronounced [z]** — identical. An Urdu or Persian speaker reading ذَٰلِكَ, زَادَ, ضَرَبَ and ظَلَمَ produces the same consonant four times and hears nothing wrong, because in their own language nothing *is* wrong. Turkish does the same for ذ and ز.

  The consequence for teaching: **ز is not the letter to fix first.** ز is the one they already have. The work is showing them that [[Dhal]], [[Za-heavy]] and [[Dad]] are three *different* letters that merely sound like their ز. Put all four in a row and drill the makhraj, not the sound:
  - **ز** — tip low, near the bottom teeth, whistling.
  - **ذ** — tip on the **edges** of the upper teeth, visible, light.
  - **ظ** — tip on the **edges** of the upper teeth, back of tongue clamped, heavy.
  - **ض** — **side** of the tongue against the upper molars, heavy, dragged forward.
- **Over-whistling** into a buzz-saw.
- **Making it heavy** next to a heavy letter — ز stays light in زُلْزِلَتِ regardless.

**Spoken correction cue:** *"Keep your voice on all the way through — hold it, hold it — and keep your tongue low and behind your teeth. If your tongue touches your top teeth, you've made a ذ."*

## Confused with

- [[Seen]] — the voicing pair.
- [[Dhal]] — the letter most often collapsed into ز by Persian, Urdu and Turkish speakers.
- [[Za-heavy]] — same collapse, plus the loss of isti'la and itbaq.
- [[Dad]] — the same collapse again, and the most damaging one, since ض also loses its istitalah.

## In tajweed

- **[[Ikhfa-Haqiqi|Ikhfa']].** ز is one of the fifteen ikhfa' letters: noon sakinah before ز is hidden with a **light** ghunnah, as in أَنزَلَ.
- **[[Istifal|Istifal]]** — ز never makes a following ر heavy.
- **[[Idgham-Mutamathilayn|Idgham mutamathilayn]]** — sakin ز into ز.
- **[[Jahr|Jahr]] in [[Waqf-Types|waqf]]** — a sakin ز at a stop keeps its voicing and its buzz; it must not be turned into a س.
- ز is not a [[Qalqalah-sifah|qalqalah]] letter — it has [[Rakhawah|rakhawah]], not [[Shiddah|shiddah]].
