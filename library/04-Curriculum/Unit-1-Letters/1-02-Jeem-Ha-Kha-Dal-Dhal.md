## Objectives

- Student can identify and pronounce ج, ح, خ, د, ذ in isolation on sight.
- Student can consistently distinguish ح (heavy, mid-throat) from خ (upper-throat) and from ه (light — taught later, flagged now as "the sound you'll meet later that's NOT this").
- Student can distinguish د (plain "d") from ذ (voiced "th" as in "this").

## Hook

Run the dot game from last lesson first, cold: ب ت ث on the board in random order, thirty seconds, no help.

Then introduce the second family the same way — **ج ح خ are one shape with the dot moving** — and immediately give her the sound that has no English equivalent. Say **ح**, then say **h** as in *house*, then **ح** again. Ask which one felt like it came from further down. That question is the whole lesson: ح is not an English *h*, and the difference is depth, not effort.

## What they already know

From 1-01: the right-to-left habit, the four letters ا ب ت ث, and — more useful than any of them — **the idea that a family shares a shape.** She already knows to look at the dots before the outline. Today applies that twice.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. The throat has three zones

- The throat divides into three zones: deep (hamza — a glottal-stop mark — and ه, both met later), mid (ع/ح — ع met later), upper (غ/خ — غ met later)
- In the picture: teal = upper throat (Adna al-Halq — غ خ), violet = mid throat (Wasat al-Halq — ع ح), rose = deep throat (Aqsa al-Halq — ء ه)
- Two of today's five letters are throat letters — often the single most error-prone family in the whole alphabet for learners coming from your language background
- Today we meet the mid-zone ح and the upper-zone خ

### 2. The letters themselves

| Letter | Name | Makhraj | What to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ج** | jeem | middle of the tongue against the roof of the mouth | Shape-family with ح and خ — ج has one dot BELOW |
| **ح** | haa | middle of the throat | Same body-family as ج/خ — ح has NO dot |
| **خ** | kha | upper throat / back of tongue against the soft palate | Same body-family as ج/ح — خ has one dot ABOVE |
| **د** | dal | tip of tongue against the upper gum ridge | Shape-family with ذ — same body, د has NO dot |
| **ذ** | dhal | tip of tongue between the teeth (same point as ث, but voiced) | Shape-family with د — same body, ذ has one dot ABOVE |

Full letter notes: [[Jeem]] · [[Ha-throat]] · [[Kha]] · [[Dal]] · [[Dhal]].

**In plain terms:** ج ح خ are one shape with the dot in three places, and د ذ are another. The hard part is not the shapes — it is that ح and خ are made deeper in the throat than anything in English.

## Drills

**ح vs خ vs ه — the #1 mistake in the alphabet.** Listen to all three back to back (ه is only a preview here — it's formally taught in Lesson 1.6), then point to which one you heard.

**د vs ذ — minimal-pair listening.** Listen for either د or ذ, then point to which glyph you heard.

**Cumulative shuffle — all 9 letters so far.** First the 5-letter shuffle of today's family (ج ح خ د ذ), then the cumulative 9-letter shuffle with lesson 1.1 (ا ب ت ث ج ح خ د ذ), fully randomized order.

**Guided sound-imitation round.** Listen to each of today's 5 sounds modeled 3× before you imitate it — your teacher will correct your makhraj (where the sound is produced) live, with extra time on ح/خ.

**Flashcard grid.** Name each letter + produce its sound. Shuffle your order every round.

**Minimal-pair listening: ح vs خ vs ه(preview).** Someone says ح, خ, or ه — point to the glyph you heard. (ه isn't formally taught yet — used only as a contrast foil.)

**Minimal-pair listening: د vs ذ.** Someone says د or ذ — point to the glyph you heard.

**Cumulative 9-letter shuffle.** All letters from lessons 1.1–1.2 in fully randomized order: name + sound each.

## Listen-for

The teacher's checklist as it ships. **These are the shipped plain-string form**,
not the structured `ListenForSchema` the tajweed units use — see the banner above.

- ح substituted with خ or with the light ه — flagged in the sourced research as "the single most-cited non-Arab error" (teaching-mistakes-assessment.md A.1); no equivalent phoneme exists in English, Turkish, Uzbek, Persian, or Urdu. Give this the most correction time in the lesson.
- خ losing its heaviness, flattened toward the student's L1 "x" sound (A.1) — watch that it stays a genuine back-of-throat fricative, not a softened palatal one.
- ج over- or under-affricated (some traditions say "zh" or hard "g" — this course follows the Husary/Muallim standard soft-j articulation; watch for L1-transfer toward a hard "g").
- ذ collapsing toward a plain "z" — explicitly documented as a Persian/Turkish/Urdu-speaker pattern (A.3: "the emphatic sounds become just another way to say 'Z' in these languages") and directly relevant to an Uzbek-background student; confirm this specific substitution against the student's actual speech in this lesson, don't assume.
- د/ذ merged into a single sound generally.

## Teacher script

- Open with a cold-call recap of ا ب ت ث before introducing today's family.
- Explain the three throat zones in plain English before naming ج ح خ ذ د individually.
- Model ح and خ 3× each before asking the student to imitate — correct the FIRST wrong repetition, not the third.
- Self-check your own ح/خ against the linked Arabic101 Makharij videos before the lesson — this is the single lesson to audition hardest.
- Budget the most correction time of the lesson on ح vs خ vs ه, per the sourced research's #1-mistake flag.

## Homework

In the student's notebook: each new letter (ج ح خ د ذ) written 4 full rows, saying its name and sound aloud while writing, then memorized; daily 2-minute throat warm-up (ح, خ, ه(anticipatory) 5× each, self-recorded if possible and compared against the linked Arabic101 videos); cumulative shuffle of all 9 letters learned so far. No printed sheets.

**Daily checklist:**

- Write today's letters (ج ح خ د ذ) 4 rows each in your notebook, saying the name and sound aloud as you write
- Flashcard round ×3 (ج ح خ د ذ, mixed order)
- Throat warm-up: ح → خ → ه(anticipatory), 5× each, slow
- Cumulative 9-letter shuffle, reviewing lesson 1.1's letters too (ا ب ت ث ج ح خ د ذ)
