## Objectives

- Student can identify and pronounce ر, ز, س, ش in isolation on sight.
- Student can produce ر with the correct light tongue-tap quality (not a static English/Uzbek "r").
- Student can distinguish ز from ذ (already taught) without collapsing them.

## Hook

Ask her to say the English word *rose*, then the Arabic **ر**. They are not the same letter and the difference is where the tongue goes — but do not correct it yet. **Today she only has to produce a ر with a real tongue contact;** whether it taps once or several times is lesson 3-04's problem, and naming it now would stall the fluency this lesson exists to build.

Then س and ش, which are one shape with and without dots, and are the easiest pair in the alphabet for an ear that speaks any European language.

## What they already know

From 1-02: two more families, and the growing habit of reading the dots first. She now has nine letters and has met the first sound with no English equivalent (ح), so the idea that a letter can be genuinely new is no longer a surprise.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. The letters themselves

| Letter | Name | Makhraj | What to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ر** | ra | tip of tongue curled slightly, touching the gum ridge | A light single tongue-flap/trill — NOT the English or Uzbek "r" |
| **ز** | zay | tip of tongue near the lower teeth, sibilant | Shape-family with ر — same body, ز has one dot ABOVE |
| **س** | seen | tip of tongue near the lower teeth, thin hiss | Shape-family with ش — same body shape, س has NO dots |
| **ش** | sheen | middle of tongue against the roof of the mouth | Shape-family with س — same body, ش has three dots ABOVE |

Full letter notes: [[Ra]] · [[Zay]] · [[Seen]] · [[Sheen]].

**In plain terms:** ر needs your tongue to actually touch — an English *r* never does. س and ش are the same shape, and the three dots are the only difference between them.

## Drills

**Guided ر drill.** Repeat after your teacher, slowly, isolating only the tongue-tap motion — 10 slow repetitions; your teacher gives a thumbs up/down each rep.

**ز vs ذ — minimal-pair listening.** The exact D→Z-family collapse risk flagged in lesson 1.2, re-tested here with ز specifically. Listen for either ز or ذ, then point to which glyph you heard.

**س vs ش — minimal-pair listening.** Listen for either س or ش, then point to which glyph you heard.

**Cumulative shuffle — all 13 letters so far.** First the 4-letter shuffle of today's family (ر ز س ش), then the cumulative 13-letter shuffle (ا ب ت ث ج ح خ د ذ ر ز س ش), fully randomized order.

**Guided sound-imitation round.** Listen to each of today's 4 sounds modeled by your teacher, then repeat it — your teacher will correct you live, with extra time on ر.

**Flashcard grid.** Name each letter + produce its sound. Shuffle your order every round.

**ر isolation drill.** Repeat ر 10× focusing only on the tongue-tap motion; your teacher gives a thumbs up/down each repetition.

**Minimal-pair listening: ز vs ذ.** The exact D→Z-family collapse risk from lesson 1.2, re-tested here specifically with ز. Someone says ز or ذ — point to the glyph you heard.

**Cumulative 13-letter shuffle.** All letters from lessons 1.1–1.3 in fully randomized order: name + sound each.

## Listen-for

The teacher's checklist as it ships. **These are the shipped plain-string form**,
not the structured `ListenForSchema` the tajweed units use — see the banner above.

- ر produced as a static, untapped English/Uzbek-style "r" instead of a light single flap — per Arabic101's own framing ("90% of RA mistakes happen here").
- ز drifting toward ذ or vice versa — re-confirm the D→Z-family collapse pattern flagged in lesson 1.2 is (or isn't) present for this specific student (A.3).
- س and ش losing crispness when said quickly back to back (rushed, blurred sibilants).

## Teacher script

- Open with a cumulative cold-call of all 9 letters learned so far before introducing ر.
- Model ر as a single light tongue-flap, not a static English/Uzbek "r" — isolate the tongue-tap motion before asking for imitation.
- Give ر dedicated correction time; Arabic101's own research flags it as unusually error-prone even among fluent readers.
- Re-test the ز vs ذ collapse risk flagged in lesson 1.2 specifically for this student, not from memory.
- Keep س/ش crisp even at speed — don't let rushed sibilants blur together.

## Homework

In the student's notebook: each new letter (ر ز س ش) written 4 full rows, saying its name and sound aloud while writing, then memorized; daily ر-only drill of 15 slow repetitions focusing purely on the tongue-tap; cumulative 13-letter shuffle reviewing lessons 1.1–1.2's letters too (ا ب ت ث ج ح خ د ذ ر ز س ش), timed just for the student's own sense of growing fluency, not graded. No printed sheets.

**Daily checklist:**

- Write today's letters (ر ز س ش) 4 rows each in your notebook, saying the name and sound aloud as you write
