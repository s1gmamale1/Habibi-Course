## Objectives

- Student can identify and pronounce ص, ض, ط, ظ in isolation on sight.
- Student can explain, in plain English, what "heavy" (tafkheem) vs "light" (tarqeeq) means and demonstrate the difference against each letter's light counterpart (س/ت/ذ already taught, د already taught).
- Student is aware that ض is documented as the single hardest letter in the alphabet for non-native speakers and does not feel discouraged by early imperfection on it.

## Hook

This is the lesson where the alphabet stops being a set of shapes.

Say **سَا** and then **صَا**, twice each, and ask what changed. She will say the second is *deeper*, or *darker*, or *bigger* — take whatever word she gives you and keep it. That word is the one you will use for the rest of the course.

Then tell her the truth about difficulty: **these four are the hardest letters in the alphabet for her, and they take weeks, not one session.** Saying so now prevents her concluding in three days that she cannot learn Arabic.

## What they already know

From 1-01 to 1-03: twelve letters, all of them light. Today introduces the contrast that the entire tajwīd course is later built on — and it is introduced as a *sound*, not as a rule. She is not told the word *tafkhīm* and does not need it.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. Heavy vs light letters

- Mouth cavity fills up, the back of the tongue rises, and the sound gets a fuller/darker quality
- Contrast a light letter you already know against its heavy twin: س↔ص, د↔ض(closest), ت↔ط, ذ↔ظ
- First formal introduction of tafkheem (heavy) / tarqeeq (light) — full rule-naming is Phase 3, but the physical sensation is taught now

### 2. The heaviness rides through the whole syllable

- A heavy letter also darkens any vowel sound that follows it — a light forward-preview of Phase 3's tafkheem/tarqeeq rule, kept purely sensory here
- You'll test this for real once Unit 1.3 adds short vowels

### 3. The letters themselves

| Letter | Name | Makhraj | What to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ص** | saad | same point as س, but back of tongue raised, mouth cavity fuller — heavy twin of س | Heavy twin of س — say س then ص back to back to feel the difference |
| **ض** | daad | side of the tongue against the upper molars — a lateral release unique to this letter | No light twin in this set (closest reference: د) |
| **ط** | taa | same point as ت, but full mouth cavity — heavy twin of ت | Heavy twin of ت — say ت then ط back to back |
| **ظ** | dhaa | same point as ذ, but full mouth cavity — heavy twin of ذ | Heavy twin of ذ — say ذ then ظ back to back |

Full letter notes: [[Sad]] · [[Dad]] · [[Ta-heavy]] · [[Za-heavy]].

**In plain terms:** Each of these four is the heavy twin of a letter you already know: ص of س, ض of د, ط of ت, ظ of ذ. Same place in the mouth, but the back of the tongue lifts and the sound goes dark.

## Drills

**Paired contrast: س → ص.** Say the light one then its heavy twin, 5× each pair.

**ض isolation drill.** Repeat ض 10 times, slowly, while your teacher demonstrates the side-of-tongue placement — no grading pressure, since this is documented as the hardest letter in the alphabet.

**Paired contrast: ت → ط.** Say the light one then its heavy twin, 5× each pair.

**Paired contrast: ذ → ظ.** Say the light one then its heavy twin, 5× each pair.

**Cumulative shuffle — all 17 letters so far.** First the 4-letter shuffle of today's family (ص ض ط ظ), then the cumulative 17-letter shuffle, fully randomized order.

**Guided sound-imitation round.** Listen to each of today's 4 sounds modeled 3× before you imitate it — your teacher will correct your makhraj (where the sound is born in your mouth or throat) live, with extra time on ض.

**Flashcard grid.** Name each letter + produce its sound, shuffled.

**Paired-contrast drill.** Say the light one then its heavy twin, 5× each pair: س→ص, د→ض(closest), ت→ط, ذ→ظ.

**ض isolation drill.** Repeat ض 10 times, slowly, while your teacher demonstrates the side-of-tongue placement — no grading pressure, since this is documented as the hardest letter in the alphabet.

**Cumulative 17-letter shuffle.** All letters from lessons 1.1–1.4 in fully randomized order: name + sound each.

## Listen-for

The teacher's checklist as it ships. **These are the shipped plain-string form**,
not the structured `ListenForSchema` the tajweed units use — see the banner above.

- ص pronounced as plain س — loses the "thick hiss"/full-mouth quality (teaching-mistakes-assessment.md A.2).
- ض — weak or absent lateral release, produced as a plain د or a "z"-like sound instead; explicitly documented as the hardest letter for all non-native speakers and specifically named for Persian/Turkish/Urdu-background learners (A.2, A.3) — do not expect mastery in one lesson, but do flag every instance for homework focus.
- ط pronounced like plain ت — loses the held, forceful, full-mouth-echo release (A.2).
- ظ pronounced like plain ذ — loses heaviness (A.2); note even some native Arabic dialects merge ظ/ض, so approximate imperfection here is normal, not alarming.
- Watch for tafkheem "bleeding into the vowel" starting to show up even at this pre-vowel stage — if the student adds any vowel-like release after a heavy letter, check that it already sounds "backed"/rounded, not flat (A.2 — this becomes fully testable once Unit 1.3 adds real vowels).

## Teacher script

- Open with a cumulative cold-call of all 13 letters learned so far, then introduce "heavy vs light" as a felt sensation before naming any heavy letter.
- Pair each heavy letter with its light twin (س→ص, ت→ط, ذ→ظ) and model both back to back 3× before asking for imitation.
- Give ض the most time and zero pressure — it is documented as the hardest letter in Arabic for virtually all non-native speakers; slow, exaggerated repetitions only.
- Self-audition the linked Arabic101 videos before the lesson — tafkheem quality is subtle and easy to under-teach from memory alone.
- Correct on the first wrong repetition, but frame every ض attempt as expected imperfection, not failure.
