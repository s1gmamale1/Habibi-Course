## Objectives

- Student can identify and pronounce ع, غ, ف, ق in isolation on sight.
- Student can produce ع without collapsing it into ء (hamza/glottal stop).
- Student can distinguish ق from ك (introduced next lesson, previewed today as the contrast target).

## Hook

**ع is the letter that makes adults give up, so spend the hook on it and nothing else.**

Do not define it. Have her put a hand flat on her throat and say a long *aaa*, then squeeze the middle of her throat while the sound is running. The strangled sound she produces is closer to ع than anything she will get from a description. Then tell her that this letter is in the first line of al-Fātiḥa, in **نَعْبُدُ**, so she will be saying it every day whether it is perfect or not.

## What they already know

From 1-04: the heavy four, and the idea that a letter can take weeks. That framing matters today — ع and ق are the next two on that list, and she should meet them expecting difficulty rather than being ambushed by it.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. The letters themselves

| Letter | Name | Makhraj | What to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ع** | ayn | middle of the throat | A mid-throat muscular squeeze/constriction, voiced — genuinely has no equivalent in English or Turkic languages |
| **غ** | ghayn | upper throat, same zone as خ but voiced | A uvular fricative, described as close to a French uvular "r" — must stay a fricative, not a stop |
| **ف** | fa | inner lower lip against the edge of the upper front teeth | Sound: a plain "f" |
| **ق** | qaaf | back of tongue against the soft palate | A deep, uvular "cough-like" stop from the very back of the tongue — heavier and further back than ك (next lesson) |

Full letter notes: [[Ayn]] · [[Ghayn]] · [[Fa]] · [[Qaf]].

**In plain terms:** ع and غ come from the middle of your throat, deeper than anything in English. ق is made further back than the English *k* — say *k* and then slide it backwards until it sounds hollow.

## Drills

**Guided ع drill — throat-squeeze vs glottal stop.** Repeat after your teacher: the throat-squeeze of ع, contrasted against a plain glottal stop (ء — just a quick aside here, not yet formally taught). Listen for the difference — a real muscular squeeze, not just "starting a vowel abruptly".

**غ vs خ — minimal-pair listening.** Your teacher says either غ or خ — find which one you heard and point to it. This tests the voiced/unvoiced distinction at the same throat zone.

**Preview-contrast: ق vs ك.** ك is not formally taught until next lesson, but don't flatten ق into it — listen to both letters, then pronounce ق keeping it deep and uvular. قال (he said) vs a ك-flattened version is a genuine meaning-changing mix-up, so precision matters here.

**Cumulative shuffle — all 21 letters so far.** First shuffle and pronounce today's 4-letter family (ع غ ف ق), then shuffle and name + pronounce all 21 letters learned so far, fully randomized.

**Guided sound-imitation round.** Repeat after your teacher for each of today's 4 sounds — your teacher will correct your makhraj (where the sound is born) live.

**Flashcard grid.** Name each letter + produce its sound, shuffled.

**Minimal-pair listening: ع vs ء(anticipatory).** Someone says ع or ء (ء is just an aside here, not yet formally taught) — point to the glyph you heard. This tests whether you can tell a real throat-squeeze from a plain glottal stop, early.

**Minimal-pair listening: غ vs خ.** Someone says غ or خ — point to the glyph you heard. This tests the voiced/unvoiced distinction at the same throat zone.

**Cumulative 21-letter shuffle.** All letters from lessons 1.1–1.5 in fully randomized order: name + sound each.

## Listen-for

The teacher's checklist as it ships. **These are the shipped plain-string form**,
not the structured `ListenForSchema` the tajweed units use — see the banner above.

- ع substituted with ء — flattening the muscular throat-squeeze into a plain glottal stop, described in the sourced research as happening because "no equivalent phoneme exists in English or Turkic languages" (teaching-mistakes-assessment.md A.1). This is the single highest-priority correction in this lesson.
- غ confused with خ, or blended toward ق — same-zone voicing confusion (A.1).
- ق flattened to plain ك — loses its deep uvular quality (A.4); use the قال/كال (he said / he measured) meaning-changing pair as a concrete teaching moment for why precision matters here.
- ف — usually low-risk, but do not let "easy-looking" letters get sloppy articulation just because they're comfortable (per Arabic101's own framing of lesson 14's "easy letters with tricky mistakes").

## Teacher script

- Open with a cumulative cold-call of all 17 letters learned so far before introducing ع.
- Model ع as a genuine mid-throat muscular squeeze, explicitly contrasted against a plain glottal stop — this has no equivalent in English or Turkic languages.
- Give ع the highest-priority correction time in the lesson; per the sourced research it is commonly flattened toward ء.
- Use the قال/كال (he said / he measured) meaning-changing pair as a concrete teaching moment when previewing ق vs ك.
- Don't let ف get sloppy just because it's comfortable — easy-looking letters deserve real articulation too.
