## Objectives

- Student can identify and pronounce ك, ل, م, ن, ه, و, ي in isolation on sight.
- Student can correctly recite and produce the sound of all 28 letters, in any (non-sequential, shuffled) order, on sight — the exact skill Checkpoint 1 will test.
- Student can name at least one thing to "listen for" on themselves for each of the 6 previously-flagged high-risk letters (ح, ض, ع, ق, ذ/ز pattern, ص).

## Hook

Put all 28 letters on the board at once, in random order, and ask her to name every one she can.

She will get most of them, and that is the point of doing it before you teach anything: **she is five sessions in and she can read the alphabet.** The seven letters left today are the easiest in the set — ك ل م ن ه و ي — and several of them she already half-knows from seeing them in words.

Then close the alphabet. Say it plainly: **after today there are no new letters, only new things to do with them.**

## What they already know

Twenty-one letters across five lessons, including the two hardest groups. Today is the only lesson in Unit 1 where the new material is easier than the revision.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. The letters themselves

| Letter | Name | Makhraj | What to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ك** | kaaf | back of tongue against the soft palate, shallower in the mouth than ق | Contrast directly against last lesson's ق — plain "k", shallower in the mouth |
| **ل** | lam | tip/sides of tongue against the gum ridge | A clear, light "l" |
| **م** | meem | the two lips, nasal cavity | Sound: "m", nasal |
| **ن** | nun | tip of tongue against gum ridge, nasal cavity | A two-part sound: an oral release plus nasal resonance — foreshadowing Phase 3's ghunna/noon-sakinah rules |
| **ه** | ha | light, breathy throat release | A soft, breathy "h" — contrasted directly against ح (lesson 1.2), which is a much stronger, deeper throat sound |
| **و** | waw | rounded lips (consonant use) | Taught here purely as a consonant ("w") — its much bigger role as a long-vowel/madd letter is Phase 2 content |
| **ي** | ya | middle of tongue toward the roof of the mouth (consonant use) | Taught here purely as a consonant ("y") — same note as و, returns as a long-vowel letter in Phase 2 |

Full letter notes: [[Kaf]] · [[Lam]] · [[Meem]] · [[Noon]] · [[Ha-soft]] · [[Waw]] · [[Ya]].

**In plain terms:** These last seven are the easy ones — most have a close English equivalent. After today the alphabet is complete, and everything that follows is about joining them up.

## Drills

**ك vs ق — minimal-pair listening.** Recheck from lesson 1.5 — watch that you don't flatten ق toward ك. Listen for either letter and point to the one you heard.

**ح vs ه — the single most important recheck.** Listen for ح or ه, point to the one you heard, and pronounce it back. This is the single most-confused pair for non-Arab learners, and your last scheduled chance to catch it before Checkpoint 1.

**Full 28-letter cumulative shuffle.** Read each letter aloud, in randomized order, for every letter taught in Unit 1.1 — a confidence check ahead of Checkpoint 1.

**Guided correction round — the 6 high-risk sounds.** Repeat each letter after your teacher one more time: ح, ض, ع, ق, ذ/ز pattern, ص.

**Flashcard grid.** Name each letter + produce its sound, shuffled.

**Contrast drill: ك vs ق.** Listen for ك or ق, then point to which one you heard and pronounce it.

**Contrast drill: ح vs ه.** Listen for ح or ه, then point to which one you heard and pronounce it — the single most important recheck of the whole unit.

**Full 28-letter recognition race.** Name and pronounce each letter, shown in fully randomized order (not grouped by family anymore). Your teacher will time it informally and note any hesitation on a simple 28-row checklist — this doubles as Checkpoint 1 prep data.

## Listen-for

The teacher's checklist as it ships. **These are the shipped plain-string form**,
not the structured `ListenForSchema` the tajweed units use — see the banner above.

- ك not kept shallow/light enough relative to ق (student may flatten ق toward ك instead — recheck from lesson 1.5).
- ح vs ه still confused — this is the single most important recheck in all of Phase 1; per the research this is "the single most-cited non-Arab error" and this lesson is the last scheduled chance to catch it before Checkpoint 1 (teaching-mistakes-assessment.md A.1).
- ن produced without any nasal quality at all (oral-only "n") — plant the seed now for Phase 3's ghunna requirement, even though the formal rule isn't taught yet.
- و / ي pronounced with English glide quality instead of Arabic consonantal و/ي — minor at this stage, worth a light correction only.
- Cumulative check across the full 28-letter race: log every hesitation/error by letter, not just pass/fail, to feed directly into the Checkpoint 1 weak-spot mapping.

## Teacher script

- Open with a cumulative cold-call of all 21 letters before introducing today's final 7.
- Contrast ك against last lesson's ق, and ه against lesson 1.2's ح, immediately after each is introduced.
- Model ن explicitly as a two-part oral+nasal sound — plant the seed for Phase 3's ghunna, even though the formal rule isn't taught yet.
- Run the full 28-letter cumulative shuffle as an informally-timed confidence check, not a graded test.
- Spend the guided-correction round revisiting all 6 previously-flagged high-risk sounds one more time each, logging hesitations per letter for Checkpoint 1 prep.
- Close by explicitly naming the milestone: the student now knows all 28 letters.
