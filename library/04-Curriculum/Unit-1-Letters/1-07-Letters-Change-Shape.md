## Objectives

- Student can explain that most Arabic letters take up to 4 different shapes depending on position in a word (isolated / initial / medial / final), while the sound never changes.
- Student can recognize the initial, medial and final forms on sight for all 22 connecting letters — including the hard shape-shifters ج ح خ, ع غ and ه, whose forms look like different letters.
- Student can read a 2–3 letter connected cluster that mixes shape-families, naming both component letters and the form each one took.
- Student can read at least 20 real, unvoweled, fully-connecting Arabic words by decoding the letters inside them.

## Hook

Write **بـ ـبـ ـب ب** on the board and ask how many letters she is looking at.

She will say three or four. It is one. That answer is the whole lesson, and it is worth letting her be wrong first — the shock of *one letter, four faces* is what makes the next hour make sense.

Then say the thing that removes the fear: **the sound never changes.** A ب is *b* wherever it sits and whatever it looks like. She is not learning 112 new letters; she is learning that 28 letters each have up to four handwriting positions.

## What they already know

All 28 letters from Unit 1.1, recognised in isolation. That is exactly the wrong shape for reading a real page, and today is where that becomes obvious — **every word in the muṣḥaf is written in joined forms, and she has so far only seen the isolated ones.**

## Teaching sequence

### 1. Letters change shape — but never their sound

- A letter's sound never changes, no matter where it sits in a word.
- But many letters' SHAPE changes depending on position: isolated, word-initial, word-medial, or word-final.
- Today's focus letters: the dotted-body group ب ت ث ن ي (near-identical connecting body, differ mainly by dot count/position) plus ك ل م for contrast (more visually distinct positional forms).

### 2. Worked example: ب in all 4 positions

- Same dot, different body shape depending on position.
- Isolated: ب — no connections on either side.
- Initial (starts a word, connects forward only): بـ
- Medial (connects on both sides): ـبـ
- Final (connects backward only): ـب

### 3. Worked example: ت / ث / ن / ي in all 4 positions

- Same body family as ب — only the dots/tails distinguish them, in every position.
- The joining mechanics are identical across the whole family.

### 4. Worked example: ك / ل / م in all 4 positions

- More visually distinct shape changes than the dotted-body group — a good contrast case.
- The underlying rule is the same: sound never changes, shape does.

### 5. The bowl family: ج ح خ — the biggest shape jump

- Same three letters from lesson 1.2 — same sounds, same dots. Only the body changes.
- Isolated, the bowl sits open with a deep tail below the line: ج ح خ
- At the start of a word the tail is cut away and the bowl leans forward: جـ حـ خـ
- In the middle it flattens into a small wedge riding along the line: ـجـ ـحـ ـخـ
- At the end the full deep tail comes back: ـج ـح ـخ
- This is the largest visual change in the whole alphabet — the initial and isolated shapes barely look related. The dots are your anchor: ج one below, ح none, خ one above, in every position.

### 6. ع and غ — the letters that change the most

- ع and غ differ only by a dot, and both take four shapes that look like four different letters.
- Isolated: an open head above a deep tail — ع غ
- Initial: just the small open head, tail gone — عـ غـ
- Medial: the head closes into a small triangle riding the line — ـعـ ـغـ
- Final: the head reopens and the deep tail returns — ـع ـغ
- Watch the dot, not the body: no dot = ayn, one dot above = ghayn, in every single position.

### 7. ه — one letter, four faces

- ه is the most shape-shifting letter in the alphabet: its four forms share almost nothing visually.
- Isolated: a round loop — ه
- Initial: a tall, narrow double loop — هـ
- Medial: a small squashed shape sitting on the line — ـهـ
- Final: a plain round tail — ـه
- The sound never moves: a light, breathy release from the throat, every time.

### 8. The teeth and the plates: س ش ص ض

- س and ش share a three-tooth body; ص and ض share a flat plate with a loop.
- Initial and medial forms drop the big end-bowl and keep only the teeth or the plate: سـ ـسـ / صـ ـصـ
- Isolated and final forms carry the big bowl-tail: س ـس / ص ـص
- Dots are again the only difference inside each pair: س none, ش three above; ص none, ض one above.

### 9. ط ظ ف ق — small changes and one tail trick

- Not every letter transforms. ط and ظ keep the same plate-and-stem shape in all four positions — the easiest letters in the alphabet to track.
- ف and ق both have a round head on a tooth-like body, and they shift the way the dotted family does.
- The trick is at the end of a word: ـف keeps a small flat tail, but ـق drops into a deep bowl below the line.
- Dots stay constant: ف one above, ق two above, in every position.

**In plain terms:** One letter, up to four shapes depending on where it sits in the word. The shape changes; the sound never does.

## Drills

**Guided recognition drill — name the position.** Look at each isolated letter or position-form card shown. Name the letter and state which position it's in (isolated/initial/medial/final), for each of the 8 focus letters: ب ت ث ن ي ك ل م.

**Guided recognition drill — the 14 harder connectors, all 4 positions.** Every remaining connecting letter, in all four positions. Teacher points to a cell in random order; student names the letter, produces its sound, and states which position-form it is. The 6 non-connectors (ا د ذ ر ز و) are deliberately absent — they are lesson 1.8's topic.

**Guided recognition drill #2 — connected 2-letter clusters.** Look at each 2-letter connected (unvoweled) cluster shown. Identify both component letters despite the shape change.

**Combination drill — ج ح خ meet the dotted family.** Each cluster pairs a bowl letter (ج ح خ) with a dotted-body letter (ب ت ث ن ي). Read right to left. Name both letters and say which form each one took — notice how completely the bowl letter changes depending on whether it leads the cluster or ends it.

**Combination drill — م joins everything.** م is one of the most frequent letters in Arabic and it turns up in every position. Read each cluster right to left, naming both letters and the form م took. The last row puts م in the middle, where it hangs its loop below the line.

**Combination drill — ع غ ه inside clusters.** The three biggest shape-shifters, now in context. Name both letters in each cluster and the form each took — especially in the last row, where the medial forms look nothing like the isolated letters.

**Reading drill — 2–3 letter unvoweled clusters.** Read across today's focus-letter clusters, naming each component letter regardless of position-shape.

**Cumulative check — the concept applies beyond today's 8 letters.** Read each cluster below aloud, built from Unit 1.1 letters outside today's focus set (ح ص ق) combined with already-taught focus letters. Name every component letter regardless of its positional shape.

**Reading set A — 13 real words, no vowel marks.** Real Arabic words, written the way you will always meet them: joined, unvoweled. Every letter here is a connector, so every joint is visible — nothing breaks yet. Read right to left; name each letter and its form first, then say the word. You are not learning vocabulary here, you are proving you can see the letters inside a real word.

**Reading set B — 13 more real words.** Same task, now weighted toward today's hard shape-shifters (ع غ ه ج ح خ ص ض ط ظ). Read right to left, letter by letter, then blend.

**Position-form flashcards — the 8 familiar letters.** ب ت ث ن ي ك ل م in isolated / initial / medial / final form. Name the letter, its sound, and its position.

**Position-form flashcards — the 14 harder connectors.** ج ح خ س ش ص ض ط ظ ع غ ف ق ه in all four positions. Same task. These are the ones that change the most — do this set daily until the medial forms are instant.

**Cluster-reading drill (2-letter, unvoweled).** Read each cluster, naming the two component letters and which form each took.

Name both letters and say which form each one took — notice how completely the bowl letter changes depending on whether it leads the cluster or ends it.

## Listen-for

The teacher's checklist as it ships. **These are the shipped plain-string form**,
not the structured `ListenForSchema` the tajweed units use — see the banner above.

- Student mentally reverting to isolated-letter shapes and stalling when a letter appears in medial form — expect this most on ـهـ, ـعـ and ـجـ, and coach through it rather than treating it as an error.
- ه medial (ـهـ) misread as ب or م — the squashed medial form is the single most confusable glyph in the alphabet at this stage.
- ع and غ initial forms (عـ غـ) read as each other because the student is reading the body and not checking the dot.
- ج ح خ distinguished by guessing rather than by dot position once the tail is gone in initial/medial form.
- Student reading left-to-right out of habit (Cyrillic/Latin Uzbek literacy carries over) — watch the very first cluster of every drill.
- Sounds regressing on letters that were solid in isolation (especially ث, ح, ض, ع, ق) once they sit inside a cluster or word — this tells you whether Unit 1.1 fixes were real or isolation-only.
- Heaviness (tafkheem) on ص ض ط ظ dropping away inside a word even when it was correct on the flashcard.

## Teacher script

- Open with the 28-letter shuffle recap — this is the last lesson before joining mechanics get complicated, so confirm the sound inventory is still solid before adding shape load.
- Teach the rule first (slide 3): the sound never changes, the shape does. Say it once, then prove it eight times.
- Work the easy families first (ب / ت ث ن ي / ك ل م). These barely change — the student should feel early success here.
- Then the hard families in order of difficulty: bowl (ج ح خ), then ع غ, then ه, then the teeth and plates (س ش ص ض), then the small-change letters (ط ظ ف ق). Explicitly tell the student that ط ظ are the easy reward after ه.
- For ج ح خ and ع غ and ه, cover the isolated form with your hand and ask the student to name the letter from the medial form alone. This is the actual skill; the four-form table is only the reference.
- Run the two flashcard drills as recognition rounds, not recitation — point in random order, never alphabetical.
- The three combination drills are the core of the lesson. Do not skip the reversed rows (bowl letter at the end) — that is where the shape difference is largest and where students stall.
- Reading sets A and B are all fully-connecting words on purpose: every joint is visible and nothing breaks. Do not explain gaps yet — the 6 non-connectors are lesson 1.8 and introducing them here will confuse the joining picture.
- If time runs short, cut the abstract cluster drills (slides 14 and 19) before cutting the reading sets. Reading real joined words is the transferable skill.

## Homework

Reading sets A and B (26 fully-connecting words) aloud 2× daily, the three combination drills 3× through, and the four forms of the six hardest shape-shifters written 4 rows each. Student ticks anything they hesitated on.

**Daily checklist:**

- Run the 14-letter hard-connector flashcard sheet once through, naming letter + sound + position for every cell.
- Read the 26 words in Reading sets A and B aloud, 2× through — letter names first, then the blended word.
- Re-read the ج ح خ, م, and ع غ ه combination drills, 3× through.
- Write the four forms of ج ح خ ع غ ه, 4 rows each in your notebook, saying the name and sound aloud as you write.
- Tick any word or cluster that made you hesitate, for teacher follow-up next lesson.
