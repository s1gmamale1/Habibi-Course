## Objectives

- Student can explain that a fatha (a small diagonal stroke above a letter) makes that letter produce a short "a" sound immediately after its consonant sound.
- Student can read any of the 28 letters + fatha on sight, in randomized order (the full letter × fatha grid, 28 cells).
- Student notices (with teacher prompting) that the "a" sound changes quality — darker/backed toward "o" — after a heavy letter (ص ض ط ظ ق غ خ), as a first sensory exposure to tafkheem-affecting-the-vowel, ahead of the formal Phase 3 rule.

## Hook

Write a bare **ب** and ask her to say it.

She will say *ba* — and she should not be able to. A bare letter has no vowel and therefore no sound she can pronounce on its own. **The letters she has been naming for nine lessons were names, not readings.**

Then put a small stroke above it — **بَ** — and now it is readable. That stroke is the first thing in the course that turns letters into language.

## What they already know

All 28 letters in all four positions, and the six that break the chain. She can look at a word and see its letters. What she cannot do is say it, because nothing so far has told her which vowel goes where.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. Every letter, ten-fold

- Ibn Mas'ud reported the Messenger of Allah ﷺ as saying: "Whoever recites a letter from the Book of Allah, he will have a reward, and that reward will be multiplied by ten. I do not say that Alif-Lam-Mim is one letter, but Alif is a letter, Lam is a letter, and Mim is a letter." (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2910, Hasan)
- Starting today, every one of the 28 letters carries its own vowel sound — each one still carries that same ten-fold reward, letter by letter.

### 2. Harakat — marks that add a vowel sound

- Harakat are small marks placed above or below a letter that tell you which short vowel sound follows the consonant.
- Today's mark is fatha ( ﹷ ), a short "a".
- Note: alif's own vowel-grid cell is conventionally written as أَ (alif carrying hamza + fatha), since bare alif does not itself carry a short-vowel mark in real orthography — a teaching convention, not a new letter.

### 3. Worked example: بَ

- بَ (ba + fatha) — sounded out slowly: "b" + short "a".

### 4. Worked example: تَ, ثَ

- Same pattern, dotted family: تَ (ta + fatha), ثَ (tha + fatha).

### 5. A heavy letter: صَ

- Same fatha mark, but notice the "a" now sounds fuller/darker ("saw"-like, not flat "sa") — this is your first taste of tafkheem (the heavier, fuller quality some letters give their vowels).
- Say سَ then صَ back to back to feel the difference.

**In plain terms:** A small stroke above the letter says "add an *a*". Without a mark, a letter has no vowel and cannot be read on its own.

## Drills

**Full letter × fatha grid (28 cells).** Read each cell aloud, left to right: أَ بَ تَ ثَ جَ حَ خَ دَ ذَ رَ زَ سَ شَ صَ ضَ طَ ظَ عَ غَ فَ قَ كَ لَ مَ نَ هَ وَ يَ — the full grid, revealed together for the first time.

**Guided grid drill, first half (letters 1–14).** Read each cell aloud in order: أَ through ذَ.

**Guided grid drill, second half (letters 15–28).** Read each cell aloud in order: رَ through يَ.

**Randomized-order re-drill.** Read each cell aloud in this new shuffled order — don't rely on the order you memorized; this tests whether you truly recognize each letter.

**Contrast check — light vs. heavy fatha.** سَ/صَ — تَ/طَ — ذَ/ظَ — كَ/قَ. Say each pair back to back, noticing the vowel-darkening on the heavy side.

**Full letter × fatha grid.** Read each cell aloud. Then self-quiz: cover rows and re-reveal, in randomized order.

**Randomized-order re-drill.** Read each cell aloud in this shuffled order — don't rely on the sequence you memorized; this tests real recognition.

**Heavy-vs-light fatha contrast pairs.** سَ/صَ — تَ/طَ — ذَ/ظَ — كَ/قَ. Say each pair back to back, 3 reps.

## Listen-for

The teacher's checklist as it ships. **These are the shipped plain-string form**,
not the structured `ListenForSchema` the tajweed units use — see the banner above.

- Fatha lengthened into a full madd ("aa" instead of a short "a") — this is the single most common vowel-timing error and matters enormously later (madd rules are a major Phase 3 unit); catch it here at the source.
- Heavy letters' fatha not darkening at all (student produces a flat "a" even after ص ض ط ظ ق غ خ) — the first testable instance of the tafkheem-bleeds-into-the-vowel pattern named in the sourced research (teaching-mistakes-assessment.md A.2).
- Any regression on the underlying consonant sound now that a vowel is attached (a letter that was solid in isolation coming out wrong once voweled) — re-flag using the same high-risk-six watchlist from Unit 1.1.

## Teacher script

- Open with the Tirmidhi 2910 hadith (reward ×10 per letter, Alif-Lam-Mim) — today every letter starts carrying its own vowel, so frame it as the reward multiplying letter by letter.
- Introduce fatha as a short, crisp 'a' — model بَ/تَ/ثَ slowly, exaggerating that the vowel is SHORT, never lengthened into a madd.
- Demonstrate the heavy-letter darkening explicitly on صَ — say سَ then صَ back to back so the 'a' vs fuller/darker 'a' contrast is unmistakable.
- Since no vetted 'hear the whole grid' video is wired in yet (candidates are unverified/audition-required per verified-resources.md §4), demonstrate the full 28-cell grid live yourself, at natural pace, before the randomized re-drill.
- Run the grid in its natural order first (two guided halves), then re-drill fully randomized — the randomized pass is the real recognition test, not the ordered one.
- Close with the heavy-vs-light contrast pairs, listening specifically for whether the 'a' darkens correctly after ص ط ظ ق.
