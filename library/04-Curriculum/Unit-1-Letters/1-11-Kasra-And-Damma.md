## Objectives

- Student can explain that kasra (a mark below the letter) makes a short "i" sound and damma (a small loop above the letter) makes a short "u" sound.
- Student can read any of the 28 letters + kasra, and any of the 28 letters + damma, on sight, in randomized order (56 additional cells).
- Student can correctly read a letter with any of the 3 harakat now taught (fatha, kasra, damma) when the teacher calls them out in mixed, unpredictable sequence.

## Hook

Put **بَ · بِ · بُ** on the board and read them in order, then out of order, and have her call which is which.

The mark's *position* is the whole rule — above, below, above-and-curled — and it is the only thing distinguishing three different syllables. Say it once and then drill it, because there is nothing here to understand; there is only something to recognise instantly.

## What they already know

From 1-10: the fatḥa, and the idea that a bare letter cannot be read. Today completes the set of three short vowels — after this she can read any fully-voweled syllable in the language.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. Kasra and damma — two more vowel marks

- Kasra ( ﹻ , below the letter) = short "i" — as in بِ (bi).
- Damma ( ﹹ , above the letter) = short "u" — as in بُ (bu).
- Same darkening-of-the-vowel phenomenon re-checked here after heavy letters: kasra tends to stay closer to a genuine short "i" even after heavy letters, but damma darkens toward a fuller "u"/"o"-adjacent quality — a real, sourced-in-tajweed-tradition phonetic effect, kept sensory/experiential here, not formally named.

### 2. Worked example: بِ, بُ — contrasted against بَ

- بِ (ba + kasra) and بُ (ba + damma), sounded slowly, contrasted against بَ from last lesson.

**In plain terms:** Below the letter is *i*, the little curl above is *u*, and the stroke above is *a*. Three marks, three sounds, and the position is the only difference.

## Drills

**Full letter × kasra grid reveal (28 cells).** إِ بِ تِ ثِ جِ حِ خِ دِ ذِ رِ زِ سِ شِ صِ ضِ طِ ظِ عِ غِ فِ قِ كِ لِ مِ نِ هِ وِ يِ — the full kasra grid, revealed together for the first time. Read each cell aloud as your teacher reveals it.

**Guided kasra grid drill (both halves).** Read each cell aloud in order: إِ through يِ, first half then second half.

**Full letter × damma grid reveal (28 cells).** أُ بُ تُ ثُ جُ حُ خُ دُ ذُ رُ زُ سُ شُ صُ ضُ طُ ظُ عُ غُ فُ قُ كُ لُ مُ نُ هُ وُ يُ — the full damma grid, revealed together for the first time. Read each cell aloud as your teacher reveals it.

**Guided damma grid drill (both halves).** Read each cell aloud in order: أُ through يُ, first half then second half.

**Randomized-order re-drill — kasra and damma mixed.** Read each cell aloud in this shuffled mix of both 28-cell grids (56 cells total) — this tests your recognition, not memorized sequence or vowel-block grouping.

**Full 3-vowel spot-check.** Listen for your teacher calling out any letter + any of the 3 harakat (the short-vowel marks) — e.g. "ز + damma" — verbally, with nothing shown in print, then produce it from memory. This checks that you can produce the sound, not just read it aloud.

**Contrast check — heavy vs. light, kasra and damma.** Kasra: سِ/صِ — تِ/طِ. Damma: سُ/صُ — تُ/طُ. Say each pair back to back, a few reps each.

**Full letter × kasra grid.** Read each cell aloud, all 28 letters + kasra.

**Full letter × damma grid.** Read each cell aloud, all 28 letters + damma.

**Randomized mixed-order re-drill.** Read each cell aloud in this shuffled mix of both grids (56 cells total).

**Heavy-vs-light contrast pairs — kasra and damma.** Kasra: سِ/صِ — تِ/طِ. Damma: سُ/صُ — تُ/طُ. Say each pair back to back, a few reps each.

**"Call and produce" spot-check.** Listen for your teacher naming a letter + a vowel verbally (no text shown), then produce the sound from memory, unprompted by print — 10 rounds, randomized across all 28×3 combinations learned so far.

## Listen-for

The teacher's checklist as it ships. **These are the shipped plain-string form**,
not the structured `ListenForSchema` the tajweed units use — see the banner above.

- Kasra diphthongized (turning into "ee" or "ya"-like glide) instead of staying a crisp short "i."
- Damma lengthened toward "oo" instead of staying a crisp short "u."
- Kasra/damma confused with each other under time pressure during the randomized mixed-order drill (a genuinely common beginner mix-up simply from mark-position similarity — above vs below — being new).
- Continued heavy-letter vowel-darkening pattern (A.2), now checked across all 3 vowels, not just fatha.

## Teacher script

- Open with a 10-cell randomized sample of last lesson's fatha grid before introducing kasra and damma.
- Model بِ and بُ slowly, directly contrasted against بَ from last lesson, before revealing either full grid.
- Since no vetted 'hear the whole grid' video is wired in yet (candidates are unverified/audition-required per verified-resources.md §4), demonstrate both the kasra grid and the damma grid live yourself before their respective guided drills.
- If the damma grid doesn't fit in the hour, stop — 1.12's dry-run slot absorbs the overflow; do not rush 56 cells.
- Run the mixed 56-cell re-drill only after both grids have had their own guided pass — this is the real recognition test, not the ordered reveal.
- Close with the heavy-vs-light kasra/damma contrast pairs, listening specifically for whether damma darkens more than kasra after a heavy letter.
