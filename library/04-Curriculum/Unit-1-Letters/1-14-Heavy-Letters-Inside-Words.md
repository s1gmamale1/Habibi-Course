## Objectives

- Student can read fully-vocalised words containing ص ض ط ظ while sustaining tafkhīm through the whole letter, and can hear the heaviness in the neighbouring vowel rather than only in the consonant.
- Student can distinguish ص from س, ض from د, ض from ظ, and ط from ت in real minimal pairs, and can self-test using سَا/صَا and ضِدّ without the teacher.
- Student can produce qalqalah on a ط carrying sukūn while correctly HOLDING a ظ carrying sukūn — the contrast that proves the two heavy letters are not interchangeable.
- Student can read وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ with a correct ض, a held shadda and a six-count madd, and can name what goes wrong when it is read as ٱلظَّالِّينَ.

## Hook

She met ص ض ط ظ in lesson 1-04 as isolated sounds. Today they appear inside real words, and the thing to listen for is not the letter.

Read **سَا** and **صَا** again, and this time ask her where she can actually *hear* the difference. It is not on the consonant — it is on the **vowel after it**. That is the single most useful thing in this lesson: a heavy letter darkens the vowel that follows, and that is how a listener knows it was heavy.

Then give her **وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ** and tell her she says it seventeen times a day.

## What they already know

From 1-04: the four heavy letters as sounds. From 1-13: sukūn, and the beginnings of qalqalah. Today puts the heavy letters into words where a vowel follows them — which is the only place their heaviness is audible.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. The vowel is the tell

- ص ض ط ظ are the heavy letters. Heavy does not mean loud — it means the back of your tongue lifts toward the roof of your mouth while the front does the work.
- Here is the thing nobody tells beginners: you cannot really hear that dome on the consonant itself. You hear it on the VOWEL beside it.
- After a heavy letter, a fatha backs toward the ‘o’ in ‘hot’ — صَ is closer to ‘ṣo’ than to ‘sa’. A damma goes deep and round. A kasra resists most, but still pulls back a little.
- A long alif after a heavy letter is the clearest tell of all — a heavy alif, dark and resonant.
- Your own diagnostic, and you can run it without me: say سَا, then صَا. If they sound the same, the dome is not happening yet.

### 2. ض and ظ, one āyah apart

- In Sūrah al-Qiyāmah two consecutive āyāt read: وُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ نَاضِرَة (75:22) — ‘faces that day are radiant’ — then إِلَىٰ رَبِّهَا نَاظِرَة (75:23) — ‘looking toward their Lord’. (Written here without tanwīn so the two words line up; the full mushaf spelling is in the reciter drill at the end of the lesson.)
- One letter apart. Both heavy. Both correct in their own place, and each is wrong in the other’s.
- This is the clearest proof in the whole Qurʾān that ض and ظ are different letters and not two spellings of one sound.
- The difference is not weight — both are heavy. It is WHERE the tongue is: ض on the molars with the tip down, ظ with the tip out between the teeth.

### 3. وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ — the ض you say seventeen times a day

- The last words of al-Fātiḥah: وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ — ‘and not of those who go astray’. You say this in every rakʿah, so any error here sets hard.
- One — do not say ظ. ٱلظَّالِّينَ is a different word from a different root, meaning ‘those who persist’. Keep the tongue-tip DOWN and away from the teeth; all the work is at the molars.
- Two — do not say د. Dropping the dome turns it into ‘ad-dāllīn’.
- Three — the madd is long. The alif here is followed by a doubled lām, which makes it a six-count lengthening: the longest in tajwīd. It should feel uncomfortably long. Beginners give it two.
- Four — hold the shadda. The ل of الـ has assimilated into the ض, which is why it is doubled: al- + ḍāllīn becomes aḍ-ḍāllīn. Two full counts of molar contact.
- Five — keep the alif heavy. After a ض the long ‘aa’ must stay dark and backed. A bright ‘aa’ undoes a correct ض.

### 4. The letters themselves

| Letter | Name | Makhraj | What to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ص** | ṣaad | tip of the tongue against the lower front teeth, with the tongue-body raised | Revision letter from Lesson 1.4 — today it lives inside words, which is much harder than saying it alone. |
| **ض** | ḍaad | the side of the tongue against the upper molars | Revision letter from Lesson 1.4, and the most mis-taught letter in Arabic. |
| **ط** | ṭaa | tip of the tongue against the upper gum ridge, with the tongue-body raised | Revision letter from Lesson 1.4. ط is the heavy twin of ت, at exactly the same place. |
| **ظ** | ẓhaa | tip of the tongue between the front teeth, with the tongue-body raised | Revision letter from Lesson 1.4. ظ is the heavy twin of ذ, at exactly the same place. |

**In plain terms:** You cannot really hear heaviness on the letter itself — you hear it in the vowel right after it. Say *sā* then *ṣā* and listen to the *ā*, not the consonant.

## Drills

**ص inside real words — start, middle, end.** Read right to left. Before each word, say the light twin out loud (سَا) and then the heavy one (صَا), so the tongue knows which shape to take.

**ص or س? — read down each column.** Same makhraj, same whistle. The ONLY difference is whether the back of the tongue is domed. Read each pair back to back.

**ض inside real words — start, middle, end.** Read right to left. ض is made with the SIDE of the tongue against the upper molars — not the tip. Check ضِدّ first: if the ض and the د sound alike, stop and fix it before going on.

**ض or د? — read down each column.** ض uses the side of the tongue on the molars; د uses the tip on the gum ridge. Different places, not just different weights.

**ض or ظ? — read down each column.** Both are heavy, so weight will not tell them apart. Watch the tongue TIP: if you can see it between the teeth it is ظ; if it stays down it is ض.

**ط inside real words — start, middle, end.** Read right to left. Every ط is heavy in every vowel. Where a ط carries a sukūn it also bounces — those are marked in the cue.

**ط or ت? — read down each column.** The heavy twin and the light twin, same makhraj. Listen to the VOWEL, not the consonant — it is the vowel that gives it away.

**ظ inside real words.** ظ is the heavy twin of ذ: tongue-tip BETWEEN the teeth, voiced, heavy. If you cannot see the tip, it is not ظ.

**ظ or ذ or ز? — read down each column.** ذ is the same place but light. ز is a whistle from behind the teeth. Only ظ is both between-the-teeth and heavy.

**Bounce or hold? — ط against ظ.** ط is a qalqalah letter and bounces on a sukūn. ظ is NOT and must be held. Read across, twice.

**Hear a reciter — ص and ض in the Qurʾān.** The exact words as they appear in the Qurʾān. Tap to hear a qāriʾ, then copy him. Listen for how the vowel darkens after the heavy letter.

## Listen-for

The teacher's checklist as it ships. **These are the shipped plain-string form**,
not the structured `ListenForSchema` the tajweed units use — see the banner above.

- Heaviness present on the consonant but absent from the vowel — she produces a recognisable ص and then a bright, front ‘a’ after it. This is the commonest partial success and it is easy to mistake for a pass.
- Tafkhīm collapsing at the moment the letter stops, especially on a sukūn (فَضْل, عَصْر). Attention goes to the stop and the dome drops.
- ض produced as a heavy د — tongue-tip on the gum ridge instead of the side on the molars. Check with ضِدّ, not with a word where ض stands alone.
- ض produced as ظ, or ظ as ض. Watch the tongue-tip: visible between the teeth means ظ, hidden means ض. This is a place error, so weight cues will not catch it.
- ظ collapsing to ز — the tip retreats behind the teeth and the sound whistles. The single most likely ظ error, more likely than ذ.
- ظ bounced like ط. ظ is not a qalqalah letter; a bounce means she has swapped in a heavy د.
- ط going light under a kasra (بَاطِل, طِين) even when it was heavy under a fatha. Heavy consonant plus bright vowel is the hardest combination in the lesson.
- The ر after a heavy letter failing to go heavy (عَصْر, ضَرَبَ) — a knock-on effect she will not notice unless you name it.
- In ٱلضَّآلِّينَ specifically: a short madd (two counts instead of six) — mangled more often than the ض itself — and a rushed shadda.

## Teacher script

- Open with the vowel, not the consonant. Say سَا then صَا back to back and ask her which sound moved — she will say the ‘a’. That is the whole lesson in ten seconds, and it hands her a self-test she can run at home.
- Teach ص first. It is the easiest heavy letter because س is already solid, so it isolates one single change: the tongue-body dome.
- For ض, spend the time on PLACE before weight. Say plainly that ض is not a heavy د — د is the tongue-tip on the gum ridge, ض is the tongue-side on the molars. Have her feel the contact with a finger against her cheek if that helps.
- ضِدّ is the diagnostic. Do not move past ض while ضِدّ comes out with two identical sounds. It is worth stalling the lesson here.
- Show her 75:22–23 side by side on the page. نَاضِرَة then نَاظِرَة, one āyah apart. Nothing you can say convinces a student that ض and ظ are genuinely different letters as fast as seeing the Qurʾān use both in consecutive lines.
- Note on ض for you, not for her: the early grammarians describe a lateral sound that no living reciter produces. Teach the modern standard — a pharyngealised stop kept distinct from د and ظ. Do not chase the reconstructed classical ض; it will confuse her and no recitation tradition uses it.
- For ط, join it back to last lesson: it is the only letter that is both heavy and bouncing. بَطْن and نُطْفَة are where both facts have to be true at once.
- Then immediately contrast ظ. عَظْم against بَطْن is the single most useful drill on the page — same shape of word, one bounces and one does not.
- Save al-Fātiḥah for the end, once ض is actually working. Correcting ٱلضَّآلِّينَ before the letter is secure just adds anxiety to something she recites seventeen times a day.
- Run every minimal-pair set ACROSS, never down. Reading down a column lets her set the tongue once and coast; reading across forces a switch on every item.
- Finish on the reciter clips. Hearing a qāriʾ darken the vowel after ص and ط is worth more than another explanation from you.

## Homework

Every session opens with سَا/صَا and تَا/طَا three times each. Daily: the ص, ض, ط and ظ word sets read aloud in full; the ضِدّ self-test; the bounce-or-hold row (بَطْن — عَظْم — قُطْن — عَزْم) twice through; the four minimal-pair sets read across rather than down. Tap through both reciter rows once a day and copy the qāriʾ aloud. Finish with وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ said slowly once, counting the madd to six. Watch the ص-vs-س video and the ض-vs-ظ video once each this week.

**Daily checklist:**

- Warm-up: سَا / صَا and تَا / طَا, three times each
- ص word set and ض word set, all rows, read aloud
- ضِدّ self-test — ض and د must not sound alike
- ط word set, bouncing every ط that carries a sukūn
- ظ word set, holding every ظ
- Bounce-or-hold across: بَطْن — عَظْم — قُطْن — عَزْم, twice
- The four minimal-pair sets read ACROSS: ص/س, ض/د, ض/ظ, ط/ت
- Both reciter rows tapped and copied aloud
- وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ once, with the madd counted to six
