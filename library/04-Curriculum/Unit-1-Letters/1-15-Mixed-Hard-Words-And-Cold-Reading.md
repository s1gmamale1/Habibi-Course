## Objectives

- Student can read fully-vocalised words containing any of ع ح ص ض ط ظ in mixed, unpredictable order, without a warm-up cue telling her which letter is coming.
- Student can read words that place two hard letters in the same short word (بَعْض, صُبْح, عَظْم, مُحِيط) without the second one degrading the first.
- Student can decode a set of genuinely unseen words unaided, combining sukūn, hamza and the heavy letters, and can sustain a timed twelve-word run with accuracy prioritised over speed.
- Student can run the six self-tests unprompted and name her own remaining weak letters and positions specifically, ahead of Checkpoint 1.

## Hook

Hand her a list of unseen words and say nothing except *read them*.

This is the last lesson of Phase 1 and its job is not to teach — it is to find out what she can do when nobody is standing beside her naming the rule. **Whatever breaks here is the real state of Phase 1**, and it is far better to find it now than at Checkpoint 1.

Then teach the part that outlasts the course: how to check herself when there is no teacher in the room.

## What they already know

The whole of Phase 1 — 28 letters, four positional forms, six non-joiners, three short vowels, sukūn, hamza, and the four heavy letters inside words. Nothing new is added today.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. How to check yourself, without a teacher

- You now have a test for every one of these, and none of them needs anyone else in the room.
- ع vs ء — put a finger on your throat. ع buzzes the whole way through; ء is one click.
- ص vs س — say سَا then صَا. If the ‘a’ does not move backwards, the dome is not happening.
- ض vs د — say ضِدّ. If the two sounds are the same, the ض is wrong.
- ط vs ت — say طِين then تِين. Again, listen to the vowel, not the consonant.
- ط vs ظ on a sukūn — بَطْن bounces, عَظْم is held.
- ح vs ه — a hand on the throat: ح is tight, ه is loose.
- Run the relevant test BEFORE the word, not after the mistake. Setting the tongue first is far easier than correcting it mid-word.

### 2. What to tell your teacher

- At the end of this lesson, name your own weak spots out loud. Being specific is what makes the next session useful.
- Say which single letter still feels least reliable, and in which position — start, middle, or end of a word.
- Say which minimal pair you still cannot hear the difference in, if any remain.
- Say whether the cold-read set felt like decoding letter by letter, or like reading. Both are acceptable right now; which one it is decides what comes next.
- This is the last lesson before Checkpoint 1. Anything you name here gets drilled before the gate — anything you hide gets tested at the gate instead.

**In plain terms:** Nothing new today. This is where you find out what you can read on your own — and learn how to catch your own mistakes when nobody is listening.

## Drills

**Mixed set A — ع and ح.** Read right to left, one row at a time. No warm-up on these — go straight in.

**Mixed set B — ص and ض.** Read right to left. Listen to the vowel after each heavy letter, not just the letter itself.

**Mixed set C — ط and ظ.** Read right to left. Bounce every ط that carries a sukūn; hold every ظ.

**Two hard letters in one word.** The real test. Each of these makes you do two difficult things in a row, with no easy letter in between to recover on.

**Minimal-pair gauntlet — read ACROSS.** Six pairs, shuffled and interleaved. Read straight across the row, never down the column — the whole point is that you have to switch on every word.

**Sukūn, hamza and a heavy letter at once.** Everything from Unit 1.4 in eight words. Name what is happening in each before you read it.

**Cold read — words you have not seen.** None of these appeared in Lesson 1.13 or 1.14. Read each one unaided, with no model first. Hesitating is fine; guessing is not — sound it out.

**Pacing check — twelve words, timed.** Read all twelve aloud while your teacher times you. Accuracy first, speed second. Note the time, and compare it next week.

## Listen-for

The teacher's checklist as it ships. **These are the shipped plain-string form**,
not the structured `ListenForSchema` the tajweed units use — see the banner above.

- The first hard letter in a word coming out correct and the second degrading — بَعْض with a good ع and a collapsed ض. This is the specific failure Unit 1.4 exists to surface, and only the two-hard-letters slide reliably exposes it.
- Accuracy that holds when the letter is announced and drops when it is not. If the grouped sets are clean and the gauntlet is not, the sound is not yet automatic — it is being produced by conscious effort, which will not survive Phase 2 reading.
- Reaching for a familiar word instead of decoding, especially in the cold-read set (رَصَد read as رَشَد, حَفِيظ as حَافِظ, نَضْرَة as 1.14’s نَاضِرَة). Note it as a reading-strategy issue, not a pronunciation one — the fix is different.
- Speed bought at the cost of makhraj in the pacing check. If the time improves while the quality drops, the time is not an improvement — say so plainly and re-run it slower.
- Whether she can name her own errors. A student who says ‘the ض at the end of words’ is ready for Phase 2 in a way that a student who says ‘I think it was okay’ is not, regardless of how they actually sounded.

## Teacher script

- Frame the lesson honestly: 1.13 and 1.14 taught the letters one at a time, with a warm-up before each. Today there is no warm-up and no grouping — that is the entire point, because real reading never announces which letter is next.
- Start by having HER run the six self-tests on herself, out loud, before you read a single word. If she can run them unprompted, she owns them; if she cannot, re-teach the tests before touching the word sets.
- Mixed sets A, B and C are still grouped by letter pair — they are the bridge. Do not skip them straight to the gauntlet even if she is doing well.
- ‘Two hard letters in one word’ is the highest-value slide in the lesson. بَعْض, عَظْم, صُبْح and مُحِيط are where a student who has been coping by concentrating on one target at a time finally comes unstuck. Spend real time here.
- Run the gauntlet strictly across the row. If she starts anticipating, shuffle by reading the rows bottom-to-top instead.
- For the cold-read set, model NOTHING first. Say explicitly that hesitating is fine and guessing is not — she should sound the word out rather than reach for a word she already knows that looks similar.
- Time the pacing check with a real timer and write the number down. It is the only quantitative measure in Unit 1.4, and it is the one she will find most motivating between now and the checkpoint.
- Close with her naming her own two weakest letters. If she names the same two you would have named, her self-monitoring is working, and that matters more for Phase 2 than any individual letter.
- Then go into Checkpoint 1. Unit 1.4 was added because letter recognition arrived before letter production — the gate has not changed, but she should reach it with far more reading behind her.

## Homework

Daily until Checkpoint 1: the six self-tests before reading anything; one of mixed sets A/B/C, rotating; all ten ‘two hard letters in one word’ items; the minimal-pair gauntlet read across, twice. Weekly only: the cold-read set (so it stays a real test) and the timed pacing check, with the time written down each week so the trend is visible. Before the checkpoint session, she should be able to name her two weakest letters and the position they fail in.

**Daily checklist:**

- Six self-tests run before reading anything
- One of mixed sets A / B / C, rotating daily
- ‘Two hard letters in one word’ — all ten, every day
- Minimal-pair gauntlet read across, twice
- Cold-read set — once a week only
- Pacing check — once a week, timed and written down
