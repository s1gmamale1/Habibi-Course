# Lesson 2-01 — Tanwīn: the three nunation marks

## Objectives

- Student can name and point to the three tanwīn marks — ـٌ ـً ـٍ — and say the sound each one adds: *-un*, *-an*, *-in*.
- Student can read a fully-vocalised noun carrying any of the three, producing the *n* sound without looking for a written ن.
- Student can explain that the alif written under a fatḥatān (كِتَابًا) is a carrier and is **not** lengthened when reading on.
- Student can read a shuffled column of 20 nouns marked with all three tanwīns at roughly one word per two seconds, without a warm-up cue.

## Hook

Lead with the error, out loud, before you define anything:

> "Read this: كِتَابًا."

Most students who have finished an alphabet course do one of two things here. Either they hunt the page for a ن that is not there and stall — because they were taught that every sound has a letter — or they see the alif and stretch it: *ki-taa-baa*.

Both come from the same wrong assumption: **that Arabic writes every sound as a letter.** It does not. Tanwīn is a sound with no letter of its own, and the alif that shows up under a fatḥatān is a chair, not a sound.

Say the wrong version deliberately — *ki-taa-baa* — then the right one — *kitāban* — and ask which one has an *n* in it. Then show the mark.

## What they already know

This builds directly on **Unit 1.2 and 1.3**, where the student learned that fatḥa, kasra and ḍamma open a letter into *a*, *i*, *u*, and on **lesson 1-13**, where sukūn was introduced as the mark that *closes* a letter with no vowel after it.

Tanwīn is the third thing a final vowel can do, and it is best introduced as exactly that: **the same three harakāt, drawn twice.**

The student also met ن as a letter long ago and can already read مِنْ. That is the hook for the whole lesson — the *n* in مِنْ is written; the *n* in كِتَابٍ is not; the two sound identical.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Anchor on the shape, not the name.** Write بُ, then بٌ. Ask what changed. The student will say "there are two now." Confirm it, then supply the sound: one ḍamma is *bu*, two ḍammas is *bun*. Do the same for kasra (بِ → بٍ, *bi* → *bin*) and fatḥa (بَ → بً, *ba* → *ban*). Doubling the mark adds an *n*. That is the entire rule, and it takes ninety seconds.

2. **Give the name once, then drop it.** *Tanwīn* (تنوين) means "adding a nūn" — nunation. The three marks have names too: **ḍammatān** (two ḍammas), **fatḥatān** (two fatḥas), **kasratān** (two kasras). Say them once so the student recognises them from a teacher or a book, then use plain English for the rest of the lesson.

3. **Kill the missing-nūn hunt immediately.** Write مِنْ and بَابٍ side by side. Both end in the sound *n*. One writes the letter, one draws the mark. Say the pair aloud several times: *min … bābin … min … bābin*. The student must hear that no new sound has been introduced today — only a new way of writing a sound they can already make.

4. **Now the carrying alif, and give it a whole step of its own.** Write بَابًا. The fatḥatān almost always needs something to sit on, so an alif is written after the last letter purely to hold it. **That alif is silent when you carry on reading.** بَابًا read into the next word is *bāban*, not *bābā*.
   - Two spellings take no extra alif: a word ending in tāʾ marbūṭah — سَاعَةً (*sāʿatan*) — and a word already ending in a hamza on the line after an alif — مَاءً (*māʾan*).
   - The student does not need to predict which is which. Recognition only: *if you see ـًا, do not lengthen it.*

5. **Where tanwīn is allowed.** Only on nouns and adjectives, and only on the **last letter of the word**. A verb never takes it. Nothing follows a tanwīn inside a word. If the student thinks she has found a tanwīn in the middle of a word, she has misread a shadda or a fatḥa — and both are lessons ahead of this one.

6. **Flag forward, then close the door.** What that unwritten *n* does to the letter that follows it — sometimes hidden, sometimes hummed, sometimes swapped for an *m* — is the largest single topic in Unit 3. Say the sentence, do not demonstrate it, and do not answer the follow-up question beyond "Unit 3". Today the *n* is a clean, plain *n* every time.

Qurʾānic words she can already read are too few to shuffle.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the same word, three ways.** Read across, then down. Say each row three times before moving on.

| | *-un* | *-an* | *-in* |
|---|---|---|---|
| door | بَابٌ | بَابًا | بَابٍ |
| pen | قَلَمٌ | قَلَمًا | قَلَمٍ |
| boy | وَلَدٌ | وَلَدًا | وَلَدٍ |
| lesson | دَرْسٌ | دَرْسًا | دَرْسٍ |
| book | كِتَابٌ | كِتَابًا | كِتَابٍ |
| house | سُوقٌ | سُوقًا | سُوقٍ |

Point to each mark with your finger and say what you see *before* you say the word: "two ḍammas — *bābun*." Do that for the first two rows only, then stop naming and just read.

**Drill 2 — written n against unwritten n.** Read the pair, left then right, six times:

مِنْ … بَابٍ  ·  عَنْ … قَلَمٍ  ·  كُنْ … وَلَدًا  ·  إِنْ … دَرْسٌ

Both halves of each pair end in the same sound. If the second half sounds shorter or weaker than the first, you are under-saying the tanwīn.

**Drill 3 — the silent alif.** Read these and stop your voice cleanly on the *n*. Nothing comes after it:

كِتَابًا · قَلَمًا · وَلَدًا · دَرْسًا · جَبَلًا · عَمَلًا

Now read the same six with a word after them, so you can feel the *n* run straight into the next word:

كِتَابًا جَدِيدًا · قَلَمًا جَمِيلًا · وَلَدًا صَغِيرًا

**Drill 4 — the no-alif spellings.** These carry fatḥatān with no alif at all. They sound exactly like the ones above:

سَاعَةً · مَدِينَةً · مَاءً · دُعَاءً

**Drill 5 — shuffled cold read.** Twenty words, no cue as to which mark is coming. Read down the column at a steady pace. Accuracy first, then speed:

سَلَامٌ · جَبَلٍ · بَيْتًا · رَجُلٌ · طَرِيقٍ · كِتَابًا · شَجَرَةٌ · قَمَرٍ · بَحْرًا · عَمَلٌ · مِفْتَاحٍ · وَرَقَةً · دَرْسًا · صَدِيقٌ · سُوقٍ · نَهْرًا · حَدِيقَةٌ · قَلَمٍ · بَابًا · مَطَرٌ

## Listen-for

**1. Hunting for a nūn on the page.**
- *Listen for:* a stall of a half-second at the end of the word, eyes moving back over the last letter, then the word restarted.
- *Why it happens:* every previous lesson taught one sound per letter. Tanwīn is the first sound in the course with no letter of its own, and the student is doing exactly what she was trained to do.
- *Correction cue:* "The *n* is in the mark, not in a letter. Two of anything means *n*."

**2. Lengthening the carrying alif — *kitābā* for *kitāban*.**
- *Listen for:* an open *ā* at the end of the word with no consonant closing it.
- *Why it happens:* alif has meant "long a" in every word she has read so far. This is the first time it means nothing.
- *Correction cue:* "That alif is a chair for the mark to sit on. Close the word with an *n*." Cover the alif with a fingertip and have her read the word again.

**3. Under-saying the *n* until it disappears.**
- *Listen for:* *bābu* instead of *bābun*, especially by the third or fourth word of a column, when concentration drops.
- *Why it happens:* nothing on the page enforces it, so it is the first thing to be dropped under load.
- *Correction cue:* "I heard *bābu*. Where did the *n* go?" Then re-run the مِنْ / بَابٍ pair from Drill 2 — hearing the written *n* next to the unwritten one restores it faster than any explanation.

**4. Adding a vowel after the *n* — *bābuna*.**
- *Listen for:* an extra beat at the end of the word; a two-syllable ending where there should be one.
- *Why it happens:* the same reflex that puts a helper vowel after a sukūn, which you diagnosed in lesson 1-13. It is one habit, not two.
- *Correction cue:* "Stop the sound dead on the *n*." Clap on the stop and have her land on your clap — the same diagnostic she already knows from sukūn.

**5. Reading the fatḥatān as *-am* or as a plain *-a*.**
- *Listen for:* a lip closure at the end of the word, or no consonant at all.
- *Why it happens:* the fatḥatān is the least visually distinct of the three, and it is the only one with an extra letter next to it — so it gets guessed at.
- *Correction cue:* "All three tanwīns end in the same letter sound. Only the vowel before it changes."

**Gate for the teacher.** Do not start lesson 2-02 in the same session if the carrying alif is still being lengthened. Drill 3 is the pass condition — six clean *-an* endings in a row, twice.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **Three minutes — the marks alone.** Write ـٌ ـً ـٍ on a scrap of paper and say the sound of each, in a shuffled order, until you never hesitate.
2. **Five minutes — Drill 1 aloud.** All six rows, read across. Record thirty seconds of it on your phone, play it back, and check every word ends in an audible *n*.
3. **Five minutes — Drill 5 cold read.** Time yourself on the second run only. Do not chase the clock before day three.
4. **Three minutes — the silent alif.** Drill 3, first block. Put a fingertip over each carrying alif as you read.
5. **Write four words.** Copy any four words from Drill 5 by hand and put a different tanwīn on each one. Writing the mark is what makes you stop confusing ـً with a plain fatḥa.

Bring to the next session: the one word from Drill 5 you got wrong most often, and whether the error was the *n* or the alif.
