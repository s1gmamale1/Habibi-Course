# Lesson 2-04 — Sukūn II: two sukūns colliding, and the bouncing letters

## Objectives

- Student can explain why Arabic never lets two closed letters run together in continuous reading, and can point to the printed helper vowel that fixes it in قُمِ ٱلَّيْلَ.
- Student can read a phrase in which a long vowel is dropped before a following sukūn — فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ read as *fil-arḍ*.
- Student can produce a clean bounce on ق ط ب ج د carrying a sukūn, and can state that the bounce is not a vowel.
- Student can read a mixed set in which some ق ط ب ج د are closed and some carry a vowel, bouncing only the closed ones.

## Hook

Two errors today, so lead with both. Write the phrase and ask her to read it exactly as it looks:

> **قُمِ ٱلَّيْلَ**

Nearly everyone reads *qum … al-layl*, with a break, because that is what the two words look like standing apart. Then point at the kasra sitting under the مـ and ask what it is doing there. It was not in قُمْ a moment ago.

Now the second one. Say *ib* and then *ib-uh*, and ask which sounded like the ب at the end of a word. Most students at this stage produce one of the two, and neither is right — the first is silent and dies, the second has a vowel glued on.

The corrective sentence for the whole lesson: **Arabic will not let two closed letters touch, and it will not let a closed ق ط ب ج د die silently. Both problems have the same cause — a consonant with no vowel to lean on — and the page has already solved both for you. Today you learn to see the solution instead of being surprised by it.**

## What they already know

- **Lesson 1-13** named the five bouncing letters — ق ط ب ج د, remembered as **قطب جد** — and had her bounce them. It also gave the gate that still applies today: a bounce is only safe once a plain sukūn is clean, because "add a little bounce" is heard by a beginner as permission to add a vowel.
- **Lesson 1-14** gave the contrast that this lesson depends on: **ط bounces, ظ is held.** Both are heavy; only one is in قطب جد.
- **Lesson 1-13** also introduced hamzat al-waṣl in beginner form — the ٱ of الـ that is skipped when you join from the previous word. That skip is precisely what causes today's collisions, so remind her of it before step 2 rather than after.
- **Lesson 2-03** built the clusters. Today is what happens when a cluster has no room left.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Re-gate the bounce, in thirty seconds.** بَعْ, حَمْ, صَبْ with the clap test from 1-13. If a helper vowel is present on a plain sukūn, **teach part A only and postpone part B to the next session.** A wrong qalqalah is worse than no qalqalah; 1-13 already made that call and it still stands.

### Part A — two sukūns colliding

2. **Show the collision before the fix.** Write قُمْ, then ٱلَّيْلَ. She reads them separately: *qum*, *al-layl*. Now join them. The ٱ of الـ is skipped when joining — she knows this from 1-13 — so what is left is مْ meeting لْ. Two closed letters, back to back. Ask her to say it. She cannot, or she inserts a break. **That is the point.** Arabic speech does not contain that shape.

3. **The three fixes, in order of how often she will meet them.**
   - **A helper vowel on the first one — almost always a kasra.** قُمِ ٱلَّيْلَ → *qumi-l-layl*. قُلِ ٱللَّهُمَّ → *quli-llāhumma*. **The vowel is printed in the mushaf.** Say that plainly and let her check it with her eyes; it converts a rule she would have to apply into a mark she only has to notice.
   - **A handful of words take a different vowel.** مِنْ becomes مِنَ before الـ — *mina-llāhi*. The endings ـتُمْ and ـهِمْ take a ḍamma — *antumu-l-*, *ʿalayhimu-l-*. Do not systematise this. It is printed too; she reads what she sees. The full account is in lesson 2-09.
   - **A long vowel is dropped altogether.** فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ is *fil-arḍ*, not *fī-l-arḍ*. The long *ī* has no helper vowel to give, so it simply goes. This one is **not** obvious from the page and is the fix she will actually stumble on.

4. **Make the "already printed" point stick.** Read three collisions straight off the page with no explanation, having her follow the vowels with her finger. The takeaway is a posture, not a rule: *when a word ends in an unexpected vowel, it is not a misprint and you have not lost your place. It is the join.*

### Part B — the bouncing letters

5. **Bring back قطب جد and add the one condition.** These five bounce **only when they are closed**. When they carry a vowel they behave like any other letter. Write the contrast pair on the board:
   - ٱهْدِنَا — the د carries a kasra. **No bounce.**
   - لَمْ يَلِدْ — the د is closed. **Bounce.**
   This is the single most common qalqalah error in the wild and it is worth the whole board.

6. **Define the bounce by what it is not.** It is a dry echo of the letter itself: *ib*, with a small detached release at the end. It is **not** *iba*, not *ibi*, not *ibu*. Any vowel colour in the bounce is wrong. Say all three wrong versions deliberately so she can hear the difference between an echo and a vowel — she has to be able to catch this herself at home.

7. **Two places a bounce happens.**
   - **Inside a word,** where the letter carries a written sukūn: يَقْطَع, أَبْصَار, يَجْعَل, يَدْخُل, يَطْمَع.
   - **At the end of a word when you stop on it.** Stopping makes the last letter closed even if the text shows a vowel — so وَتَبَّ, stopped, is held for the doubled ب and then bounced. She met the holding half of that in 1-14 with ٱلضَّآلِّينَ.

8. **Name what you are not teaching.** The bounce is stronger at a stop than in the middle of a word, and the grades have names — see [[Qalqalah-Sughra]] and [[Qalqalah-Kubra]]. That is Unit 3.4. Today: bounce them all the same, cleanly. Do not open the door; 1-13 already closed it once.

## Examples

| Text | Ref | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| قُمِ ٱلَّيْلَ | 73:2 | collision fixed by a printed kasra — *qumi-l-layl* |
| قُلِ ٱللَّهُمَّ | 3:26 | the same fix on a different verb — *quli-llāhumma* |
| ٱهْدِنَا | 1:6 | هْ into د; the د has a vowel, so **no** bounce |
| لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ | 112:3 | two closed dāls — **both** bounce; the mīms are plain |
| وَتَبَّ | 111:1 | at the stop — hold the doubled ب, then bounce |

**Teacher note.** 112:3 is the ideal drill line for this lesson because it puts a bouncing letter and a non-bouncing sukūn in the same short āyah four times over. Have her mark the two دْ with a pencil before reading.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the collision, on the page.** Read each phrase and put a finger on the helper vowel as you say it:

قُمِ ٱلَّيْلَ · قُلِ ٱللَّهُمَّ · مِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ · أَنِ ٱعْبُدُوا

**Drill 2 — the dropped long vowel.** These do not show you the fix. Read them as one piece:

فِى ٱلْبَيْتِ · فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · إِلَى ٱلْمَسْجِدِ · عَلَى ٱلْبَابِ

Say each one twice: once wrong, with the long vowel kept — *fī-l-bayt* — and once right — *fil-bayt*. You need to hear both to know which one you are producing.

**Drill 3 — bounce or no bounce.** Read down. Bounce only where the letter is closed:

| Closed — bounce | Vowelled — no bounce |
|---|---|
| يَقْطَع | قَلَم |
| أَبْصَار | بَاب |
| يَجْعَل | جَبَل |
| يَدْخُل | دَرَس |
| يَطْمَع | طَرِيق |

Read **across** each row, not down. The pair is the drill; a single column teaches nothing.

**Drill 4 — bouncing letter against a non-bouncing one.** All of these are closed. Only the left column bounces:

| Bounces | Does not |
|---|---|
| صَبْر | صَرْف |
| فَجْر | فَحْص |
| وَقْت | وَصْف |
| يَبْدَأ | يَحْمِل |
| مَطْبَخ | مَسْجِد |

**Drill 5 — the 1-14 contrast, back again.** ط bounces, ظ is held:

بَطْن · عَظْم · قَطْر · نَظْم · يَطْلُب · يَظْلِم

**Drill 6 — stopping.** Read each word and stop on it. The last letter becomes closed, so the bouncing letters bounce:

كِتَابْ · قَرِيبْ · مَسْجِدْ · صَدِيقْ · طَرِيقْ · وَلَدْ

**Drill 7 — the real line.** 112:3 — لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ. Read it four times: slow with a pencil mark on each دْ, then at pace with no marks. Then listen to a reciter and check that your bounce is as dry as theirs.

## Listen-for

**1. Breaking the phrase at the collision — *qum · al-layl*.**
- *Listen for:* a stop and restart, usually with a breath.
- *Why it happens:* two written words look like two units, and she has no reason yet to expect a vowel to appear from nowhere.
- *Correction cue:* "The kasra is printed. Read what you see." Have her point at it before she starts the phrase.

**2. Keeping the long vowel — *fī-l-arḍ*.**
- *Listen for:* a full two-count *ī* or *ā* immediately before a closed letter.
- *Why it happens:* the page gives her no signal at all here — the long vowel is written and simply not read. This is genuinely counter-intuitive and needs repetition, not explanation.
- *Correction cue:* Cover the ى with a fingertip and have her read again.

**3. Bouncing a letter that carries a vowel — *ihdinā* with a bounced د.**
- *Listen for:* an extra beat inside a perfectly ordinary word.
- *Why it happens:* over-application, and it appears the moment the five letters are named. She has learned to look for the letter instead of the mark.
- *Correction cue:* "Which mark is on it?" Do not say the answer — make her read the mark. The condition is the sukūn, not the letter.

**4. The bounce carrying a vowel colour — *ib-uh*, *ib-ah*.**
- *Listen for:* an identifiable vowel after the release. If you can transcribe it as *a*, *i* or *u*, it is wrong.
- *Why it happens:* this is the helper-vowel habit from lesson 2-03 wearing a different hat, and it is exactly the risk 1-13 flagged when it gated qalqalah. It is one error, not two.
- *Correction cue:* "Echo, not a syllable." Knock once on the table for the letter and let the sound die — the fading knock is the model.

**5. No bounce at all — the closed ق ط ب ج د going silent.**
- *Listen for:* a missing letter at the end of a stopped word; *ṣadī* for *ṣadīq*.
- *Why it happens:* over-correcting number 4, and the correct fix for the leaky letters in 2-03 ("cut it") applied to letters that need the opposite.
- *Correction cue:* "That one needs a release. Let me hear the end of it."

**6. Bouncing ظ, or holding ط.**
- *Listen for:* Drill 5 read down instead of across, with both columns treated the same.
- *Why it happens:* both letters are heavy, both are closed, and heaviness is the feature she was drilling most recently in 1-14.
- *Correction cue:* "Heavy is not the test. Is it in قطب جد?" Have her recite the five letters, then answer her own question.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **Two minutes — warm-up.** قطب جد said aloud, then the five letters each with a sukūn: قْ طْ بْ جْ دْ. Every day.
2. **Four minutes — Drill 3, read across.** The pair is the whole point; never read one column alone.
3. **Four minutes — Drill 2, the dropped long vowel.** Wrong version then right version, both out loud.
4. **Three minutes — Drill 5.** ط against ظ. If you cannot hear a difference between بَطْن and عَظْم, mark it down and say so next session.
5. **Four minutes — Drill 7.** 112:3 four times, then once against a recording.

Bring to the next session: whether your bounce has a vowel in it. Record ten seconds of Drill 6 and listen back at half speed — that is the only reliable way to tell, and it costs nothing.
