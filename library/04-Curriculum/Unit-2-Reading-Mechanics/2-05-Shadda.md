# Lesson 2-05 — Shadda: the doubling mark

## Objectives

- Student can state what a shadda is mechanically — one letter written once and said twice, the first closed and the second carrying the vowel — and can split any shadda'd word into its two halves out loud.
- Student can hold a doubled letter for a full two counts, and can hear the difference between دَرَسَ and دَرَّسَ well enough to say which one she just read.
- Student can find the vowel that belongs to a shadda, including the kasra written under the letter rather than under the mark.
- Student can read 20 shadda'd words cold without either collapsing the doubling or splitting the word into two.

## Hook

Write two words on the board with no explanation:

> **دَرَسَ** — he studied
> **دَرَّسَ** — he taught

Ask her to read both. Almost everyone reads them the same. Then say them properly, one after the other, and give the meanings again. One small mark, and the man in the sentence has changed sides of the classroom.

Then the sentence that carries the whole lesson: **a shadda is not emphasis, and it is not decoration. It is a second letter that nobody wrote down.**

And the error to name before it happens, because it is the one that will actually occur: **beginners do not usually forget the shadda. They see it, they acknowledge it, and they hold it for about a third as long as it needs.** Say *rab* and then *rab-b*, and ask her to tell you which one took longer.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 1-14** already put a shadda in front of her, in the hardest possible word: **وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ**. She was told to hold it — "two full counts of molar contact" — and told why it is there: the ل of الـ assimilated into the ض, so *al-* + *ḍāllīn* becomes *aḍ-ḍāllīn*. Start from that word. She has been holding a shadda in every rakʿah since that lesson; today she finds out what the mark is called and that there is a system behind it.
- **Lesson 1-13** gave sukūn, and this lesson depends on it completely — the first half of every doubled letter *is* a sukūn. If she can read بَعْ she can read the front half of عَبَّ.
- **Lesson 2-04** gave the collision rule: two closed letters cannot touch. A shadda is the one shape where a closed letter is immediately followed by the same letter opened — which is why it is legal and why it must be held rather than broken.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Build it from sukūn, do not define it.** Write the two halves separately first:
   - رَبْ + بُ
   Read them as two pieces, slowly, with a gap. Then close the gap. Then write **رَبُّ** and say: this is the same thing, written once, with the shadda standing in for the letter you deleted. The student should watch the second ب disappear off the board.

2. **Name it once.** The mark is a **shadda** (شَدَّة, "strengthening"), also called **tashdīd**. The classical name for the property is *shiddah* — see [[Shiddah]]. Use "shadda" for the rest of the course.

3. **Where the vowel lives, and this trips people up on real pages.** The shadda itself carries no sound. The vowel of the second half sits **with** it:
   - fatḥa and ḍamma go **above** the shadda — رَبَّ, رَبُّ
   - kasra normally goes **below the letter**, underneath the shadda — رَبِّ
   In some print, the kasra is tucked under the shadda instead. Show her both, so a different mushaf does not stop her. **The rule she needs: find the shadda, then look above and below it for the vowel. There is always exactly one.**

4. **Timing, and give this its own block of the session.** Two counts. Use the same finger-tap you used for length in lesson 2-02, but tap on the *consonant* rather than a vowel. Three ways to feel it, and different students take to different ones:
   - **مّ and نّ** — you can hum them. Have her hold the hum and count. These two are the easiest entry point, so start here.
   - **لّ, رّ, سّ, شّ** — you can sustain them. Same method.
   - **بّ, تّ, دّ, كّ, قّ** — you cannot sustain them; what you hold is the *closure*, and the two counts are silence with the mouth shut. This is the group that gets under-held, because holding nothing feels like doing nothing.

5. **The hum, flagged and required.** When the doubled letter is **نّ or مّ**, the hold is nasal — a two-count hum through the nose. Pinch the nose and say *inna*: it should be blocked. The rule has a name, [[Ghunnah]], and belongs to Unit 3; the *sound* is not optional today, because إِنَّ and ٱلنَّاس and ثُمَّ are unavoidable in any page she opens.

6. **Meaning pairs, to prove it is not cosmetic.** This is the step that makes the two counts feel worth the effort:
   - دَرَسَ *he studied* / دَرَّسَ *he taught*
   - عَلِمَ *he knew* / عَلَّمَ *he taught*
   - كَسَرَ *he broke it* / كَسَّرَ *he smashed it to pieces*
   Read each pair and have her tell you which is which by ear alone, with the board covered.

7. **One structural fact, said once.** A word can never *begin* with a shadda, because the first half is closed and a closed letter needs something in front of it to lean on. This is the same reason hamzat al-waṣl exists — she met it in 1-13, and it is the subject of lesson 2-09.

## Examples

| Text | Ref | Split |
|---|---|---|
| ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ | 112:2 | *al-lā-hu · aṣ-ṣa-mad* |
| تَبَّتْ | 111:1 | *tab-bat* |
| إِيَّاكَ | 1:5 | *iy-yā-ka* |
| بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ | 114:1 | *bi-rab-bi · an-nās* — and the نّ hums |

**Teacher note on إِيَّاكَ.** A doubled ي is the hardest one to hear, because the sound is close to a vowel and the two halves blur. Use it as the last example, not the first, and check it with the meaning: إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ is *You alone we worship*. She says it seventeen times a day.

**Teacher note on ٱلصَّمَدُ and ٱلنَّاسِ.** Both doublings come from a lām of الـ assimilating — the same thing that happened in ٱلضَّآلِّينَ in 1-14. Point at it, name it as "the same thing as in al-Fātiḥah", and leave the system for lesson 2-06.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — split and join.** Say the left column, then the right, then the left again:

| Two pieces | Written once |
|---|---|
| رَبْ + بُ | رَبُّ |
| حَقْ + قُ | حَقُّ |
| أُمْ + مُ | أُمُّ |
| سِنْ + نُ | سِنُّ |
| كُلْ + لُ | كُلُّ |
| جَدْ + دُ | جَدُّ |

**Drill 2 — the hum.** Pinch your nose on each one. If the sound is not blocked, you are not humming:

إِنَّ · أَنَّ · ثُمَّ · أُمَّة · جَنَّة · مُحَمَّد · ٱلنَّاس · عَمَّ

**Drill 3 — the silent hold.** These double a letter you cannot sustain. The two counts are silence, with the mouth shut. Do not rush them:

حَقّ · جَدّ · شَكّ · حَبّ · قِطّ · مُدّ · سَتّ · دَقّ

Count the two beats on your fingers *during* the silence. That is the only way to know you gave it the time.

**Drill 4 — the meaning pairs.** Read across. Then have someone read one at random and say which you heard:

| No shadda | Shadda |
|---|---|
| دَرَسَ | دَرَّسَ |
| عَلِمَ | عَلَّمَ |
| كَسَرَ | كَسَّرَ |
| نَزَلَ | نَزَّلَ |
| فَرَقَ | فَرَّقَ |
| قَدَمَ | قَدَّمَ |

**Drill 5 — find the vowel.** Read each word and say the vowel of the shadda aloud before you read it — "fatḥa", "kasra", "ḍamma":

رَبَّ · رَبِّ · رَبُّ · حَقَّ · حَقِّ · حَقُّ · أُمَّ · أُمِّ · أُمُّ

**Drill 6 — cold column.** Twenty words. No cue:

مُدَرِّس · سَيَّارَة · طَيِّب · قُوَّة · مَرَّة · شِدَّة · جَنَّة · سُكَّر · ذَرَّة · حَمَّام · بَقَّال · صَحَّة · فِضَّة · قِصَّة · مُحَمَّد · عَمَّان · لَذَّة · دُكَّان · نَجَّار · خَيَّاط

## Listen-for

**1. The doubling collapsing entirely — *rabu* for *rabbu*.**
- *Listen for:* a word with one beat where it needs two. Check against the no-shadda partner in Drill 4: if دَرَّسَ and دَرَسَ sound the same, it has collapsed.
- *Why it happens:* the shadda has no sound of its own on the page, so under reading load it is treated as an accent mark and skipped.
- *Correction cue:* "That was *darasa*. I asked for *darrasa*." Give the meaning back — the meaning is what makes it matter.

**2. Under-holding — a doubling that is present but short.**
- *Listen for:* a doubling you can hear but could not count. This is the priority error for this lesson and it is far more common than a full collapse.
- *Why it happens:* she has satisfied herself that she "did the shadda", and there is nothing on the page specifying how long. Nothing in Uzbek, Persian or Turkish requires a two-count consonant.
- *Correction cue:* Tap twice on the table and have her fit the letter into the two taps. Do not describe it — give the beat. Same method as the long vowel in 2-02, deliberately, because it is the same underlying weakness.

**3. Breaking it into two separate letters — *rab · bu*.**
- *Listen for:* a gap, sometimes a tiny breath, between the halves.
- *Why it happens:* over-correction, and it usually shows up right after you fix error 1. It is also exactly what Drill 1's left column asks for, so it is a drill artefact — mention that when it happens.
- *Correction cue:* "One letter, held. Not two letters with a door between them." Have her run the left and right columns of Drill 1 back to back until the difference is obvious to her.

**4. A helper vowel between the halves — *rab-u-bu*.**
- *Listen for:* three beats instead of two.
- *Why it happens:* the front half of a shadda is a closed letter, so the cluster-breaking habit from lesson 2-03 comes straight back. Diagnose it as that, not as a new fault.
- *Correction cue:* Go back to the clap test on بَعْ. Fix the sukūn first, then return.

**5. No hum on نّ and مّ.**
- *Listen for:* an oral *n* or *m*, held but not nasal. The pinch test settles it in one second.
- *Why it happens:* she is holding the consonant as instructed and nobody told her the air goes through the nose.
- *Correction cue:* "Pinch your nose and say it again." Physical, immediate, self-verifying.

**6. Missing the kasra under the letter — reading رَبِّ as *rabba*.**
- *Listen for:* the wrong vowel on the second half, especially with kasra.
- *Why it happens:* the shadda sits above the letter and pulls the eye upward, so a mark below it goes unseen.
- *Correction cue:* "Look under the letter too." In Drill 5 make her name the vowel out loud before reading — naming forces the eye down.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **Three minutes — Drill 1.** Split, then join, then split. Every day; this is the drill that builds the mechanism.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 2 with the nose pinch.** If a word is not blocked, do that word five more times.
3. **Four minutes — Drill 3.** The silent holds, counting the two beats on your fingers during the silence.
4. **Four minutes — Drill 4.** Read across. Then read one column only into your phone, play it back the next day, and see whether you can tell which word you read.
5. **Three minutes — Drill 6.** Cold, once slow and once at pace.

Bring to the next session: one word from Drill 6 you are not sure you are holding long enough. We will time it.
