# Lesson 2-06 — Shadda in words

## Objectives

- Student can read ٱلـ correctly in front of any letter: pronouncing the lām before a moon letter, and dropping it into a doubling before a sun letter.
- Student can hold a doubled letter at the end of a word when stopping on it, including releasing a doubled ق or ب with a bounce.
- Student can read a word carrying both a shadda and a tanwīn — سِجِّيلٍ, حَقًّا — without losing either.
- Student can read a mixed page-like column of 24 items containing sun lāms, moon lāms and mid-word shaddas, cold.

## Hook

Write the two words and ask her to read them:

> **ٱلْقَمَر** — the moon
> **ٱلشَّمْس** — the sun

Most students read the second one *al-shams*. Say it back to her that way, then say *ash-shams*, and ask which one she has heard in the mosque.

Now the corrective frame, and it is a big one: **you have been reading الـ since lesson 1-13, and for half the letters in the alphabet you have been reading it wrong.** Not because you were taught wrong, but because الـ has two behaviours and you were only given one.

Then the second surprise of the lesson, which usually arrives on its own: the lām does not vanish. **It turns into the next letter.** That is why the next letter has a shadda on it. The doubling she learned to hold last lesson is the lām she thinks she is dropping.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 2-05** — what a shadda is, that it is one letter said twice, that it is held for two counts, and that نّ and مّ hum.
- **Lesson 1-14** — ٱلضَّآلِّينَ, where she was told directly that *al-* + *ḍāllīn* becomes *aḍ-ḍāllīn* because the lām assimilated. **That sentence is the whole of today's rule, and she has already been given it once.** Open the lesson with it if the hook does not land: she has been reading a sun letter correctly, in one word, since Unit 1.4.
- **Lesson 1-13** — hamzat al-waṣl and the ٱ of الـ, which is skipped when joining. The alif behaves the same way regardless of what follows; only the lām changes.
- **Lesson 2-04** — the bounce on ق ط ب ج د when closed. It comes back today on doubled letters at a stop.
- **Lesson 2-02** — stopping. Every word in Drill 4 below is a stop.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Sort the alphabet in front of her, do not hand her a list.** Write الـ and try it against a handful of letters, reading each aloud: ٱلْبَاب, ٱلْقَمَر, ٱلْمَاء — the lām speaks. ٱلشَّمْس, ٱلنَّاس, ٱلتِّين — it does not. Ask what she notices about *where* the second group is made in the mouth. She may or may not get it; either way, the answer is worth saying: **the sun letters are the ones made near the lām, at or around the front of the tongue.** The lām gives up and becomes them.

2. **Give the two names and the visual test.** **Sun letters** (الشمسية) — the lām is silent and the next letter doubles. **Moon letters** (القمرية) — the lām is read with a sukūn on it. The formal treatment is [[Lam-Shamsiyyah]] and [[Lam-Qamariyyah]] in Unit 3; what she needs today is the reading, and the reading is decidable by eye:
   - **If the letter after the lām carries a shadda, the lām is silent.**
   - **If the lām itself carries a sukūn, read it.**
   The mushaf prints both marks. She does not memorise fourteen letters to read the page — she memorises them to *write*, which is not this lesson.

3. **Say the fourteen once, for recognition.** Sun: **ت ث د ذ ر ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ل ن**. Everything else is a moon letter. Do not drill the list; drill the words.

4. **Doubling at the end of a word — the case Drill 4 exists for.** حَقّ, رَبّ, أُمّ. When she stops on one of these, the doubling is still there: the closure is held and *then* released. Two sub-cases:
   - An ordinary letter — أُمّ is held and released quietly.
   - A **bouncing** letter — ٱلْحَقّ is held and then **bounced**, because at a stop the ق is closed and ق is in قطب جد. She met this exact shape in lesson 2-04 with وَتَبَّ.

5. **Shadda plus tanwīn in one word.** حَقًّا, جَنَّةٌ, سِجِّيلٍ. Two marks doing two different jobs, and the eye has to take both. Slow this down: read the doubling, *then* the ending. The commonest outcome is that one of the two gets dropped, and it is usually the shadda, because the tanwīn is at the edge of the word where the eye lands last and stays longest.

6. **Two shaddas in one word.** مُدَرِّسِيَّة is too much; use ٱلصِّدِّيق, ٱلْحَجَّاج, مُتَّقِين. Both get their full two counts. This is where under-holding reappears even in a student who fixed it last week — the second one gets rushed because the word is now long.

7. **Read a real line, joined.** End the session by reading 114:6 — مِنَ ٱلْجِنَّةِ وَٱلنَّاسِ — which contains a moon lām and a sun lām eight letters apart, plus a doubled نّ that hums, plus a collision fixed by a printed fatḥa (مِنَ) from lesson 2-04. Four lessons in one āyah. Say that to her; it is the first time the unit has visibly stacked.

| بِٱلْحَقِّ | 103:3 | moon lām; doubled ق held then bounced at a stop |
| سِجِّيلٍ | 105:4 | mid-word shadda under a word-final tanwīn |

**Teacher note on 1:1.** She has recited this line more times than any other in her life and almost certainly says it correctly by ear already. Use that: read it, then show her *why* what she already says is right. Confirming an existing correct habit is worth more here than correcting a new error, because it tells her the system describes real recitation rather than replacing it.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — sort by eye.** Read each one and say "sun" or "moon" *before* you read it:

ٱلْقَمَر · ٱلشَّمْس · ٱلْبَيْت · ٱلنَّهْر · ٱلْكِتَاب · ٱلدَّرْس · ٱلْوَلَد · ٱلرَّجُل · ٱلْمَسْجِد · ٱلطَّرِيق · ٱلْجَبَل · ٱلصَّدِيق

The test is mechanical: shadda on the next letter means sun, sukūn on the lām means moon.

**Drill 2 — the same noun, with and without الـ.** Read across. The bare word tells you what the doubling is made of:

| Bare | With الـ |
|---|---|
| شَمْس | ٱلشَّمْس |
| نَاس | ٱلنَّاس |
| دَرْس | ٱلدَّرْس |
| رَجُل | ٱلرَّجُل |
| تِين | ٱلتِّين |
| صَدِيق | ٱلصَّدِيق |

**Drill 3 — sun and moon side by side.** One breath per pair:

ٱلْقَمَر وَٱلشَّمْس · ٱلْبَيْت وَٱلطَّرِيق · ٱلْكِتَاب وَٱلدَّرْس · ٱلْجَبَل وَٱلنَّهْر

**Drill 4 — stopping on a doubling.** Stop dead on each. Hold the last letter, then release it:

أُمّ · حَبّ · شَكّ · جَدّ · ٱلْحَقّ · ٱلرَّبّ

The last two release with a **bounce** — ق and ب are in قطب جد. The first four do not.

**Drill 5 — shadda and tanwīn together.** Read the doubling first, then the ending:

حَقًّا · جَنَّةٌ · قُوَّةٍ · مَرَّةً · سِجِّيلٍ · شِدَّةً

**Drill 6 — two shaddas in one word.** Give both their full time. The second one is the one that gets rushed:

ٱلصِّدِّيق · ٱلْحَجَّاج · مُتَّقِين · مُسْتَقِرًّا · ٱلْمُصَلِّين · ٱلسَّجَّاد

**Drill 7 — cold column, 24 items.** Sun lāms, moon lāms and mid-word shaddas mixed, no warning:

ٱلْقَلَم · مُدَرِّس · ٱلسَّمَاء · ٱلْبَاب · سَيَّارَة · ٱلذَّهَب · ٱلْعَمَل · قُوَّة · ٱلزَّيْت · ٱلْحَدِيقَة · جَنَّة · ٱلثَّوْب · ٱلْغُرْفَة · سُكَّر · ٱلضَّيْف · ٱلْفِيل · مَرَّة · ٱلظُّهْر · ٱلْيَوْم · فِضَّة · ٱللَّيْل · ٱلْهَوَاء · قِصَّة · ٱلنَّجَّار

**Drill 8 — the stacked āyah.** 114:6 — مِنَ ٱلْجِنَّةِ وَٱلنَّاسِ. Read it four times, then once against a reciter. Check three things in your own reading: the printed fatḥa on مِنَ, the humming نّ of ٱلنَّاس, and that you did not say a lām in ٱلنَّاس.

## Listen-for

**1. Reading the lām of a sun word — *al-shams*.**
- *Listen for:* an audible *l* immediately before a doubled letter.
- *Why it happens:* the letter لـ is on the page, and every lesson so far has taught that letters on the page get said. This is the first time one does not.
- *Correction cue:* "Is there a sukūn on that lām?" Make her look. The absence of the sukūn is the evidence, and finding it herself makes it stick.

**2. Dropping the lām *and* the doubling — *ashams* with a single s.**
- *Listen for:* a word that is a beat short. Compare against the bare noun in Drill 2.
- *Why it happens:* she has correctly learned that the lām is silent, and heard "silent" as "delete". Nobody told her the lām went *into* the next letter.
- *Correction cue:* "The lām did not disappear, it became the ش. That is what you are holding." This sentence is the whole lesson; expect to say it several times.

**3. Losing the shadda on a word that also carries a tanwīn.**
- *Listen for:* *ḥaqan* for *ḥaqqan*, *janatun* for *jannatun*.
- *Why it happens:* the eye travels to the end of the word to resolve the ending — the freshest and most attention-hungry thing she has learned — and the mid-word mark is passed over on the way.
- *Correction cue:* "Read it in two halves. Doubling first, ending second." Cover the tanwīn with a fingertip, have her read the word, then uncover it.

**4. The second shadda in a long word getting under-held.**
- *Listen for:* the first doubling clean, the second rushed, in Drill 6.
- *Why it happens:* the word is long and she is budgeting effort. It is a stamina fault, not a knowledge fault, so explaining the rule again will not fix it.
- *Correction cue:* Read the word backwards in pieces — *-diq*, *-ṣid-dīq*, *aṣ-ṣid-dīq* — so the last doubling is the one she practises most.

**5. No bounce when stopping on ٱلْحَقّ or ٱلرَّبّ.**
- *Listen for:* a held closure that simply stops, with no release.
- *Why it happens:* two rules are stacked and only one fires. She is concentrating on the hold from 2-05 and the bounce from 2-04 does not get through.
- *Correction cue:* "Hold it — now let it go." Two separate instructions, two separate gestures with your hand.

**6. A gap between الـ and the noun — *al · ḥamdu*.**
- *Listen for:* a break after a moon lām.
- *Why it happens:* الـ is a separate idea in her head, so it becomes a separate word in her mouth.
- *Correction cue:* "الـ is not a word, it is the front of the word." Trace under the whole word with your finger, starting on the alif.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **Three minutes — Drill 1.** Say "sun" or "moon" before every word. The naming is the drill; do not skip it to save time.
2. **Four minutes — Drill 2, read across.** The bare word next to the الـ word is what makes the assimilation audible.
3. **Three minutes — Drill 4.** Stopping on a doubling, with the bounce on the last two.
4. **Four minutes — Drill 5 and Drill 6.** Both marks, both doublings, full time on each.
5. **Four minutes — Drill 7 cold column.** Once slow, once at pace. Mark any word where you had to go back.
6. **Once this week — mark up a page.** On a printed page of the last juzʾ, circle five sun lāms and five moon lāms. Do not read the page; just find them.

Bring to the next session: any word in Drill 7 where you could not decide sun or moon on sight. That means you are reading the letter instead of the mark, and it is a five-minute fix.
