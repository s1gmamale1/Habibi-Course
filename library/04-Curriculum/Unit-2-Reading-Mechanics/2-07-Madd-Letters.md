# Lesson 2-07 — The three madd letters ا و ي

## Objectives

- Student can state the condition that turns ا, و or ي into a lengthening — sākin, with a matching vowel in front of it — and can apply it to decide whether any given و or ي is a long vowel or a consonant.
- Student can hold every ordinary long vowel for a steady two counts, and keep that length constant across a twenty-word column instead of shortening as she tires.
- Student can read the dagger alif (ٰ) as a full-length alif, and can skip a letter marked with the small circle.
- Student can identify a و or ي after a fatḥa — خَوْف, بَيْت — as **not** a madd, and can say what she is not yet allowed to do with it.

## Hook

Say two words and ask her which one she just heard:

> **قَل** — *he became less*
> **قَال** — *he said*

Then say them again in the other order. Then a third pair: **عَلِم** / **عَالِم** — *he knew* / *a scholar*.

The corrective sentence, and it needs to be said plainly because it contradicts a lifetime of habit: **in Arabic, length is not decoration and it is not style. A long vowel and a short vowel are different letters and they make different words.**

Then name the specific risk, out loud, because it is hers:

> "You are going to shorten these. Not because you cannot hear them — because the language you think in does not need them, and it will take the shortcut whenever you stop watching."

Arabic vocabulary reached Uzbek largely through Persian, which had already flattened the long/short distinction before passing it on. See [[Common-Mistakes]]. This is the single error that will follow her through every remaining lesson of the course, and today is where you name it and give her a way to measure it.

## What they already know

- **She has been reading these letters as long vowels since Unit 1.2**, in words like كِتَاب, مُحِيط and يَقُول. Say so — this lesson is not new sounds, it is the system behind sounds she already makes. It is the same move as lesson 2-06, where she found out the الـ she had been reading had a rule behind it.
- **Lesson 1-13** gave sukūn, which is half of today's condition: a madd letter is a letter with a sukūn on it.
- **Lesson 1-14** already made her read a **six-count** madd in ٱلضَّآلِّينَ and told her it should feel uncomfortably long, and that beginners give it two. That word is the far end of the scale. Today establishes the near end — the ordinary two — so that the six she has been doing finally has something to be measured against.
- **Lesson 2-02** gave her the finger-tap count for the *ā* at the end of قَلِيلًا. Same count, same method, wider application.

## Teaching sequence

1. **State the condition once, in the exact shape she will apply it.** A letter lengthens only when **it is sākin and the vowel before it matches it**:

   | Letter | Vowel before | Example |
   |---|---|---|
   | ا | fatḥa | قَال |
   | و | ḍamma | يَقُول |
   | ي | kasra | قِيل |

   *Match* is the whole test. The mouth is already in position from the vowel, and the letter simply holds it. Have her say *a—* and then *ā*; the second is the first, continued.

2. **The alif is the easy one — say why, then move on.** An alif is *always* sākin and is *always* preceded by a fatḥa. It has no other job. There is no alif in Arabic that is not a lengthening, once you set aside the alif that carries a hamza (أ, إ) — which she has been reading as a hamza-seat since lesson 1-13, not as a letter.

3. **و and ي are the ones that need a decision, and this is the core of the lesson.** Both are consonants half the time:
   - **وَلَد** — the و carries a fatḥa. Consonant *w*.
   - **يَد** — the ي carries a fatḥa. Consonant *y*.
   - **يَقُول** — the و is sākin after a ḍamma. Long *ū*.
   - **قِيل** — the ي is sākin after a kasra. Long *ī*.
   Teach the decision as two questions asked in order: *does it have a sukūn? does the vowel in front of it match?* Two yeses, it lengthens. Anything else, it does not.

4. **Fix the length at two counts, and give her a measurement she can use alone.** The ordinary lengthening is **two counts** — *ḥarakatān*, the name is [[Madd-Tabii]] and it belongs to Unit 3. What she needs today is the beat:
   - Tap a finger twice, evenly, at about walking pace. The vowel fills both taps.
   - A short vowel is one tap. The long one is exactly double, not "a bit more".
   - **Set the tempo before the word, not during it.** A count decided mid-vowel is always short.

5. **The dagger alif, because the mushaf is full of it.** A miniature vertical stroke above a letter — **ٰ** — is a full alif that simply was not written out: ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ is *ar-Raḥmān*, هَٰذَا is *hādhā*. Two counts, exactly like a written alif. Show her three on a real page. Students who miss this read *ar-Raḥman* with a short *a* for years.

6. **The small circle, in one minute.** A tiny circle above a letter — most often on the alif of ءَامَنُوا۟ or a wāw — means **do not pronounce this letter at all**. It is a spelling leftover. See [[Silent-Letters]]. Recognition only.

7. **Now the two boundaries, and be very clear about both.**

   **Boundary one — و and ي after a fatḥa are NOT madd letters.** خَوْف, بَيْت, ٱلصَّيْف. The vowel does not match the letter, so the condition fails. These are the **leen letters** — see [[Leen]] and [[Madd-Leen]] — and they have their own behaviour, which is **the subject of lesson 2-08, the next lesson.**
   > **Teacher: do not teach leen today.** Today's instruction to the student is one sentence and no more: *"When you see a و or ي with a sukūn after a fatḥa, read it soft and short and do not stretch it. What it does when you stop on it is the next lesson."* This lesson owns identifying them; **2-08 owns treating them.**

   **Boundary two — some madds are longer than two.** When a hamza or a sukūn comes right after a madd letter, it stretches — four, five or six counts. ٱلشِّتَآءِ has one. ٱلضَّآلِّينَ from lesson 1-14 has the longest of them. The rules are [[Madd-Muttasil]] and [[Madd-Lazim]] and they are **Unit 3**. Today: **if the mushaf prints the extra sign (ـٓ) over the madd, hold it longer and copy the reciter. Otherwise, two.** That is an honest working rule for a Unit 2 reader and it is what she will actually do at the muṣḥaf this month.

8. **Finish on consistency, not on knowledge.** Read the cold column at the end and listen to whether the length is the *same* on word twenty as on word one. Knowing the rule is a ten-minute job. Holding it under fatigue is the whole term.

## Examples

| Text | Ref | Madd letter |

| يَدْخُلُونَ فِى دِينِ ٱللَّهِ | 110:2 | و after ḍamma, then ي after kasra, twice |
| نَسْتَعِينُ | 1:5 | ي after kasra — *nastaʿīn* |
| ٱلْفِيلِ | 105:1 | ي after kasra — *al-fīl* |
| ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ | 1:1 | dagger alif — *ar-Raḥmān* |
| ٱلشِّتَآءِ وَٱلصَّيْفِ | 106:2 | a longer madd (Unit 3) next to a leen ي that is **not** a madd (lesson 2-08) |

**Teacher note on 110:2.** This is the best single line in the short surahs for this lesson — a wāw madd and two yāʾ madds in five words, with no leen and no long madd to complicate it. Use it as the main reading anchor.

**Teacher note on 106:2.** Use it last, and only to *point at* the two boundaries. Read it for her. It is here so she meets both edges of the rule from a real page rather than from a made-up example, not so she can analyse it.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — short against long.** Read across. Two taps on the right-hand word:

| Short | Long |
|---|---|
| قَل | قَال |
| عَلِم | عَالِم |
| سُر | سُور |
| قُل | قُول |
| سِر | سِير |
| دَرَس | دَارِس |

**Drill 2 — the three conditions, one at a time.**

- **alif:** قَال · بَاب · كِتَاب · سَلَام · نَار · مَال
- **wāw:** يَقُول · نُور · سُوق · طُول · يَكُون · مَوْجُود
- **yāʾ:** قِيل · دِين · طَوِيل · كَبِير · جَدِيد · سَعِيد

**Drill 3 — consonant or long vowel?** Say which, then read it:

وَلَد · يَقُول · يَد · دِين · وَقْت · نُور · يَوْم · قِيل · وَرَقَة · طُول

Exactly one word in that row has a و or ي sākin sitting after a **fatḥa** — find it. Read it short and soft and move on; it is next lesson's business.

**Drill 4 — dagger alif.** All of these hold two counts on the small stroke:

ٱلرَّحْمَٰن · هَٰذَا · ذَٰلِك · لَٰكِن · ٱلسَّمَٰوَات · إِلَٰه

**Drill 5 — hold it under load.** Twenty words. The length must be identical on word twenty and word one:

كِتَاب · نُور · دِين · سَلَام · طَوِيل · سُوق · مَال · كَبِير · يَقُول · بَاب · جَدِيد · طُول · نَار · سَعِيد · يَكُون · مَدِينَة · قَال · مَعْلُوم · قَرِيب · حِسَاب

Record this column and play it back. You are listening for one thing: **do the long vowels get shorter as the column goes on?** They almost certainly do at first. That is the point of recording it.

**Drill 6 — the real anchor.** 110:2 — يَدْخُلُونَ فِى دِينِ ٱللَّهِ. Read it slowly with a finger tap on each long vowel, then at pace with no taps, then against a reciter.

**Drill 7 — the boundary, recognition only.** Point at each و or ي and say **"madd"** or **"not madd"**. Do not try to read the not-madd ones any particular way:

بَيْت · سِير · خَوْف · نُور · يَوْم · يَقُول · صَيْف · دِين · قَوْم · قِيل

## Listen-for

**1. Long vowels coming out short — *kitab* for *kitāb*.**
- *Listen for:* a long vowel you could not fit two taps into. If you are unsure, you are hearing a short one; a correct madd is unmistakable.
- *Why it happens:* the priority L1 error for this student. Uzbek's Arabic vocabulary came through Persian, which had already collapsed vowel length. She is not being careless — she is speaking the version of these words she has heard all her life.
- *Correction cue:* Tap twice and have her fill both taps. Never say "longer"; give the beat. "Longer" produces one-and-a-half counts, every time.

**2. The length shortening as the column goes on.**
- *Listen for:* word one correct, word fifteen half-length. Do not interrupt — mark the word number where it starts.
- *Why it happens:* the correct length is still conscious, and conscious control is the first thing that goes under load. Exactly the same profile as the sukūn collapse in lesson 2-03.
- *Correction cue:* Tell her the word number afterwards. Then have her re-read from that word only. Fixing the collapse point is worth more than re-reading the column.

**3. Over-lengthening — three or four counts on an ordinary madd.**
- *Listen for:* a swelling, sung vowel, usually in a student who has just been corrected for error 1, or one who listens to slow reciters.
- *Why it happens:* over-correction, plus melodic imitation. A reciter in slow *tartīl* stretches everything; she is copying the performance rather than the count.
- *Correction cue:* "Two, not as long as you can." Read the word yourself at the target length immediately — the model does more than the instruction.

**4. Stretching a leen letter — *bayyt*, *khawwf*.**
- *Listen for:* a lengthened و or ي after a fatḥa.
- *Why it happens:* she has just been told that و and ي lengthen, and she is applying it to every و and ي on the page. This is the predictable cost of teaching the rule, and it is why Drill 7 exists.
- *Correction cue:* "Which vowel is in front of it?" Make her name it. A fatḥa in front of a و or ي is the disqualifier, and the answer to her next question is "lesson 2-08."

**5. Reading a consonant و or ي as a long vowel — *ūlad* for *walad*.**
- *Listen for:* a word with a beat too many at the start.
- *Why it happens:* she has stopped checking for the sukūn and is deciding by letter identity alone — the same fault as bouncing a vowelled د in lesson 2-04.
- *Correction cue:* "Sukūn first, then the vowel in front." Two questions, in that order, out loud until it is automatic.

**6. Missing the dagger alif — *ar-Raḥman*.**
- *Listen for:* a short *a* in ٱلرَّحْمَٰن, هَٰذَا, ذَٰلِك.
- *Why it happens:* the stroke is small, sits where a sukūn or a fatḥa would sit, and looks like a diacritic rather than a letter.
- *Correction cue:* "That stroke is an alif. Give it its two counts." Have her trace over it with a pencil in three words on a real page — the hand fixes what the eye skims.

**7. Pronouncing a letter marked with the small circle.**
- *Listen for:* an extra syllable in ءَامَنُوا۟ and similar words.
- *Why it happens:* she has been taught for fifteen lessons that letters get said.
- *Correction cue:* "Circle means silent." One sentence, no elaboration; it is a recognition fact, not a rule.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **Three minutes — Drill 1.** Read across, two taps on the long word. Every day; this is the pair that keeps the contrast alive.
2. **Four minutes — Drill 2.** One block per day rotating, or all three if there is time.
3. **Three minutes — Drill 3 and Drill 7.** Name it before you read it. The naming is the drill.
4. **Three minutes — Drill 4.** Dagger alifs, full length.
5. **Five minutes — Drill 5, recorded.** Record it on day one and day five and compare the two. This is the most useful five minutes in the week: it is the only way to see whether the length is holding under fatigue.
6. **Two minutes — Drill 6.** 110:2 against a reciter.

Bring to the next session: the day-one and day-five recordings of Drill 5, and the word number where the length first started to slip. We will start the next lesson from that word.

**Scope note for the teacher.** The **leen letters** (و and ي sākin after a fatḥa — خَوْف, بَيْت, ٱلصَّيْف) are **identified** in this lesson and **taught** in lesson **2-08**. Drill 7 is recognition only. Do not teach their behaviour at a stop here; 2-08 owns it.
