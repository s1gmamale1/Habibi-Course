# Lesson 2.9 — Hamzat al-Waṣl in Full

## Objectives

- Student can decide, unaided, which of the three vowels (**a / u / i**) to put on a waṣl hamzah when **starting** on the word.
- Student can drop the waṣl hamzah completely when **joining**, including after a word ending in a sukūn, where a helping vowel appears on the previous word instead.
- Student can read a waṣl hamzah followed by a shadda — ٱلصَّمَدُ, ٱلنَّاسِ — as one fused sound rather than two.
- Student can point to a **ٱ** and a **أ** on a real page and say which one disappears.

## Hook

Ask her to read this, starting fresh at each word:

> **ٱلْحَمْدُ · ٱدْخُلُوا۟ · ٱمْشُوا۟ · ٱبْنُ · ٱهْدِنَا**

Five words, one identical opening letter, and — this is the point — **four different
correct answers.** She will read all five the same way, almost certainly all with a
kasrah: *il-ḥamdu, idkhulū, imshū, ibnu, ihdinā*. Two of those are right by accident.

Then say the thing that makes the lesson worth an hour:

> **"You already know this letter. In 1-13 you learned that it disappears when you join.
> What you were not told is what to do when you *start* on it — and from lesson 2-12
> onward you will be starting on words all the time, because you will be stopping and
> resuming. So this is not grammar. This is the tool you need next week."**

## What they already know

- **Hamza in all five seats** — ء أ إ ؤ ئ — and its sound (1-13).
- **Hamzat al-waṣl versus hamzat al-qaṭʿ at beginner level** (1-13): the ٱ with the little
  **ص** on it goes silent when you join; the أ never does. That much is already hers.
- **Sukūn as a closed syllable** (1-13, 2-03) — which is *why* this hamzah exists at all.
- **Shadda held as two letters** (2-05, 2-06) — needed for §4 below.

What is new today is **only the starting vowel**, plus what happens on the *previous* word
when you join. The rule note is [[Hamzat-Wasl]].

## Teaching sequence

### 1. Re-establish why it exists — thirty seconds, not longer

Arabic will not begin a word on a sākin letter. **ٱلْحَمْدُ** really begins with a sākin
lām. So a prop is added at the front to lean on. Once there is a previous word to lean on
instead, the prop is not needed and vanishes.

That is the whole justification, and it makes every branch below feel like arithmetic
rather than a list. Give it once and move on.

### 2. Joining: it disappears — and something happens next door

**وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ** is *wastaghfirhu*. Not *wa-istaghfirhu*. Have her say both and hear
that the second has a bump in it — a small cough between the wāw and the sīn.

Now the part usually left out. When the **previous word ends in a sukūn**, dropping the
hamzah would collide two sākin letters, so the previous word borrows a vowel:

| Written | Read as | What moved |
|---|---|---|
| قَالَتِ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ (3:45) | *qālati-l-malāʾikatu* | تْ → **تِ**, a helping **kasrah** |
| عَلَيْكُمُ ٱدْخُلُوا۟ (16:32) | *ʿalaykumu-dkhulū* | مْ → **مُ**, a helping **ḍammah** |
| أَنِ ٱمْشُوا۟ (38:6) | *ani-mshū* | نْ → **نِ** |

Tell her the muṣḥaf has already printed the helping vowel for her. Her job is to *not add
a second one on the hamzah*. This is the most common single error in the whole lesson.

**In plain terms:** when you run two words together, this hamzah is simply not there. If
the word before it grew an extra vowel, that is the join — read it and do not add another.

### 3. Starting: the three branches

Only now, with joining secure, teach the starting vowel. Draw it as a decision she runs on
the word in front of her.

**Branch A — is it ٱل, the definite article?** → **fatḥah**. Always. No exceptions.

> ٱلْحَمْدُ → *al-ḥamdu* · ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ → *ar-raḥmān* · ٱلنَّاسِ → *an-nās* ·
> ٱلَّذِينَ → *alladhīna*

**Branch B — is it one of the seven nouns?** → **kasrah**. There are seven in the whole
Qurʾān; she can hold seven.

> ٱسْم · ٱبْن · ٱبْنَة · ٱمْرُؤ · ٱمْرَأَة · ٱثْنَان · ٱثْنَتَان

> عِيسَى ٱبْنُ مَرْيَمَ (3:45) → start on it and it is *ibnu*.
> وَٱمْرَأَتُهُۥ (111:4) → *imraʾatuhū*. This one is on the last page of the muṣḥaf, so
> she will meet it in 2-14.

**Branch C — is it a verb?** → look at the verb, in this order:

| Verb | Vowel | Test | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long verbs (5 or 6 letters), past or command | **kasrah** | just count the letters | ٱسْتَغْفِرْ · ٱنطَلَقَ · ٱقْتَرَبَ |
| Command of a 3-letter verb | **look at the present tense** | third letter of the present tense has an **original ḍammah** → **ḍammah** | ٱدْخُلُوا۟ (يَدْخُلُ) · ٱنظُرْ (يَنظُرُ) · ٱعْبُدُوا۟ (يَعْبُدُ) |
| Command of a 3-letter verb | | third letter has **fatḥah or kasrah** → **kasrah** | ٱهْدِنَا (يَهْدِى) · ٱضْرِبْ (يَضْرِبُ) · ٱذْهَبْ (يَذْهَبُ) |

**Teach the trick, not the table:** *say the present tense in your head first.* يَدْخُلُ —
ḍammah on the khāʾ — so *udkhulū*. يَهْدِى — kasrah — so *ihdinā*. She can do this on any
verb she has ever heard, without knowing grammar terminology.

**The one exception worth naming:** if that ḍammah is only there temporarily, because a
weak letter dropped out, it does not count — go back to kasrah. **أَنِ ٱمْشُوا۟** (38:6)
is *imshū*, not *umshū*. Do not try to teach her how to spot a temporary ḍammah. Give her
the three words it affects in the hifz range and move on; this is a lookup, not a skill.

**In plain terms:** starting a word with ٱ, you have to pick its vowel. *Al-* takes *a*,
the seven nouns take *i*, and for a verb say the present tense first and copy its vowel.

### 4. When a shadda follows — what changes and what does not

Ask her: does ٱلصَّمَدُ start with a fatḥah or a kasrah?

**Fatḥah** — it is Branch A, the article, and nothing overrides Branch A. But what she
*hears* is *aṣ-ṣamad*, with no lām at all, because the lām has assimilated into the ṣād
and been written as a shadda. See [[Lam-Shamsiyyah]].

Make the distinction explicit, because this is exactly where learners "correct" themselves
into an error:

> **The shadda changes what you hear. It does not change the vowel you start with.**

| Word | Start with | You hear |
|---|---|---|
| ٱلْحَمْدُ | fatḥah | *al-ḥamdu* — lām audible ([[Lam-Qamariyyah]]) |
| ٱلصَّمَدُ (112:2) | fatḥah | *aṣ-ṣamadu* — lām gone, ṣād doubled |
| ٱلنَّاسِ (114:1) | fatḥah | *an-nāsu* |
| ٱللَّهُ (112:2) | fatḥah | *Allāhu* — doubled lām, heavy ([[Lam-Jalalah]]) |

The doubled letter still has to be **held**. *aṣ-ṣamad* with a short ṣād is a different
error wearing this rule's clothes; check the hold, not just the vowel.

### 5. Starting on ٱئْ and ٱؤْ — flag it, do not drill it

If the waṣl hamzah is followed by a **sākin hamzah**, starting on the word turns the second
hamzah into a long vowel matching the first: **ٱئْتِ** is started as *īti* (10:15).

Mention it once, show it once, and say it does not occur in the hifz set. She needs to
recognise it, not produce it. It is fully covered in [[Hamzat-Wasl]] §D for whenever it
comes up in real reading.

## Examples

The article, across the hifz set — all fatḥah:
**ٱلْحَمْدُ** (1:2) · **ٱلصِّرَٰطَ** (1:6) · **ٱلصَّمَدُ** (112:2) · **ٱلْفَلَقِ** (113:1)
· **ٱلنَّاسِ** (114:1) · **ٱلْحَطَبِ** (111:4) · **ٱلْكَوْثَرَ** (108:1)

Verbs:
**ٱهْدِنَا** (1:6) → kasrah · **ٱدْخُلُوا۟** (16:32) → ḍammah ·
**وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ** (110:3) → kasrah if started, silent as written ·
**فَلْيَعْبُدُوا۟** (106:3) → contains no waṣl hamzah at all, because فَ and لْ are already
in front of it; use it as a negative example

Nouns:
**ٱبْنُ** (3:45) · **وَٱمْرَأَتُهُۥ** (111:4)

Contrast, hamzat al-qaṭʿ — never silent:
**أَنْعَمْتَ** (1:7) · **أَحَدٌ** (112:1) · **أَعُوذُ** (113:1) · **إِذَا** (110:1)

## Drills

**Drill 1 — spot the two hamzahs.** Here is one page. Circle every **ٱ** in pencil and box
every **أ / إ**. Do not read yet. When you are done, tell me the difference in one
sentence.

**Drill 2 — start / join.** I will say a word twice. The first time, start on it. The
second time, I will give you a word to join from. You give me both readings:
ٱسْتَغْفِرْ → *istaghfir* / وَٱسْتَغْفِرْ → *wastaghfir*.
Do the same with ٱلْحَمْدُ, ٱهْدِنَا, ٱبْنُ, ٱلنَّاسِ.

**Drill 3 — the present-tense trick.** I give you a command, you say the present tense
first, out loud, then read the command with the right vowel:
ٱكْتُبْ (يَكْتُبُ → *uktub*) · ٱجْلِسْ (يَجْلِسُ → *ijlis*) · ٱفْتَحْ (يَفْتَحُ →
*iftaḥ*) · ٱنظُرْ (يَنظُرُ → *unẓur*) · ٱذْهَبْ (يَذْهَبُ → *idhhab*).

**Drill 4 — the seven nouns, out loud, in order.** Twice a day until they are automatic.
ٱسْم · ٱبْن · ٱبْنَة · ٱمْرُؤ · ٱمْرَأَة · ٱثْنَان · ٱثْنَتَان.

**Drill 5 — no bump.** Read this joined, and I am listening only for a cough in the middle:
وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ · فَٱذْكُرُونِى · قَالَتِ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ · عَلَيْكُمُ ٱدْخُلُوا۟ ·
عِيسَى ٱبْنُ مَرْيَمَ. If I hear a glottal stop, we go back to Drill 2.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** at a start, a clean single vowel on the alif — one of *a*, *u*, *i*, chosen
deliberately. At a join, **nothing at all** where the hamzah is written, and the vowel
landing on the last letter of the *previous* word.

- **The mistake:** every waṣl hamzah started with a kasrah — *il-ḥamdu*, *idkhulū*.
  **Why it happens:** kasrah is the default in most cases she will have heard, and it is
  also the shape of the isolated letter إ she learned in 1-13. She has generalised from a
  majority, which is the correct instinct applied to the wrong feature.
  **Say:** *"Is it ٱل? Then it's an* a *— every time, no thinking. Only verbs need
  thought."*

- **The mistake:** an audible glottal stop mid-phrase — *wa-istaghfirhu*, *ʿalaykum
  idkhulū*.
  **Why it happens:** two causes, and they need different fixes. Either she is reading
  word-by-word rather than in phrases, so every word is a "start"; or she comes from a
  Persian- or Urdu-influenced speech habit where the glottal onset is preserved. Test
  which by asking her to read the same phrase fast — if the bump vanishes at speed, it is
  the first cause.
  **Say:** *"Slide the last letter of the first word straight into the next one. No new
  breath. Wastaghfirhu — one word, as far as your mouth is concerned."*

- **The mistake:** doubling up the vowel — reading قَالَتِ ٱلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةُ as
  *qālati-ilmalāʾikatu*, with both the helping kasrah **and** a started hamzah.
  **Why it happens:** she has correctly noticed the printed kasrah on the tāʾ and has
  treated it as belonging to the previous word only, then started the next word fresh.
  **Say:** *"That kasrah is the bridge. Once you've crossed it, you're already inside the
  next word — there's nothing left to start."*

- **The mistake:** *al-ṣamad* — pronouncing the lām before a sun letter.
  **Why it happens:** she can see a lām on the page, and she has just been told the article
  takes a fatḥah, so she reads what is written rather than what the shadda instructs.
  **Say:** *"The shadda is the lām. It moved into the next letter. Say the ṣād twice and
  don't say the lām at all."*

## Homework

1. Take Sūrat al-Fātiḥa and mark every **ٱ** and every **أ / إ**. Counting the basmalah as
   āyah 1, there are **fourteen** waṣl hamzahs and **three** qaṭʿ hamzahs. Find them all;
   if your count is short, you have missed one inside a word, not at the start of one.
2. For each **ٱ** you marked, write the vowel you would use if you started there. Bring the
   sheet; we check it together, not you alone.
3. The seven nouns, aloud, morning and evening.
4. Read 110:3 — **فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ** — ten times joined, then
   once starting from وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ. Notice that the two readings genuinely differ. That
   difference is the entire lesson.
