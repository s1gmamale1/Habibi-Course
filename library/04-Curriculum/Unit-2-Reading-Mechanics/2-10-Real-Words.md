# Lesson 2.10 — Real Words

## Objectives

- Student can read an unseen, fully-voweled Arabic word of four to seven letters, first time, without being told which marks it contains.
- Student can run a **scan-then-read** routine — look the whole word over before opening the mouth — instead of decoding letter by letter aloud.
- Student can name which mark caused an error when she makes one, rather than only that she "got it wrong".

## Hook

Put **ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ** in front of her with no warning and ask her to read it.

She will stall. Let her stall — this is the only lesson in the unit where the hook is
supposed to be uncomfortable, and it works because the discomfort is *diagnostic*, not
punitive. Then take it apart with her, out loud, in five seconds:

> ٱ — start with *a*, it is the article. **نّ** — sun letter, doubled, hold it. **فّ** —
> doubled, hold it. **ٰ** — daggered alif, two counts. **ثَ** — plain. **ٰ** — two counts.
> **تِ** — kasrah, and stop.

Then have her read it again. It will come out. Say the point explicitly:

> **"Nothing in that word was new. Every single mark in it you have already learned. What
> was new was meeting them all at once, with nobody telling you what was coming."**

That is what Unit 2 has been building toward, and it is exactly what Checkpoint 2 tests.

## What they already know

Everything. That is the design of this lesson — **there is no new content in 2-10 at
all.** The nine marks the student now owns:

| Mark | Where it was taught |
|---|---|
| The three short vowels, and sukūn | Phase 1 (1-13) |
| Tanwīn — ً ٍ ٌ | 2-01, 2-02 |
| Multi-sukūn clusters | 2-03, 2-04 |
| Shadda, including on sun letters | 2-05, 2-06 |
| The three madd letters, two counts | 2-07 |
| Daggered alif ٰ | 2-07 |
| Madd + shadda / sukūn → six | 2-08 |
| Līn wāw and yāʾ → no length | 2-08 |
| Hamzat al-waṣl, all three vowels | 2-09 |

The teacher's job today is **not to teach**. It is to withhold help long enough for the
student to find out that she can do it.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. The scan-then-read routine

The single highest-value habit in this lesson. Most adult beginners read Arabic the way
they read a phone number — one symbol at a time, aloud, committing to each before seeing
the next. That guarantees a stumble whenever a later mark changes an earlier decision, and
in Arabic later marks constantly do.

Teach the routine as three silent seconds:

1. **Eyes across the whole word, left edge to right edge, mouth shut.**
2. **Find the doublings and the lengths first** — shaddas and madd letters, because those
   are what set the word's rhythm.
3. **Then read, once, at even speed.**

Model it yourself on ٱلْخَنَّاسِ. Say out loud what your eyes are doing. Then have her
narrate her own scan on ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ before reading it.

### 2. Read in rhythm, not in letters

A word is a shape in time. Have her tap the table once per beat while reading:

| Word | Beats |
|---|---|
| سِجِّيلٍ | si — j (held) — jī (2) — lin |
| ٱلصَّمَدُ | aṣ (held) — ṣa — ma — du |
| بِحِجَارَةٍ | bi — ḥi — jā (2) — ra — tin |

If the tapping and the reading come apart, the reading is letter-by-letter. Go back to §1.

### 3. Name the mark that broke

Every time she errs, do not correct the word. Ask: **"Which mark?"**

This is the operational core of the lesson and it is the same principle the checkpoint
scores on — errors are logged per letter and per rule, never only per word. See
[[Assessment]] §2. A student who can say *"I missed the shadda on the fāʾ"* has a fixable,
nameable problem. A student who only knows she "read it wrong" has nothing to practise.

Keep a running sheet in front of you with a column per mark. By the end of the session you
will have her actual weak marks in a form that names lessons: three misses on shadda →
redo 2-05 drills; three misses on līn → redo 2-08 §4.

### 4. Word length ladder

Work up. Do not start at seven letters.

- **Four letters, one mark:** قُلْ · لَهَبٍ · حَسَدَ · وَقَبَ · مَلِكِ
- **Five to six letters, two marks:** ٱلصَّمَدُ · سِجِّيلٍ · ٱلْفَلَقِ · ٱلْحَطَبِ · ٱلْيَتِيمَ
- **Seven-plus, three or more marks:** ٱلْكَوْثَرَ · بِحِجَارَةٍ · ٱلْخَنَّاسِ · ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ · ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ

Give each rung four or five words. When she clears a rung with no help, move up. When she
misses two on a rung, drop back one rung — never repeat the same rung after a miss,
because the second attempt at a failed rung is scored by memory rather than by reading.

### 5. Genuinely unseen words

Reserve the last ten minutes for words she has never met. **Non-Qurʾānic is better here**,
and deliberately so: Qurʾānic words in the hifz set are contaminated by memory, and a word
she can recite is not a word she has read. Ordinary Arabic vocabulary gives you a clean
measurement.

> مَكْتَبَة · مُدَرِّسُون · ٱلْمَدِينَة · مُسْتَشْفَى · سَيَّارَة · ٱلطَّائِرَة ·
> صَدِيقِي · مُهَنْدِسَة · ٱلْجَامِعَة · كِتَابُهُم · مُتَخَصِّصُون · ٱلشَّمْس

Do not translate them. She is not learning vocabulary; she is proving decoding.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — silent scan.** I show you a word for three seconds, then cover it. Tell me how
many shaddas and how many long vowels it had. Then I uncover it and you read it. You are
training your eyes to arrive before your mouth.

**Drill 2 — which mark broke?** Every time you slip, I will not say the word. I will say
"which mark?" If you can name it, we move on. If you cannot, we look at the word together
and you name it then.

**Drill 3 — the ladder.** Four-letter words until five in a row are clean, then six, then
seven. If you miss two on a rung we step back one rung and come at it again next session.

**Drill 4 — twelve cold words, timed.** Twelve unseen non-Qurʾānic words, one minute.
**Accuracy over speed** — a clean nine beats a rushed twelve. Write down which ones you
missed and what mark caused it.

**Drill 5 — read it backwards.** Take a word list and read it bottom to top, then in a
random order I point to. This kills the memorisation that a fixed list quietly builds.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** one continuous word — an even flow from first letter to last, with the
shaddas held and the madds counted, and **no pause in the middle for thinking**.

- **The mistake:** the word comes out in two pieces with a gap — *al-khan… nāsi*.
  **Why it happens:** she is decoding forward and hit a mark she had not seen coming. This
  is not a knowledge failure; it is a scanning failure, and correcting the pronunciation
  will not fix it.
  **Say:** *"Don't read it again yet. Look at the whole word first — all of it — then read
  it in one piece."*

- **The mistake:** the shadda is pronounced as a doubled letter but not *held* — *aṣ-ṣamad*
  with a ṣād no longer than a plain one.
  **Why it happens:** she is treating the shadda as a fact to acknowledge rather than a
  duration to produce. Very common once a rule becomes familiar; the knowledge stays and
  the execution decays.
  **Say:** *"Lean on it. Stay on the ṣād until I nod, then move."*

- **The mistake:** madd letters flatten to two counts everywhere, including where six is
  required, once the word gets long.
  **Why it happens:** cognitive load. On a seven-letter word with three marks, the length
  is the first thing to be dropped, because it is the only requirement that costs *time*
  rather than a decision.
  **Say:** *"Slow the whole word down, not just the madd. If the word is too fast, the
  madd has nowhere to live."*

- **The mistake:** guessing the word from its first two letters, especially in the hifz
  surahs.
  **Why it happens:** she knows these surahs by heart, so recognition beats decoding to the
  answer. It produces right answers and no learning, and it collapses the moment the word
  is unfamiliar.
  **Say:** *"Cover the end of the word with your finger. Now read only what you can see."*
  Then switch to the non-Qurʾānic list.

## Homework

1. Twenty unseen non-Qurʾānic words, written out by you or by me, read once daily. Mark
   with a dot every word you did not get first time.
2. Keep the **mark sheet**: a row per session, a column per mark (tanwīn · sukūn · shadda ·
   madd 2 · madd 6 · līn · waṣl hamzah). One tick per miss. Bring it every lesson.
3. The eight Qurʾānic words in the Examples table, read cold each morning **before** you
   recite anything — reading first, memory second, so memory cannot do the work.
4. Practise the three-second silent scan on every single word this week, even the easy
   ones. The habit is the homework; the words are just what it is practised on.
