# Lesson 2.11 — Phrases

## Objectives

- Student can read a two-to-five-word phrase as **one continuous sound**, with no gap between words.
- Student can carry a word's final vowel across the join into the next word, and drop a waṣl hamzah in flight.
- Student can hold a phrase-length rhythm — shaddas held and madds counted — without the length collapsing as the phrase gets longer.

## Hook

Ask her to read **بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ** — the phrase she has said more
often than any other sentence in her life.

Then ask her to read it *the way she has been reading words in 2-10*: carefully, one word
at a time.

The result is unmistakable, and she will hear it herself: **bismi. Allāhi. ar-raḥmāni.
ar-raḥīmi.** Four separate words, three glottal stops that should not be there, and a
phrase that no longer sounds like the phrase.

Say it:

> **"Careful word-reading and correct phrase-reading are two different skills, and the
> first one, done too well, breaks the second. Today you learn to let go of the word."**

## What they already know

- Every mark in the writing system, and the scan-then-read routine (2-10).
- **The waṣl hamzah disappears when joining**, and the previous word may take a helping
  vowel (2-09) — taught there in isolated pairs. This lesson is where that becomes a
  reflex rather than a calculation.
- Shadda held, madds counted, līn flat (2-05 to 2-08).

Nothing new is introduced today either. **2-10, 2-11, 2-12, 2-13 and 2-14 are one skill at
five lengths** — word, phrase, sentence, fluent sentence, page. What changes each time is
only how much has to stay correct at once.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. Arabic joins; it does not stack

Say a phrase in her own language and point out that she does not pause between words in
it. Nobody does, in any language. The pause she is producing in Arabic is an artefact of
learning to read, and it has to be actively removed.

The technical name for reading joined is **waṣl**, and it is the *default* state of
recitation. Stopping — [[Waqf-Types]] — is the marked case, the thing you do deliberately.
She has, until now, been doing the marked case at every single word.

### 2. What actually happens at the seam

Three things, and only three. Show each on a real phrase.

| At the seam | Example | Read as |
|---|---|---|
| **Vowel + consonant** — just run on | مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ (113:2) | *min sharri mā khalaq* |
| **Vowel + waṣl hamzah** — the hamzah vanishes | هُوَ ٱللَّهُ (112:1) | *huwa-llāhu* |
| **Sukūn + waṣl hamzah** — the hamzah vanishes and the previous word takes a helping vowel | عَلَيْكُمُ ٱدْخُلُوا۟ (16:32) | *ʿalaykumu-dkhulū* |

The muṣḥaf prints the helping vowel. She does not compute it; she reads it. Tell her that
outright — it removes the fear that joining requires grammar.

**In plain terms:** words run into each other with no gap. The only thing that ever
disappears at the join is the ٱ, and any extra vowel you need is already on the page.

### 3. The finger-slide

The most effective physical technique in this lesson. Have her put a finger under the line
and **move it continuously at a steady speed** while reading, never stopping it, never
lifting it, all the way to the end of the phrase.

The finger cannot pause, so the voice cannot either. When the voice falls behind the
finger, that gap is exactly where her reading is unstable — and now both of you can see
it, not just hear it.

### 4. Two words, then three, then five

Build the ladder inside the lesson, the same way 2-10 built it inside the word.

- **Two words:** مِن شَرِّ · قُلْ هُوَ · لِإِيلَٰفِ قُرَيْشٍ · مَلِكِ ٱلنَّاسِ · نَارُ ٱللَّهِ
- **Three words:** هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ · مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ · بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ
- **Four to five words:** ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · إِذَا جَآءَ نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلْفَتْحُ · فَصَلِّ لِرَبِّكَ وَٱنْحَرْ

At each rung the requirement is the same and it is binary: **one sound, or not one sound.**
Accuracy of the individual words is assumed by now — if a word breaks, that is a 2-10
problem and gets logged as one.

### 5. Breath

Introduce breath here, lightly, because it is the mechanical precondition for everything in
2-12.

> **Take the breath before the phrase, not during it.**

Have her breathe, then read the whole phrase, then breathe. If she cannot reach the end,
the phrase is too long for her current breath and **that is information, not failure** —
it is the beginning of the stopping plan she will build in 2-12. Note which phrases she
cannot finish; you will use that list next lesson.

Do not yet let her stop mid-phrase to breathe. Today the rule is simple and absolute:
finish the phrase or start it again. The nuance arrives in 2-12.

## Examples

Work the phrase set in this order — it is graded, and the grading matters more than the
content:

| # | Phrase | Ref | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ | 113:2 | four short words, no joins to manage |
| 2 | لِإِيلَٰفِ قُرَيْشٍ | 106:1 | a long madd and a līn, two words |
| 3 | قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ | 112:1 | first dropped waṣl hamzah in a phrase |
| 4 | فَصَلِّ لِرَبِّكَ وَٱنْحَرْ | 108:2 | shadda into a lām, then a joined waṣl hamzah |
| 5 | ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ | 1:2 | start *on* a waṣl hamzah, then two joins |
| 6 | إِذَا جَآءَ نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلْفَتْحُ | 110:1 | four words, a long madd in the middle |
| 7 | بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ | 1:1 | three waṣl hamzahs, all silent |
| 8 | سَلَٰمٌ عَلَيْكُمُ ٱدْخُلُوا۟ | 16:32 | the helping ḍammah, in the wild |

Phrase 7 is the assessment item for this lesson. If she can read the basmalah as one
unbroken sound with all three hamzahs gone, she has 2-11.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the hum.** Hum the phrase first, on one note, with the right rhythm and length
and no words at all. Then read it. The hum has no letters to get stuck on, so it gives you
the shape before you have to produce the sounds.

**Drill 2 — finger-slide.** Finger under the line, moving at a steady speed, never stopping.
Read to keep up with the finger. If your voice falls behind, we slow the finger — we do not
stop it.

**Drill 3 — one breath.** Breathe. Read the whole phrase. Breathe. If you run out before
the end, tell me *where* you ran out. Do not push through on empty lungs; that is how
stopping mid-word gets learned.

**Drill 4 — glue the seam.** I say the last syllable of word one and the first syllable of
word two, glued: *huwal-lā*. You say it back. Then read the whole phrase. Do this for every
seam in phrases 3 to 8.

**Drill 5 — the trap.** Read a phrase you know by heart, then the same phrase with one word
covered by my finger, and tell me what is under my finger. If you cannot, you were
reciting, not reading. Then read it uncovered, slowly, actually looking.

**Drill 6 — non-Qurʾānic phrases**, so nothing is memorised:
> ذَهَبَ ٱلْوَلَدُ إِلَى ٱلْمَدْرَسَةِ · فِي ٱلْبَيْتِ كِتَابٌ جَدِيدٌ ·
> ٱلْمُدَرِّسَةُ فِي ٱلْجَامِعَةِ · هَذَا صَدِيقِي مُحَمَّدٌ

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** one unbroken stream of sound from the first letter of the phrase to the
last, with the word boundaries audible only as rhythm — never as silence.

- **The mistake:** a small silence at every word boundary — *bismi · Allāhi · ar-raḥmāni*.
  **Why it happens:** she has spent nine lessons being rewarded for reading each word
  correctly, and a pause is where she checks her work. The pause is a **confidence
  behaviour**, not an ignorance behaviour, which is why explaining the rule again does not
  remove it.
  **Say:** *"Don't check. Trust the word you just read and keep going. If it was wrong I
  will tell you at the end."*

- **The mistake:** an audible glottal stop before every ٱ — *huwa ʾal-lāhu*.
  **Why it happens:** the alif is visible on the page, and everything she has learned so
  far says *read what is written*. Silence is the one instruction the page gives by
  omission.
  **Say:** *"The alif is a step, not a sound. Step over it — huwallāhu, one word."*

- **The mistake:** madds and shaddas shorten as the phrase gets longer — correct in a
  two-word phrase, gone by five words.
  **Why it happens:** length is the first casualty of divided attention, exactly as in
  2-10, and phrase-level joining is a heavier load than word-level accuracy.
  **Say:** *"Halve the speed of the whole phrase. Everything in it gets longer, not just
  the madd."*

- **The mistake:** she reaches the end of her breath and either stops mid-word or forces a
  strained, thin last two words.
  **Why it happens:** no breath plan — she began reading before deciding where she was
  going to end.
  **Say:** *"Look at where the phrase ends before you breathe in. Breathe for the distance,
  not for the first word."* Log the phrase; it goes on next lesson's stopping list.

## Homework

1. The eight phrases in the Examples table, one breath each, five times a day, finger
   sliding.
2. Sūrat al-Falaq (113), read **phrase by phrase, not word by word**. Pencil a small arc
   over each group of words you intend to say in one breath, before you begin.
3. Write down every phrase you could not finish in one breath. Bring the list — 2-12 is
   built out of it.
4. The four non-Qurʾānic phrases from Drill 6, daily. These are the honest measurement;
   the Qurʾānic ones are half-remembered and will flatter you.
