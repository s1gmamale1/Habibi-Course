# Lesson 2.14 — First Muṣḥaf Page

> This is the payoff of the whole unit. Everything from 2-01 onward existed so that this
> lesson could happen.

## Objectives

- Student can read one full page of a plain Uthmani muṣḥaf aloud, unaided, from a copy with no colour coding, no transliteration and no teacher prompting.
- Student can plan and execute her own stops across a whole page, not just a sentence.
- Student can distinguish **reading** the page from **reciting** it from memory, and can prove she is reading.
- Student can name what she does **not** yet know on the page, and carry on regardless.

## The page

**The final page of the muṣḥaf — Sūrat al-Masad (111), al-Ikhlāṣ (112), al-Falaq (113) and
an-Nās (114).** In the standard King Fahd Madinah layout this is page 604.

> **Before the lesson, open your own copy to the last page and check what is on it.** Page
> breaks differ between editions and some begin the page at al-Ikhlāṣ. Whatever your copy's
> final page holds, **that** is the page for this lesson — every surah in that range is in
> the course's hifz set either way ([[Hifz-Method]] §scope), so the lesson works under any
> layout.

Why this page and not al-Fātiḥa:

- It is a **genuinely full page** of continuous text, not a short opening.
- It contains, between four surahs, almost every mark Unit 2 taught: sun and moon lāms,
  shaddas, tanwīn in all three forms, madd letters, a six-count-shaped word, a samāʿī waṣl
  noun, and thirty-odd stops.
- It ends the muṣḥaf. Finishing Unit 2 on the last page of the book is worth the small
  theatre it costs.

**No colour muṣḥaf today.** A plain Uthmani text, exactly as Checkpoint 2 and the final
Test 1 use it (see [[Assessment]] §4). Colour is a later teaching aid; introducing it here
would let her read the colours instead of the letters.

## Hook

Do not start with the page. Start by putting the muṣḥaf in her hands, closed, and asking:

> **"Can you read this?"**

Whatever she answers, open it to the last page and say: *"Read it."*

Then be quiet. Do not help. Do not correct. Let her get to the end.

The reading will not be clean. That is not the point of this lesson and you should say so
before she starts worrying about it:

> **"Nine lessons ago you could not read a word of this. You are about to read a page of
> it. Errors today are data, not verdicts."**

## What they already know

Every single thing on this page, with two named exceptions. Lay this out for her
explicitly — it is the moment the unit becomes visible as a structure rather than a
sequence of sessions.

| On the page | Learned in |
|---|---|
| Short vowels, sukūn, hamza in all seats | 1-13 |
| Tanwīn — ً ٍ ٌ | 2-01, 2-02 |
| Sukūn clusters — وَلَمْ يَكُن | 2-03, 2-04 |
| Shadda, sun letters — تَبَّتْ, ٱلنَّاسِ, ٱلصَّمَدُ | 2-05, 2-06 |
| Madd letters, daggered alif — أَعُوذُ, ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ | 2-07 |
| Long madd before hamzah — يَدَآ | 2-08 |
| Waṣl hamzah, all three starting vowels — وَٱمْرَأَتُهُۥ | 2-09 |
| Stopping: ḥarakah drops, ة → هْ, long vowel stretches | 2-12, 2-13 |

**The two exceptions** — tell her about these *before* she reads, so they do not derail
her mid-page:

1. **The small superscript letters** — the ۢ over أَحَدٌۢ in 112:4, the ۭ shapes elsewhere.
   These are tajweed instructions and they belong to Unit 3. **Today: ignore them
   completely.** Read the word as if they were not there.
2. **The little circles and the letters above the line** — āyah numbers and stopping signs
   ([[Waqf-Signs]]). Today: the **circle with a number in it** is an āyah end and a safe
   stop. Everything else above the line, ignore.

Naming what to ignore is as important as naming what to read. An adult who hits an
unexplained symbol mid-page will stop and ask, and the momentum this lesson is built on
will be gone.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. Survey before reading

Ten minutes, mouth closed, before a single word is read aloud.

- **Find the four surah headings.** Four surahs on one page.
- **Count the āyah circles.** Five, four, five, six — twenty.
- **Find every ٱ on the page** and mark them lightly in pencil.
- **Find every shadda.**
- **Find the longest word on the page.** (It is ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ, 113:4.)

By the end of the survey the page has stopped being a wall of script and has become a set
of objects she recognises. This step is not optional and it is not a warm-up; it is the
transferable skill. Every unfamiliar page for the rest of her life gets surveyed like this.

### 2. Draw the plan

Pencil arcs, by her, as in 2-13 — but now across a whole page.

The default plan for this page is the easy one and she should use it: **stop at every āyah
end.** Twenty āyāt, twenty stops. Nothing on this page requires an internal stop, and the
longest āyah (113:4 or 114:5) is within an ordinary breath.

Check the plan before she reads. Then leave it alone; it is hers.

### 3. Prove it is reading, not reciting

She knows these four surahs by heart. Every student who reaches this lesson does — that is
what it means for the page to be in the hifz set — and a page recited from memory teaches
nothing about reading.

So build the proof into the lesson. Four techniques, use at least two:

- **Finger under every word**, moving, always. If the finger and the voice come apart, the
  memory has taken over.
- **Out of order.** "Read āyah 4 of al-Falaq. Now āyah 2 of al-Masad. Now the last āyah on
  the page." Memory is sequential; reading is not.
- **Cover and name.** Put a finger over a word and ask what is under it. If she cannot say,
  she was not looking at it.
- **The wrong word.** Point at a word and read it aloud yourself, wrongly — *al-nāsi* for
  ٱلنَّاسِ. Ask if that was right. A reader catches it instantly; a reciter agrees.

Say the purpose out loud rather than testing her covertly:

> **"You know these surahs. That is good, and it is not what we are checking today. Today I
> need to know that your eyes can do it, because next week the page will be one you don't
> know."**

### 4. The read

Whole page, aloud, uninterrupted. **You do not correct during it.** Take notes.

Log errors the way the checkpoint does — **per letter and per rule, never only per
passage** ([[Assessment]] §2). "Three misses on stopping ة" names a lesson to redo; "messy
on al-Falaq" names nothing.

Time it, and note where she slows. Slowing is not an error, but it maps her weak spots
onto the page better than the error list does.

### 5. The debrief

Go through your notes with her, grouped by cause, not by order of occurrence:

- Errors of **decoding** — a mark misread. → 2-10.
- Errors of **joining** — a bumped seam, a pronounced waṣl hamzah. → 2-09, 2-11.
- Errors of **stopping** — a ḥarakah kept, a ة as *t*, a clipped long vowel. → 2-12, 2-13.
- Errors of **planning** — a stop in the wrong place, breath gone. → 2-13.

Four causes, four addresses. This grouping is exactly the revision map Checkpoint 2 will
use, so running it here means the checkpoint's output format is already familiar.

### 6. Second read

Same page, straight after the debrief. It will be visibly better, and the improvement
belongs to her rather than to your corrections, because you did not correct during the
first read. End the lesson there.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the survey.** Ten minutes, silent. Four headings, twenty circles, every ٱ,
every shadda, the longest word. Do this on any new page, for the rest of your life.

**Drill 2 — out of order.** I name an āyah, you find it and read it. Al-Falaq 4. Masad 2.
The last āyah on the page. The shortest āyah on the page.

**Drill 3 — cover and name.** I cover a word. You tell me what it is. If you can't, you
were reciting.

**Drill 4 — the wrong word.** I read a word off the page incorrectly. You tell me whether I
was right. Sometimes I will be.

**Drill 5 — the whole page, timed.** Straight through, no help. I write, I do not speak.
Then we go through my notes together, grouped by cause.

**Drill 6 — the unknown page.** Once, at the end of the lesson: open the muṣḥaf at random,
anywhere, and read three lines. You will not know them. That is the point — three lines of
an unfamiliar page is the honest measure of what Unit 2 gave you, and it is what Checkpoint
2 will ask for.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** a page read at one steady pace, with chosen stops, and — the marker that
matters most today — **eyes and voice moving together**, the finger arriving at each word
just before it is spoken.

- **The mistake:** the reading is fluent and correct, and she is not looking at the page.
  **Why it happens:** she knows these four surahs by heart. This is the central risk of this
  lesson and it produces a *good* reading that measures nothing.
  **Say:** *"Stop — read me āyah three of al-Falaq. Now the second word of al-Masad."*
  Then run Drill 3.

- **The mistake:** she stalls completely at a symbol she does not recognise — the ۢ, a
  waqf letter above the line.
  **Why it happens:** she has been taught that every mark means something, correctly, and
  she has no permission to skip one.
  **Say:** *"That one isn't yours yet. Read straight past it — it's Unit 3."* Give the
  permission again at the start of the second read; once is not enough.

- **The mistake:** momentum collapses after the first real error — the pace drops and does
  not recover for the rest of the page.
  **Why it happens:** she has started monitoring her own performance instead of reading,
  and monitoring consumes the attention the reading needs.
  **Say:** *"Keep going. I'm writing it down, so you don't have to remember it. Your only
  job is the next word."*

- **The mistake:** stops are correct in the first surah and degrade by the fourth.
  **Why it happens:** fatigue. A page is genuinely longer than anything she has read, and
  this is the first time endurance has been a factor at all.
  **Say:** nothing, during. Note it, and in the debrief: *"Your stops were fine until āyah
  fourteen. That's stamina, not knowledge — and stamina is built by reading pages, which is
  exactly what you now do."*

- **The mistake:** stopping mid-word when the breath runs out on a long āyah.
  **Why it happens:** the plan was made for the first surah's āyāt and never revisited for
  the longer ones later on the page.
  **Say:** *"Back to the start of that word. Then re-mark your arcs from here to the bottom
  of the page."*

## Homework

1. **The page, once a day, out loud, finger under every word.** Not from memory — from the
   page. If you catch yourself reciting, cover the next word with your thumb and uncover it
   one at a time.
2. Keep the marked copy with your breath arcs. Re-mark it fresh each week rather than
   reusing last week's, so the planning stays a live skill.
3. **Three lines of an unfamiliar page, twice a week.** Open anywhere. Read three lines.
   Write down what stopped you. This is the single most useful thing you can do between now
   and Checkpoint 2.
4. Bring the four error groups from the debrief — decoding, joining, stopping, planning —
   with your own count against each. You will need them: **Checkpoint 2 is next**, and it
   asks for exactly this, on a page you have never seen.
