# Checkpoint 2 — Reading Mechanics

**The biggest gate in the course.** Checkpoint 1 asked whether she could produce the
sounds. Checkpoint 2 asks whether she can **read** — unseen, fully-voweled text, unaided,
off a plain page. Everything in Unit 3 assumes a yes.

It is scored on the standard course rubric; this note specifies how that rubric is applied
here. The rubric itself, and the reasoning behind it, is [[Assessment]].

## 1. Test structure — four rungs, in order

**Allow 45–60 minutes.** This is not transmitted from anywhere — it is derived from the
item counts below (20 + 10 + 6 + a full page, read unaided, with a mark sheet filled in per
item) against Checkpoint 1's transmitted 30–40 for a shorter session. **Treat it as a
planning figure, not a limit.** The checkpoint is not timed and nothing about the result
depends on how long she takes; a slow, accurate reading is a pass.

The same ladder Unit 2 climbed, run as an assessment. **All material must be unseen.** Not
"not recently practised" — genuinely not previously read.

| Rung | What she reads | Count | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Words** | Unseen fully-voweled words, 4–8 letters | 20 | Non-Qurʾānic |
| **2. Phrases** | Unseen phrases, 2–5 words | 10 | Non-Qurʾānic |
| **3. Sentences** | Unseen complete sentences, read with her own stops | 6 | Non-Qurʾānic |
| **4. Muṣḥaf page** | One full page of plain Uthmani text | 1 | Qurʾānic, unseen |

**Rungs 1–3 are non-Qurʾānic, and this is not a compromise — it is the measurement.** Any
Qurʾānic word from the hifz set is contaminated by memory, and a word she can recite is not
a word she has read. Ordinary Arabic vocabulary gives a clean reading score.

**Rung 4 must be Qurʾānic and must be a page she has never read.** Not al-Fātiḥa, not the
last page from 2-14, not anything in surahs 105–114. Pick a page from the middle of the
muṣḥaf. She will not know the words; that is correct and she should be told so before she
begins.

Conditions for all four rungs:

- **Unaided.** No prompting, no starting syllable, no "look at the shadda".
- **Plain text.** No colour muṣḥaf, no transliteration, no annotation.
- She may **plan her stops** on rungs 3 and 4 before reading — planning is a skill this
  unit taught and it is being assessed, not excluded.
- No tajweed rules are tested. Ghunnah, ikhfāʾ, madd counts, qalqalah — **none of it is in
  scope.** She has not been taught them. Marking them here is the commonest examiner error
  at this checkpoint.

## 2. Rubric

Standard course scale ([[Assessment]] §1):

| Error | Deduction |
|---|---|
| **Laḥn Jali** — major, clear: wrong letter, wrong makhraj, a word that is not Arabic | **−2** |
| **Laḥn Khafi** — minor, hidden: quality degraded, meaning intact | **−1** |
| **Self-correction after prompt** — she fixes it once alerted | **−1** |
| **Examiner has to correct** — she cannot fix it even when alerted | **−2** |

### What counts as which, at this checkpoint

The general test holds: *if a native listener with no tajweed training would notice, it is
Jali.* Applied to Unit 2's material:

| Error | Tier | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong letter or wrong makhraj | **Jali** | The Checkpoint 1 standard, still live |
| Shadda not doubled at all | **Jali** | Different word |
| Madd letter read short enough to change the word | **Jali** | كَاتِب / كَتَب are different words |
| Waṣl hamzah pronounced when joining | **Jali** | Inserts a sound that is not in the language |
| Waṣl hamzah started with the wrong vowel | **Jali** | *il-ḥamdu* is not a word |
| Final ḥarakah kept at a stop | **Jali** | No Arabic word ends in a vowel at a stop |
| **ة** read as a **t** at a stop | **Jali** | Named as a priority error for this unit |
| **Stopping mid-word** | **Jali** | Produces a non-word; also stop the reading and restart the item |
| Shadda doubled but not held long enough | **Khafi** | Right letter, thin execution |
| Madd audibly present but short of two counts | **Khafi** | Quality, not identity |
| Līn given a small stretch when joining | **Khafi** | Audible to a trained ear only |
| Long vowel clipped at a stop | **Khafi** | The stretch is optional in length at this stage |
| Uneven pace, word-by-word delivery | **Khafi**, once per rung | Fluency, not accuracy — do not stack it per word |
| Hesitation without error | **−0.5** | Non-fluency |
| A Unit 3 rule not applied | **not an error** | Out of scope. Do not mark it |

Marking convention, as elsewhere in the course: **Jali in red, Khafi in yellow. Mark all
Jali first.**

## 3. Pass condition

**Pass if either of the following is true:**

1. **Numeric ≥ 70%** of the available points across the four rungs, **or**
2. **No Jali error survives an examiner correction** — that is, every major error she made,
   she was able to fix when prompted.

Either one is a pass. They are deliberately different tests: the first says her reading is
good enough overall, the second says her *internal monitor* works, which is the better
predictor of whether Unit 3 will land. A student can pass on (2) with a numeric score in
the sixties, and that student is genuinely ready — she makes errors but she owns them.

The reverse case — a high numeric score with one Jali error she could not fix even when
told something was wrong — is **not** a pass on (2) alone, but is a pass on (1) with the
unfixed error carried forward as a homework flag. A category she has not yet formed will
not repair itself; name it and attach drills.

## 4. Errors are logged per letter and per rule, never only per passage

This is the operational requirement that makes the whole checkpoint worth running, and it
is stated at length in [[Assessment]] §2. Restated for this checkpoint specifically:

A result of **"−9 on the muṣḥaf page"** is unactionable. A result of

> −2 stopping-ة (×2) · −2 waṣl-hamzah-start · −1 shadda-hold (×3) · −1 madd-2 (×1)

says: **she cannot stop on a tāʾ marbūṭah and she cannot start on a waṣl hamzah. Redo 2-12
§3 and 2-09 §3. Her shadda and her madds are fine.** That is a revision plan, and it took
one extra column on the mark sheet to obtain.

Use a mark sheet with a row per item and a column per **cause**:

> letter/makhraj · tanwīn · sukūn cluster · shadda · madd 2 · madd 6 · līn · waṣl hamzah
> (join) · waṣl hamzah (start) · stop: ḥarakah · stop: ة · stop: long vowel · stop:
> placement · pace

Fourteen columns. Every deduction gets a tick in exactly one. The totals down the columns
are the result that matters; the total across the bottom is only the number.

Carry the sheet forward. A cause that costs a deduction at Checkpoint 1, in weekly
sessions, and again here is **fossilising**, and it goes to the front of the queue
regardless of what the checkpoint total says. See [[Common-Mistakes]].

## 5. Revision map — weak spot to lessons

Read down the column totals; each one names its own remedy.

| Weak column | Redo | Then re-test |
|---|---|---|
| letter / makhraj | Phase 1 letter lessons for the specific letters; [[Common-Mistakes]] track | Rung 1 only |
| tanwīn | 2-01, 2-02 | Rung 1 |
| sukūn clusters | 2-03, 2-04 | Rung 1 |
| shadda — missing, or not held | 2-05, 2-06 | Rung 1 |
| madd 2 — short or absent | 2-07 | Rung 1 |
| madd 6 — the shadda/sukūn case | 2-08 §2 | Rung 1 |
| līn — stretched when joining | 2-08 §4–5 | Rungs 1–2 |
| waṣl hamzah pronounced when joining | 2-09 §2, 2-11 §2 | Rung 2 |
| waṣl hamzah — wrong starting vowel | 2-09 §3 | Rungs 2–3 |
| stop: final ḥarakah kept | 2-12 §3 (item one) | Rung 3 |
| stop: ة read as *t* | 2-12 §3 (item two) | Rung 3 |
| stop: long vowel clipped | 2-12 §3 (item three), 2-13 §5 | Rung 3 |
| stop: placement — mid-word, or mid-idea | 2-12 §1–2, 2-13 §1 | Rungs 3–4 |
| pace — word-by-word, uneven | 2-11 §3, 2-13 §2 | Rung 4 |
| general: fluent but not looking at the page | 2-14 §3 | Rung 4 |

**Re-test only the named rungs.** A student whose only weak column is stopping does not
re-read twenty words.

## 6. Outcomes — three, and none of them is a restart

| Outcome | Meaning | Next |
|---|---|---|
| **Pass** | Advance to Unit 3. | Unit 3.1 |
| **Pass-with-homework-flag** | Advance, with named drills attached to the next block of sessions. The flag is carried in writing and cleared explicitly, never quietly forgotten. | Unit 3.1, plus the drills from §5 |
| **Revise-and-retest** | Repeat **only the lessons named in §5**, then re-test **only the failed rungs**. | Typically 2–4 sessions |

**A failed Checkpoint 2 never triggers a restart of Unit 2, and never a restart of the
course.** This is a design commitment ([[Assessment]] §5), not a kindness. The column
totals already say exactly which lessons failed; re-teaching material she demonstrably
holds burns sessions and, for an adult learner, is the most reliable way to lose her.

There is one hard floor, and it is worth stating so it is never improvised in the room:
**a student who cannot pass Rung 4 does not proceed to Unit 3**, even with rungs 1–3 clean.
Unit 3 teaches rules applied to running text on a real page. A student who cannot get
through a page has nothing to apply them to. That is a revise-and-retest on 2-13 and 2-14,
not a failure of the unit.

## 7. Priority errors to watch for

From this unit's own teaching notes, in the order they cost the most:

1. **Running out of breath mid-āyah because there was no stopping plan.** The upstream
   cause of most of the rest. Look at whether she planned, not only at what she produced.
2. **Stopping mid-word.** Always Jali. Stop the item and restart it.
3. **Pronouncing the final ḥarakah at a stop.** The most common single error at this
   checkpoint, and the most forgivable — the vowel is printed and she is obeying the page.
4. **Reading ة as a ت at a stop.** Second most common, and the fastest to fix.

## 8. What this result is and is not evidence of

It is evidence about **reading production**, scored by a qualified teacher present in the
room. It says nothing about tajweed, which has not been taught, and nothing about
memorisation, which is scored separately ([[Hifz-Method]]).

Reported to the student, the result should be a sentence and a list, in that order: *"You
can read. These four things need work."* The number is for the mark sheet, not for her.
