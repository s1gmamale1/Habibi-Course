# Lesson 3-01 — Makhārij consolidated: the 17 points in 5 zones

## Objectives

- Student can name the **five zones** (*al-jawf, al-ḥalq, al-lisān, ash-shafatān, al-khayshūm*) in order and say how many makhārij each contains, without a prompt.
- Student can place any single letter you say to her into its zone, and for the throat and the tongue can say *which* point inside that zone.
- Student can find her own makhraj physically — by the sukūn test — rather than by imitating the sound she thinks she hears.
- Student can read the six example words above and point to where in her own mouth each letter is being made.

## Hook

Do not open with a diagram. Open with a wrong answer.

Say to her: **"Where is ك made?"** Almost every student who has done Phase 1 answers "in the throat", because ك *feels* deep and because ق and ك were taught back to back and ق is genuinely a throat-adjacent sound. It is the wrong answer. **ك is a tongue letter** — the deepest part of the tongue against the *hard* palate, one notch forward of ق, and nowhere near the throat proper.

Then the second one: **"Where is ن made?"** She will say "the tip of the tongue." Half right. The tip of the tongue makes the *closure*; the sound itself comes out of **the nose**. That is a separate zone with its own name, and it is the reason a pinched nose kills the letter.

Now the sentence this lesson exists to install:

> **You have been making all 29 of these sounds for a year. What you have not had is a map.** Today you get the map, and from now on every correction I give you has an address: not "that was wrong" but "that was made two millimetres too far forward."

Name the payoff plainly. Everything in Unit 3 — heaviness, the bounce, the rā', the nasal rules — is a statement about a *place* or a *behaviour at a place*. Without addresses, the rest of the unit is a list to memorise. With addresses, it is a description of her own mouth.

## What they already know

- **Phase 1 taught the letters one makhraj at a time, and she has the hard ones already.** Lesson 1-13 drilled ع and ح at the middle of the throat and made her hold a bare ع at the end of a word — that is the single hardest makhraj in the language and she has it. Lesson 1-14 gave her ص ض ط ظ as *heavy* letters and taught the ط-bounces / ظ-is-held contrast. **She has the points. She does not yet have the ordering.**
- **Lesson 2-03 and 2-04** gave her sukūn, and the sukūn is the tool this whole lesson runs on. You cannot locate a makhraj on a vowelled letter, because the vowel drags the sound forward into the mouth. You locate it on a *closed* letter.
- **Checkpoint 2** established that she can read unseen voweled text unaided. That is the precondition for this unit: from here on, the work is not "can you read it" but "is what you read correct."

## Teaching sequence

1. **Give the definition once, and give it as a test, not as a sentence.**
   A **makhraj** (مَخْرَج, "exit point") is where a letter's sound leaves. The classical method of finding it is a physical procedure, and you should run it on her in the first five minutes:

   > Put a hamzah in front of the letter and close the letter with a sukūn. Say it. **Where the sound stops is the makhraj.**

   Do it live: أَبْ · أَتْ · أَقْ · أَعْ. Have her point at her own face each time — lips, front of mouth, back of mouth, throat. She will be right every time, which is the point: **she already knows this and has never been asked.**

2. **Draw the five zones before naming a single letter.** Five, from the inside out:

   | # | Zone | Arabic | What it is | Makhārij |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | **al-jawf** | الجوف | the empty space of mouth and throat | 1 |
   | 2 | **al-ḥalq** | الحلق | the throat | 3 |
   | 3 | **al-lisān** | اللسان | the tongue | 10 |
   | 4 | **ash-shafatān** | الشفتان | the two lips | 2 |
   | 5 | **al-khayshūm** | الخيشوم | the nasal passage | 1 |

   **1 + 3 + 10 + 2 + 1 = 17.** Write the arithmetic on the board. The number 17 is worth nothing on its own; the *shape* — one, three, ten, two, one — is what she should be able to reproduce cold, because it tells her the tongue is more than half the alphabet and therefore where the errors will be.

3. **Zone 1 — al-jawf, one makhraj, three letters, no contact.**
   The three **madd letters** — ا, و, ي — when they are carrying length. They have no point of contact at all; they are shaped by the empty column of air and stopped only by the breath running out. This is exactly why she cannot "find" an alif by feel: there is nothing to feel.
   Say clearly: **the و and ي of al-jawf are the long ones.** A consonantal و (as in وَقَبَ) or ي is made in the lips or on the tongue and belongs to those zones. Same letter, two homes, decided by whether it is carrying madd.

4. **Zone 2 — al-ḥalq, three makhārij, six letters. Deepest first.**

   | Point | Arabic | Letters |
   |---|---|---|
   | deepest, nearest the chest | أَقْصَى الحلق | **ء · ه** |
   | middle | وَسَط الحلق | **ع · ح** |
   | nearest the mouth | أَدْنَى الحلق | **غ · خ** |

   Three pairs, and each pair is one voiced + one breathed: ء/ه, ع/ح, غ/خ. Point that out — it turns six items into three, and it previews the next lesson, where "voiced vs breathed" gets its proper name.
   **The order matters and she will get it backwards.** Most students place ه at the middle and ع at the bottom, because ع *feels* like more effort. Effort is not depth. Run the sequence outward — أ ه · ع ح · غ خ — three times, slowly, with a fingertip tracing down her own throat from jaw to collarbone and back up.

5. **Zone 3 — al-lisān, ten makhārij. Do this at the board, back to front.**
   This is 60% of the lesson. Go from the deepest point forward, and after each one, make her say the letters closed by a sukūn.

   | # | Where | Letters |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 | deepest tongue against the **soft** palate | **ق** |
   | 2 | slightly forward, against the **hard** palate | **ك** |
   | 3 | middle of the tongue against the palate above it | **ج · ش · ي** (non-madd) |
   | 4 | one or both **side-edges** against the upper molars | **ض** |
   | 5 | the tongue's edge, rear to tip, at the gums of the upper front teeth | **ل** |
   | 6 | tip at the gums of the upper incisors, just below lām | **ن** |
   | 7 | back of the tip, slightly deeper than nūn | **ر** |
   | 8 | tip against the **roots** of the two upper incisors | **ط · د · ت** |
   | 9 | tip **between** the incisors, closer to the lower | **ص · س · ز** |
   | 10 | tip against the **lower edges** of the upper incisors | **ظ · ذ · ث** |

   Four things to say out loud while you build this table, because each of them is a downstream rule in disguise:

   - **Points 1 and 2 are the ق/ك pair,** and they are one notch apart. Soft palate, then hard palate. If she cannot separate them, everything about ق being heavy and ك being light is unteachable.
   - **Point 4 is ض, alone, on the side of the tongue.** It is the only makhraj in the language that uses the tongue's *edge* rather than its tip, back or middle. Lesson 3-08 is about this one point. Flag it now and move on.
   - **Points 5, 6, 7 — ل, ن, ر — are three neighbours,** and they are the letters that will misbehave for the rest of the course. ل is a wide edge-contact, ن is the tip a hair below it, ر is the *back* of the tip a hair deeper still. Have her say لْ · نْ · رْ and feel the contact walk backwards.
   - **Points 8, 9, 10 are three rows of teeth,** and each row holds a heavy letter beside its light twin: ط with د/ت, ص with س/ز, ظ with ذ/ث. That is not a coincidence and it is the whole architecture of lesson 3-03. Say so.

6. **Zone 4 — ash-shafatān, two makhārij, four letters.**

   | Point | Letters | How |
   |---|---|---|
   | inside of the **lower lip** against the edges of the upper incisors | **ف** | one lip, not two |
   | **both lips** | **ب · م · و** | ب closed firmly · م closed lightly, with nasal resonance · و rounded but **not** closed |

   The distinction to drill is ب / م / و as three different *degrees of lip closure*, not three unrelated letters. Firm, light, open. Have her make all three in a row with her eyes shut and a hand on her lips.

7. **Zone 5 — al-khayshūm, one makhraj, no letters of its own.**
   The nasal passage produces **ghunnah** (غُنَّة, "nasal hum") — the sound that comes out of ن and م, not the letters themselves. This is the zone she will meet properly in lesson 3-15 and it is the reason she has been pinching her nose since lesson 2-05.
   Say it exactly this way: **ن and م have two addresses.** The closure is in the mouth; the sound is in the nose. Pinch and prove it on ٱلنَّاسِ.

8. **Close by mapping one word, not by reviewing the table.**
   Take **ٱلْحَمْدُ** (1:2) and walk it letter by letter: ء (deepest throat) → ل (tongue, point 5) → ح (middle throat) → م (lips, with the nose) → د (tongue, point 8) → و of the ḍamma. Five zones in six sounds. Then hand her **بِأَصْحَٰبِ** (105:1) and make her do it herself.

## Examples

| Text | Ref | Zones travelled |
|---|---|---|
| ٱلْحَمْدُ | 1:2 | throat → tongue → throat → lips → tongue |
| أَنْعَمْتَ | 1:7 | deepest throat → tongue **+ nose** → middle throat → lips → tongue |
| ٱلْفَلَقِ | 113:1 | lip-and-teeth → tongue tip → **deepest** tongue |
| بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ | 114:1 | lips → tongue (points 7, 5, 6) **+ nose** → jawf → tongue (point 9) |
| ٱلصِّرَٰطَ | 1:6 | tongue points **9 → 7 → 8**, three neighbours in a row |
| بِأَصْحَٰبِ | 105:1 | lips → throat → tongue → throat → jawf → lips |

**Teacher note on ٱلصِّرَٰطَ.** This is the best single word in the Qur'an for this lesson. The ص, ر and ط are made at three *adjacent* points on the tongue — teeth-gap, back-of-tip, tooth-roots — so the tongue has to make three precise, small, forward-and-back moves in one syllable-and-a-half. A student who slurs this word is not being careless; she is failing to distinguish three neighbouring addresses. It comes back in 3-03, 3-07 and 3-10.

**Teacher note on بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ.** She has been saying this in every prayer of refuge she has ever made. Use it to show that the nose zone is not exotic: the نّ of ٱلنَّاسِ is two counts of pure khayshūm with the tongue merely holding the door shut.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the sukūn probe.** This is the drill that teaches the *method*, so run it first every time. For each letter, say it with a hamzah in front and a sukūn on it, then point to where it stopped:

أَقْ · أَكْ · أَجْ · أَضْ · أَلْ · أَنْ · أَرْ · أَطْ · أَصْ · أَظْ · أَبْ · أَفْ

Do not accept a sound without a pointing finger. Naming the zone out loud comes after the finger, not before.

**Drill 2 — down the throat and back.** Six letters, three pairs, outward from the chest:

**ء ه** → **ع ح** → **غ خ** → back up: **خ غ** → **ح ع** → **ه ء**

Then mixed and cold: ح · ء · خ · ع · ه · غ. She names the depth — "deepest", "middle", "nearest" — before saying the letter.

**Drill 3 — walk the tongue.** Ten points, deepest to frontmost, one representative letter each:

قْ · كْ · جْ · ضْ · لْ · نْ · رْ · طْ · صْ · ظْ

Run it forwards, then backwards, then have her do it with her eyes closed while you watch her jaw. The tongue should feel like it is walking, not jumping.

**Drill 4 — the three neighbours.** The ل / ن / ر cluster, which is where half the remaining course lives:

لْ نْ رْ · نْ رْ لْ · رْ لْ نْ · لْ رْ نْ

Then in words: ٱلْفَلَقِ (113:1) · بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ (114:1) · ٱلَّذِينَ (1:7).

**Drill 5 — the three tooth rows.** Say each row across, heavy letter first, and feel the tip change height:

| Row | Letters |
|---|---|
| tooth **roots** | طْ · دْ · تْ |
| **between** the teeth | صْ · سْ · زْ |
| tooth **edges** | ظْ · ذْ · ثْ |

Now down the columns instead: طْ صْ ظْ (all heavy) · دْ زْ ذْ (all light, all voiced) · تْ سْ ثْ (all light, all breathed). The columns are next lesson's subject; today they are just proof that the table is organised, not arbitrary.

**Drill 6 — lip degrees.** Eyes closed, hand on the lips: بْ (firm) · مْ (light, nose open) · وْ (rounded, never closed) · فْ (lower lip only). Then in words: ٱلْبَيْتِ (106:3) · تَبَّتْ (111:1) · ٱلْفِيلِ (105:1).

**Drill 7 — map a word cold.** Give her these and have her call out the zone of every letter, in order, with no table in front of her:

ٱلْحَمْدُ · أَنْعَمْتَ · ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ · بِأَصْحَٰبِ · ٱلصِّرَٰطَ · وَٱنْحَرْ

## Listen-for

**1. ك pulled back into ق, or ق pushed forward into ك.**
- *Listen for:* the two letters sounding like one sound with two spellings. Test with أَقْ / أَكْ back to back.
- *Why it happens:* they are one notch apart on the same organ, and no European or Turkic language has the ق at all, so the nearest available sound is ك.
- *Correction cue:* "Same tongue, one step further back — and let the back of the tongue drop away from the roof, not just slide."

**2. ه placed at the wrong depth, usually too shallow.**
- *Listen for:* an English *h* made at the mouth rather than a throat ه made beside the hamzah.
- *Why it happens:* ه is easy, so it gets no attention, and English has a shallow *h* pre-installed.
- *Correction cue:* "أ then ه. Same floor of the throat. They are neighbours — make them sound like neighbours."

**3. ض made with the tip of the tongue.**
- *Listen for:* a heavy د. Ask her to hold the ض for two seconds; a tip-made ض stops dead, a correct one continues.
- *Why it happens:* every other tongue letter she knows uses the tip, back or middle. The side-edge is a motion her mouth has never been asked for.
- *Correction cue:* "Not the tip — the side. Push the side of your tongue out into your back teeth." Full treatment in 3-08; today, just name it.

**4. ل, ن and ر collapsing into one contact point.**
- *Listen for:* an ر that sounds like a flap of the same ن she just made, or a ل and ن you cannot tell apart with your eyes shut.
- *Why it happens:* the three points are within a few millimetres of each other, and in most languages there is no reason to keep them apart that precisely.
- *Correction cue:* "لْ — wide. نْ — tip, and open the nose. رْ — back of the tip, deeper. Feel it walk backwards." Run Drill 4.

**5. The nasal sound produced in the mouth.**
- *Listen for:* an ن or م that survives the pinch test. If pinching the nose does not change it, it is not being made in the khayshūm.
- *Why it happens:* she was taught the *closure* (tongue, lips) and nobody named the *exit* (nose). This lesson is where that gets fixed.
- *Correction cue:* "Pinch. Say it again. If you can still hear it, the sound is coming out of the wrong door."

**6. Answering "where is it made" from the sound rather than from the body.**
- *Listen for:* her guessing zones fluently but incorrectly, especially on ك, ه and ج.
- *Why it happens:* she is matching the letter to a remembered *description* rather than running the sukūn probe. This is the failure mode that makes the whole map useless later.
- *Correction cue:* "Don't tell me. Say أَـ‑ـْ and point." The finger comes first, always.

## Hifz for this session

**New Sabaq — al-Fātiḥa 1:1–1:2.**

> بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ
> ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ

This is the first session of the memorisation strand, so set the machinery up properly before setting the portion.

**Session order, every session from now on: Manzil → Sabqi → new Sabaq.** Revision before new memorisation, without exception. Today there is no Manzil and no Sabqi, so say the rule out loud anyway and write it at the top of her page — it is the habit, not today's content, that matters.

**Repetitions: 20–40 on the new portion.** Method: read it **three times from the page** with full attention to pronunciation, then look away and recite. If it fails, find the exact word where it failed, repeat *that*, and alternate look / no-look until it holds.

**Ten extra repetitions after any caught error**, on the corrected portion alone, as its own step. Give it its own tick-box; folded into "keep going until it's right", it never actually happens.

She recites these two ayat in every rakʿah already, so expect the memory to be nearly free and the *tajweed* to be the work. Hold her to the makhārij: the ح of ٱلْحَمْدُ at the middle of the throat, the ل of لِلَّهِ light and clear, the ع of ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ squeezed and held.

## Homework

**20–30 min/day, five days.** Split it: about ten minutes of makhārij, about fifteen of hifz.

1. **Two minutes — Drill 1, the sukūn probe.** Twelve letters, finger on the face each time. This is the method; everything else is content.
2. **Two minutes — Drill 2**, down the throat and back, then the six mixed cold.
3. **Three minutes — Drill 3**, the ten tongue points forwards and backwards, eyes closed.
4. **Two minutes — Drill 4**, the ل / ن / ر cluster, then the three words.
5. **One minute — Drill 6**, the lip degrees with a hand on the lips.
6. **Hifz — 1:1–1:2.** Read from the page, not from memory of the prayer. Twenty to forty reps, then recite it aloud once to your phone and listen back for the ح, the ل and the ع.

**Bring to the next session:** the five zones with their counts, written out from memory, and one letter whose zone you are not certain of. We will probe it together.
