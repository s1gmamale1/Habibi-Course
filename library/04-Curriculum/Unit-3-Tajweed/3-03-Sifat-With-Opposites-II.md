# Lesson 3-03 — Ṣifāt with opposites II: isti'lā / istifāl, iṭbāq / infitāḥ

> **This is the load-bearing lesson of Unit 3.1.** The seven letters she leaves with today are the input set for the rā' rules, the lām of the Divine Name, the whole tafkhīm/tarqīq system, and the heavy variant of the ghunnah in ikhfā'. Nothing downstream works if these seven are not solid. Teach it as though the rest of the course depends on it, because it does.

## Objectives

- Student can recite **خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ** and name its seven letters, and can state that these seven are heavy in **every** position and with **every** vowel.
- Student can distinguish the two *degrees* of heaviness — the clamped four ص ض ط ظ against the unclamped three خ غ ق — and can hear which she is listening to.
- Student can keep a light letter light when it sits next to a heavy one, in both directions.
- Student can say why isti'lā' is the ṣifah that everything later in the course is built on, and can name at least two rules that take these seven letters as their input.

## Hook

Write **ٱلصِّرَٰطَ** on the board and ask her to read it.

Listen to the ص. It carries a **kasra**, and under a kasra almost every student in the world flattens the ص into a plain س. She will too. The word becomes *as-sirāṭ*, and she will not hear that anything happened.

Then say both versions back to her — the flattened one and the correct one — and give her the rule that this lesson turns on:

> **The seven heavy letters are heavy under every vowel. The vowel changes the degree. It never changes the fact.**

Then the second error, which is the mirror of the first. Write **طَعَامِ** (107:3). She will get the ط heavy — she has had that since 1-14. Now listen to what comes *after* it. In most students the ع, the alif and the م all come out dark too, dragged along by the ط. That is **bleeding**, and it is the more common of the two faults at her level.

The sentence for the lesson:

> **The skill is not heaviness. She already has heaviness. The skill is the SWITCH — heavy, then light, on demand, with no leakage in either direction.**

## What they already know

- **Lesson 1-14 taught her ص ض ط ظ as "the heavy letters" and taught them well** — including the point most courses miss, that you cannot really hear the dome on the consonant itself, you hear it **in the neighbouring vowel**. She already has the self-test from that lesson: say سَا, then صَا; if they sound the same, the dome is not happening. **Today does not teach her heaviness. It gives her the ṣifah behind a cue she has had for a year, adds three more letters to the set, and splits the set into two degrees.**
- **Lesson 1-14 also gave her ٱلضَّآلِّينَ** with a correct ض, a held shadda and a six-count madd, and told her what goes wrong when it is read as *aẓ-ẓāllīn*. That word is today's centrepiece too.
- **Lesson 3-01** placed ص ض ط ظ on the tongue map — the three tooth rows, each with a heavy letter beside its light twin, plus ض alone on the side of the tongue. Today explains what makes each row's heavy member heavy.
- **Lesson 3-02** established the two axes she must not mix. **Today adds two more axes, and neither of them is about breath or about flow.** Say that before you start.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Pair 3 — al-isti'lā' / al-istifāl. This is about the BACK OF THE TONGUE.**

   **Isti'lā'** (الاسْتِعْلاء, "elevation, seeking to rise") is the **rising of the rear of the tongue toward the roof of the mouth** during the letter. The raised tongue body enlarges the space behind it and darkens the sound. The letter is heard as *mufakhkham* — heavy.

   **Istifāl** (الاسْتِفال, "lowering") is the opposite: the tongue body stays on the floor of the mouth, the resonance stays forward and bright, and the letter is *muraqqaq* — light.

   Seven isti'lā' letters. **Write the mnemonic large and leave it on the board for the rest of the unit:**

   > # خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ
   > *khuṣṣa ḍaghṭin qiẓ*
   > **خ · ص · ض · غ · ط · ق · ظ**

   Everything else — all twenty-two remaining letters — is istifāl.

   Say the count out loud: **7 and 22.** Then say the thing that makes istifāl teachable: **istifāl is not something you do. It is what remains when you do nothing.** She is not learning to make twenty-two letters light; she is learning to stop leaking heaviness into them.

2. **Drill the mnemonic until it is automatic, before going further.** Three minutes, now, not later. خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ, said as a phrase, then unpacked into seven letters, then re-said. This phrase is going to be cited in every heaviness lesson from here to the end of the course.

3. **The kasra test — and this is the moment the lesson either lands or does not.**
   These seven are heavy **in every position and with every vowel**. The instinct to flatten them under a kasra is overwhelming, because a kasra pulls the tongue forward and up at the front while isti'lā' needs it up at the back.
   Run the trap deliberately:

   **ٱلصِّرَٰطَ** (1:6) — the ص has a kasra, and it is still heavy.
   Then **قِ · طِ · ضِ · ظِ · غِ · خِ** in isolation, each held, each dark.

   If the ص goes light under the kasra, isti'lā' has not been internalised, whatever she says about the mnemonic.

4. **Pair 4 — al-iṭbāq / al-infitāḥ. This is about HOW MUCH tongue meets the palate.**

   **Iṭbāq** (الإطْباق, "clamping, putting a lid on a pot") is the clamping of the **body** of the tongue flat up against the roof of the mouth, sealing the airspace between them and trapping the sound in the pocket underneath. Where isti'lā' merely *raises* the rear of the tongue, iṭbāq brings a whole stretch of tongue **flat against** the palate. This is the strongest heaviness in the language.

   **Infitāḥ** (الانْفِتاح, "opening") is the opposite: a gap stays open between tongue and palate and the air passes out between them. Twenty-five letters — everything except the four.

   Four iṭbāq letters: **ص · ض · ط · ظ**

5. **Now say the structural fact, and draw it, because it is what makes heaviness have *degrees*.**

   > **Iṭbāq is a strict subset of isti'lā'. Every clamped letter is heavy. Not every heavy letter is clamped.**

   | | | |
   |---|---|---|
   | **isti'lā' + iṭbāq** | ص · ض · ط · ظ | the heaviest class in the language |
   | **isti'lā' + infitāḥ** | خ · غ · ق | heavy, but audibly lighter |
   | **istifāl + infitāḥ** | the other 22 | light |

   There is no fourth cell. A letter cannot be clamped without being raised.

   This is exactly why **ص is heavier than خ, and ط heavier than ق.** Demonstrate it: صَا then خَا, back to back. Both heavy. Not equally.

6. **Each of the four clamped letters has a light twin at or near its makhraj — and that is where the errors are.**

   | Clamped | Collapses to | Test word |
   |---|---|---|
   | ص | س | فَصَلِّ (108:2) |
   | ض | د, or a plain "z" | ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ (1:7) |
   | ط | ت | أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ (108:1) |
   | ظ | ذ, or a plain "z" | — |

   The **half-heavy letter** is the characteristic failure and the commonest intermediate plateau: the student raises the back of the tongue (isti'lā' achieved) but never flattens it against the palate (iṭbāq missing). The result sounds *sort of* heavy.

   **The correction is tactile, not auditory.** Have her feel the width of tongue contact on ط — a broad, flat seal — then transfer that same contact to ص and ظ. Do not try to fix a half-heavy letter by ear; she cannot hear the difference yet, which is why it is a plateau.

   Flag the **ذ / ز / ظ / ض collapse to a plain "z"** now, and say whose problem it is: in Persian and Urdu all four of those letters are genuinely pronounced identically as [z], and Turkish and Uzbek speakers arrive with much the same merger. A student from that background is not being careless — her language has no distinction here at all, so the contrast has to be built from nothing. It is the highest-priority error in this family to catch early. Lesson 3-08 is devoted to the hardest pair of it.

7. **The bleeding drill — the switch, in both directions.**
   Take **طَعَامِ** (107:3) apart: **طَـ ‑ عَـ ‑ ا ‑ مِ.** The ط is heavy. The ع, the alif and the م are light. Say them slowly with a deliberate *drop* after the ط, then speed up without losing the drop.

   Then the reverse direction on **تَضْلِيلٍ** (105:2): a light ت, a clamped sākin ض, and then a ل that must come back light immediately.

   Then reset entirely on **بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ** (114:1) — a whole phrase with nothing heavy in it. **Always end a heaviness drill on a light word.** The skill is the switch, not the heaviness.

   And name the mirror error while you are here: **over-lightening.** A student freshly drilled on tarqīq starts thinning everything into a nasal, English-sounding register, and eventually thins ص back into س. Light is not thin.

8. **Point forward, explicitly, and do not skip this step.**
   Tell her where the seven letters go:

   - **Tafkhīm and tarqīq (lesson 3-07)** are isti'lā' restated as a performance rule, with five degrees by vowel. The input set is these seven.
   - **The rā' rules (3-10, 3-11)** decide heavy or light partly by whether a **neighbouring letter is one of these seven**. The five-word exception list she will meet in 3-11 exists entirely because of isti'lā'.
   - **The lām of the Divine Name (3-12)** is the other conditional letter, calibrated against this set.
   - **Ikhfā' (Unit 3.4)** conceals a nūn behind a two-count hum — and **that hum is heavy when the following letter is one of ص ض ط ظ ق, and light before the other ten.** The other two isti'lā' letters, خ and غ, are throat letters and take a different rule. A student who has not internalised isti'lā' produces one flat ghunnah everywhere and cannot execute ikhfā' at all.

   Say the summary sentence: **خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ is the single most reused set of letters in the syllabus. Learn it once, properly, today.**

## Examples

| Text | Ref | What it demonstrates |
|---|---|---|
| ٱلصِّرَٰطَ | 1:6 | ص heavy **under a kasra** — the diagnostic, and the commonest flattening |
| ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ | 1:7 | غ (raised, unclamped) then ض (clamped) — both degrees, one word |
| ٱلضَّآلِّينَ | 1:7 | ض clamped and held through a shadda; the alif after it inherits |
| خَلَقَ | 113:2 | خ and ق — heavy without iṭbāq, audibly lighter than the four |
| طَعَامِ | 107:3 | the drop: ط heavy, then ع, ا, م all light |
| ٱلْحَمْدُ | 1:2 | istifāl throughout — nothing rises |
| بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ | 114:1 | the reset phrase; end every heavy drill here |
| تَضْلِيلٍ | 105:2 | sākin ض clamped mid-word, then a light ل immediately |

**Teacher note on ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ.** This word is worth five minutes on its own. It contains both degrees of heaviness in sequence — the غ raised but open, then the ض raised *and* clamped — with a light م and a light و around them. If she can read this word with four correct weights in a row, she has the lesson. It is also in the ayah she says seventeen times a day, so the drill is free.

**Teacher note on خَلَقَ.** Use this as the demonstration that heavy is not one thing. Say **خَلَقَ** and then **ٱلصِّرَٰطَ** back to back and ask which is heavier. The answer is the ص, by a clear margin, and she should be able to hear it by the end of the session.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the mnemonic.** Every day, first:

> **خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ**

Say the phrase three times, then name its seven letters, then say the phrase again. Then say the count: **seven heavy, twenty-two light.**

**Drill 2 — the kasra trap.** All seven letters under a kasra. Each one must stay dark:

خِ · صِ · ضِ · غِ · طِ · قِ · ظِ

Then in words: ٱلصِّرَٰطَ (1:6) · فِى تَضْلِيلٍ ... — and hold each heavy letter for a full second so you can hear whether it stayed dark for the whole letter or only at the start.

**Drill 3 — the two degrees.** Say each pair back to back. Both are heavy; the first is heavier:

| Clamped | Unclamped |
|---|---|
| صَا | خَا |
| طَا | قَا |
| ضَا | غَا |
| ظَا | قَا |

If the two members of a pair sound identical, iṭbāq is missing from the left one.

**Drill 4 — clamped against its light twin.** The minimal pairs that carry the whole system. Say each pair four times:

سَا / صَا · تَا / طَا · دَا / ضَا · ذَا / ظَا

Then reversed: صَا / سَا · طَا / تَا · ضَا / دَا · ظَا / ذَا. **If you cannot hear the difference in the pair, you cannot yet produce either member of it reliably.**

**Drill 5 — the drop.** Heavy, then immediately light. Say it slowly with a deliberate gap, then close the gap:

طَـ ‑ عَـ ‑ ا ‑ مِ → طَعَامِ (107:3)
تَـ ‑ ضْـ ‑ لِيلٍ → تَضْلِيلٍ (105:2)
ٱلْـ ‑ مَـ ‑ غْـ ‑ ضُـ ‑ وبِ → ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ (1:7)
ٱلْـ ‑ صِّـ ‑ رَٰ ‑ طَ → ٱلصِّرَٰطَ (1:6)

**Drill 6 — the reset.** After every round of Drill 5, read one word with nothing heavy in it and check that nothing is dark:

ٱلْحَمْدُ (1:2) · بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ (114:1) · أَنْعَمْتَ (1:7) · ٱلْبَيْتِ (106:3)

**Drill 7 — sort them cold.** Say each letter and answer with two words — "heavy, clamped" / "heavy, open" / "light":

ط · ك · ص · خ · د · ظ · ب · ق · ض · س · غ · ل · ت · ز · ن

**Drill 8 — the "z" patrol,** if the four-way collapse is present at all. Four letters that must be four different sounds:

ذْ · زْ · ظْ · ضْ

Hand on the throat (all four are voiced), tongue tip visible on ذ and ظ, tongue tip *nowhere near the front* on ض. If any two are the same, they are both wrong.

## Listen-for

**1. A heavy letter going light under a kasra.**
- *Listen for:* ٱلصِّرَٰطَ read as *as-sirāṭ*. Also قِيلَ-shaped words, and any طِ or ضِ.
- *Why it happens:* the kasra pulls the front of the tongue up and forward; isti'lā' needs the *back* up. The two feel like they compete, and the vowel wins.
- *Correction cue:* "The vowel changes how heavy. It never changes whether. Say صِ and keep the back of your tongue where it was for صَ."

**2. Heaviness bleeding onto the following letters.**
- *Listen for:* the ع, ا and م of طَعَامِ coming out dark. Also the ل after a sākin ض in تَضْلِيلٍ.
- *Why it happens:* inertia. The tongue is still up from the previous letter when the light one arrives. She is not mispronouncing the heavy letter; she is failing to switch back.
- *Correction cue:* "Heavy fills the letter and stops at its edge." Then Drill 5 slowly, with the gap, until the drop is deliberate.

**3. The half-heavy letter — isti'lā' without iṭbāq.**
- *Listen for:* a ص or ظ that is "sort of heavy". Compare against ط, which she already makes correctly.
- *Why it happens:* raising the back of the tongue is intuitive; flattening a broad stretch of it against the palate is not, and nothing in her first language asks for it.
- *Correction cue:* Tactile only. "Say ط. Feel how wide the contact is. Now make ص with that same width." Do not try to fix this by ear.

**4. ض, ظ, ذ and ز all arriving as the same "z".**
- *Listen for:* two of the four sounding identical. If any two match, both are wrong.
- *Why it happens:* Persian and Urdu genuinely pronounce all four as [z]; Turkic speakers land in nearly the same place. There is no distinction to transfer.
- *Correction cue:* "Where is your tongue tip? ذ and ظ — on the edge of your top teeth, where I can see it. ض — nowhere near the front; the side of the tongue, against your molars." Full treatment in 3-08.

**5. Confusing heavy with loud.**
- *Listen for:* volume rising on the heavy letters and dropping on the light ones.
- *Why it happens:* "heavy" is a metaphor, and loudness is the nearest available interpretation of it.
- *Correction cue:* "Tafkhīm is resonance, not volume. Say ق quietly and heavy. Now say it loud and light. Both are possible — so they are not the same thing."

**6. Dragging the makhraj backwards to "deepen" a letter.**
- *Listen for:* a ص whose tongue tip has slid back off the teeth, producing something between ص and a retroflex.
- *Why it happens:* she is trying to add depth with the only movement she knows.
- *Correction cue:* "The tip does not move. Only the back rises." Put a finger where the tip should stay.

**7. Over-lightening after a tarqīq drill.**
- *Listen for:* the whole recitation thinning into a bright, nasal register, and ص drifting back toward س.
- *Why it happens:* over-correction, and it always follows a successful bleeding fix. Name it as a drill artefact when it appears.
- *Correction cue:* "Light is not thin. The light letters are just normal — you are not doing anything to them."

## Hifz for this session

**Manzil / Sabqi first.** 1:1–1:4 from memory before anything new. Ten dedicated repetitions on any word that needed correcting.

**New Sabaq — al-Fātiḥa 1:5–1:6.**

> إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ
> ٱهْدِنَا ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ

Twenty to forty repetitions from the page. Three things from today live in these two ayat, and you should name them as she memorises rather than after:

- **ٱلصِّرَٰطَ** is today's diagnostic word. She is now memorising it, so the heavy ص under a kasra and the heavy ط get locked in correctly the first time — or locked in wrong for years. Spend the time here.
- **إِيَّاكَ** has a doubled ي she already knows to hold from lesson 2-05. Nothing in it is heavy; use it as a reset.
- **نَعْبُدُ** and **نَسْتَعِينُ** both hang on the ع from last lesson — squeezed, voiced, holdable, no click.

## Homework

**25–35 min/day, five days.** This lesson gets more time than the others in the unit. Say so.

1. **One minute — Drill 1.** خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ, three times, then the seven letters, then the count.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 4, the minimal pairs.** سَا/صَا · تَا/طَا · دَا/ضَا · ذَا/ظَا, four times each, both directions.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 2, the kasra trap.** All seven under a kasra, each held for a full second.
4. **Two minutes — Drill 3, the two degrees.** If a pair sounds identical, do that pair ten more times.
5. **Three minutes — Drill 5, the drop**, then **Drill 6, the reset.** Always in that order, always ending light.
6. **Two minutes — Drill 7, sort them cold.** Two words per letter, no hesitation.
7. **Hifz — Manzil/Sabqi (1:1–1:4), then new Sabaq (1:5–1:6).** Revision first, every day.

**Bring to the next session:** خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ from memory, and a recording of yourself reading ٱهْدِنَا ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ. We will listen to the ص under its kasra together.
