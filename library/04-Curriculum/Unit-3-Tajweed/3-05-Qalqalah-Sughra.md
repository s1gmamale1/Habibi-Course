# Lesson 3-05 — Qalqalah ṣughrā: the minor bounce

## Objectives

- Student can name the five qalqalah letters via **قُطْبُ جَدٍّ** and can **derive** them from shiddah + jahr rather than only recalling them.
- Student can produce a bounce that is audible, brief and **carries no vowel**, on a sākin qalqalah letter mid-word.
- Student can tell a **lāzim** (original, written) sukūn from an 'āriḍ (stopping) one, and can say why today's rule is the ṣughrā one.
- Student can read eight ayat containing sākin qalqalah letters without either swallowing the bounce or turning it into a syllable.

## Hook

Lead with the sound she is probably making.

Ask her to read **وَيَقْطَعُونَ** (2:27). Listen to the ق. In about four students out of five you will hear *ya‑qa‑ṭa'ūn* — a small vowel has appeared after the sākin ق, and the word has grown a syllable that is not in it.

Say the correct one back: *yaq‑ṭa'ūn*. Then say the wrong one. Then say the rule:

> **The bounce is a RELEASE, not a vowel. It has no length you could count and no colour you could name. If you can hear an "a", "i" or "u" in it, it is wrong.**

Then the second error, which sits at the opposite end. Ask her to read **يَجْعَلْ** (105:2). Many students, told to avoid the phantom vowel, produce a dead, swallowed ج instead — the letter simply disappears into silence and the word arrives as *ya'al*.

So: **too much and it becomes a syllable; too little and the letter vanishes.** The whole lesson lives in the narrow space between those two.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 1-13 already gave her the five letters and had her bouncing them.** Its own objective says it: *"can name the five qalqalah letters (ق ط ب ج د) and bounce them."* She has been doing this for a year. **Today does not teach her the bounce. It formalises the ṣifah behind a cue she already has, tells her why those five and no others, and splits the rule into its two degrees.** Say that to her in exactly those terms — she should feel she is being given a reason, not a task.
- **Lesson 1-14** drilled the ط-bounces / ظ-is-held contrast. That contrast *is* today's derivation, learned by ear: ط is blocked, so it must pop; ظ flows, so it fades. She has the evidence; today she gets the theory.
- **Lesson 3-02, two sessions ago,** built the grid that answers the "why those five" question. Go back to it — the answer is already on her page.
- **Lesson 2-03/2-04** gave her the sukūn and the rule that closed letters do not take helper vowels. The phantom-vowel error in the hook is that same weakness re-surfacing under a new load. Diagnose it as that when it appears.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Derive the five letters before naming them.** Do not open the mnemonic yet. Ask her two questions:

   - Which letters have **shiddah** — the sound completely blocked? *أَجِدْ قَطٍ بَكَتْ*: ء ج د ق ط ب ك ت.
   - Of those, which are **jahr** — the breath stopped too? Cross out ك and ت, which are hams.

   What is left is **ء ج د ق ط ب**. Now cross out the hamzah — excluded by transmission, because it is released as a clean glottal stop, not a bounce.

   **What remains is five: ق ط ب ج د.**

   Now say the reasoning as a sentence: **a sākin letter that is both blocked and voiced has nowhere to send the sound. When the articulators part, the trapped voice escapes as an audible bounce. That is qalqalah.** It is not an extra property bolted onto five arbitrary letters; it is the necessary consequence of two ṣifāt occurring together.

   Only *now* give her the mnemonic, as a memory aid rather than as the rule:

   > **قُطْبُ جَدٍّ** — *quṭbu jaddin*, "the pole of a grandfather"
   > ق · ط · ب · ج · د

2. **Name the ṣifah and the two rules built on it.**
   **Qalqalah** (القَلْقَلَة) linguistically means disturbance, shaking, agitation. Technically it is a **disturbance in the makhraj when the letter is sākin**, heard as a small bounce at the moment of release.
   There are two applied rules, split by *why* the letter is sākin:

   | Situation | Degree |
   |---|---|
   | Sukūn written into the word, mid-word — يَجْعَلْ | **ṣughrā** — today |
   | Word-final sukūn, and you read straight on — لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ… | **ṣughrā** — today |
   | Word-final, and you **stop** there — …وَلَمْ يُولَدْ | kubrā — next lesson |

3. **Define the trigger precisely, in two conditions.** Both must hold:
   1. The letter is one of **ق ط ب ج د**.
   2. It carries an **original (lāzim) sukūn** — permanent, part of how the word is written — either mid-word, or at the end of a word you continue past in waṣl.

   **Ṣughrā** means "minor", and it is deliberately the *lighter* of the two degrees. Say that now, because the commonest stylistic fault at this stage is giving every mid-word bounce a full dramatic kick.

4. **Teach the performance in four steps, and make her do each one separately first.**
   1. **Close the makhraj completely**, as the letter requires — both lips for ب, tongue tip at the tooth roots for د and ط, middle of the tongue for ج, deepest tongue for ق.
   2. **Release it sharply**, letting the trapped voice escape as a short click of sound. That released puff *is* the qalqalah.
   3. **Keep it brief.** It is a release, not a syllable. It has no measurable duration in ḥarakāt — you cannot count it, because there is nothing there to count.
   4. **Add no vowel colour.** يَجْعَلْ is *yaj‑'al*, never *yaja‑'al*.

   **In plain terms:** shut your mouth on the letter, then pop it open. That pop is the whole thing — no vowel after it, and nothing to count.

5. **The bounce carries the weight of its letter — and this is where 3-03 pays off.**
   Two of the five are isti'lā' letters, so **their bounce is heavy**: ق and ط. The other three — ب, ج, د — are istifāl, so their bounce is **light**.

   Demonstrate both, back to back:
   - **يُبْصِرُونَ** (2:17) — a light ب, bouncing light.
   - **وَيَقْطَعُونَ** (2:27) — a heavy ق, bouncing heavy, immediately followed by a heavy ط.

   Say the rule as a sentence: **qalqalah and tafkhīm are two independent properties applying at once, not one rule.** ق and ط are heavy *and* they bounce. ب, ج and د bounce while staying light. Students conflate the two constantly because ق and ط are the loudest examples of both.

6. **Name the ط problem explicitly, because it is the hardest single sound in the language.**
   A correct sākin ط must be **clamped** (iṭbāq), **heavy** (isti'lā'), and **bounced** — all three at once, with no vowel added. Nothing else in Arabic asks for that combination.
   Drill it on its own: **أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ** (108:1) and **أَطْعَمَهُم** (106:4) and **مَطْلَعِ** (97:5). In the last two, the letter straight after the ط is a light one, so she has to bounce heavy and land light in the same breath.

7. **Two exclusions, said once and clearly.**

   - **Nothing else bounces.** A sākin ل, ن, ر, س or م is released cleanly with no echo. The sākin ل of ٱلْفَلَقِ is the standard contrast: clearly articulated, released, **not** bounced. If she starts bouncing ل, she has generalised the rule; send her back to the derivation, because a ل is tawassut, not shiddah, so there is nothing trapped to escape.
   - **Do not bounce the first half of a shadda mid-word.** The first half of a shadda *is* sākin, but qalqalah is not applied to it in waṣl. تَبَّتْ (111:1) has a held ب, not a bounced one. The final ت of that same word is not a qalqalah letter at all.

8. **Name the disagreement once, as [[Methodology]] requires.**
   The **jumhūr** (majority) hold that the bounce carries **no vowel colour at all**, inclining at most very slightly toward fatḥa in every case. A minority, associated with some Egyptian schools, hold that it inclines toward the vowel that *precedes* it, so the bounce in يُبْصِرُونَ would carry a kasra tint.
   **This course teaches the jumhūr position: no vowel colour.** Say why the choice is also pedagogically right — it makes the commonest error, adding a vowel, unambiguously an error.

## Examples

| Text | Ref | Letter | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| يَجْعَلْ | 105:2 | ج | original sukūn, mid-word — light bounce |
| يَلِدْ | 112:3 | د | word-final, read on through — still ṣughrā |
| أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ | 108:1 | ط | clamped, heavy and bouncing at once |
| أَطْعَمَهُم | 106:4 | ط | heavy bounce, then a light ع immediately |
| يُبْصِرُونَ | 2:17 | ب | light bounce mid-word |
| ٱلْقَدْرِ | 97:1 | د | light bounce, sākin mid-word |
| وَيَقْطَعُونَ | 2:27 | ق | heavy bounce straight into a heavy ط |
| مَطْلَعِ | 97:5 | ط | heavy bounce, then a light ل |

**Teacher note on يَجْعَلْ and يَلِدْ.** Start here, every time. Both sit in surahs she is either memorising now or will memorise inside this unit, and both are light bounces, which is the easier half. Do not open on ق or ط.

**Teacher note on يَلِدْ.** This is the word that teaches the ṣughrā/kubrā line before she has met kubrā. **لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ** (112:3) contains both degrees in one ayah: the د of يَلِدْ is ṣughrā because you carry on, and the د of يُولَدْ becomes kubrā the moment you stop there. Read the whole ayah and point at the difference. Next lesson is that difference.

**Teacher note on the reference examples.** يُبْصِرُونَ (2:17), ٱلْقَدْرِ (97:1), وَيَقْطَعُونَ (2:27) and مَطْلَعِ (97:5) lie **outside the surahs you are memorising** — they are reading practice, not hifz. Say that to her so she does not try to bank them.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — derive, then name.** Every session, out loud, before anything else:

> "Shiddah letters: أَجِدْ قَطٍ بَكَتْ. Remove the hams ones — ك and ت. Remove the hamzah. **قُطْبُ جَدٍّ.**"

Deriving beats reciting. A student who can derive the five never needs to recall them.

**Drill 2 — the bare bounce.** Hamzah in front, sukūn on the letter. Five letters, ten times each, listening for a click with nothing after it:

أَقْ · أَطْ · أَبْ · أَجْ · أَدْ

Then the negative control, immediately after — five letters that must **not** bounce:

أَلْ · أَنْ · أَرْ · أَسْ · أَمْ

**Drill 3 — light bounces first.** ب, ج, د only. Read each word and let the bounce stay light:

يَجْعَلْ · يَلِدْ · يُبْصِرُونَ · ٱلْقَدْرِ · أَبْصَٰرِهِمْ · وَٱلْفَجْرِ

**Drill 4 — heavy bounces.** ق and ط. Heavy through the closure, heavy through the release:

أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ · أَطْعَمَهُم · مَطْلَعِ · وَيَقْطَعُونَ

**Drill 5 — bounce heavy, land light.** The hardest combination in the lesson. In each word the letter after the bounced ط is light, and it must arrive light:

مَطْلَعِ (ط → ل) · أَطْعَمَهُم (ط → ع) · أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ (ط → ي)

**Drill 6 — the phantom-vowel test.** Read each word twice: once deliberately wrong with the extra vowel, once correctly. Naming the error out loud is what makes it audible:

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| *ya‑qa‑ṭa'ūn* | *yaq‑ṭa'ūn* |
| *ya‑ja‑'al* | *yaj‑'al* |
| *yu‑ba‑ṣirūn* | *yub‑ṣirūn* |
| *al‑qa‑dar* | *al‑qadr* |

**Drill 7 — do not bounce these.** Sākin letters that are released cleanly and silently. If any of them kicks, stop and re-derive:

ٱلْفَلَقِ (113:1) · ٱلْحَمْدُ (1:2) · أَنْعَمْتَ (1:7) · وَٱنْحَرْ (108:2) · ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ (1:6)

**Drill 8 — cold reading.** Read straight through, bouncing every qalqalah letter and nothing else:

> لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ (112:3) — but **do not stop** on يُولَدْ; carry on to the next ayah so both stay ṣughrā.
> أَلَمْ يَجْعَلْ كَيْدَهُمْ فِى تَضْلِيلٍ (105:2)
> إِنَّآ أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ ٱلْكَوْثَرَ (108:1)

## Listen-for

**1. A vowel on the bounce — the priority error.**
- *Listen for:* an extra syllable. *ya‑qa‑ṭa'ūn*, *yu‑ba‑ṣirūn*, *al‑qa‑dar*. Count the syllables if you are not sure.
- *Why it happens:* the closed letter needs to be released, and a vowel is the only release her mouth has been trained to produce. It is the same cluster-breaking habit that lesson 2-03 fixed, resurfacing under a harder load. Diagnose it as that, not as a new fault.
- *Correction cue:* "That was a syllable. I want a click." Then clap once, sharply, and say: "That long. Nothing after it."

**2. No bounce at all — the letter swallowed.**
- *Listen for:* يَجْعَلْ arriving as *ya'al*; a dead ج with nothing on the release.
- *Why it happens:* a fully blocked, fully voiced sākin letter is genuinely inaudible without the bounce. She closed it correctly and simply never opened it.
- *Correction cue:* "I could not hear the ج at all. Close it — now let it go, and let me hear it go."

**3. Over-bouncing — the echo becomes an extra beat.**
- *Listen for:* a bounce with length, or a second smaller bounce behind the first, like a stutter.
- *Why it happens:* over-correction, and it arrives immediately after you fix error 2. Say so when it happens.
- *Correction cue:* "One bounce, not two, and no length. It is a door closing, not a ball dropping."

**4. Treating ṣughrā like kubrā.**
- *Listen for:* a full, strong, end-of-ayah bounce in the middle of a word.
- *Why it happens:* she has heard reciters bounce dramatically at the ends of ayat and has generalised that volume everywhere.
- *Correction cue:* "Minor. Save the big one for next lesson." Contrast يَجْعَلْ mid-word with a stopped يُولَدْ so she hears the difference in size.

**5. Losing the heaviness while bouncing ق or ط.**
- *Listen for:* a ط that bounces but arrives as a bounced ت; a ق that lightens on the release.
- *Why it happens:* she is spending all her attention on the bounce, and iṭbāq is the newest and least automatic of the three demands.
- *Correction cue:* "Clamp first, then bounce. The bounce comes out of the clamp — do not let go early." Back to Drill 4 in 3-03 if it is not fixable in one go.

**6. Bouncing a letter that does not bounce, especially ل and ر.**
- *Listen for:* a kick on the sākin ل of ٱلْفَلَقِ or ٱلْحَمْدُ, or on the ر of وَٱنْحَرْ.
- *Why it happens:* generalisation. A sākin ر in particular *feels* bouncy.
- *Correction cue:* "Can you hold it? Then it is not blocked, so there is nothing to bounce." Have her hold the ل for two seconds to prove it.

**7. Bouncing the first half of a shadda.**
- *Listen for:* تَبَّتْ read as *tab‑bat* with a kick between the halves.
- *Why it happens:* the first half of a shadda *is* sākin, so the rule looks like it should fire. It does not, in waṣl.
- *Correction cue:* "Hold it, do not bounce it. That is lesson 2-05's two counts, unchanged."

## Hifz for this session

**Manzil / Sabqi first.** 1:1–1:6 from memory. Ten dedicated repetitions on any corrected word.

**New Sabaq — al-Fātiḥa 1:7.**

> صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ

**One ayah only, and it gets the whole session's hifz budget.** This is by some distance the longest ayah in al-Fātiḥa — roughly as much text as 1:3 and 1:4 together — so do not let the "one ayah" make it sound small. Twenty to forty repetitions, and expect to need the upper end.

With this ayah **al-Fātiḥa is complete.** Have her recite all seven, unaided, at the end of the session, and mark the date.

Three tajweed points to hold while she memorises it, all from the last two lessons:

- **ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ** — the two degrees of heaviness in sequence, غ then ض, with light letters on either side. This is the word from lesson 3-03.
- **ٱلضَّآلِّينَ** — clamped ض, held through the shadda, alif inheriting the heaviness, and the long madd she was given in 1-14.
- **أَنْعَمْتَ** — all light, with a squeezed ع and a clean final ت that keeps its hams. Use it as the reset after the two heavy words.

Note for her: there is **no qalqalah anywhere in al-Fātiḥa's seventh ayah**. Today's rule does not fire here. Say that explicitly, because a student who has just learned a rule starts finding it everywhere.

## Homework

**25–35 min/day, five days.**

1. **One minute — Drill 1.** Derive the five from shiddah + jahr. Out loud. Do not just recite قُطْبُ جَدٍّ.
2. **Two minutes — Drill 2**, the bare bounce, then the five negative controls straight after.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 3**, light bounces.
4. **Two minutes — Drill 4**, heavy bounces, then **Drill 5**, bounce heavy and land light.
5. **Two minutes — Drill 6.** Say each word wrong, then right. Hearing your own error deliberately is what makes it audible accidentally.
6. **One minute — Drill 7.** The letters that must not bounce.
7. **Hifz — Manzil/Sabqi (1:1–1:6), then new Sabaq (1:7).** Revision first. At the end of the week, recite all seven ayat of al-Fātiḥa unaided once, and record it.

**Bring to the next session:** your recording of al-Fātiḥa, and the answer to this question — in لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ, why is the first د a smaller bounce than the second one when you stop at the end?
