# Lesson 3-06 — Qalqalah kubrā, and the case some call kubrā jiddan

## Objectives

- Student can state that kubrā is **a decision, not a property of the word** — the same word is ṣughrā if she reads on and kubrā if she stops.
- Student can stop on a word whose final letter is one of ق ط ب ج د and produce a clearly audible bounce with no vowel and no added length.
- Student can predict, before stopping, what the final letter will become when its vowel or tanwīn drops.
- Student can stop on a **mushaddad** qalqalah letter with one release after the full hold, not two.

## Hook

Ask her to read Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ and stop at the end of each ayah. Then listen to how ayah 1 and ayah 4 end.

**أَحَدٌ.** In most students it arrives as *aḥa* — the د is simply not there. Not swallowed, not weak: absent. She dropped the tanwīn correctly, because lesson 2-12 taught her to, and then there was nothing left to say, so she said nothing.

Give her the sentence:

> **When you stop, nothing follows the letter to carry it. If you do not bounce it, the letter does not exist. The listener hears a word that ends one letter early.**

Then the second error, at the other end again: *aḥada*, *al‑falaqa*, *wa‑qaba*. Having been told to make the ending audible, she makes it audible by putting a vowel on it — which is the one thing the rule forbids.

And a third, specific to today: **lengthening.** A bounce that turns into a small hummed beat, so that يُولَدْ ends on something you could count. **Stronger, not longer.** Say that phrase now and repeat it at least three times during the session; it is the whole difference between kubrā and ṣughrā.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-05, last session,** gave her the derivation (shiddah + jahr, minus the hams two, minus the hamzah), the five letters قُطْبُ جَدٍّ, the four-step performance, and the ṣughrā rule. **Today is the same bounce, in one new position, at one degree stronger.** Nothing about the letters or the mechanics changes.
- She left last session with a question to answer: *in لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ, why is the first د a smaller bounce than the second one when you stop at the end?* **Open by taking her answer.** That question is this lesson.
- **Lessons 2-12 to 2-14** taught practical stopping: drop the final ḥarakah, tāʾ marbūṭah becomes hāʾ, choose a place that does not break the sense. Everything about *how to stop* is already hers. Today adds one thing that happens **because** she stopped.
- **Lesson 2-05** taught her to hold a shadda for two full counts. That hold is unchanged today; only what happens at the *end* of it is new.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Draw the line between the two degrees, and draw it as a question about the sukūn.**

   | Kind of sukūn | Arabic | Where it comes from | Rule |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **lāzim** | لازِم | written into the word, permanent | ṣughrā |
   | **'āriḍ** | عارِض | produced by your stop, temporary | **kubrā** |

   **'Āriḍ** means accidental, incidental — a sukūn that was not there a moment ago and will not be there if you read the ayah again without stopping.

2. **State the trigger in one line.** You stop (waqf) on a word whose **final letter** is one of **ق ط ب ج د**.

   That is the entire rule. There is no letter list to learn, no exception, no second condition.

   **In plain terms:** same five letters, same pop — but this time you are stopping on it, so it is the last thing she hears. Make it bigger.

3. **Show what stopping does to the ending, because this is the step she has to do in her head before the bounce.**

   | In waṣl | At waqf | Bounce |
   |---|---|---|
   | أَحَدٌ | أَحَدْ | د |
   | ٱلْفَلَقِ | ٱلْفَلَقْ | ق |
   | وَقَبَ | وَقَبْ | ب |
   | ٱلْعُقَدِ | ٱلْعُقَدْ | د |
   | يُولَدْ | يُولَدْ | د — already sākin |

   The first four all change shape when she stops. The last one does not — it was already sākin, and stopping only changes the *size* of its bounce.

   Make her do the transformation out loud before reading: "أَحَدٌ … stopping … أَحَدْ … bounce the د."

   **In plain terms:** when you stop, the last letter loses its vowel and goes closed. That is what creates the bounce — you are not adding a rule, the stop hands you one.

4. **Say the sentence that is the actual content of this lesson.**

   > **Kubrā is a decision, not a property of the word.**

   The same word is ṣughrā if you read on and kubrā if you stop. **لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ** (112:3) contains both, ten syllables apart: the د of يَلِدْ is ṣughrā because you carry on into وَلَمْ, and the د of يُولَدْ is kubrā because the ayah ends and you stop.

   Read the ayah three ways with her:
   - Straight through to the next ayah — **both د's are ṣughrā.**
   - Stopping at the end — **first ṣughrā, second kubrā.**
   - Stopping after يَلِدْ (legitimate, if unusual) — **first kubrā.**

   Same letters. Same word. Different rule. This is her first encounter with a rule that depends on her own choice, and it will not be her last — she will meet exactly the same idea in the rā' rules in lesson 3-11.

5. **Teach the performance as a modification of last lesson's four steps.**
   1. **Close the makhraj fully and hold it a fraction longer** than for ṣughrā.
   2. **Release with a clear, firm bounce** — noticeably stronger than the mid-word version, because it is the last thing the listener hears.
   3. **Still no vowel colour.**
   4. **Still no added duration.** Say it again: **stronger, not longer, and not vowelled.**

   The difference between the two degrees is amplitude only. Demonstrate it: bounce a mid-word ج in يَجْعَلْ, then bounce a stopped د in يُولَدْ, and let her hear that the second is louder and no longer.

6. **The mushaddad case — and the naming question.**
   Stop on **وَتَبَّ** (111:1) or **بِٱلْحَقِّ** (103:3), and the final letter carries a **shadda**. Two things must both happen, in order:
   1. **Hold the doubled consonant for its full measure** — the two counts from lesson 2-05, unchanged.
   2. **Then release one firm bounce at the end.**

   Written out: وَتَبّْ, **not** وَتَبْ‑بْ as two separate bounces. One hold, one release.

   **On the name.** Some scholars give this a third, strongest degree of its own — **qalqalah akbar** (القَلْقَلَةُ الأَكْبَر), which is also met as *kubrā jiddan*, "very major". Others fold it straight into kubrā and hold that there are only **two** degrees.

   **This course teaches two degrees**, with the third named and demonstrated as a note rather than as a category she will be examined on. Say why the distinction does not change her work: **the instruction is identical either way — hold the shadda, then release one bounce.**

7. **The exclusion, and it is the one she will trip on this week.**
   **Only the five letters bounce.** Stopping on ٱلنَّاسْ or نَسْتَعِينْ or ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمْ produces a clean, silent stop on س, ن and م. No echo.
   This error appears reliably about a week after this lesson, because kubrā is the most audibly satisfying rule she has learned and the temptation to apply it at every ayah-end is strong. Warn her now, then check for it in lesson 3-09.

8. **Close on Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ, read end to end.**
   It is the ideal first drill: every one of its four ayat ends on a qalqalah letter or on a stop that produces one — أَحَدْ, ٱلصَّمَدْ, يُولَدْ, أَحَدْ. Four ayat, four kubrā bounces, one short surah she can already recite. Have her do it twice: once stopping at every ayah, once reading straight through so that only the last one is kubrā.

| In waṣl | At waqf | Ref | Letter |
|---|---|---|---|
| يُولَدْ | يُولَدْ | 112:3 | د — already sākin, ayah-final |
| أَحَدٌ | أَحَدْ | 112:1 | د — tanwīn dropped |
| ٱلْفَلَقِ | ٱلْفَلَقْ | 113:1 | ق — kasra dropped, and it is heavy |
| وَقَبَ | وَقَبْ | 113:3 | ب — fatḥa dropped, light |
| ٱلْعُقَدِ | ٱلْعُقَدْ | 113:4 | د — kasra dropped, light |
| وَتَبَّ | وَتَبّْ | 111:1 | ب with shadda — hold, then one release |
| بِٱلْحَقِّ | بِٱلْحَقّْ | 103:3 | ق with shadda — hold, then one release |
| يَلِدْ | — | 112:3 | د — **ṣughrā**, because you read on |

**Teacher note on ٱلْفَلَقِ and ٱلْعُقَدِ.** These are both in Sūrat al-Falaq, which she memorises in lessons 3-10 to 3-13 — she is meeting them now as reading and will meet them again as hifz. Point that out; it makes today's work feel like deposit rather than detour. ٱلْفَلَقْ is also the best heavy kubrā in her range: a clamped-adjacent, raised ق, bounced hard, at the end of a line she says every night.

**Teacher note on وَتَبَّ.** Sūrat al-Masad's first ayah ends on it, and the two counts of the shadda plus one firm bounce is a genuinely difficult sequence to time. Do it slowly with a finger tap: tap, tap, *release*.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — predict, then stop.** For each word, say the waqf form **before** you read it, then read it with the bounce:

أَحَدٌ → ? · ٱلْفَلَقِ → ? · وَقَبَ → ? · ٱلْعُقَدِ → ? · ٱلصَّمَدُ → ? · يُولَدْ → ?

Predicting first is what stops the vowel surviving into the stop.

**Drill 2 — Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ, stopping at every ayah.** Four ayat, four kubrā bounces:

> قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدْ · ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدْ · لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ · وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدْ

Then read it again straight through, stopping only at the end. Notice how many of the bounces just changed size.

**Drill 3 — the same word, both degrees.** Read the ayah three ways, as in the teaching sequence:

> لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ (112:3)

1. Through to the next ayah — both ṣughrā.
2. Stopping at the end — ṣughrā then kubrā.
3. Stopping after يَلِدْ — kubrā then ṣughrā.

**Drill 4 — light kubrā.** ب, ج, د at a stop. Light bounce, strong bounce:

أَحَدْ · يُولَدْ · وَقَبْ · ٱلْعُقَدْ · حَسَدْ — *(the last is حَسَدَ, 113:5, at waqf; note the د, not the س)*

**Drill 5 — heavy kubrā.** ق and ط at a stop. Heavy through the closure, heavy through the release:

ٱلْفَلَقْ · خَلَقْ *(113:2 at waqf)* · بِٱلْحَقّْ *(103:3)*

**Drill 6 — the shadda hold.** Two counts, tapped on the table, **then** one release. Never two releases:

وَتَبّْ (111:1) · بِٱلْحَقّْ (103:3)

Count the two beats on your fingers during the hold. The bounce happens after the second beat, not between them.

**Drill 7 — do not bounce these.** Stop on each of these and produce a clean, silent ending:

ٱلنَّاسْ (114:1) · نَسْتَعِينْ (1:5) · ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمْ (1:6) · ٱلضَّآلِّينْ (1:7) · ٱلْكَوْثَرْ (108:1)

If any of them kicks, you have generalised the rule. Go back and name the five letters.

**Drill 8 — Sūrat al-Falaq, cold.** Read all five ayat, stopping at each one, and bounce exactly where the rule fires:

> ٱلْفَلَقْ (ق — bounce) · خَلَقْ (ق — bounce) · وَقَبْ (ب — bounce) · ٱلْعُقَدْ (د — bounce) · حَسَدْ (د — bounce)

Five ayat, five bounces. This surah is unusually dense in kubrā, which is why it is the cold-read.

## Listen-for

**1. Stopping with no bounce — the defining kubrā error.**
- *Listen for:* *aḥa* instead of *aḥad*; a word that ends one letter early.
- *Why it happens:* she dropped the ḥarakah correctly and there was nothing left to carry the letter. Nothing in her reading habit says a bare final consonant needs help.
- *Correction cue:* "You stopped one letter early. The د is still there — let me hear it leave."

**2. A full vowel on the way out.**
- *Listen for:* *aḥada*, *al‑falaqa*, *waqaba*. Count syllables.
- *Why it happens:* over-correction of error 1, and the vowel is the only release she has practised. Exactly the same mechanism as in ṣughrā.
- *Correction cue:* "A click, not a syllable." Clap once and match it.

**3. Lengthening the bounce into a beat.**
- *Listen for:* a hum or a small hold after the release, something you could count.
- *Why it happens:* "stronger" gets interpreted as "bigger", and the nearest way to make a sound bigger is to make it longer.
- *Correction cue:* "Stronger, not longer." Then bounce a mid-word ج and a stopped د back to back so she hears that only the volume moved.

**4. Two bounces on a shadda.**
- *Listen for:* وَتَبْ‑بْ, a stutter with a release on each half.
- *Why it happens:* the shadda genuinely is two letters, and she has been taught to hear it as two. The instruction "one release" contradicts a correct instinct, so it needs stating explicitly rather than implying.
- *Correction cue:* "One hold, one release. The bounce belongs to the end of the hold, not to the middle of it." Tap the two beats and release on the third.

**5. Bouncing a final letter that is not one of the five.**
- *Listen for:* a kick on ٱلنَّاسْ, نَسْتَعِينْ, ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمْ — the ayah-ends she says most often.
- *Why it happens:* generalisation, and it arrives about a week after this lesson. It is the predictable cost of teaching a satisfying rule.
- *Correction cue:* "Which letter is that? Is it one of the five?" Make her answer rather than telling her.

**6. Applying kubrā mid-ayah because a qalqalah letter looks final.**
- *Listen for:* a full kubrā bounce on يَلِدْ when she is reading straight on.
- *Why it happens:* the word *ends* in a qalqalah letter, and "word-final" has been heard as the trigger. The trigger is the **stop**, not the position.
- *Correction cue:* "Did you stop there? Then it is the small one."

**7. Losing the heaviness of ق at a stop.**
- *Listen for:* ٱلْفَلَقْ ending on something closer to a bounced ك.
- *Why it happens:* the kasra that was holding the vowel colour has just been dropped, and with it goes the cue that was keeping the tongue back.
- *Correction cue:* "The vowel left. The heaviness did not." Then ٱلْفَلَقِ and ٱلْفَلَقْ back to back.

## Hifz for this session

**Manzil / Sabqi first.** Al-Fātiḥa, all seven ayat, unaided. This is the first session where there is a complete surah to revise — treat it as the model for how Manzil will run from now on. Ten dedicated repetitions on any word that needed correcting.

**New Sabaq — an-Nās 114:1–114:3.**

> قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ
> مَلِكِ ٱلنَّاسِ
> إِلَٰهِ ٱلنَّاسِ

Three ayat, but three of the shortest in the Qur'an — together they are less text than 1:7 was last session. Twenty to forty repetitions from the page.

Two things to hold her to:

- **ٱلنَّاسِ** repeats three times, and each one is a doubled ن — a two-count nasal hum, from lesson 2-05, with the closure at tongue point 6 and the sound in the khayshūm, from lesson 3-01. Three chances to get the same sound right.
- **None of these three ayat ends on a qalqalah letter.** Stopping at each ayah gives a clean, silent س. Say so — it is today's negative control, arriving in her own memorisation, for free.

## Homework

**25–35 min/day, five days.**

1. **Two minutes — Drill 1.** Predict the waqf form out loud *before* reading. Every word, every time.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 2**, Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ, stopping at every ayah, then straight through.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 3.** The same ayah three ways. This is the drill that teaches the *idea*.
4. **Two minutes — Drills 4 and 5.** Light bounces, then heavy ones.
5. **Two minutes — Drill 6.** The shadda hold, with the two beats tapped out.
6. **One minute — Drill 7.** The five endings that must not bounce.
7. **Two minutes — Drill 8.** Sūrat al-Falaq cold, five stops, five bounces.
8. **Hifz — Manzil (al-Fātiḥa complete), then new Sabaq (114:1–114:3).** Revision first, always.

**Bring to the next session:** a recording of Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ with a stop at every ayah. We will check whether "stronger" came out as "longer".
