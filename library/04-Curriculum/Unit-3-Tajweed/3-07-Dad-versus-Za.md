# Lesson 3-07 — ض against ظ

> Ibn al-Jazarī devoted an entire chapter of [[Muqaddimah-Jazariyyah]] to keeping these two apart — **بَابُ الضَّادِ وَالظَّاء**. If a scholar of the ninth century AH thought native Arab reciters needed a chapter on this pair, expect to teach it explicitly rather than hoping it settles by itself.

## Objectives

- Student can produce **ض** from the **side-edge** of the tongue against the upper molars, held and dragged forward, and can prove it with the cheek test.
- Student can produce **ظ** from the **tip** of the tongue on the lower edges of the upper incisors, visibly protruding, held and heavy.
- Student can say what separates the two — nothing but the makhraj and istiṭālah — and can hear the difference in a blind pair test.
- Student can read ٱلضَّآلِّينَ with a correct ض and say what the word becomes if the ض slips to a ظ.

## Hook

Write **وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ** on the board, and beside it write **وَلَا ٱلظَّالِّينَ**.

Then say: **only one of these is in the Qur'an.** The second one is not a variant, not a dialect, not a minor imprecision. It is a different word from a different root, and she has probably been saying it, or something between the two, since she started praying.

She met this in lesson 1-14 — that lesson's own objective asks her to *"name what goes wrong when it is read as ٱلظَّالِّينَ."* Take her answer, then say the thing 1-14 could not yet say:

> **These two letters are identical in every ṣifah you have learned. Both are voiced. Both flow. Both are raised. Both are clamped. There is nothing on the ṣifāt table that separates them.**
>
> **What separates them is the makhraj — and one ṣifah you have not met.**

And then the third possibility, which for a Persian, Urdu, Turkish or Uzbek speaker is the likeliest of all: neither ض nor ظ, but a plain **z**. Ask her to say ض, ظ, ذ and ز in a row. If any two of the four match, both are wrong, and this lesson is about building a contrast her first language does not contain.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 1-14 already drilled this exact pair.** It taught ص ض ط ظ as heavy letters, asked her to distinguish ض from د and ض from ظ in real minimal pairs, gave her the ط-bounces / ظ-is-held contrast, and put ٱلضَّآلِّينَ at the centre. **She has the contrast. What she does not have is the reason, and without the reason the contrast erodes.** Today supplies the reason and re-tightens the sound.
- **Lesson 3-01** placed both letters on the tongue map, and the placement *is* the answer: **ض is point 4**, the only makhraj in the language that uses the tongue's side-edge; **ظ is point 10**, the frontmost tongue point, sharing it with ذ and ث. They are at opposite ends of the same organ.
- **Lesson 3-02** gave her rakhāwah — the sound flows and can be held. Both of these letters have it, and the holding test is today's main diagnostic.
- **Lesson 3-03** gave her isti'lā' and iṭbāq, and both letters carry both. That is precisely why the ṣifāt cannot separate them.
- **Lesson 3-06** gave her the alif rule, which fires in ٱلضَّآلِّينَ: the alif after the ض is heavy, inherited.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Show the two makhārij, physically, before saying anything about sound.**

   | | ض | ظ |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Part of the tongue** | one or both **side-edges** | the **tip** |
   | **Against** | the inside faces of the upper **molars** | the lower **edges** of the two upper incisors |
   | **Visible?** | no — it is inside the cheek | **yes** — the tip protrudes and you can see it |
   | **Point on the map** | 4 | 10 |

   Say it as one sentence and make her repeat it: **ظ is the tip on the front teeth where I can see it; ض is the side against the back teeth where I cannot.**

2. **Build ظ first, because it is the easier of the two and it gives her a reference weight.**
   ظ shares its exact makhraj with **ذ** and **ث**. All three are rakhāwah; the air flows past the tongue tip. They differ only in voice and weight:

   | | voice | weight |
   |---|---|---|
   | **ظ** | voiced | **heavy** — isti'lā' + iṭbāq |
   | **ذ** | voiced | light |
   | **ث** | breathed | light |

   So **ظ is ذ made heavy**, exactly as ص is س made heavy. That is the fastest route in for a student who already has ذ.

   Cue for her, said as you demonstrate: *"Tip of your tongue out onto the edges of your top teeth — I should be able to see it. Now lift the back of your tongue and clamp it to the roof of your mouth, and let the sound flow out over the tip."*

   Then hold it: **aẓẓẓẓ**, three seconds, voiced the whole way.

3. **Build ض, and give it the time it needs — this is the hardest letter in the language.**
   Arabic calls itself **لُغَةُ الضَّاد**, "the language of the ḍād", because no other language has this sound. Say that to her; it reframes the difficulty as expected rather than as her failing.

   Four demands must be met **at the same time**:
   1. **Makhraj** — the side-edge of the tongue against the opposing upper molars.
   2. **Iṭbāq** — the tongue clamped flat against the palate.
   3. **Rakhāwah** — the sound flows; it must be holdable, not stopped.
   4. **Istiṭālah** — the sound **travels forward** along the tongue's edge while it flows.

   Drop any one and a different letter appears:

   | Drop | You get |
   |---|---|
   | rakhāwah + istiṭālah | a heavy **د** — the commonest substitution worldwide, and the default error for Turkic speakers |
   | iṭbāq | a plain **z** |
   | istiṭālah only, keeping the clamp | a **ظ** |

   Cue: *"Push the side of your tongue out sideways into your back teeth — like you're pressing it into the gap. Hold it there, make the sound heavy, and slide it forward along your teeth before you let go."*

   **Three valid variants, and do not correct any of them.** Left edge alone is easiest and is what most classical authorities describe as commonest. Right edge alone is correct and harder. Both edges at once is correct and hardest. If she has found the left, leave it.

   **ض is not made with the tip of the tongue.** If the tip is doing the work, the letter is wrong however heavy it sounds.

4. **Name the ṣifah that does the separating.**
   **Istiṭālah** (الاسْتِطالة, "stretching out, extending in length") is the extension of the sound **along the edge of the tongue**, running from the rear molars forward. The letter is not produced at a point; it is produced along a **line**, and the sound travels that line while it is being made.

   Two things to say immediately, because both are misread:
   - It is a **spatial** elongation, not a durational one. **It is not madd and it adds no counts.**
   - It has **no opposite.** It belongs to the ṣifāt without partners, alongside ghunnah, ṣafīr, inḥirāf and the rest — see [[Sifat]].

   **Istiṭālah belongs to exactly one letter: ض.** It is the sole thing separating ض from ظ, and therefore the sole reason the two are different letters.

5. **The two tests, and run both live.**

   **The duration test.** Ask for a sustained sākin ض, three seconds. A correct ض continues; a substituted heavy د stops instantly. Do the same for ظ — it should hiss on and on. **Any of these letters that dies in under two seconds has become a stop.**

   **The cheek test.** Have her put a fingertip against her cheek, over the upper molars. During a correct ض she should **feel the tongue pressing outward against the teeth from the inside.** During a ظ she should feel nothing there at all, because the tongue is at the front of her mouth.

   This second test is the one that settles arguments, because it does not rely on her ear — which is the ear that produced the error in the first place.

6. **The contrast with ط, which she already owns, used as scaffolding.**
   Lesson 1-14 taught her: **ط bounces on a sukūn; ظ is held.** Restate it now with the reason attached:

   - **ط** has **shiddah** — the air is completely stopped, so a sākin ط must pop out. That pop is qalqalah, from lesson 3-04. قَطْ ends with a kick.
   - **ظ** has **rakhāwah** — the air never stops, so a sākin ظ is simply **sustained and faded**. حَفِظْ ends with a long heavy hiss.

   Say them back to back for her: **aṭ — aẓẓẓẓ.** One pops, the other lasts. **ض behaves like ظ here, not like ط**: neither ض nor ظ can carry qalqalah, because both are rakhāwah, and there is nothing blocked to bounce out of.

7. **Name the stakes, once, plainly.**
   Confusing ض with ظ or د **changes the meaning** in the Qur'an. It is not a matter of polish. It falls in the category of *laḥn jalī* — the plain, obvious error — rather than the hidden one. ظَلَمَ with a devoiced or de-heavied ظ becomes a different root entirely.

   Two practical notes to hand her:
   - **The ظ words are a closed set.** Roughly a few dozen roots in the whole Qur'an use ظ. Some teachers give students the list. It is a legitimate crutch for *reading*, but it does not fix the *sound* — drill the makhraj as well.
   - **ض and ظ do not merge into each other.** A sākin ذ does merge into a following ظ (إِذ ظَّلَمُوٓا۟, 4:64), and that is the one place these front-teeth letters legitimately become one. It belongs to Unit 3.5; mention it only if she asks.

8. **Close on ٱلضَّآلِّينَ, slowly, three times.**
   It is the highest-stakes instance in the Qur'an: recited many times a day, carrying a **shadda** that exposes the letter for a full two-count hold, and sitting immediately beside a **ل**, which invites the tongue to slide forward too early — straight into ظ territory.

   Take it in pieces: **ٱض ‑ ضَـ ‑ آ ‑ لِّـ ‑ ينَ.** Hold the doubled ض on the side of the tongue for two counts. Keep the alif heavy (lesson 3-06). Only **then** bring the tongue tip up for the ل.

## Examples

| Text | Ref | What it demands |
|---|---|---|
| ٱلضَّآلِّينَ | 1:7 | doubled ض, held two counts on the tongue's edge, heavy alif after it |
| ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ | 1:7 | sākin ض after a heavy غ, then light م, و, ب |
| تَضْلِيلٍ | 105:2 | sākin ض, then a **light** ل — the drag must finish before the ل begins |
| تَلَظَّىٰ | 92:14 | doubled ظ, held, tongue tip visible — *reference example, outside your memorized surahs* |
| ظَهْرَكَ | 94:3 | ظ at the head of a word — *reference example, outside your memorized surahs* |
| ظَلَمُونَا | 2:57 | the root a wrong ظ destroys — *reference example, outside your memorized surahs* |
| طَعَامِ | 107:3 | the contrast: ط blocks and bounces; ظ and ض never do |

**Teacher note on the reference examples.** There is **no ظ anywhere in al-Fātiḥa or in surahs 105–114.** That is a real fact about the text and it is worth telling her: she can complete this entire unit's memorisation without once needing to produce a ظ. Which is exactly why the letter has to be drilled deliberately here — it will not come up on its own, and it will be waiting for her the first time she opens a longer surah.

**Teacher note on تَضْلِيلٍ.** The sequence ض → ل is the trap in miniature. The ض ends by dragging the sound *forward* along the tongue's edge; the ل needs the tongue tip up at the front. If the tip arrives early, the ض turns into a ظ on its way out. Slow it down: hold the ض, finish the drag, **then** move.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — build ظ from ذ.** Say each pair; the only change is the back of the tongue rising:

ذَا / ظَا · ذِي / ظِي · ذُو / ظُو · أَذْ / أَظْ

If the two members sound the same, iṭbāq is missing from the second.

**Drill 2 — the four that must be four.** Voiced, tip-visible, tip-visible-and-heavy, and front-of-mouth. If any two match, both are wrong:

**ذْ · زْ · ظْ · ضْ**

Hand on the throat throughout — all four are voiced. Tongue tip visible on ذ and ظ. Tongue tip **nowhere near the front** on ض.

**Drill 3 — the duration test.** Three seconds each, on a clock:

ضْ · ظْ · صْ · ذْ

Any one that dies before two seconds has become a stop. Ten extra repetitions on that letter alone.

**Drill 4 — the cheek test.** Fingertip on the cheek over the upper molars:

ضْ — you should feel the tongue pressing outward from inside.
ظْ — you should feel nothing there; the tongue is at the front.

Do it five times, alternating. This is the drill that does not depend on your ear.

**Drill 5 — the hold-versus-pop.** The contrast from lesson 1-14, with the reason attached:

أَطْ — pops · أَظْ — lasts · أَضْ — lasts · أَدْ — pops

Say them in that order. Two bounces and two holds, alternating.

**Drill 6 — ض against its three substitutes.** Four sounds, one of which is correct:

| Correct | Substitute | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|---|
| ضَا | دَا (heavy) | tip took over; rakhāwah and istiṭālah both gone |
| ضَا | ظَا | tip slipped forward to the incisors |
| ضَا | زَا | iṭbāq lost entirely |

Say each pair four times. Then say ضَا alone and check all four demands: side, clamp, flow, drag.

**Drill 7 — the words.** Slowly first, then at pace:

ٱلضَّآلِّينَ (1:7) · ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ (1:7) · تَضْلِيلٍ (105:2) · تَلَظَّىٰ (92:14) · ظَهْرَكَ (94:3) · ظَلَمُونَا (2:57)

**Drill 8 — the word that matters, in pieces.**

> **ٱض ‑ ضَـ ‑ آ ‑ لِّـ ‑ ينَ**

Two counts on the side of the tongue. Heavy alif. **Then** the ل. Ten repetitions, and ten more after any error, as its own step.

**Drill 9 — blind pairs.** Have someone say one of these at random with the text covered, and identify which you heard:

ضَا / ظَا · ضِي / ظِي · ضُو / ظُو

If you cannot tell them apart when someone else says them, you cannot yet reliably make them.

## Listen-for

**1. ض arriving as a heavy د.**
- *Listen for:* a ض that stops instead of flowing. The duration test settles it: ask for three seconds and it will die at once.
- *Why it happens:* the tongue tip takes over, because every other tongue letter she knows uses the tip, back or middle. The side-edge is a movement her mouth has never been asked to make. This is the commonest error worldwide and the default for Turkic speakers.
- *Correction cue:* *"Not the tip — the side. Push the side of your tongue into your back molars, keep the sound running, and drag it forward. Hold it. Hold it. Now release."*

**2. ض arriving as ظ.**
- *Listen for:* a heavy, voiced, flowing letter made at the **front** teeth. It sounds plausible, which is exactly what makes it dangerous — every ṣifah is right and only the address is wrong.
- *Why it happens:* the tongue tip slides forward to the incisors, which is the makhraj of ظ. In تَضْلِيلٍ and ٱلضَّآلِّينَ the following ل pulls it there.
- *Correction cue:* Cheek test, immediately. "Can you feel your tongue against your back teeth? No? Then that was a ظ."

**3. ظ arriving as ذ.**
- *Listen for:* the tip is in the right place but the back of the tongue never rises. The letter is light.
- *Why it happens:* it is the mirror of ص → س: the makhraj is easy, the heaviness is the work, and the heaviness is what gets dropped under reading load.
- *Correction cue:* "Tip stays. Back rises. ذَا — ظَا." Drill 1.

**4. Any two of ذ, ز, ظ, ض collapsing into one plain "z".**
- *Listen for:* run Drill 2 and listen for matches. If two match, both are wrong.
- *Why it happens:* in Persian and Urdu all four of these letters are genuinely pronounced identically as [z], and Turkish and Uzbek speakers arrive with much the same merger. **She is not being careless — her language has no distinction here at all.** The contrast has to be built from nothing, and saying so out loud matters, because a student who thinks she is being sloppy stops trying.
- *Correction cue:* Build them one at a time from the makhraj, never by imitation. "Where is your tongue tip? Show me."

**5. A short, clipped ض with no travel.**
- *Listen for:* the letter is over instantly even though the tip is not involved. Istiṭālah has been skipped.
- *Why it happens:* she has found the side-edge and thinks the job is done. Nothing told her the sound has to *move*.
- *Correction cue:* "It's a stroke, not a touch. Slide it forward along your teeth before you let go."

**6. Losing the heaviness of ض under a kasra.**
- *Listen for:* a ضِ that flattens. Same trap as ص in lesson 3-03.
- *Why it happens:* the kasra pulls the front of the tongue up and forward and the clamp releases.
- *Correction cue:* "Heavy under every vowel. Say ضَ, then ضِ, and do not let the back of your tongue drop between them."

**7. Bouncing a sākin ظ or ض.**
- *Listen for:* a kick at the end of أَظْ or أَضْ, by analogy with ط.
- *Why it happens:* ط is the nearest heavy letter she knows and it does bounce. Generalisation.
- *Correction cue:* "Can you hold it? Then it is not blocked, so there is nothing to bounce." Drill 5.

**8. The tongue tip not protruding on ظ.**
- *Listen for:* a ظ made *behind* the teeth. It comes out as a heavy ز.
- *Why it happens:* putting the tongue between the teeth feels exaggerated to an adult, so she keeps it tucked.
- *Correction cue:* "I should be able to see it. Show me the tip."

## Hifz for this session

**Manzil / Sabqi first.** Al-Fātiḥa (Manzil), then 114:1–114:5 (Sabqi). Ten dedicated repetitions on any corrected word.

**New Sabaq — an-Nās 114:6.**

> مِنَ ٱلْجِنَّةِ وَٱلنَّاسِ

One short ayah, and with it **Sūrat an-Nās is complete.** Have her recite all six ayat unaided at the end of the session and mark the date, as you did with al-Fātiḥa.

**Promotion check.** Al-Fātiḥa has now had several clean sessions. The course's rule is **three consecutive clean recitations promotes a surah from Sabqi to Manzil** — and be honest with her that this number is the course's own default, not something the classical sources specify; they say "when it is solid" and stop there. If al-Fātiḥa has run clean three sessions in a row, promote it and start the two-surahs-per-day Manzil rotation. If it keeps decaying after promotion, raise the threshold to four or five and say that you are doing so.

Two things to hold in this ayah:
- **ٱلْجِنَّةِ** has a doubled ن — two counts of hum — and a **ج** that is shiddah: a real closure with silence inside it, not a fricative. Lesson 3-02's diagnostic.
- **وَٱلنَّاسِ** for the sixth and last time in the surah. If the doubled ن and the light alif are right here, they are right everywhere in an-Nās.

## Homework

**25–35 min/day, five days.**

1. **One minute — Drill 2.** ذْ · زْ · ظْ · ضْ. If any two match, both get ten extra repetitions.
2. **One minute — Drill 4, the cheek test.** Five alternations. This is the one that does not lie to you.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 3, the duration test**, on a clock.
4. **Two minutes — Drill 1**, building ظ from ذ, and **Drill 5**, hold versus pop.
5. **Three minutes — Drill 6**, ض against its three substitutes.
6. **Three minutes — Drill 8.** ٱض ‑ ضَـ ‑ آ ‑ لِّـ ‑ ينَ, ten repetitions, slowly.
7. **Two minutes — Drill 9, blind pairs**, with anyone who will help. If nobody will, record them in a random order and test yourself two days later.
8. **Hifz — Manzil (al-Fātiḥa), Sabqi (114:1–114:5), then new Sabaq (114:6).** Then recite an-Nās complete, once, unaided.

**Bring to the next session:** an-Nās recited whole, and a recording of ٱلضَّآلِّينَ said three times. Next session is revision — bring any question from Unit 3.1 that has not been answered.
