# Lesson 3-07 — Tafkhīm and tarqīq, and the alif that has no ruling of its own

> ## ⚠ The one thing that must land today
>
> **الْأَلِفُ تَتْبَعُ مَا قَبْلَهَا — the alif follows what precedes it.**
>
> The alif is **not** a heavy letter. It is **not** a light letter. It has **no independent ruling at all** and simply extends the colour of whatever came before it. In قَالَ it is heavy; in كَانَ the identical written alif is light.
>
> This is the single highest-frequency error in this family. A student who memorises "alif is a light letter" reads قَالَ wrong every time; a student who over-corrects reads كَانَ wrong every time. If only one thing survives this session, make it this.

## Objectives

- Student can state the definitions of **tafkhīm** and **tarqīq** and say which ṣifah each one is the audible result of.
- Student can run one heavy letter through the **five levels** and hear the difference between the top and the bottom of the scale.
- Student can state and apply the alif rule, and can produce **قَالَ / كَانَ** as an audibly different pair.
- Student can keep the twenty-two light letters light next to a heavy neighbour, in both directions, without over-thinning them.

## Hook

Say two words to her and ask what the difference is:

> **قَالَ** — he said
> **كَانَ** — he was

She will say the first letter is different. It is — one is heavy, one is light, and she has had that since lesson 1-14. Now ask her the question that matters:

**"What about the alif in the middle? Is that the same sound in both words?"**

Almost every student says yes. It is written identically, it is the same length, and nobody has ever suggested otherwise. **It is not the same sound.** The alif of قَالَ is dark and back; the alif of كَانَ is bright and forward. Say them back to back, twice, exaggerated, until she hears it.

Then the rule, in Arabic, on the board, boxed:

> **الْأَلِفُ تَتْبَعُ مَا قَبْلَهَا**

And the second error, which is the mirror of the first and arrives about ten minutes after you fix it: **contamination.** In **طَعَامِ** (107:3) the ط is heavy and the ع, the alif and the م are light. Most students darken all four. The heavy letter is correct; what fails is the switch back.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 1-14 taught her heaviness by ear, and taught it the right way round.** Its own words: *you cannot really hear the dome on the consonant itself — you hear it on the VOWEL beside it.* It gave her the "a fatḥa after a heavy letter backs toward the *o* in *hot*" cue, and gave her the self-test سَا / صَا. **Today does not teach heaviness. It gives the ṣifah its performance rule, adds the five degrees, and closes the alif gap that 1-14 left open.** Say that.
- **Lesson 3-03, three sessions ago,** gave her خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ, the count of seven and twenty-two, and the two degrees (clamped ص ض ط ظ against unclamped خ غ ق). **Today is 3-03 turned from a property into an instruction.** If the seven letters are not solid, stop and re-drill them before going on — everything today assumes them.
- **Lesson 3-05 and 3-06** established that ق and ط carry two properties at once — they bounce *and* they are heavy. That is the model for today: independent properties stacking on one letter.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Name the two, and connect each to its ṣifah.**

   **Tafkhīm** (التَّفْخِيم, "thickening") — the back of the tongue rises toward the soft palate, the mouth fills, and the letter comes out fat and rounded. The articulatory quality behind it is **isti'lā'**, which she has had since 3-03. **Isti'lā' is the permanent attribute of the letter; tafkhīm is the audible result.** Same thing, seen from the tongue and from the ear.

   **Tarqīq** (التَّرْقِيق, "thinning") — the sound comes out slim and forward, the back of the tongue stays **low** (istifāl), and the mouth does not round.

   Then the framing sentence that makes tarqīq teachable at all:

   > **Tarqīq is not a rule you apply. It is what happens when you don't do anything. The question is never "how do I make it light" — it is "how do I stop leaking heaviness into it."**

2. **State the trigger set, and it is not new.**
   **Seven letters are always heavy** — خ ص ض غ ط ق ظ, **خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ**. Never light, in any position, with any vowel. Everything else is light by default.

   Then the four standing exceptions, listed once and then deferred, so she knows the shape of what is coming:

   | Letter | Where it turns heavy | Lesson |
   |---|---|---|
   | **ر** | depending on its vowel and the vowel before it | 3-10, 3-11 |
   | **ل** of لفظ الجلالة | after a fatḥa or a ḍamma | 3-12 |
   | **ا** alif | whenever the letter before it is heavy | **today** |
   | the ghunnah of a concealed ن / م | whenever the letter *after* it is heavy | Unit 3.4 |

   Say the count out loud: **two letters in the whole language switch — ر and the ل of the Divine Name.** The alif does not switch; it has no position of its own to switch from. And that is the entirety of Unit 3.2, which starts in three sessions.

3. **Teach the performance in three steps.**
   1. Say the letter light first — a plain English "s" — then **raise the back of the tongue** toward the roof of the mouth **without moving the tip.** The vowel colour darkens.
   2. The mouth **rounds and fills.** Tafkhīm is felt as *volume*, not as loudness. Getting louder is not getting heavier — prove it by saying a quiet heavy ق and a loud light ك.
   3. **Do not move the makhraj.** The tongue tip stays exactly where the letter is articulated; only the rear rises. Sliding the tip backwards produces a different letter, not a heavier one.

4. **The five levels — marātib at-tafkhīm. Teach them as one letter run through five vowels.**

   Every heavy letter is heavy, but not equally. From heaviest down:

   | Level | Condition | Example | Ref |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **1** | fatḥa **followed by an alif** | غَاسِقٍ | 113:3 |
   | **2** | fatḥa alone | خَلَقَ | 113:2 |
   | **3** | ḍamma | صُدُورِ | 114:5 |
   | **4** | sukūn | أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ | 108:1 |
   | **5** | kasra | ٱلصِّرَٰطَ | 1:6 |

   Then the drill that makes the scale real — **one letter, all five**:

   > **قَالَ · قَدْ · قُلْ · يَقْطَعُ · قِيلَ**

   Run it down and back up. The point is not the ordering; the point is that **قِيلَ and قَالَ must not sound equally thick.** Flattening the five levels into one uniform "heavy" is the polish error at this stage.

   **Name the disagreement once.** Ibn al-Jazarī and most later works give **five** levels in this order. Some works give four, merging sukūn into a neighbouring level; a number of Egyptian teachers **swap levels 3 and 4**, putting sukūn above ḍamma. The audible difference is slight. **This course teaches five, in Ibn al-Jazarī's order**, and names the alternative.

5. **The alif. Give this its own block of the session — do not fold it into the levels.**

   > **الْأَلِفُ تَتْبَعُ مَا قَبْلَهَا** — *the alif follows what precedes it.*

   The alif is never listed among the heavy letters and never among the light ones. It carries no independent heaviness whatsoever; it prolongs whatever colour the preceding letter gave it.

   The diagnostic pair, and she should leave the room able to do it:

   > **قَالَ** (10:81) — heavy alif · **كَانَ** (110:3) — light alif

   Same written letter. Same length. Different colour. Say them back to back until the difference is unmistakable to her, then have her say them while you look away and tell her which one you heard.

   Then in the words she is memorising:
   - **غَاسِقٍ** (113:3) — the alif is heavy purely because غ is.
   - **ٱلضَّآلِّينَ** (1:7) — the alif inherits from ض.
   - **ٱلنَّاسِ** (114:1) — the alif is light, because ن is.

   Tell her where the mistake comes from: many books state the light letters as **21** and list the alif among them, which produces exactly this error. Where the sources disagree on the count, **they agree on the practice** — the alif takes the colour of what precedes it.

6. **The letters that need vigilance, and the drill that fixes them.**
   Nobody heavies a ك or a ت by accident. These are the ones a heavy neighbour bleeds into:

   > **ا · ب · ل · م · ن · ه · ء**

   Test words: **طَعَامِ** (107:3) — ط heavy, ع ا م light. **ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ** (1:7) — heavy غ and ض with light م, و and ب threaded between them. **ٱلصِّرَٰطَ** (1:6) — a light ر between two heavy letters, which is the hardest shape in the set.

   The mechanism to name: **inertia.** The tongue is still up from the previous heavy letter when the light letter arrives. Drill heavy → light transitions slowly, with a deliberate gap (طَـ ‑ عَـ ‑ ا ‑ مِ), until the drop is conscious.

   And the standing rule she needs for the whole rest of the course: **every ل outside the Divine Name is light, always.** صَلَاة and طَلَبَ have heavy neighbours and light lāms. So does ٱلصِّرَٰطَ's ر — but that one has its own rules, in three lessons.

7. **The mirror error — and end the session on it, because it is what you will hear next week.**
   **Over-lightening.** A student freshly drilled on tarqīq thins everything into a bright, nasal, English-sounding register, and eventually thins ص back toward س. **Light is not thin.** The light letters are not being *done to*; they are simply normal.

   Also flag the **dark English "l"**: English speakers default to the velarised /ɫ/ of *feel* and install it in every lām. Arabic wants the clear /l/ of *leaf*, everywhere except one word.

8. **One honest note about the app, worth thirty seconds.**
   Tell her that tafkhīm and tarqīq are **not colour-coded** in the mushaf highlighting she will see in the app. The annotation dataset the course draws on covers eighteen rules and heaviness is not one of them. **This family has to be heard and reasoned, not looked up.** That is why it gets drills and a mnemonic rather than a colour.

## Examples

| Text | Ref | Why |
|---|---|---|
| غَاسِقٍ | 113:3 | level 1 — غ with fatḥa + alif; the alif is heavy because غ is |
| خَلَقَ | 113:2 | level 2 — خ with a bare fatḥa |
| صُدُورِ | 114:5 | level 3 — ص with a ḍamma |
| أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ | 108:1 | level 4 — sākin ط, heavy through the closure and the bounce |
| ٱلصِّرَٰطَ | 1:6 | level 5 — ص with a kasra; heavy at its lightest |
| ٱلضَّآلِّينَ | 1:7 | the alif inherits heaviness from ض |
| كَانَ | 110:3 | light ك → light alif |
| قَالَ | 10:81 | the same alif shape, heavy, because ق is |
| طَعَامِ | 107:3 | ط heavy, then an immediate drop: ع, ا, م all light |

**Teacher note on غَاسِقٍ.** This is the best level-1 example she has, because it is inside a surah she memorises in three sessions. غَا is a heavy letter with a fatḥa and an alif — the fullest heaviness in the language — and the alif is doing all the audible work. Contrast it directly with the alif of ٱلنَّاسِ.

**Teacher note on ٱلصِّرَٰطَ, again.** It has now appeared in three consecutive lessons and it will appear in three more. Today it is level 5 — heavy under a kasra, the lightest heavy there is — and it contains a **light ر between two heavy letters**, which is exactly the environment where contamination is hardest to resist. Do not fix the ر today; just notice it. Lesson 3-10 owns it.

**Teacher note on قَالَ.** This is the only example here from outside al-Fātiḥa and the last ten surahs. It is worth the exception because no clean level-1 minimal partner for كَانَ exists inside her range. Label it for her: **reference example — outside your memorized surahs.**

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the five levels, one letter.** Down and back up, every day:

> **قَالَ · قَدْ · قُلْ · يَقْطَعُ · قِيلَ**

Then the same with ص: **صَالِح · صَدْ · صُدُورِ · وَٱلْعَصْرِ · ٱلصِّرَٰطَ.** If levels 1 and 5 sound the same, the scale has flattened.

**Drill 2 — the alif pair.** The most important sixty seconds of the week:

> **قَالَ / كَانَ · كَانَ / قَالَ**

Ten times, alternating. Then have someone say one at random with the board covered, and identify it by the **alif** alone, not by the first letter.

**Drill 3 — alifs in context.** Say which way each alif leans, then read the word:

| Word | Ref | Alif |
|---|---|---|
| غَاسِقٍ | 113:3 | heavy |
| ٱلضَّآلِّينَ | 1:7 | heavy |
| ٱلنَّاسِ | 114:1 | light |
| كَانَ | 110:3 | light |
| طَعَامِ | 107:3 | **light** — the ط is two letters back, and only the *immediately* preceding letter counts |
| مَالُهُۥ | 111:2 | light |

**Drill 4 — the drop.** Heavy, then immediately light, with a deliberate gap that you then close:

طَـ ‑ عَـ ‑ ا ‑ مِ → طَعَامِ · ٱلْـ ‑ مَـ ‑ غْـ ‑ ضُـ ‑ و ‑ بِ → ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ · أَعْـ ‑ طَيْـ ‑ نَٰـ ‑ كَ → أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ

**Drill 5 — the reset.** After every round of Drill 4, one word with nothing heavy in it:

ٱلْحَمْدُ (1:2) · بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ (114:1) · ٱلْبَيْتِ (106:3) · أَنْعَمْتَ (1:7)

**Always end a heaviness drill on a light word.** The skill is the switch.

**Drill 6 — quiet and heavy, loud and light.** Four seconds, to kill the volume confusion:

Say **قْ** as quietly as you can and keep it heavy. Then say **كْ** as loudly as you can and keep it light.

**Drill 7 — every ل is light.** Ten words with a lām next to something heavy. Every lām stays clear and forward:

ٱلْفَلَقِ · ٱلصِّرَٰطَ · مَطْلَعِ · ٱلْقَارِعَةُ · تَضْلِيلٍ · ٱلضَّآلِّينَ · ٱلْقَدْرِ · ٱلْفِيلِ

**Drill 8 — level-tagging, cold.** Read each word and call the level number before you read it:

صُدُورِ (?) · خَلَقَ (?) · ٱلصِّرَٰطَ (?) · أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ (?) · غَاسِقٍ (?) · وَٱلْعَصْرِ (?)

## Listen-for

**1. A uniformly light alif — "alif is a light letter".**
- *Listen for:* قَالَ, طَابَ, ٱلضَّآلِّينَ read with a thin, forward alif. This is the flagship error of the whole family.
- *Why it happens:* many books list the alif among the twenty-one light letters, and it is the intuitive reading of "everything except the seven is light".
- *Correction cue:* "الْأَلِفُ تَتْبَعُ مَا قَبْلَهَا. What came before it?" Then قَالَ / كَانَ back to back.

**2. A uniformly heavy alif — the over-correction.**
- *Listen for:* كَانَ, مَالُهُۥ, ٱلنَّاسِ read with a dark alif. Expect this within one session of fixing error 1.
- *Why it happens:* she has learned that the alif can be heavy and has applied it as a property rather than as an inheritance.
- *Correction cue:* "It follows. It does not decide." Same pair, reversed order.

**3. Contamination from a heavy neighbour.**
- *Listen for:* the ع, ا and م of طَعَامِ arriving dark; the ل of a word with a heavy letter in it going velar.
- *Why it happens:* inertia. The tongue has not come down yet. She is not mispronouncing the heavy letter; she is failing to switch back.
- *Correction cue:* "Heavy fills the letter and stops at its edge." Drill 4 slowly, with the gap.

**4. Confusing heavy with loud.**
- *Listen for:* volume tracking heaviness across a whole line.
- *Why it happens:* "heavy" is a metaphor and loudness is the nearest available reading of it.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 6. Quiet and heavy, loud and light. It settles the question in four seconds and does not need explaining.

**5. Dragging the makhraj back to "deepen" a letter.**
- *Listen for:* a ص whose tip has slid off the teeth, giving something between ص and a retroflex. This is a real mispronunciation, not a stylistic one.
- *Why it happens:* she is trying to add depth with the only movement available to her.
- *Correction cue:* "The tip does not move. Only the back rises." Hold a finger where the tip should stay.

**6. Flattening the five levels.**
- *Listen for:* قِيلَ and قَالَ equally thick.
- *Why it happens:* she has learned "always heavy" and applied it as a switch. It is a scale.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 1, down and back up, and ask her which end she is at.

**7. Over-thinning after a tarqīq drill.**
- *Listen for:* the whole recitation getting bright and nasal, and ص drifting toward س.
- *Why it happens:* over-correction, always following a successful contamination fix. Name it as a drill artefact when it appears.
- *Correction cue:* "Light is not thin. You are not doing anything to the light letters — that is the point."

**8. The dark English "l" everywhere.**
- *Listen for:* the velarised /ɫ/ of *feel* in ٱلْحَمْدُ, ٱلْفَلَقِ, ٱلَّذِينَ.
- *Why it happens:* English installs it below the level of awareness, and it sounds plausible because there genuinely is one heavy lām in the language.
- *Correction cue:* "*leaf*, not *feel*." Then Drill 7.

## Hifz for this session

**Manzil / Sabqi first.** Al-Fātiḥa complete (Manzil track), then 114:1–114:3 (Sabqi). Ten dedicated repetitions on any corrected word.

**New Sabaq — an-Nās 114:4–114:5.**

> مِن شَرِّ ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ ٱلْخَنَّاسِ
> ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ فِى صُدُورِ ٱلنَّاسِ

Twenty to forty repetitions from the page. Today's rule fires in both ayat, so use them:

- **صُدُورِ** (114:5) is today's **level 3** example — a heavy ص with a ḍamma — and the ر straight after it is heavy too, for reasons she will get in lesson 3-10. Get the ص right now; leave the ر alone.
- **ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ** and **ٱلْخَنَّاسِ** are almost entirely light. The alif in each is light, because س and ن are. Use them as the reset against صُدُورِ.
- **ٱلْخَنَّاسِ** has a doubled ن — two counts of hum, and a **light** alif immediately after it. This is the cleanest light-alif in her memorisation set.

## Homework

**25–35 min/day, five days.**

1. **One minute — Drill 2, the alif pair.** قَالَ / كَانَ, ten alternations. Every day, first. Nothing else in this lesson matters as much.
2. **Two minutes — Drill 1, the five levels**, down and back up, on ق and then on ص.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 3.** Call the alif's lean before reading the word.
4. **Three minutes — Drill 4, the drop**, then **Drill 5, the reset.** In that order, always ending light.
5. **Ten seconds — Drill 6.** Quiet and heavy, loud and light.
6. **Two minutes — Drill 7.** Eight words, every lām clear and forward.
7. **Two minutes — Drill 8.** Call the level number before reading.
8. **Hifz — Manzil (al-Fātiḥa), Sabqi (114:1–114:3), then new Sabaq (114:4–114:5).**

**Bring to the next session:** a recording of yourself saying قَالَ and كَانَ five times each, in a random order you write down. We will see whether I can tell them apart from the alif alone.
