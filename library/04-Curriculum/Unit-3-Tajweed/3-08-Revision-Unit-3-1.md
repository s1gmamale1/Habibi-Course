# Lesson 3-08 — Revision and Q&A: Unit 3.1

> **No new rule today, and no new memorisation.** Everything in this session has already been taught. The work is retrieval — pulling each rule out cold, in mixed order, without the lesson it came from as a cue — and answering the questions that have piled up and had nowhere to go.
>
> **The hifz strand does not pause.** Sabqi and Manzil run as normal; only the new Sabaq is suspended, so the memorisation muscle rests for one session while the retrieval work happens.

## Objectives

- Student can produce any rule from Unit 3.1 on demand, out of order, without being told which rule is being asked for.
- Student can **self-diagnose** — given her own error, name which lesson it belongs to and which drill fixes it.
- Student can state the dependency chain of the unit: makhraj → ṣifah → isti'lā' → tafkhīm → everything in Unit 3.2 and beyond.
- Student leaves with her accumulated questions answered and with a written list of exactly which two or three things need work before Unit 3.2.

## Hook

Do not review. **Test, cold, in the first three minutes, before any warm-up.**

Put six words in front of her, in this order, and say nothing except "read them."

> **ٱلصِّرَٰطَ · طَعَامِ · يَجْعَلْ · غَاسِقٍ · ٱلضَّآلِّينَ · ٱلْحَمْدُ**

Then tell her what you were listening for, one word at a time. Every rule in the unit is in that line:

| Word | What was being tested |
|---|---|
| ٱلصِّرَٰطَ | three adjacent makhārij; heavy ص under a kasra (3-01, 3-03) |
| طَعَامِ | the switch — heavy, then three light letters (3-06) |
| يَجْعَلْ | qalqalah ṣughrā, light, mid-word (3-04) |
| غَاسِقٍ | an inherited **heavy** alif (3-06) |
| ٱلضَّآلِّينَ | ض on the side of the tongue, held, heavy alif (3-07) |
| ٱلْحَمْدُ | the reset: nothing heavy, nothing bounced (3-06) |

Then the framing for the session:

> **You have not been asked to do any of this without being told which rule it was. That is the difference between recognising a rule and owning it. Today everything comes at you unlabelled.**

## What they already know

Everything. That is the point. But name the **spine** out loud, because the unit has a shape and she should be able to draw it:

> **Makhraj (3-01) tells you WHERE.**
> **Ṣifah (3-02, 3-03) tells you HOW.**
> **Two ṣifāt together give you qalqalah (3-04, 3-05).**
> **One ṣifah — isti'lā' — gives you the whole heaviness system (3-06).**
> **And one letter, ض, needs a ṣifah of its own to exist at all (3-07).**

Then say where it goes: **isti'lā's seven letters are the input to everything in Unit 3.2**, which starts next session — the rā', the lām of the Divine Name — and to the heavy ghunnah of ikhfā' in Unit 3.4. Nothing in this unit was preparation for a test. It was preparation for the rest of the course.

## Part A — the mixed drill

Run these in order. The order is deliberate: it interleaves, so no two consecutive items come from the same lesson. **Do not group by rule.** Grouping is what makes a student look competent in the session and fail a week later.

**Drill 1 — zone call, cold.** You say the letter; she says the zone, and for the throat and tongue, the point:

ك · ه · ض · ف · ق · ع · ظ · م · ر · خ · ن · ب · ل · غ · ص

*Watch for:* ك called "throat". ه placed at the middle instead of the deepest. Any answer given without the sukūn probe behind it.

**Drill 2 — two axes, cold.** She gives **both** answers, breath first, sound second:

ت · ز · ص · ل · ق · ش · د · ع · ط · ف · ن · ج

*Watch for:* answering the wrong axis, which is the diagnostic that lesson 3-02 did not fully land.

**Drill 3 — heavy or light, and if heavy, which degree.** Three possible answers: "light", "heavy, open", "heavy, clamped":

س · ط · خ · ب · ظ · ق · ل · ص · ك · غ · ض · ن

*Watch for:* خ, غ, ق called clamped. That is the single most common wrong answer in this drill.

**Drill 4 — the alif, blind.** Say the word; she says whether the alif is heavy or light, **before** reading it:

غَاسِقٍ · ٱلنَّاسِ · قَالَ · كَانَ · ٱلضَّآلِّينَ · طَعَامِ · مَالُهُۥ · ٱلْخَنَّاسِ

*Watch for:* طَعَامِ answered "heavy". The ط is two letters back; only the **immediately** preceding letter counts. This item is in the drill specifically to catch that.

**Drill 5 — bounce or no bounce, ṣughrā or kubrā.** Read each and decide. Some of these require her to ask "am I stopping?":

يَجْعَلْ · ٱلْفَلَقْ *(stopping)* · ٱلنَّاسْ *(stopping)* · يَلِدْ *(reading on)* · أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ · نَسْتَعِينْ *(stopping)* · وَتَبّْ *(stopping)* · ٱلْحَمْدُ

*Watch for:* bounces on ٱلنَّاسْ and نَسْتَعِينْ. This is the over-generalisation you warned her about in 3-05 and it should be arriving right about now.

**Drill 6 — the four that must be four.**

ذْ · زْ · ظْ · ضْ

Cheek test on the ض. Duration test on all four.

**Drill 7 — the five levels, cold.** Read the word and call the level number:

قَالَ · ٱلصِّرَٰطَ · صُدُورِ · أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ · خَلَقَ · وَٱلْعَصْرِ

**Drill 8 — the switch, at pace.** Read straight through, no stopping, and let nothing bleed:

> ٱهْدِنَا ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ (1:6)
> صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ (1:7)
> وَلَا يَحُضُّ عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ ٱلْمِسْكِينِ (107:3)
> أَلَمْ يَجْعَلْ كَيْدَهُمْ فِى تَضْلِيلٍ (105:2)

**Drill 9 — the cold page.** Read Sūrat al-Falaq and Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ end to end, stopping at every ayah. Say nothing beforehand. Then hand her the list of what fired and where, and let her mark her own misses.

## Part B — troubleshooting: the questions that actually come up

These are the questions an adult student accumulates over seven sessions and has no natural place to ask. Answer them whether or not she asks; if she does not ask them this week, she is carrying them silently.

---

### "Is heavy the same as loud? Because when I make it heavy I get louder."

No, and the confusion is structural rather than careless — "heavy" is a metaphor, and volume is the nearest thing in English it could map onto.

**The four-second proof:** say a **ق** as quietly as you possibly can, keeping it heavy. Then say a **ك** as loudly as you can, keeping it light. Both are possible. Therefore they are not the same property.

Tafkhīm is **resonance and volume of space** — the mouth fills. It is felt as fatness, not as force.

---

### "Why do ق and ط both bounce AND get heavy? Is that one rule or two?"

**Two rules, applying at once to the same letter, from two unrelated ṣifāt.**

- The bounce comes from **shiddah + jahr** (blocked and voiced, so the trapped sound escapes on release).
- The heaviness comes from **isti'lā'** (back of the tongue rises).

Nothing connects them. ب, ج and د bounce and stay light. خ, غ and ظ are heavy and never bounce. ق and ط happen to have both, which is why they are the loudest examples of each and why they get conflated.

This is worth dwelling on for a moment, because it is the general lesson of the unit: **ṣifāt compose.** They are not labels stuck on letters; they are independent properties that stack, and the stack predicts the behaviour.

---

### "How do I know whether a sukūn is 'original' or from stopping? They look the same on the page."

They do not look the same, and this is the useful answer: **read the word without stopping and see whether the sukūn survives.**

- **يَجْعَلْ** — the sukūn on the ج is printed, and it is there whether you stop or carry on. **Lāzim. Ṣughrā.**
- **أَحَدٌ** — there is no sukūn printed at all; there is a tanwīn. The sukūn only appears **because you stopped**. **'Āriḍ. Kubrā.**

The test question, and make her say it out loud before every qalqalah decision: **"Would this sukūn still be here if I kept reading?"**

---

### "In لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ both د's look identical. Why are they different rules?"

Because the rule is not about the word. **It is about what you decided to do.**

The د of **يَلِدْ** is followed by وَلَمْ, so you read on, so the sukūn is permanent and the bounce is minor.
The د of **يُولَدْ** is where you stop, so the sukūn is caused by the stop, and the bounce is major.

**Kubrā is a decision, not a property of the word.** If you read the whole surah in one breath and stopped only at أَحَدٌ, the د of يُولَدْ would be ṣughrā too.

This is her first rule that depends on her own choice. It is not the last: she will meet exactly the same structure in the rā' rules in lesson 3-10, where ٱلْقَدْرِ is light while you read on and heavy the moment you stop.

---

### "You keep saying ص ض ط ظ are heavier than خ غ ق. But they are all in خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ. So which is it?"

Both, and the mnemonic is not the problem — the mnemonic answers a different question.

- **خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ answers "is it heavy?"** Seven letters. Yes for all seven, in every position, with every vowel.
- **Iṭbāq answers "how heavy?"** Four of those seven — ص ض ط ظ — additionally clamp the tongue flat against the palate. That is a *stronger* form of the same elevation.

So: **iṭbāq is a strict subset of isti'lā'.** Every clamped letter is heavy; not every heavy letter is clamped. There is no fourth possibility — you cannot clamp without raising.

The audible consequence: **ص is heavier than خ, and ط heavier than ق.** Say صَا / خَا back to back. Both heavy. Not equally.

---

### "My ض and my ظ sound the same to me. Are they actually different, or is this a fine point?"

They are entirely different letters, and it is not a fine point — mixing them changes the word, which puts it in the *laḥn jalī* category, the plain error rather than the hidden one.

Here is why they sound the same to you, and it is a real fact rather than a failing: **every ṣifah on both letters is identical.** Both voiced, both flowing, both raised, both clamped. The ṣifāt table cannot tell them apart. Two things separate them:

1. **The makhraj**, and it is not a small difference — they are at opposite ends of the tongue. **ظ is the tip on the front teeth, visible. ض is the side-edge against the back molars, invisible.**
2. **Istiṭālah**, which only ض has: the sound **travels forward** along the tongue's edge while it is being made.

**Do not try to fix this by ear.** Your ear is the thing that produced the error. Use the **cheek test**: fingertip on the cheek over the upper molars. On a correct ض you feel the tongue pressing outward from inside. On a ظ you feel nothing there.

---

### "In Persian and Urdu, ذ ز ض ظ are all just 'z'. Is that wrong?"

In those languages, no — that is genuinely their phonology, and a speaker of them says *zarurat* and *zulm* and hears nothing missing, because in their own language nothing is.

In Qur'anic recitation, yes, it is wrong, and it is the highest-priority error to catch early.

The important part of the answer: **you are not being careless.** You are transferring a merger that your first language actually has. The distinction has to be **built from nothing**, one makhraj at a time, rather than corrected. That is why the fix is Drill 6 and the cheek test rather than "listen harder".

---

### "Why does the alif not have its own ruling? Every other letter does."

Because an alif is not really a consonant. It has **no makhraj of contact at all** — it comes from al-jawf, the empty space, shaped by nothing and stopped only by the breath running out. There is no tongue position to be high or low.

So it takes the position the previous letter left the tongue in, and holds it for two counts. **الْأَلِفُ تَتْبَعُ مَا قَبْلَهَا.**

And the warning: some books state the light letters as **21** and list the alif among them. That is where the error comes from. Where sources disagree on the count, they agree on the practice.

**Diagnostic pair, forever: قَالَ / كَانَ.**

---

### "Am I supposed to be able to hear all this in my own recitation?"

Honestly: **not yet, and not entirely, ever, without another ear present.**

An adult's perception is filtered through her first language. A student whose first language merged two sounds hears her own wrong version as correct — the error is invisible from the inside. That is why the drills in this unit are so heavily **physical**: the palm test, the throat test, the cheek test, the duration test. Each one gives you an answer that does not depend on the ear that made the mistake.

The three habits that partly substitute for an external ear, and she should be running all three:
1. **Record yourself**, then listen back two days later. Delay is what breaks the loop between intention and perception.
2. **Use the physical tests** rather than judging by sound.
3. **Compare against a reciter** on the same word, not on a whole surah — one word at a time, repeatedly.

And say the honest boundary, because it is in [[Methodology]] and she deserves it plainly: **this course teaches the rules and trains the ear. It cannot hear you.** Tajweed has always been received face to face. What this does is make time with a teacher shorter and sharper.

---

### "Which of my mistakes should I actually be worrying about?"

Give her a ranked list, written down, and keep it to three. As of the end of Unit 3.1 the ranking is almost always:

1. **The four-way z-collapse** (ذ ز ظ ض), if it is present at all. It changes words.
2. **A flat alif** — قَالَ and كَانَ sounding the same. Highest frequency in the whole family.
3. **Heaviness bleeding onto the light letters after a heavy one.**

Everything else — the exact size of a ṣughrā bounce, the ordering of levels 3 and 4 — is polish, and polish comes after the three above are solid.

---

### "What if a reciter I listen to does it differently?"

Sometimes they are wrong. Sometimes the difference is transmitted and both are correct. Three places in this unit where a difference is real and named:

- **Levels 3 and 4 of tafkhīm.** Ibn al-Jazarī gives ḍamma above sukūn; a number of Egyptian teachers swap them. **We teach five levels in Ibn al-Jazarī's order.**
- **The colour of the qalqalah bounce.** The jumhūr say no vowel colour; a minority say it inclines toward the preceding vowel. **We teach the jumhūr position.**
- **Two degrees of qalqalah or three.** Some add *qalqalah akbar* / *kubrā jiddan* for stopping on a mushaddad qalqalah letter. **We teach two, and name the third.**

The habit to build: when you hear a difference, first ask **"is this one of the named disagreements?"** before assuming anyone is wrong. Unit 3.2 has a much bigger one waiting — there are words where "heavy or light?" has no single right answer at all.

---

## Hifz for this session

**No new Sabaq.** Say that to her explicitly and say why: **revision lessons pause new rules, and they pause new memorisation too, so the memorisation muscle gets one session's rest while retrieval does the work.** Nothing is being skipped.

**Manzil — al-Fātiḥa,** whole, unaided. If it has run clean for three consecutive sessions, it stays promoted and enters the two-surahs-per-day rotation.

**Sabqi — an-Nās, all six ayat,** whole and unaided. This is its first full-surah revision session. Expect the middle ayat (114:4, 114:5) to be the weak ones; they are the longest and the newest.

**Ten dedicated repetitions after any caught error,** on the corrected portion alone. Give it its own tick-box on the sheet.

**Assessment note for the teacher.** Score the hifz on **two separate axes**, and keep them apart:
- **Memorisation fidelity** — words omitted, added or transposed.
- **Tajweed** — everything in this unit.

Clean recall with rough recitation and clean recitation with shaky recall need opposite remedies, and a single combined score cannot tell you which one you are looking at. Write down both numbers.

## Homework

**25–35 min/day, five days.** This week is consolidation, not new load.

1. **Three minutes — the cold six.** ٱلصِّرَٰطَ · طَعَامِ · يَجْعَلْ · غَاسِقٍ · ٱلضَّآلِّينَ · ٱلْحَمْدُ, read without preparation, once, then checked against the table.
2. **Two minutes — Drill 3.** Heavy / heavy-open / heavy-clamped / light. Twelve letters, no hesitation.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 4, the alif blind test.** Call it before reading.
4. **Three minutes — Drill 5.** Bounce or no bounce, and which degree. Ask "am I stopping?" out loud every time.
5. **Two minutes — Drill 6 and the cheek test.**
6. **Three minutes — Drill 9, the cold page.** Al-Falaq and al-Ikhlāṣ, stopping at every ayah, recorded.
7. **Hifz — Manzil (al-Fātiḥa) and Sabqi (an-Nās).** No new portion this week.

**Bring to the next session:** your three-item list of what needs work, written down, and the recording from Drill 9. Next session starts Unit 3.2 and the rā' — the busiest letter in tajweed, and the one letter whose ruling changes from word to word.
