# Lesson 3-09 — Rā' I: fatḥa and ḍamma heavy, kasra light

> **[[Ra]] is the busiest letter in tajweed.** Together with the ل of the Divine Name it is one of only two letters that switch between heavy and light — but unlike that ل, which has one condition, the rā' has around eight, differs between joining and stopping, and carries a five-word exception list. This lesson takes the first two conditions and nothing else. The sākin cases, the waqf cases and the exceptions are lesson 3-10.

## Objectives

- Student can state the baseline in one line — **fatḥa or ḍamma → heavy, kasra → light** — and apply it to any vowelled rā' on sight.
- Student can produce a **light** rā' at all: forward, thin, and unmistakably different from her default.
- Student can produce a rā' as **one tap, not a trill**, in both weights.
- Student can read رِحْلَةَ and ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ back to back with two audibly different rā's.

## Hook

Write one word and ask her to read it:

> **رِحْلَةَ** (106:2)

Listen to the ر. It will be heavy. It is heavy in almost every untrained reader in the world, and she will not hear anything wrong with it.

Then say it correctly — thin, forward, the vowel like the *i* of English *rich* — and say the sentence:

> **A heavy rā' is the English default, and the Urdu default, and near enough the Turkish and Uzbek default. You arrived with it already installed. Every light rā' in this course is a habit to be un-learned, not a rule to be added.**

Then the second error, and demonstrate it deliberately so she hears it named:

> **Trilling.** *rrrabb* instead of *rabb*. When a student reaches for heaviness, the tongue relaxes and starts to roll. Ibn al-Jazarī explicitly warns against letting the trill become audible. **One tap. Never a roll.** It is the second-commonest fault in this family and it is completely independent of heavy versus light — you can trill either one.

And the third, said now so it is not a surprise in three lessons: **do not go looking for the answer in the letter before it.** For a **vowelled** rā', the only thing that decides is **the rā's own vowel**. In **لِرَبِّكَ** (108:2) there is a kasra sitting right there on the ل, and the rā' is still heavy, because the rā' has a fatḥa. The letter before only matters when the rā' has no vowel of its own — which is next lesson.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-06** gave her tafkhīm and tarqīq as performances, the five levels, and the alif rule. It also listed the four standing exceptions to "everything outside خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ is light" and said that **ر** was the biggest of them, with its own lessons. This is that lesson. She should recognise it as a promise being kept.
- **Lesson 3-06 also gave her the tool she needs today**: she can already produce a heavy sound and a light sound on demand and switch between them. **Today is not a new skill. It is the same switch, applied to one letter, on a per-word basis.**
- **Lesson 3-01** placed the rā' on the tongue map at point 7 — the *back* of the tongue tip, against the gums of the upper incisors, slightly **deeper** than ن. She drilled the ل / ن / ر cluster there. That makhraj does not change today: **heavy and light rā' are made in exactly the same place.**
- **Lesson 3-02** told her ر is **tawassut** — it can be held, and it does not bounce. Two consequences today: it is holdable enough to be examined slowly, and any kick on a sākin ر is an error left over from qalqalah.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Give the baseline first, as a single line, and make her write it down.**

   > **fatḥa or ḍamma → HEAVY**
   > **kasra → LIGHT**

   That is today. Everything else in the family — and there is a lot of it — is a refinement of that line. She should leave able to say it without thinking.

2. **Say what does NOT decide it, because that is where the confusion starts.**
   For a rā' that **has a vowel of its own**, nothing else matters:
   - not the letter before it,
   - not the letter after it,
   - not whether there is a heavy letter in the word.

   **لِرَبِّكَ** (108:2) — kasra on the ل, fatḥa on the ر: **heavy**.
   **بِرَبِّ** (113:1, 114:1) — kasra on the ب, fatḥa on the ر: **heavy**.
   **صُدُورِ** (114:5) — heavy ص two letters back, kasra on the ر: **light**.

   That last one is worth stopping on. She memorised صُدُورِ three sessions ago with a correct heavy ص. The ر after it is **light**, and every instinct she has says otherwise.

3. **Teach the heavy rā' as a body position.**
   1. The rā' is at the **tip of the tongue against the gum ridge**, the same place as ن and ل. **The makhraj does not change between heavy and light.** Say that twice.
   2. Raise the **back** of the tongue toward the soft palate. The vowel darkens: *ra* moves toward *rɑ*, the vowel of English *rob* rather than *rib*.
   3. **One tap, not a trill.** Press the tongue firmly against the ridge to suppress the extra taps. A slack tongue rolls.
   4. An alif after a heavy rā' is heavy too — lesson 3-06's rule, unchanged, applying here.

4. **Teach the light rā', and give it more time, because it is the one she does not have.**
   1. Same makhraj. Only the **back** of the tongue changes: for tarqīq it stays **down**.
   2. The vowel colour is forward and thin: *ri* as in English *rich*, not *ruh*.
   3. **Still one tap.** Lightness does not mean a weaker contact — a slack tongue produces the trill just as reliably as an over-eager one. This surprises students and needs saying.
   4. Any alif or madd letter after a light rā' is light.

   Then the diagnostic pair, and drill it now rather than later:

   > **رِحْلَةَ** (106:2) — light · **ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ** (1:3) — heavy

   Both are rā's at the head of a syllable. One has a kasra, one has a fatḥa. Say them alternately, ten times.

5. **The shadda cases, which look harder than they are.**
   A doubled rā' is two rā's, and **both halves take the ruling of the vowel on it**:

   | Word | Ref | Vowel | Ruling |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ | 1:3 | fatḥa | **heavy**, both halves |
   | شَرِّ | 113:2 | kasra | **light**, both halves |
   | بِرَبِّ | 114:1 | fatḥa | heavy |
   | لِرَبِّكَ | 108:2 | fatḥa | heavy |

   The trap is a doubling that starts heavy and lightens halfway, or the reverse. **Hold the two counts at one weight.** Lesson 2-05 gave her the two counts; today they have to be two counts of the *same colour*.

   **شَرِّ** deserves a moment on its own. It appears **four times in Sūrat al-Falaq** — 113:2, 113:3, 113:4, 113:5 — which she starts memorising today. A light doubled rā' repeated four times in five ayat is either four wins or four ingrained errors, and this is the week it is decided.

6. **Say what is coming, so the boundary of today is clear.**
   Write this on the board and leave it up:

   > **Today: the rā' HAS a vowel.**
   > **Next lesson: the rā' has NO vowel — it is sākin, or you have stopped on it.**
   > **Then the rule changes: a sākin rā' takes its ruling from the vowel BEFORE it.**

   Say the second half of that sentence out loud even though it is not today's material. It stops her building the wrong general rule out of today's specific one, and it is a genuinely different mechanism rather than an extension.

7. **One honest note, thirty seconds.**
   Tell her the rā' rules are **not colour-coded** in the app's mushaf highlighting. The annotation dataset covers eighteen rules; none of them is a rā' rule. Given that ر is the busiest letter in tajweed, that is the largest single gap in the highlighting — **this family has to be reasoned and heard, never looked up.** It is also why the drills below are hand-built word lists rather than "read this page and watch for the colour".

## Examples

| Text | Ref | Vowel on the ر | Ruling |
|---|---|---|---|
| تَرَ | 105:1 | fatḥa | heavy |
| لِرَبِّكَ | 108:2 | fatḥa | **heavy** — despite the kasra on the ل before it |
| نَصْرُ | 110:1 | ḍamma | heavy |
| رِحْلَةَ | 106:2 | kasra | **light** |
| ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ | 1:3 | fatḥa, doubled | heavy, both halves |
| شَرِّ | 113:2 | kasra, doubled | **light**, both halves |
| رِزْقَهُۥ | 89:16 | kasra | **light** — *reference example, outside your memorized surahs* |
| ٱلْعُقَدِ | 113:4 | — | no rā' at all; the negative control |

**Teacher note on ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ.** She has said this word more times than any other in her life. It is heavy — a doubled rā' with a fatḥa — and it is heavy for the whole two counts. Two things worth saying: the ر here also happens to be a merged solar lām (lesson 3-12), and **the merge and the heaviness are completely independent rules.** Do not let her conclude that a merged rā' is heavy *because* it is merged.

**Teacher note on لِرَبِّكَ.** This is the item in the set that tests whether she has understood the boundary of today's rule. A kasra sits immediately before the rā'. The rā' is still heavy, because it has its own fatḥa. Students who have half-heard the next lesson get this one wrong.

**Teacher note on ٱلْعُقَدِ.** It is here on purpose. A lesson on one letter makes students find that letter everywhere. Read this word in the middle of the drill and make her say "no rā'."

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the baseline, out loud.** Every session, first:

> **"Fatḥa or ḍamma: heavy. Kasra: light. And that is only when the rā' has a vowel of its own."**

**Drill 2 — the bare rā', both weights.** Six syllables, alternating:

رَ · رِ · رُ — then **رَ / رِ**, ten times alternating.

The middle one is the work. If رِ sounds like رَ with a different vowel stuck on it, the back of the tongue has not come down.

**Drill 3 — one tap, not a roll.** Hold the tongue firmly against the ridge and say each one exactly once:

رَ · رِ · رُ · أَرْ · رَا · رِي

Record it and count the taps. If you can hear more than one contact, press harder — the trill comes from a slack tongue, not from an eager one.

**Drill 4 — heavy column, light column.** Read down each column, then across the rows:

| Heavy (fatḥa / ḍamma) | Light (kasra) |
|---|---|
| تَرَ (105:1) | رِحْلَةَ (106:2) |
| نَصْرُ (110:1) | رِزْقَهُۥ (89:16) |
| لِرَبِّكَ (108:2) | شَرِّ (113:2) |
| بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ (113:1) | صُدُورِ (114:5) |
| ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ (1:3) | ٱلرَّحِيمِ — *(heavy: the doubled ر carries a fatḥa)* |

The last row is a deliberate trap. **ٱلرَّحِيمِ is heavy**, because the rā' is doubled with a fatḥa; the kasra later in the word belongs to the م. Read the whole of 1:1 slowly and place both rā's.

**Drill 5 — the diagnostic pair.** Ten alternations, every day:

> **رِحْلَةَ / ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ**

Then have someone say one at random with the text covered and identify which you heard.

**Drill 6 — the doubling, one colour.** Two counts at a single weight. Tap the two beats:

شَرِّ (light, light) · ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ (heavy, heavy) · بِرَبِّ (heavy, heavy) · ٱلرَّحِيمِ (heavy, heavy)

If the weight changes between the beats, slow down and hold it.

**Drill 7 — heavy neighbour, light rā'.** The words where instinct is loudest and wrong:

صُدُورِ (114:5) — heavy ص, **light ر**
رِزْقَهُۥ (89:16) — there is a heavy ق later in the word, and it changes nothing: the ر has a kasra, so it is **light**
شَرِّ (113:2) — light, four times over in al-Falaq

**Drill 8 — Sūrat al-Falaq, rā' patrol.** Read the whole surah and mark every rā':

> بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ (heavy) · شَرِّ ×4 (light) · — and nothing else.

Five ayat. One heavy rā' and four light ones. This is the highest-yield page in the Qur'an for this lesson, and she is memorising it starting today.

**Drill 9 — the negative control.** Read these and say "no rā'":

ٱلْعُقَدِ (113:4) · ٱلنَّاسِ (114:1) · ٱلْحَمْدُ (1:2) · أَنْعَمْتَ (1:7)

## Listen-for

**1. Heavy by default — the flagship error of the family.**
- *Listen for:* رِحْلَةَ and رِزْقَهُۥ. If she gets those two right, the habit is broken. Also شَرِّ, four times in al-Falaq.
- *Why it happens:* the heavy rā' is the default in English, Urdu, and near enough in Turkish and Uzbek. She is not adding heaviness; she is failing to remove something she arrived with.
- *Correction cue:* "*rich*, not *rob*. Let the back of your tongue drop." Then Drill 5, ten alternations.

**2. Trilling.**
- *Listen for:* more than one contact. Record and count — the ear misses this at speed, the recording does not.
- *Why it happens:* the tongue relaxes when the student is concentrating on weight instead of contact. It happens on light rā's as often as heavy ones, and it is a **separate fault** from heaviness — do not let a fixed trill get credited as a fixed weight.
- *Correction cue:* "One tap. Press harder, not softer." Ibn al-Jazarī's own warning is worth quoting to her once: the trill is a ṣifah of the letter that the reciter is supposed to *suppress*, not perform.

**3. Deciding from the letter before, on a vowelled rā'.**
- *Listen for:* لِرَبِّكَ or بِرَبِّ read light because of the kasra on the preceding letter.
- *Why it happens:* she has heard, correctly, that the preceding vowel matters — but that is the **sākin** rule, and it is next lesson. She has generalised early.
- *Correction cue:* "Does the rā' have a vowel of its own? Then that is the only one that counts."

**4. Deciding from a heavy letter elsewhere in the word.**
- *Listen for:* صُدُورِ read with a heavy ر because of the ص.
- *Why it happens:* lesson 3-06 spent a whole session on contamination from heavy neighbours, and the rā' looks like the same situation. It is not: this is a rule, not a bleed.
- *Correction cue:* "What vowel is on the rā'? Kasra. Then it is light, and the ص is not invited."

**5. A doubling that changes weight halfway.**
- *Listen for:* شَرِّ starting heavy and thinning, or ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ starting heavy and thinning. Usually the second half drifts toward the following vowel.
- *Why it happens:* the two counts are long enough for the tongue to move, and nothing in her practice has required a *sustained* posture.
- *Correction cue:* Tap the two beats and hold one colour across both. "Same weight, twice."

**6. Thinning the rā' until it stops being a rā'.**
- *Listen for:* a light rā' that has drifted toward a ل or an English flap and lost the letter's identity.
- *Why it happens:* over-correction of error 1, and it arrives within a session of fixing it. Name it as such.
- *Correction cue:* "Light, not absent. Same tap, same place — just let the back of the tongue down."

**7. Bouncing a rā'.**
- *Listen for:* a kick on a sākin ر, especially at وَٱنْحَرْ (108:2).
- *Why it happens:* left over from lessons 3-04 and 3-05. A sākin ر genuinely feels bouncy.
- *Correction cue:* "Can you hold it? Then there is nothing blocked to bounce out of. ر is tawassut."

**8. Finding rā's that are not there.**
- *Listen for:* hesitation on words with no rā' in them at all, mid-drill.
- *Why it happens:* a single-letter lesson primes the eye. It passes in a week.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 9. Say "no rā'" out loud; do not just move on.

## Hifz for this session

**Manzil — al-Fātiḥa**, whole and unaided, on its rotation.
**Sabqi — an-Nās**, all six ayat. Ten dedicated repetitions on any corrected word.

**New Sabaq — al-Falaq 113:1–113:2.**

> قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ
> مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ

Twenty to forty repetitions from the page. This is an unusually good pairing of hifz and rule: **both of today's rulings appear in the two ayat she is memorising**, and one of them repeats four more times across the rest of the surah.

- **بِرَبِّ** — doubled ر with a fatḥa, **heavy**, both counts.
- **شَرِّ** — doubled ر with a kasra, **light**, both counts. She will meet it three more times in the next three sessions. Get it right today and the other three are free.
- **ٱلْفَلَقِ** — heavy ق, clean sākin ل before it with no bounce, and a **kubrā bounce on the ق** when she stops at the end of the ayah (lesson 3-05).
- **خَلَقَ** — heavy خ, heavy ق, and a light ل between them. Lesson 3-06's switch, in three letters.

## Homework

**25–35 min/day, five days.**

1. **Ten seconds — Drill 1.** Say the baseline out loud before anything else.
2. **Two minutes — Drill 2.** رَ / رِ, ten alternations. The light one is the work.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 3, one tap.** Record it once this week and count the contacts.
4. **Three minutes — Drill 4**, both columns and then across. Do not skip the ٱلرَّحِيمِ row.
5. **Two minutes — Drill 5, the diagnostic pair.** Every day.
6. **Two minutes — Drill 6, the doubling.** Two counts, one colour.
7. **Two minutes — Drill 7.** The three words where instinct is loudest and wrong.
8. **Two minutes — Drill 8.** Sūrat al-Falaq, rā' patrol: one heavy, four light.
9. **Hifz — Manzil (al-Fātiḥa), Sabqi (an-Nās), then new Sabaq (113:1–113:2).**

**Bring to the next session:** a recording of رِحْلَةَ and ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ said alternately five times, and this question answered — in صُدُورِ, why is the ر light when the ص right before it is heavy?
