# Lesson 3-11 — The lām of lafẓ al-jalālah

## Objectives

- Student can state the rule in one line — **fatḥa or ḍamma before it → heavy; kasra → light; beginning on the word → heavy** — and apply it without hesitation.
- Student can read **بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ** with a light lām.
- Student can say why every **other** lām in the Qur'an is light, always, and can keep them light next to heavy letters.
- Student can produce the heavy and light lām as an audibly different pair, on demand, in isolation.

## Hook

Ask her to say the basmalah.

Listen to the lām of **ٱللَّهِ**. In most untrained readers it comes out **heavy** — *bismillōh* — and it should be **light**, because the word before it ends in a kasra. It is the first phrase of the Qur'an. She says it dozens of times a day. And it is, by a wide margin, the most commonly mispronounced lām in the language.

Say both versions to her, one after the other, and then say the rule:

> **بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ — light. نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ — heavy. The word ٱللَّه did not change. The vowel in front of it did.**

Then name the second error before it appears, because it will appear within the hour:

> **Looking at the wrong letter.** The rule reads the vowel on the letter **immediately before the word** — not the vowel on the lām itself, and not anything inside ٱللَّه. Students who have just come from three lessons on the rā' start hunting inside the word. There is nothing to find in there.

And a third, said once: **a uniformly heavy lām everywhere**. In several of the languages spoken around this student, the Divine Name is habitually pronounced *Allōh* in all environments. That habit does not carry into recitation.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-06** gave her tafkhīm and tarqīq and listed the four standing exceptions to the default. **ر** was one; **the ل of لفظ الجلالة** was another. This is that lesson.
- **Lessons 3-09 and 3-10** were the rā' — eight-ish conditions, a five-word exception list, a waṣl/waqf split, and three words where the answer is "either". **Today is the same idea with ONE condition, no exception list, and no waṣl/waqf split.** Say that out loud in the first two minutes: after two hard sessions, she should know this one is small.
- **Lesson 3-01** put the lām on the tongue map at point 5 — the tongue's edge, from its rear side to its tip, meeting the gums of the upper front teeth. **That makhraj does not change between heavy and light.** Only the back of the tongue moves. Exactly as with the rā'.
- **Lesson 2-05** taught her to hold a shadda for two full counts. ٱللَّه has a doubled lām, so today's weight has to be sustained across both counts.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Say what the rule is about, in one sentence, before any table.**

   > **The ل in the word ٱللَّه is the only lām in the language that alternates between heavy and light. Every other lām in the Qur'an — in صَلَاة, in ٱلْحَمْدُ, in ٱلْفَلَقِ, anywhere at all — is always light, without exception.**

   Together with the rā', that is **two letters in the entire language** whose heaviness is conditional. She has now met both. The set is closed.

2. **The rule, in a four-row table. Read the letter immediately before the word.**

   | Preceding vowel | Example | Ruling |
   |---|---|---|
   | **fatḥa** | قَالَ ٱللَّهُ | **heavy** |
   | **ḍamma** | نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ (110:1) | **heavy** |
   | **kasra** | بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ (1:1) | **light** |
   | **nothing — you begin on the word** | ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ (112:2) | **heavy** |

   The fourth row is worth a moment. When she starts a recitation on ٱللَّه there is no preceding vowel at all, so there is nothing to make it light. **Default is heavy.**

3. **The kasra makes it light in every form — and this is where it differs from the rā'.**

   Unlike the rā' rules, **no distinction is made between original and temporary kasras here**. All of these are light:

   | Kind of kasra | Example |
   |---|---|
   | an **original** kasra on the previous word | دِينِ ٱللَّهِ (110:2) |
   | a **preposition** fused into the word | لِلَّهِ (1:2) · بِٱللَّهِ |
   | a **connecting** vowel | قُلِ ٱللَّهُمَّ |

   Point out the contrast explicitly, because she spent last session learning that hamzat-waṣl kasras do **not** count for the rā'. **Here they do.** Two rules, two answers; the difference is real and she should not try to unify them.

4. **Ibn al-Jazarī's line, given once, because it is the whole rule in one verse.**

   > **وَفَخِّمِ اللَّامَ مِنِ اسْمِ اللَّهِ ⁕ عَنْ فَتْحٍ أَوْ ضَمٍّ كَعَبْدِ اللَّهِ**
   >
   > *Make heavy the lām of the name of Allāh — after a fatḥa or a ḍamma, as in ʿabdu-llāh.*

   Two lines of verse, one condition. Compare it, out loud, with the size of the rā' family. She should feel the relief.

5. **Teach the two sounds physically, and give the vowel colours in English.**

   **Heavy:** the tongue tip stays exactly where a lām is made — against the gum ridge behind the upper teeth — while the **back** of the tongue rises toward the soft palate. The vowel darkens: *Allōh*, with the vowel of English **law**. The shadda is held for its full measure, **heavy throughout both counts**.

   **Light:** the ordinary clear Arabic lām, tongue flat at the back. The vowel is forward: *Allah*, with the vowel of English **cat**. Nothing rises.

   Then drill the pair back to back until the difference is unmistakable:

   > **بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ** (light) → **نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ** (heavy) → **دِينِ ٱللَّهِ** (light)

   Sūrat an-Naṣr is the ideal drill page: **نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ in ayah 1 and دِينِ ٱللَّهِ in ayah 2** — heavy and light, twenty words apart, in a surah of three ayat.

6. **Every other lām, and this needs its own block.**

   **Every lām outside ٱللَّه is light. Always. No exceptions.**

   The two things that break it:
   - **A heavy neighbour.** ٱلْقَارِعَةُ, ٱلطَّامَّة, صَلَاة, طَلَبَ — the ق, ط and ص are heavy, and the ل beside them is not. This is lesson 3-06's contamination, applied to the letter it damages most.
   - **The dark English "l".** English speakers default to the velarised /ɫ/ of *feel* and install it everywhere. Arabic wants the clear /l/ of *leaf* in every lām except one word. Say the two English words to her and let her hear that they contain two different sounds.

   Then the practical consequence: **the heavy lām she is learning today sounds a lot like the English *feel* lām.** So the fix is not "learn a new sound" — she has the heavy one already, by accident. The fix is **removing it from the other twenty thousand lāms** and keeping it for one word in three environments.

7. **Distinguish this from the other two lām rules, now, before lesson 3-12 arrives.**

   She is about to meet lām shamsiyyah and lām qamariyyah next session, and the two families get conflated constantly because both are "a lām rule". Draw the line today:

   | Family | The question it asks |
   |---|---|
   | **lām jalālah** — today | **heavy or light?** |
   | **lām shamsiyyah / qamariyyah** — next lesson | **pronounced or merged?** |

   Different question, different answer, different letter position. And the lām of the definite article, whenever it is pronounced at all, is **always light** — so the two families never actually collide.

8. **One honest note.** Like the rā' rules, **the lām jalālah is not colour-coded** in the app's mushaf highlighting; the dataset has no concept of heaviness. It annotates the solar lām, which she meets next session, and nothing for this. **The single most common tajweed error in the most frequently recited phrase in the Qur'an cannot be highlighted from the corpus.** That is why it gets drilled by hand.

## Examples

| Text | Ref | Preceding vowel | Ruling |
|---|---|---|---|
| بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ | 1:1 | kasra on بِسْمِ | **light** |
| لِلَّهِ | 1:2 | kasra of the preposition لِ | **light** |
| نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ | 110:1 | ḍamma | heavy |
| دِينِ ٱللَّهِ | 110:2 | kasra on دِينِ | **light** |
| هُوَ ٱللَّهُ | 112:1 | ḍamma on هُوَ | heavy |
| ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ | 112:2 | — you begin here | heavy |
| نَارُ ٱللَّهِ | 104:6 | ḍamma | heavy — *reference example, outside your memorized surahs* |

**Teacher note on بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ.** This is the highest-value single item in the entire heaviness family. It is the first phrase of the Qur'an, it opens every surah she recites, it is said dozens of times a day outside recitation altogether, and **it is read heavy by most untrained readers.** If one thing is fixed in this lesson, fix this. And it fixes itself repeatedly once fixed, because she will meet it at the head of every surah in her memorisation set.

**Teacher note on Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ.** Ayah 1 gives **هُوَ ٱللَّهُ** — heavy after a ḍamma. Ayah 2 gives **ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ** — heavy because she begins the ayah there. Two heavies in two ayat, for two different reasons. Have her name the reason each time rather than just producing the sound.

**Teacher note on Sūrat an-Naṣr.** Both rulings, in one three-ayah surah, one ayah apart. It is the single best drill page for this rule and she will memorise it later in this unit.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the rule, out loud.** Every session, first:

> **"Fatḥa or ḍamma before it: heavy. Kasra: light. Starting on it: heavy. Look at the letter before the word."**

**Drill 2 — the pair, in isolation.** Ten alternations:

**ٱللَّه** (heavy, vowel of *law*) / **ٱللَّه** (light, vowel of *cat*)

Then have someone say one at random with the text covered and identify which you heard. If the two are not clearly distinguishable in isolation, they will not be distinguishable in a phrase.

**Drill 3 — the three-phrase sequence.** Every day, and this is the drill that carries the lesson:

> **بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ** (light) → **نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ** (heavy) → **دِينِ ٱللَّهِ** (light)

**Drill 4 — name the vowel first.** For each phrase, say the preceding vowel out loud **before** reading it:

بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ (?) · هُوَ ٱللَّهُ (?) · نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ (?) · دِينِ ٱللَّهِ (?) · لِلَّهِ (?) · نَارُ ٱللَّهِ (?) · ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ (?)

Naming it first is what stops her deciding from habit.

**Drill 5 — the basmalah, ten times.** Slowly, with a light lām, every day:

> **بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ**

While you are here, place the two rā's as well: **ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ** and **ٱلرَّحِيمِ** are both doubled rā's with a fatḥa, so both are **heavy** (lesson 3-09). One light lām, two heavy rā's, in five words.

**Drill 6 — Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ and Sūrat an-Naṣr.** Read each and name the ruling and the reason at every ٱللَّه:

> قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ — heavy, ḍamma
> ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ — heavy, beginning
> إِذَا جَآءَ نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلْفَتْحُ — heavy, ḍamma
> ... فِى دِينِ ٱللَّهِ أَفْوَاجًا — light, kasra

**Drill 7 — every other lām is light.** Ten words with a lām beside something heavy. Every one clear and forward, the /l/ of *leaf*:

ٱلْفَلَقِ · ٱلصِّرَٰطَ · مَطْلَعِ · ٱلْقَارِعَةُ · تَضْلِيلٍ · ٱلضَّآلِّينَ · ٱلْقَدْرِ · ٱلْفِيلِ · لِلَّهِ · فَلْيَعْبُدُوا۟

**Drill 8 — the two-count hold.** ٱللَّه has a doubled lām. Tap the two beats and keep **one weight across both**:

نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ (heavy, heavy) · بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ (light, light)

If the weight changes between the beats, slow down.

## Listen-for

**1. A heavy lām after a kasra — above all in the basmalah.**
- *Listen for:* *bismillōh*. If she fixes one thing in this lesson, fix this.
- *Why it happens:* the Divine Name is habitually said heavy outside recitation, in several of the languages around her, and the basmalah is said so often and so fast that it has become one unexamined block of sound.
- *Correction cue:* "What is the vowel on بِسْمِ?" Then Drill 3, three phrases, slowly.

**2. A uniformly heavy lām everywhere.**
- *Listen for:* every ٱللَّه heavy regardless of context.
- *Why it happens:* carried over wholesale from a language where the Name is always *Allōh*.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 2 in isolation first. She has to be able to make both sounds before she can be asked to choose between them.

**3. A uniformly light lām.**
- *Listen for:* قَالَ ٱللَّهُ or نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ flattened.
- *Why it happens:* over-correction, and it arrives within a session of fixing error 1. Name it as a drill artefact when it appears.
- *Correction cue:* "Fatḥa or ḍamma. That one is heavy." Drill 4, naming the vowel first.

**4. Looking at the wrong letter.**
- *Listen for:* her hesitating and scanning inside ٱللَّه, or reading the vowel on the lām itself.
- *Why it happens:* two sessions on the rā', where the answer lives in the word, immediately precede this one.
- *Correction cue:* "Outside the word. The letter before it." Point at the page.

**5. Forgetting the connecting kasra in قُلِ ٱللَّهُمَّ.**
- *Listen for:* a heavy lām after a connecting vowel.
- *Why it happens:* she learned last session that hamzat-waṣl kasras do not count for the rā'. Here they do count. Two rules, opposite answers.
- *Correction cue:* "For the rā', temporary kasras do not count. For this lām, every kasra counts. Do not unify them."

**6. A dark English "l" in every other lām.**
- *Listen for:* the /ɫ/ of *feel* in ٱلْحَمْدُ, ٱلْفَلَقِ, ٱلَّذِينَ, لِلَّهِ.
- *Why it happens:* English installs it below awareness, and it sounds plausible because there genuinely is one heavy lām in the language — so the ear has no reason to flag it.
- *Correction cue:* "*leaf*, not *feel*." Then Drill 7.

**7. Heavying a lām beside a heavy letter.**
- *Listen for:* the ل of ٱلْقَارِعَةُ or ٱلصِّرَٰطَ going velar.
- *Why it happens:* contamination — lesson 3-06's inertia, on the letter most susceptible to it.
- *Correction cue:* "The ق is heavy. The ل is not, and it never is, outside one word."

**8. A doubling that changes weight halfway.**
- *Listen for:* ٱللَّهِ starting heavy and thinning across the two counts, or the reverse.
- *Why it happens:* two counts is long enough for the tongue to move and nothing has required a sustained posture.
- *Correction cue:* Tap the beats. "Same weight, twice."

## Hifz for this session

**Manzil — al-Fātiḥa**, on rotation.
**Sabqi — an-Nās** (whole) and **al-Falaq 113:1–113:3.** Ten dedicated repetitions on any corrected word.

**New Sabaq — al-Falaq 113:4.**

> وَمِن شَرِّ ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ فِى ٱلْعُقَدِ

Twenty to forty repetitions from the page.

- **شَرِّ** — light doubled rā', third of four. By now this should be automatic; if it is not, that is where the extra repetitions go.
- **ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ** has two doublings in one word — the نّ of the merged article and the فّ. Both held two counts, both light, and the ن hums (lesson 2-05).
- **ٱلْعُقَدِ** — a light ع and a heavy ق, and if she stops on the ayah, a **kubrā bounce on the د**: ٱلْعُقَدْ.
- And the **basmalah at the head of the surah**, with the light lām she fixed today. She recites it every time she starts al-Falaq, which makes today's rule part of her hifz reps whether or not she notices.

## Homework

**20–30 min/day, five days.** This lesson is smaller than the last two. Give the time back to the rā' and to hifz.

1. **Ten seconds — Drill 1.** The rule out loud.
2. **One minute — Drill 2.** The pair in isolation, ten alternations.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 3.** بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ → نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ → دِينِ ٱللَّهِ. Every day.
4. **Two minutes — Drill 5, the basmalah**, ten times, light lām, and place both heavy rā's while you are there.
5. **Two minutes — Drill 4.** Name the vowel before reading.
6. **Two minutes — Drill 7.** Ten other lāms, all light, all *leaf*.
7. **Two minutes — Drill 6.** Al-Ikhlāṣ and an-Naṣr, naming the reason each time.
8. **Three minutes — rā' upkeep.** Last lesson's Drill 8, mixed cold. Do not let two hard sessions decay while this easy one gets the attention.
9. **Hifz — Manzil (al-Fātiḥa), Sabqi (an-Nās + 113:1–113:3), then new Sabaq (113:4).**

**Bring to the next session:** a recording of the basmalah said five times, and this answered — in لِلَّهِ, where exactly is the kasra that makes the lām light? Next session is the **other** lām family, and the question changes from *heavy or light* to *pronounced or merged*.
