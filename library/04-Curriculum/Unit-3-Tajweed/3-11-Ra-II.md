# Lesson 3-11 — Rā' II: sākin cases, the five isti'lā' words, and jawāz al-wajhayn

## Objectives

- Student can state the sākin rule in one line — **a sākin rā' takes its ruling from the vowel BEFORE it** — and apply it on sight.
- Student can say why the kasra of a hamzat waṣl does **not** count, and read ٱرْجِعِىٓ heavy.
- Student can recognise the **five** isti'lā' exception words on sight, and knows she is not expected to derive them.
- Student can say why ٱلْقَدْرِ is light in waṣl and heavy at waqf, and can decide before she reads which one she is doing.
- Student can name the three cases where **both readings are transmitted** and stop treating them as errors.

## Hook

Write two words side by side:

> **وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ** (110:3) — and — **قِرْطَاسٍ** (6:7)

Both have a sākin rā'. Both have a kasra before it. Both have a heavy letter in the word. **One is light and one is heavy**, and if she reads them both the same way — whichever way — one of them is wrong.

Then the error to name before it happens, because it is the one that actually occurs:

> **She will read وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ heavy, because of the غ.**

And the غ is a red herring. It comes **before** the rā'. **Only a following isti'lā' letter counts.** That single fact is the difference between the two words on the board.

Then the third thing, which is the real content of the lesson and should be said as a warning:

> **This is the lesson where students start deriving. Do not. Condition 6 fires in exactly five words in the entire Qur'an. Learn to recognise five words and move on — nobody needs to derive this.**

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-10, last session,** gave her the baseline: fatḥa or ḍamma → heavy, kasra → light, **for a rā' that has a vowel of its own**. It ended with the boundary written on the board — *next lesson the rā' has no vowel, and then the rule changes.* This is that lesson. Take her answer to the homework question about صُدُورِ first.
- **Lesson 3-03** gave her **خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ**, and today is where that set does its most conspicuous work. Every one of the five exception words is an exception *because* an isti'lā' letter follows the rā'. If the seven letters are not automatic, this lesson will not stick — check them in the first two minutes.
- **Lesson 2-09** taught her **hamzat al-waṣl** — the connecting hamzah that is pronounced when you begin on a word and vanishes when you join to it. Today that fact becomes a ruling: a kasra that disappears when you join was never really the rā's.
- **Lesson 3-06** taught her that **kubrā is a decision, not a property of the word** — the same د is one rule if you read on and another if you stop. Today the identical structure appears in a completely different family. Say so explicitly: this is the second time, not a coincidence, and it is how the whole waṣl/waqf axis works.

## Teaching sequence

1. **The new baseline, in one line, written next to last lesson's.**

   > **A sākin rā' takes its ruling from the vowel BEFORE it.**

   - **fatḥa before it → heavy**
   - **ḍamma before it → heavy**
   - **kasra before it → light** *(with two qualifiers, coming next)*

   Then the whole family in two sentences, and she should be able to say both from memory by the end of the unit:

   > **A vowelled rā' takes its ruling from its own vowel.**
   > **A sākin rā' takes its ruling from the vowel before it.**

   Everything else is qualification.

2. **Drill the plain cases before touching a qualifier.** Four words, no complications:

   | Word | Ref | Before the ر | Ruling |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | تَرْمِيهِم | 105:4 | fatḥa | heavy |
   | وَأَرْسَلَ | 105:3 | fatḥa | heavy |
   | ٱلْقُرْءَانُ | 2:185 | ḍamma | heavy |
   | وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ | 110:3 | kasra | **light** |

   Note that three of those four are in surahs she is memorising or has memorised. Lead from them.

   **وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ is the one to spend time on.** There is a غ in the word — an isti'lā' letter, unmistakably heavy — and the rā' is still light, because **the غ comes before the ر.** Point at it on the page. "Which side of the rā' is the heavy letter on?"

3. **Qualifier 1 — the kasra must be ORIGINAL.**
   A kasra that only exists because of a **hamzat al-waṣl** does not count. It is a connecting vowel: it is there when you begin on the word, and it **vanishes the instant you join to the word before**. It was never the rā's own.

   > **ٱرْجِعِىٓ** (89:28) — **heavy**, not light.

   Give her the test: **"If I join this word to the one before it, does the kasra survive?"** If it disappears, ignore it.

   This is a small class of words and she does not need to hunt for them. What she needs is to not be surprised.

4. **Qualifier 2 — the five isti'lā' words. Give them as a card, not as a derivation.**

   The rule: a sākin rā' after an **original** kasra is light **unless** a **ḥarf isti'lā'** follows it **in the same word** — in which case the heavy letter pulls the rā' heavy.

   This fires in exactly **five words in the whole Qur'an**:

   | Word | Ref | The isti'lā' letter |
   |---|---|---|
   | **قِرْطَاسٍ** | 6:7 | ط |
   | **إِرْصَادًا** | 9:107 | ص |
   | **فِرْقَةٍ** | 9:122 | ق |
   | **مِرْصَادًا** | 78:21 | ص |
   | **لَبِٱلْمِرْصَادِ** | 89:14 | ص |

   **All five lie outside the surahs you are memorising — these are reference examples, not hifz.** Say that plainly. She should be able to *recognise* them if one appears; she is not banking them.

   Then say why the list is closed and worth trusting: **do not extend it by analogy.** Any sākin rā' after an original kasra with a heavy letter later in the word is *not* automatically on this list. It is five words. Recognise, do not derive.

   Two performance notes:
   - The **alif after a heavy rā' is heavy** — قِرْطَاسٍ, مِرْصَادًا. Lesson 3-07's rule, unchanged.
   - The isti'lā' letter must follow **in the same word**. A heavy letter at the start of the next word does not reach back.

5. **The waqf cases — and this is where lesson 3-06's structure returns.**

   When you **stop** on a word ending in ر, the rā' becomes sākin, and the sākin rule applies to whatever is now in front of it. Three shapes:

   | Shape | Example | Ruling at waqf |
   |---|---|---|
   | ر after a vowelled letter carrying fatḥa or ḍamma | ٱلْأَبْتَرُ → ٱلْأَبْتَرْ (108:3) | **heavy** |
   | ر after a **sākin** letter, which is itself after a fatḥa or ḍamma | ٱلْقَدْرِ → ٱلْقَدْرْ (97:1) · وَٱلْعَصْرِ → وَٱلْعَصْرْ (103:1) | **heavy** |
   | ر after a **yā' sākinah** | خَيْرٌ → خَيْرْ (97:3) | **light** |
   | ر after a sākin letter which is itself after a **kasra** | ٱلسِّحْرُ → ٱلسِّحْرْ (10:81) | **light** |
   | ر that carried a **kasra** in waṣl | مُّقْتَدِرٍ → مُقْتَدِرْ (54:42) | **light** |

   Then the sentence that carries the section:

   > **ٱلْقَدْرِ is light while you read on and heavy the moment you stop on it. The word did not change. Your stop did.**

   Read it both ways with her, twice each. Then say: **this is exactly what happened with يُولَدْ in lesson 3-06.** Same structure, different family. **The question to ask before every rā' at the end of a word is "am I stopping here?" — and it has to be asked *before* the word, not during it.**

6. **Jawāz al-wajhayn — where both readings are correct.**

   **جَوَازُ الْوَجْهَيْنِ** means literally *the permissibility of the two faces*: a small set of words where **both** a heavy and a light rā' are transmitted for Ḥafṣ, and either is right. Three cases, and that is all.

   **Case 1 — فِرْقٍ (26:63).** Sākin rā' after an original kasra, with an isti'lā' letter (ق) following in the same word. That is exactly the shape of the five words above — **but here the ق itself carries a kasra**, and a kasra'd isti'lā' letter has its own downward pull, so it does not drag the rā' heavy with any force. **Both readings transmitted; tarqīq is the more commonly preferred.**

   Say the thing this explains: **this is why the exception list is exactly five and not six.** فِرْقٍ has the same structure and a different answer. If she ever wonders whether the list is arbitrary, this is the proof that it is not.

   **Case 2 — مِصْرَ and ٱلْقِطْرِ, at waqf only.** In waṣl both are unambiguous: مِصْرَ has a fatḥa on the ر (heavy), ٱلْقِطْرِ has a kasra (light). The ambiguity exists **only when you stop**, because the rā' then becomes sākin and the letter before it is a sākin isti'lā' letter.

   | Word | Refs | Preferred at waqf | Why |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | مِصْرَ | 10:87 · 12:21 | **tafkhīm** | the rā's original vowel was a fatḥa |
   | ٱلْقِطْرِ | 34:12 | **tarqīq** | the rā's original vowel was a kasra |

   **Case 3 — words with a deleted yā', at waqf.** Where the mushaf's orthography has dropped a final yā', you may treat that yā' as present — the rā' then follows a yā' and is **light** — or as absent, in which case it is heavy. **Tarqīq is generally preferred.** يَسْرِ (89:4) · فَأَسْرِ (11:81) · نُذُرِ (54:16).

   **How to use this, in three instructions:**
   1. **Pick one wajh and hold it** for the whole recitation. Alternating within one sitting is not "both permitted"; it is inconsistency.
   2. **Teach the preferred one as the default**, and the other as recognition only.
   3. For cases 2 and 3 the ambiguity exists **only at waqf**. Read on through and there is nothing to choose.

   And the reason this section exists at all, said out loud: **so that she stops treating a different reading as an error, and stops being rattled when a reciter she trusts does it the other way.** This is a genuine, settled difference of transmission — nobody is wrong.

7. **Close by collapsing the whole family back to two lines.**
   She has now met roughly eight heavy conditions, six light ones, a five-word list and three ambiguous cases. Do not leave her holding all of it. Write:

   > **A vowelled rā' takes its own vowel.**
   > **A sākin rā' takes the vowel before it — original kasras only, and a following isti'lā' letter overrides.**

   Then: **everything else is a footnote, and the five words are a card you keep in your pocket.**

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the two lines.** Every session, out loud, before anything:

> **"A vowelled rā' takes its own vowel. A sākin rā' takes the vowel before it."**

**Drill 2 — plain sākin cases.** Say the vowel *before* the rā' out loud, then read the word:

تَرْمِيهِم (fatḥa → ?) · وَأَرْسَلَ (fatḥa → ?) · ٱلْقُرْءَانُ (ḍamma → ?) · وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ (kasra → ?) · وَفِرْعَوْنَ (kasra → ?) · ٱلْفِرْدَوْسِ (kasra → ?)

Naming the vowel first is what stops her guessing from the sound.

**Drill 3 — which side is the heavy letter on?** For each word, point at the isti'lā' letter and say "before" or "after":

| Word | Heavy letter | Side | Ruling |
|---|---|---|---|
| وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ | غ | before | light |
| قِرْطَاسٍ | ط | **after** | heavy |
| مِرْصَادًا | ص | **after** | heavy |
| وَفِرْعَوْنَ | — | none | light |

**Drill 4 — the five-word card.** Read the card twice a day. Do not derive it, do not extend it:

> **قِرْطَاسٍ · إِرْصَادًا · فِرْقَةٍ · مِرْصَادًا · لَبِٱلْمِرْصَادِ**

Then say the sixth-that-isn't: **فِرْقٍ — both readings, tarqīq preferred.**

**Drill 5 — the hamzat-waṣl test.** For each, ask "does the kasra survive if I join?":

ٱرْجِعِىٓ — no, it vanishes → **heavy**
وَفِرْعَوْنَ — yes, it is the ف's own → **light**
ٱلْفِرْدَوْسِ — yes → **light**

**Drill 6 — the same word, both ways.** Read each twice, joined and stopped, and make the two versions audibly different:

ٱلْقَدْرِ → ٱلْقَدْرْ (light / heavy)
وَٱلْعَصْرِ → وَٱلْعَصْرْ (light / heavy)
خَيْرٌ → خَيْرْ (— / light)

**Ask "am I stopping here?" out loud before every one.**

**Drill 7 — jawāz al-wajhayn, both faces.** Say each word twice, once each way, then pick one and keep it:

فِرْقٍ (light preferred) · مِصْرْ (heavy preferred) · ٱلْقِطْرْ (light preferred) · يَسْرِ (light preferred)

The point of this drill is not performance. It is so that hearing the other version never rattles you.

**Drill 8 — mixed, cold.** No warning, no grouping. Read and rule:

تَرْمِيهِم · شَرِّ · وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ · لِرَبِّكَ · قِرْطَاسٍ · صُدُورِ · ٱلْقَدْرِ *(reading on)* · ٱرْجِعِىٓ · رِحْلَةَ · وَأَرْسَلَ · ٱلْقَدْرْ *(stopping)* · ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ

**Drill 9 — Sūrat al-Fīl, rā' patrol.** Read all five ayat and place every rā':

> تَرَ (heavy — fatḥa) · رَبُّكَ (heavy — fatḥa) · وَأَرْسَلَ (heavy — sākinah after fatḥa) · طَيْرًا (heavy — fatḥa) · تَرْمِيهِم (heavy — sākinah after fatḥa) · بِحِجَارَةٍ (heavy — fatḥa)

Six rā's in the surah proper, **all heavy**. Then Sūrat al-Falaq for contrast: بِرَبِّ heavy, then شَرِّ light four times over. **Two short surahs, opposite answers.** (The basmalah at the head of each carries ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ, heavy both times, in every surah — count it separately or you will lose track.)

## Listen-for

**1. A heavy letter BEFORE the rā' pulling it heavy.**
- *Listen for:* وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ read with a heavy rā'. This is the priority error of the lesson.
- *Why it happens:* she has just learned that isti'lā' letters affect the rā', and "affect" has been heard as "anywhere in the word". Lesson 3-07 also spent a session on heaviness bleeding from neighbours, which primes exactly this.
- *Correction cue:* "Which side is the غ on?" Make her point at the page rather than answer from memory.

**2. Treating a hamzat-waṣl kasra as a real one.**
- *Listen for:* ٱرْجِعِىٓ read light.
- *Why it happens:* the kasra is printed and looks like any other. Nothing on the page says it is temporary.
- *Correction cue:* "Join it to the word before. Where did the kasra go?"

**3. Extending the five-word list by analogy.**
- *Listen for:* any sākin rā' after a kasra being read heavy because there is a heavy letter somewhere later in the word.
- *Why it happens:* she has learned the *rule* behind the five words and is applying it, which is exactly what you told her not to do. It is an intelligent error.
- *Correction cue:* "Is it on the card? Then it is light." Five words, closed.

**4. Applying the waṣl ruling at waqf, or the reverse.**
- *Listen for:* ٱلْقَدْرِ read light when she stops on it, or heavy when she reads on.
- *Why it happens:* she decided the ruling when she saw the word rather than when she decided her stop. The decision has to come first.
- *Correction cue:* "Am I stopping here?" — asked **before** the word. Make it a spoken habit for a fortnight.

**5. Correcting someone who chose the other wajh.**
- *Listen for:* her flagging a reciter's فِرْقٍ or مِصْرْ as a mistake.
- *Why it happens:* everything else in the course has one right answer, so she has generalised that too.
- *Correction cue:* "Is that one of the three?" Both are transmitted. Nobody is wrong.

**6. Switching wajh mid-recitation.**
- *Listen for:* the same word read two ways in one sitting.
- *Why it happens:* she has heard "both are allowed" as "it does not matter".
- *Correction cue:* "Pick one and hold it. Both permitted is not the same as either at random."

**7. Trilling, still.**
- *Listen for:* extra taps, especially on the heavy sākin rā's of تَرْمِيهِم and وَأَرْسَلَ, where the tongue is already braced.
- *Why it happens:* unchanged from last lesson, and a sākin rā' gives the tongue more time to roll than a vowelled one.
- *Correction cue:* "One tap. Press harder." Record and count.

**8. Losing the whole family under load.**
- *Listen for:* correct answers in the drill and random answers in continuous reading.
- *Why it happens:* eight conditions is more than working memory holds. This is expected, not a failure.
- *Correction cue:* Go back to the two lines. "Does the rā' have its own vowel? Then use it. No? Then look at the one before." Everything else is a footnote she can consult.

## Hifz for this session

**Manzil — al-Fātiḥa**, on rotation.
**Sabqi — an-Nās** (whole) and **al-Falaq 113:1–113:2.** Ten dedicated repetitions on any corrected word.

**New Sabaq — al-Falaq 113:3.**

> وَمِن شَرِّ غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ

One ayah, and it is the longest in the surah. Twenty to forty repetitions from the page.

Three things from earlier lessons converge in this one ayah, which makes it worth slowing down on:

- **شَرِّ** — light doubled rā', second of four. Same as last session. If it drifted heavy over the week, that is where the ten extra repetitions go.
- **غَاسِقٍ** — lesson 3-07's **level 1** tafkhīm, and the clearest inherited **heavy alif** she owns. The غ is heavy; the alif after it is heavy because of that and for no other reason.
- **وَقَبَ** — a light ب at the end, and if she stops on the ayah, a **kubrā bounce** (lesson 3-06): وَقَبْ.

## Homework

**25–35 min/day, five days.**

1. **Ten seconds — Drill 1.** The two lines, out loud, before anything.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 2.** Name the preceding vowel *before* reading the word. Every word, every time.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 3.** Which side is the heavy letter on? Point at it.
4. **One minute — Drill 4, the five-word card.** Twice a day. Recognise, do not derive. Then the sixth-that-isn't.
5. **One minute — Drill 5.** The hamzat-waṣl test.
6. **Three minutes — Drill 6.** The same word joined and stopped. Ask "am I stopping?" out loud first.
7. **One minute — Drill 7.** Both faces, then pick one.
8. **Three minutes — Drill 8, mixed cold**, and **Drill 9**, al-Fīl then al-Falaq.
9. **Hifz — Manzil (al-Fātiḥa), Sabqi (an-Nās + 113:1–113:2), then new Sabaq (113:3).**

**Bring to the next session:** the five-word card, said from memory, and this answered — why is the list five words and not six? Next session is the lām of the Divine Name, which is the same idea as the rā' with **one** condition instead of eight.
