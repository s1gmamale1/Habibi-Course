# Lesson 3-13 — Lām shamsiyyah and qamariyyah, and the lām of the verb

## Objectives

- Student can read the definite article correctly **from the mushaf's own marking** — sukūn means pronounce it, shaddah on the next letter means merge it — without recalling any letter list.
- Student can produce a solar lām as a **fully held doubled letter**, not as a dropped one, and can hear the difference between *ar-raḥmān* and a single-ر *a-raḥmān*.
- Student can recite the lunar mnemonic **ابْغِ حَجَّكَ وَخَفْ عَقِيمَهُ** and take the solar letters by elimination.
- Student can say what happens to the lām of a **verb** — pronounced clearly, except before ل and ر.

## Hook

Write two words and ask her to read them:

> **ٱلشَّمْس** — the sun · **ٱلْقَمَر** — the moon

Almost everyone reads the first one *al-shams*. It is *ash-shams*. The lām is not weak, not soft, not reduced: **it is not there at all.** It has vanished into the ش, which is why the ش carries a shaddah.

Say it wrong, then right, then say:

> **Reading the lām in ٱلرَّحْمَٰن identifies you as untrained within three words. And you have said ٱلرَّحْمَٰن more times than any other word in your life.**

Then the second error, which is the more interesting one and much more common than the first at her level:

> **Merging the lām but not HOLDING the result.** She drops the ل correctly and then reads ٱلرَّحْمَٰن with a single, short ر. That is not a merge — that is a deletion. **The lām has to be heard, as extra length on the following letter.** Two counts, exactly as in lesson 2-05.

And the reassurance, given immediately, because this lesson looks like it involves memorising twenty-eight letters and does not:

> **The mushaf tells you outright, every time. You will almost never need the lists.**

## What they already know

- **Lesson 1-14 already showed her this rule in action**, in the hardest possible word: she was told that in **ٱلضَّآلِّينَ** the ل of الـ assimilated into the ض, so *al-* + *ḍāllīn* becomes *aḍ-ḍāllīn*, and told to hold it for two full counts. **Lesson 2-05 said the same thing about ٱلصَّمَدُ and ٱلنَّاسِ** and deferred the system to "Unit 3". This is that lesson. Start from those three words — she has been performing this rule correctly for a year in at least three places.
- **Lesson 2-05** gave her the two-count hold and the pinch test for a doubled ن. Both are used unchanged today.
- **Lesson 2-09** taught her **hamzat al-waṣl** — the *a* of *al* that is pronounced when you begin on the word and drops when you join. Every example today rests on that.
- **Lesson 3-12, last session,** was the *other* lām family. Draw the line again at the top of the session: **that rule asked heavy or light; this one asks pronounced or merged.** Different question entirely, and the lām here is always light when it is pronounced at all.
- **Lesson 3-05** established that only ق ط ب ج د bounce. A sākin lunar lām is released cleanly and **does not bounce** — that contrast is one of today's checks.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Start from the mushaf, not from the lists. This is the whole practical rule.**

   | What you see | What it means |
   |---|---|
   | The ل carries a **sukūn** ( ـْ ) | **Qamariyyah** — pronounce it |
   | The ل carries **no sukūn**, and the **next letter carries a shaddah** | **Shamsiyyah** — merge it |

   Show her both on the page: **ٱلْحَمْدُ** with its little sukūn, and **ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ** with the bare lām and the shaddah'd ر. Then say: **you never have to recall a list while reading.** The lists are for understanding why, and for the rare unpointed text.

2. **Solar — al-lām ash-shamsiyyah.**

   When **ٱلـ** is followed by one of fourteen letters, the **ل is not pronounced at all.** It merges completely into the following letter, which doubles and carries a shaddah. Its technical name is **إِدْغَامٌ شَمْسِيّ** — solar assimilation.

   "Solar" is not a description of the letters; it is a mnemonic. The word for *sun*, **ٱلشَّمْس**, happens to begin with one and demonstrates the rule while naming it. Its partner is **ٱلْقَمَر**, *the moon*.

   The fourteen: **ط ث ص ر ت ض ذ ن د س ظ ز ش ل**

   Faster than the list, and worth giving her: **the sun letters are the ones made at or near the tongue — which is where the ل is made too.** Assimilation happens because the two articulations are already neighbours. She has the tongue map from lesson 3-01; this is a consequence of it.

3. **Lunar — al-lām al-qamariyyah.**

   When **ٱلـ** is followed by one of the other fourteen, the **ل is pronounced clearly**, with a sukūn, and nothing merges. Its technical name is **إِظْهَارٌ قَمَرِيّ** — lunar clarity.

   The mnemonic is one short phrase whose fourteen letters **are** the list:

   > **ابْغِ حَجَّكَ وَخَفْ عَقِيمَهُ**
   > *"Seek your ḥajj, and fear its barrenness."*
   > ا · ب · غ · ح · ج · ك · و · خ · ف · ع · ق · ي · م · هـ

   **Memorise this one and take the solar letters by elimination.** It is one phrase rather than fourteen separate words, so it is far easier than the solar mnemonic — which is why most teachers do it in this direction. (The solar list also has a classical verse, from Tuḥfat al-Aṭfāl: **طِبْ ثُمَّ صِلْ رُحْمًا تَفُزْ ضِفْ ذَا نِعَمْ ⁕ دَعْ سُوءَ ظَنٍّ زُرْ شَرِيفًا لِلْكَرَمْ.** Show it to her once so a book quoting it does not stop her, then set it aside.)

4. **How to perform the solar lām, in three steps.**
   1. Say the **hamzat waṣl** — the *a* of *al* — if you are starting on the word. If you are joining from the previous word, it drops.
   2. **Skip the ل entirely.** Do not touch the gum ridge; do not let a trace of an *l* out.
   3. Go straight to the following letter and **hold it doubled, for the full measure of a shaddah.**

   > **ٱلشَّمْس → *ash-shams*, never *al-shams***
   > **ٱلرَّحْمَٰن → *ar-raḥmān*, never *al-raḥmān***

   The assimilation is complete and **without a hum** in thirteen of the fourteen cases. The exception is **ن**: ٱلنَّاسِ carries a ghunnah — but that hum belongs to the **doubled ن itself**, not to any surviving trace of the ل. Say that distinction; it stops her adding a hum to the other thirteen.

5. **How to perform the lunar lām, in three steps.**
   1. Say the hamzat waṣl if you are starting on the word.
   2. **Sound the ل fully** — tongue tip on the gum ridge behind the upper teeth, clear and **light**. Every lām outside the Divine Name is light, from last session.
   3. **Release it cleanly into the next letter.** No vowel after it, no pause, and **no bounce** — the ل is sākin, but it is not a qalqalah letter.

   > **ٱلْحَمْدُ → *al-ḥamd*** · **ٱلْقَمَر → *al-qamar***

6. **The best single drill line in the Qur'an for this rule.**

   > **وَٱلشَّفْعِ وَٱلْوَتْرِ** (89:3)

   One solar lām and one lunar lām, side by side, in five syllables, in the same ayah. Read it slowly, then at pace. If she can do this line she can do the rule.

   Then the pair that is closest to home: **ٱلشِّتَآءِ وَٱلصَّيْفِ** (106:2) — **two solar lāms in one breath**, in a surah she will memorise. And al-Fātiḥah alone contains four solar lāms (ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ, ٱلرَّحِيمِ, ٱلدِّينِ, ٱلَّذِينَ) alongside three lunar ones (ٱلْحَمْدُ, ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ, ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ), which makes it the natural first drill page.

7. **The lām of the verb — lām al-fiʿl.**

   Classical works divide the lāms of Arabic into several kinds. She has now met two — the lām of the definite article and the lām of the Divine Name. **The third she needs is the lām that occurs in a verb**, carrying a sukūn.

   **The rule: pronounce it clearly. It does not merge.**

   | Word | Ref | Reading |
   |---|---|---|
   | قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ | 112:1 | clear ل |
   | قُلْ أَعُوذُ | 113:1 | clear ل |
   | قُلْ يَٰٓأَيُّهَا | 109:1 | clear ل |
   | أَلَمْ يَجْعَلْ | 105:2 | clear ل, word-final |
   | فَلْيَعْبُدُوا۟ رَبَّ | 106:3 | clear ل — the lām of command |

   **The one exception: before ل or ر, it merges**, exactly as the article does.

   > **وَقُل رَّبِّ** (23:118) → *wa-qur-rabbi* — *reference example, outside your memorized surahs*
   > **قُل لَّآ أَسْـَٔلُكُمْ** (42:23) → *qul-lā* — *reference example, outside your memorized surahs*

   Note that the mushaf marks these the same way it marks a solar lām: no sukūn on the ل, shaddah on the following letter. **Same visual cue, so she does not need a new habit.**

   And the check she already has: **a clear sākin ل is released, not bounced.** قُلْ ends with a clean ل, not a kick. Only ق ط ب ج د bounce.

8. **Draw the whole lām picture once, at the end, and leave it on the board.**

   | Which lām | The question | The answer |
   |---|---|---|
   | of **ٱللَّه** | heavy or light? | fatḥa/ḍamma → heavy; kasra → light |
   | of **ٱلـ** | pronounced or merged? | the mushaf's sukūn / shaddah decides |
   | of a **verb** | pronounced or merged? | pronounced, except before ل and ر |
   | **every other lām** | — | always light, always pronounced |

   Say the sentence that keeps the two families apart: **the heavy/light question only ever concerns one word in the language. Every lām in this table except the first one is light.**

9. **One honest note, and this time it is a positive one.**
   **Lām shamsiyyah is one of the few rules in this whole area that the app's mushaf highlighting actually covers.** The dataset annotates it, and it is coloured **grey** — the same grey used for silent letters. That is deliberate and exactly right: **the solar lām *is* a silent letter.** Grey says "written but not sounded", which is the instruction.

   The lunar lām has no colour, because there is nothing to mark — the ل is simply read as written. So the highlighting will show her the merges and stay quiet on the rest, which is the useful way round.

**Teacher note on ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ.** Three separate rules land on this one word and she now knows all three: the lām **merges** (today), the doubled ر is **heavy** because it carries a fatḥa (lesson 3-10), and it sits in the basmalah where the preceding **lām jalālah is light** (lesson 3-12). Read بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ slowly and name all three. **Do not let her conclude that the ر is heavy because it merged.** The merge and the heaviness are independent.

**Teacher note on ٱلَّذِينَ.** Lām into lām is the case that produces a stutter. It is **one held ل**, two counts, not two lāms with a seam between them. It occurs four times in al-Fātiḥah's neighbourhood and constantly everywhere else.

**Teacher note on ٱلنَّاسِ.** The one solar letter that hums. She has been humming it since lesson 2-05, so the sound is already right — what is new is knowing that the hum belongs to the **ن**, not to the vanished ل. Test with the pinch, then do ٱلدِّينِ and ٱلشِّتَآءِ with the same pinch and confirm nothing changes.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — read the marking, not the letter.** For each word, say "sukūn — pronounce" or "shaddah — merge" **before** reading it:

ٱلْحَمْدُ · ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · ٱلْفَلَقِ · ٱلدِّينِ · ٱلنَّاسِ · ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ · ٱلصِّرَٰطَ · ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · ٱلشِّتَآءِ · ٱلْبَيْتِ

**Drill 2 — the lunar mnemonic.** Say the phrase as language, three times, then extract its fourteen letters:

> **ابْغِ حَجَّكَ وَخَفْ عَقِيمَهُ**

Then say the rule: **anything not in this phrase is solar.**

**Drill 3 — wrong, then right.** Say each one deliberately wrong, then correctly. Naming the error aloud is what makes it audible later:

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| *al-raḥmān* | *ar-raḥmān* |
| *al-shams* | *ash-shams* |
| *al-nās* | *an-nās* |
| *al-dīn* | *ad-dīn* |
| *aq-qāri'ah* | *al-qāri'ah* |
| *al-ḥamd* | *al-ḥamd* — already right |

The last two are reversed on purpose: over-applying the solar rule is the error that arrives once the rule is learned.

**Drill 4 — hold the merge.** Two counts on the doubled letter, tapped out. A merge that is not held is a deletion:

ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · ٱلدِّينِ · ٱلنَّاسِ · ٱلصِّرَٰطَ · ٱلشِّتَآءِ · وَٱلصَّيْفِ · ٱلَّذِينَ

**Drill 5 — the side-by-side line.** Slowly, then at pace:

> **وَٱلشَّفْعِ وَٱلْوَتْرِ** (89:3)

Then: **ٱلشِّتَآءِ وَٱلصَّيْفِ** (106:2) — two solar lāms in one breath.

**Drill 6 — al-Fātiḥah, lām patrol.** Read all seven ayat and call each lām before you say it:

> ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ (solar) · ٱلرَّحِيمِ (solar) · ٱلْحَمْدُ (lunar) · ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ (lunar) · ٱلدِّينِ (solar) · ٱلصِّرَٰطَ (solar) · ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ (lunar) · ٱلَّذِينَ (solar) · ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ (lunar) · ٱلضَّآلِّينَ (solar)

**Drill 7 — the pinch test.** Only ن hums. Pinch the nose on each:

ٱلنَّاسِ — blocked · ٱلدِّينِ — unchanged · ٱلشِّتَآءِ — unchanged · ٱلصِّرَٰطَ — unchanged · ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ — unchanged on the ر

**Drill 8 — the lām of the verb.** Clear, light, released, and **not bounced**:

قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ (112:1) · قُلْ أَعُوذُ (113:1) · قُلْ يَٰٓأَيُّهَا (109:1) · أَلَمْ يَجْعَلْ (105:2) · فَلْيَعْبُدُوا۟ رَبَّ (106:3)

Then the exception, twice: **وَقُل رَّبِّ** (23:118).

**Drill 9 — the four lāms, cold.** For each, say which lām it is and what its rule asks:

بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ · ٱلْحَمْدُ · قُلْ · ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · لِلَّهِ · يَجْعَلْ · ٱلْفَلَقِ · نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ

Two possible questions — *heavy or light?* and *pronounced or merged?* — and she must name which one applies before answering it.

## Listen-for

**1. Pronouncing the solar lām.**
- *Listen for:* *al-raḥmān*, *al-shams*, *al-nās*. It identifies an untrained reader within three words.
- *Why it happens:* the ل is printed and the eye reads what is printed. Nothing in a Latin-script reading habit says a written letter can be silent.
- *Correction cue:* "Is there a sukūn on it? No — the shaddah is on the ر. So the ل is not there." Point at the marking rather than explaining.

**2. Merging without holding — the priority error at her level.**
- *Listen for:* a single short ر in ٱلرَّحْمَٰن. She dropped the ل and did not put anything in its place.
- *Why it happens:* "the lām is not pronounced" has been heard as "the lām is deleted". The lām is *transferred*, into the length of the next letter.
- *Correction cue:* "Where did the ل go? Into the ر. So the ر has to be twice as long." Tap two beats. Same instruction as lesson 2-05.

**3. Over-applying the solar rule.**
- *Listen for:* *aq-qāri'ah* for ٱلْقَارِعَةُ, *af-falaq* for ٱلْفَلَقِ. Expect this the week after this lesson, not during it.
- *Why it happens:* the merge is the new, interesting rule, and new rules generalise.
- *Correction cue:* "Look at the ل. Sukūn?" Then Drill 1.

**4. A vowel or a pause between the article and the letter.**
- *Listen for:* *ala-ḥamd*, *ala-falaq*, or a tiny gap after a lunar ل.
- *Why it happens:* releasing a sākin letter into another consonant is the cluster problem from lesson 2-03, unchanged.
- *Correction cue:* "Straight through. No door between them."

**5. Stuttering the lām into lām.**
- *Listen for:* ٱلَّذِينَ read as *al-ladhīn* with a seam.
- *Why it happens:* she knows there are two lāms and produces two.
- *Correction cue:* "One ل, held twice as long. Same as any other shadda."

**6. Adding a hum to the wrong sun letters.**
- *Listen for:* a nasal trace on ٱلدِّينِ or ٱلصِّرَٰطَ.
- *Why it happens:* ٱلنَّاسِ hums and is the most-heard example, so the hum gets attributed to the merge rather than to the ن.
- *Correction cue:* Pinch test, Drill 7. "Only ن. The hum is the ن's, not the lām's."

**7. Bouncing the sākin lunar lām.**
- *Listen for:* a kick on the ل of ٱلْحَمْدُ or ٱلْقَارِعَةُ, and on the ل of قُلْ.
- *Why it happens:* left over from lessons 3-05 and 3-06, and a sākin lām sits in exactly the position where she has been bouncing things.
- *Correction cue:* "Only ق ط ب ج د. Can you hold the ل? Then there is nothing blocked to bounce."

**8. Heavying the lām before a heavy letter.**
- *Listen for:* the ل of ٱلْقَارِعَةُ, ٱلْقَدْرِ, ٱلطَّامَّة going velar.
- *Why it happens:* contamination, plus the English dark *l*. Last session's material, and it will keep coming back.
- *Correction cue:* "*leaf*, not *feel*. And the ق is heavy; the ل is not."

**9. Concluding that a merged letter is heavy because it merged.**
- *Listen for:* her explaining ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ's heaviness as a consequence of the merge.
- *Why it happens:* the two rules land on one word in the phrase she says most often.
- *Correction cue:* "The ر is heavy because it has a fatḥa. It would be heavy without the article. Say رَبِّ — heavy, no article at all."

## Hifz for this session

**Manzil — al-Fātiḥa**, on rotation.
**Sabqi — an-Nās** (whole) and **al-Falaq 113:1–113:4.** Ten dedicated repetitions on any corrected word.

**New Sabaq — al-Falaq 113:5.**

> وَمِن شَرِّ حَاسِدٍ إِذَا حَسَدَ

Twenty to forty repetitions from the page. With this ayah **Sūrat al-Falaq is complete** — have her recite all five ayat unaided at the end of the session and mark the date.

**She now holds three surahs: al-Fātiḥa, an-Nās and al-Falaq — eighteen ayat.** That is the whole of this unit's memorisation slice. Take a moment on it.

Points to hold in this ayah:
- **شَرِّ** — light doubled rā', **fourth and last time** in the surah. Four for four is the target.
- **حَاسِدٍ** — a light ح, a **light alif** (inherited from the ح), and a light س. Nothing heavy in the word at all; a good reset after a session on merges.
- **حَسَدَ** — and at waqf, **حَسَدْ**, with a **kubrā bounce on the د** (lesson 3-06).
- The **basmalah** at the head of the surah, with today's solar lām in ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ and ٱلرَّحِيمِ, and last session's light lām jalālah. Three rules in five words, every time she starts.

**Promotion check.** If an-Nās has now run clean for three consecutive sessions, promote it from Sabqi to Manzil and put it into the two-surahs-per-day rotation alongside al-Fātiḥa. Remember that the three-session threshold is the course's own default rather than a sourced number — if a promoted surah starts decaying, raise it and say so.
