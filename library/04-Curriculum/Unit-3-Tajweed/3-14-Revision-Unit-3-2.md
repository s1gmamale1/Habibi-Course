# Lesson 3-14 — Revision and Q&A: Unit 3.2

> **No new rule today, and no new memorisation.** Sabqi and Manzil run as normal; only the new Sabaq is suspended, so the memorisation muscle rests for one session while retrieval does the work.
>
> Unit 3.2 was four sessions and it contained, by some margin, the most conditional material in the course so far. The purpose today is to compress it back down to something she can hold in her head at speed.

## Objectives

- Student can state the **whole rā' family in two lines** and the **whole lām jalālah rule in one**, without notes.
- Student can decide any rā' or lām on sight, out of order, with no cue about which rule is being tested.
- Student can name the three cases of **jawāz al-wajhayn** and say why they are not errors.
- Student leaves with a written two- or three-item list of what still needs work before Unit 3.3.

## Hook

Do not review. **Test, cold, in the first three minutes.**

Put one line in front of her — the line she says more often than any other — and ask her to read it slowly and then name every rule that fires in it.

> **بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ** (1:1)

There are **five** rulings in five words, and she now knows all of them:

| Item | Rule | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| the ل of ٱللَّهِ | lām jalālah (3-12) | **light** — kasra on بِسْمِ |
| the ل of ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ | solar lām (3-13) | **merged** into the ر |
| the ر of ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ | rā' (3-10) | **heavy** — doubled with a fatḥa |
| the ل of ٱلرَّحِيمِ | solar lām (3-13) | **merged** |
| the ر of ٱلرَّحِيمِ | rā' (3-10) | **heavy** — doubled with a fatḥa |

Then the framing:

> **You have said this line thousands of times. Until four sessions ago you could not have told me a single thing about it. That is what this unit was for — and the test is not whether you know the rules, it is whether you can find them without being told to look.**

## What they already know

Name the shape of the unit out loud, because four sessions of conditions need a skeleton:

> **Two letters in the entire language switch between heavy and light: ر and the ل of ٱللَّه.**
> **The rā' has about eight conditions, a five-word exception list, a waṣl/waqf split and three ambiguous words.**
> **The lām jalālah has one condition and nothing else.**
> **And there is a second, completely separate lām family that asks a different question — pronounced or merged — which the mushaf answers for you on the page.**

Then where it goes: **Unit 3.3 is ghunnah, and Unit 3.4 is the nūn sākinah rules.** The isti'lā' set from lesson 3-03 comes back there, deciding whether the two-count hum of ikhfā' is heavy or light. Nothing in this unit was a dead end.

## Part A — the mixed drill

Interleaved on purpose. **Do not group by rule.** Grouping makes a student look competent in the session and fail a week later.

**Drill 1 — the two lines and the one line, from memory.**

> **"A vowelled rā' takes its own vowel. A sākin rā' takes the vowel before it."**
> **"Lām jalālah: fatḥa or ḍamma before it, heavy; kasra, light; starting on it, heavy."**

If she cannot produce these three sentences cold, everything else today is guesswork. Fix them first.

**Drill 2 — rā', mixed and cold.** Read and rule. No warning, no grouping:

تَرَ · شَرِّ · نَصْرُ · رِحْلَةَ · لِرَبِّكَ · صُدُورِ · تَرْمِيهِم · وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ · ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · وَأَرْسَلَ · رِزْقَهُۥ · بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ

*Watch for:* صُدُورِ and وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ read heavy. Those two are the diagnostic items.

**Drill 3 — which side is the heavy letter on?** Point at the isti'lā' letter and say "before" or "after":

وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ (غ — ?) · قِرْطَاسٍ (ط — ?) · مِرْصَادًا (ص — ?) · رِزْقَهُۥ (ق — ?) · وَفِرْعَوْنَ (— none)

**Drill 4 — the five-word card, from memory.**

> **قِرْطَاسٍ · إِرْصَادًا · فِرْقَةٍ · مِرْصَادًا · لَبِٱلْمِرْصَادِ**

Then: **فِرْقٍ — the sixth that is not. Both readings; tarqīq preferred.**

**Drill 5 — am I stopping?** Read each twice, joined and stopped, and make the two audibly different:

ٱلْقَدْرِ / ٱلْقَدْرْ · وَٱلْعَصْرِ / وَٱلْعَصْرْ · خَيْرٌ / خَيْرْ · ٱلْأَبْتَرُ / ٱلْأَبْتَرْ

**Ask the question out loud before every one.**

**Drill 6 — lām jalālah, cold.** Say the preceding vowel first, then read:

بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ · نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ · دِينِ ٱللَّهِ · هُوَ ٱللَّهُ · لِلَّهِ · ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ · نَارُ ٱللَّهِ

**Drill 7 — solar or lunar, from the marking.** Call it before reading:

ٱلْحَمْدُ · ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · ٱلْفَلَقِ · ٱلدِّينِ · ٱلنَّاسِ · ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ · ٱلصِّرَٰطَ · ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · ٱلشِّتَآءِ · ٱلْبَيْتِ · ٱلَّذِينَ · ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ

**Drill 8 — which question am I being asked?** For each item she must name the **question** before the answer — *heavy or light?* or *pronounced or merged?*:

بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ · ٱلْحَمْدُ · شَرِّ · ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · قُلْ · نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ · يَجْعَلْ · ٱلْفَلَقِ · صُدُورِ · لِلَّهِ

This is the highest-value drill of the session. Nearly every error left in this unit is a wrong-question error, not a wrong-answer one.

**Drill 9 — the whole-surah sweep.** Read each straight through and mark every rā' and every lām:

> **Sūrat al-Falaq** — بِرَبِّ heavy, شَرِّ light ×4; ٱلْفَلَقِ lunar, ٱلْعُقَدِ lunar.
> **Sūrat al-Fīl** — six heavy rā's in the surah proper, no light ones; ٱلْفِيلِ lunar.
> **Sūrat an-Naṣr** — نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ heavy lām, دِينِ ٱللَّهِ light lām, وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ light rā', وَٱلْفَتْحُ lunar, ٱلنَّاسَ solar.

An-Naṣr is the single densest page in this unit's material: three ayat, both lām jalālah rulings, the trap rā', and both lām families.

**Drill 10 — the cold basmalah.** Say all five rulings in بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ without notes, then read it.

## Part B — troubleshooting: the questions that actually come up

---

### "There are so many rā' conditions. Do I really have to hold all of them?"

No, and holding all of them is the wrong goal. **Hold two sentences and one card.**

> **A vowelled rā' takes its own vowel: fatḥa or ḍamma heavy, kasra light.**
> **A sākin rā' takes the vowel before it: fatḥa or ḍamma heavy, original kasra light.**

Then two qualifiers hang off the second sentence:
- the kasra must be **original** — a hamzat-waṣl kasra does not count;
- a **following** isti'lā' letter in the same word overrides and makes it heavy, in **five words**, which are on a card.

Everything else in the family — the waqf shapes, the shadda cases — is those two sentences applied to a rā' that has just become sākin because you stopped. It is not extra material.

**The count of conditions is a bookkeeping artefact.** Some works give six conditions, some eight, some nine, and they all describe the identical recitation. They are grouping the same facts differently. Do not memorise a numbered list.

---

### "Why does the غ in وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ not make the rā' heavy? It's right there in the word."

Because it is on the **wrong side**.

The rule is about a heavy letter **following** the rā', not preceding it. Mechanically: the tongue has to still be raised **when it makes the rā'**, and a heavy letter that has already been and gone does not keep it raised — the letters in between have brought it down.

> **وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ** — غ … then ر. The غ is finished. **Light.**
> **قِرْطَاسٍ** — ر … then ط. The tongue is already rising for the ط while the rā' is being made. **Heavy.**

Do the physical test: say both words slowly and notice where your tongue is at the moment the rā' happens.

---

### "How is this different from lesson 3-07, where a heavy letter contaminated its neighbours?"

Good question, and the distinction matters.

- **Lesson 3-07 was about an ERROR.** Heaviness bleeding onto ع, ا and م in طَعَامِ is something to *stop doing*. Those letters are light and the bleed is a fault.
- **This is a RULE.** The rā' is *supposed* to change. It is one of two letters in the language licensed to do so.

So: when a heavy letter darkens a ل or an alif, **fix it.** When a following heavy letter makes a sākin rā' heavy in one of five words, **that is correct.** They look similar and they are opposite.

---

### "Is ٱلْقَدْرِ heavy or light? I keep getting told both."

Both, and neither answer is complete without saying what you are doing.

> **Reading on:** the rā' has a **kasra**. Light.
> **Stopping on it:** the kasra drops, the rā' becomes sākin, and it now sits after a sākin د which sits after a fatḥa. **Heavy.**

The word did not change. **Your stop did.**

This is the same structure as qalqalah kubrā in lesson 3-06, and it is worth noticing that it has now come up twice in different families: **stopping changes the word.** That is a general fact about recitation rather than a quirk of these two rules, and she will meet it again in the madd rules.

**The habit to build:** ask **"am I stopping here?"** *before* you reach the word, not while you are in it. A decision made mid-word arrives too late to change the sound.

---

### "Someone I listen to reads فِرْقٍ heavy. Are they wrong?"

**No.** فِرْقٍ is one of exactly three cases where both readings are transmitted for Ḥafṣ and either is correct.

1. **فِرْقٍ (26:63).** It has the shape of the five exception words — sākin rā' after an original kasra, isti'lā' letter following — **but the ق itself carries a kasra**, which weakens its pull. Both readings; **tarqīq preferred**.
2. **مِصْرَ and ٱلْقِطْرِ, at waqf only.** Ambiguous only when you stop. **مِصْرْ: tafkhīm preferred** (original fatḥa). **ٱلْقِطْرْ: tarqīq preferred** (original kasra).
3. **Words with a deleted yā', at waqf** — يَسْرِ, فَأَسْرِ, نُذُرِ. Treat the missing yā' as present (light) or absent (heavy). **Tarqīq preferred.**

And this is also **why the exception list is five words and not six**: فِرْقٍ has the same structure and a different answer, so it was never on the list.

Two rules for using this: **pick one wajh and hold it for the whole recitation**, and **do not correct someone who chose the other one.**

---

### "The lām jalālah counts every kasra, but the rā' only counts original ones. Why the inconsistency?"

Because they are two different rules from two different chapters, and neither was designed with the other in mind. There is no unifying principle to find, and looking for one costs more than memorising the difference.

| | Temporary / connecting kasra |
|---|---|
| **rā'** | does **not** count — ٱرْجِعِىٓ is **heavy** |
| **lām jalālah** | **does** count — قُلِ ٱللَّهُمَّ is **light** |

Learn them as two facts. The rā' rule is the odd one out, and it is the one to flag in your notes.

---

### "I keep mixing up the two lām rules. How do I stop?"

Ask **which question** before you try to answer anything. There are only two:

| The lām is… | The question |
|---|---|
| in the word **ٱللَّه** | **heavy or light?** |
| the definite article **ٱلـ** | **pronounced or merged?** |
| in a **verb** | **pronounced or merged?** (pronounced, except before ل and ر) |
| anywhere else | none — always light, always pronounced |

They can never both apply to the same lām, so if you have identified the lām correctly the question answers itself.

The other half of the fix: **the heavy/light question concerns exactly one word in the language.** Every lām in that table except the first is light. If you are wondering about the weight of a lām that is not in ٱللَّه, the answer is already light and you can stop wondering.

---

### "How do I know solar from lunar without memorising fourteen letters?"

**You do not need the letters. The mushaf tells you.**

> **Sukūn on the ل → pronounce it.**
> **No sukūn on the ل, shaddah on the next letter → merge it.**

That is a visual check, it takes no recall, and it is right every time.

Learn **ابْغِ حَجَّكَ وَخَفْ عَقِيمَهُ** — one short phrase, fourteen lunar letters — for understanding and for unpointed text, and take the solar letters by elimination. Do not try to memorise both lists.

If you want the reason behind the split rather than the list: **the solar letters are made at or near the tongue, which is where the ل is made.** Neighbouring articulations assimilate. You have the tongue map from lesson 3-01; the split falls straight out of it.

---

### "My merges sound right but you keep stopping me on ٱلرَّحْمَٰن. What is wrong with it?"

You are dropping the ل and not putting anything in its place. That is a deletion, not a merge.

**The lām is transferred into the length of the next letter.** ٱلرَّحْمَٰن has a **doubled** ر — two full counts — and if it comes out as one short ر, the ل has simply gone missing.

This is lesson 2-05's instruction, unchanged: **hold the shadda for two counts.** Tap the beats. It is the most common merge error at your level and it is completely invisible to the person making it, because the wrong version *feels* like it obeyed the rule.

---

### "Which of my mistakes should I actually be worrying about now?"

Keep it to three. At the end of Unit 3.2 the ranking is almost always:

1. **بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ read with a heavy lām.** Highest frequency by an enormous margin, in the phrase you say most.
2. **Heavy rā' by default** — رِحْلَةَ, رِزْقَهُۥ, شَرِّ, صُدُورِ.
3. **A merge that is not held** — ٱلرَّحْمَٰن with a single short ر.

Everything else — the five-word card, the jawāz cases, the waqf shapes — is recognition material, and recognition material does not decay the way habits do.

---

### "Can I hear any of this in my own recitation yet?"

More than at the end of Unit 3.1, and still not enough on your own. The three habits stand:

1. **Record, then listen back two days later.** Delay breaks the loop between what you intended and what you hear.
2. **Use the tests that do not depend on the ear** — for this unit, that mostly means *counting taps* on the rā' in a recording, and *tapping the two beats* on a merge.
3. **Compare against a reciter one word at a time**, not one surah at a time.

And the honest boundary, again, because it is worth repeating rather than assuming: **this course teaches the rules and trains the ear. It cannot hear you.** Tajweed has always been received face to face from a qualified reciter. What it does is make your time with a teacher shorter and sharper — you arrive knowing the rules, able to hear the difference, and knowing which sounds your own language will trip you on.

## Hifz for this session

**No new Sabaq.** Say it explicitly, and say why: revision lessons pause new rules **and** new memorisation, so the memorisation muscle gets one session's rest. Nothing is being skipped.

**This session closes the unit's memorisation slice.** She now holds **al-Fātiḥa (7), an-Nās (6) and al-Falaq (5) — eighteen ayat.** Run all three, whole and unaided, and treat this as a checkpoint rather than a rehearsal.

**Manzil — al-Fātiḥa, and an-Nās if it has been promoted.** Two surahs per day on rotation covers the pool comfortably at this size.

**Sabqi — al-Falaq**, complete, and an-Nās if it is not yet promoted.

**Promotion check.** Three consecutive clean recitations promotes a surah from Sabqi to Manzil. Be honest that the number is the course's own default — the sources say "when it is solid" and stop there. Adjust from observation: if a promoted surah keeps decaying, raise the threshold to four or five and write down that you have done so.

**Score both axes separately and write both numbers down:**
- **Memorisation fidelity** — words omitted, added or transposed.
- **Tajweed** — everything in Units 3.1 and 3.2.

Clean recall with rough recitation and clean recitation with shaky recall need opposite remedies. A single combined score cannot tell you which one you are looking at, and at this point in the course knowing which is worth more than the score itself.
