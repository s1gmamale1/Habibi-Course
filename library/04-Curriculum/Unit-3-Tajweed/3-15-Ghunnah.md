# Lesson 3-15 — Ghunnah: the sound that lives in the nose

## Objectives

- Student can produce a genuine nasal ghunnah on نّ and مّ and prove it to herself with the nose-pinch test, without being told the answer.
- Student can hold that ghunnah for two counts at her own reciting tempo, and can hear when she has given it one.
- Student can pick every نّ and مّ out of a page of text on sight, and can say why a doubled ص or ل is *not* a ghunnah.
- Student can state, in one sentence, why this rule is being taught before all the noon and meem rules that follow.

## Hook

Start with a correction, not a definition. Ask her to recite al-Kāfirūn — or anything she has, it does not matter which. Then stop her, and say one sentence:

> "Say **إِنَّ** for me. Now pinch your nose and say it again."

Roughly nine students in ten will find the sound comes out unchanged. That is the error, and it is sitting in the middle of the highest-frequency word in the Qurʾān. She has been saying a long *n* with her mouth, and the sound is supposed to be coming out of her nose.

Then do it correctly in front of her: **إِنَّ** with the nostrils held. The sound stops dead, mid-word, as though someone unplugged it. **That silence is the proof.** If pinching does not stop it, there was never a ghunnah there.

Do not explain further yet. Let her fail the test twice more on **ٱلنَّاسِ** and **ثُمَّ** before you name anything. The whole lesson lands better once she has personally discovered that a sound she makes seventeen times a day in ṣalāh has been coming out of the wrong place.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 2-05** taught the shadda as *one letter written once and said twice*, and it already told her that when the doubled letter is نّ or مّ, the hold is nasal. She was given the pinch test there and told the rule "has a name and belongs to Unit 3". **This is that lesson.** Open by saying so — she is not learning something new, she is being handed the name and the timing for something she has been doing for months.
- **Lesson 2-05 also gave her the two-count hold** on doubled letters generally, with the finger-tap. The timing skill transfers exactly; only the *place* of the sound is new today.
- **Lesson 2-04's collision rule** — two closed letters cannot touch — is why the front half of a shadda is legal. Nothing about that changes.
- She has just finished **3-10 to 3-14**, the rāʾ and lām chapter, so she is used to a rule that turns on the *identity of a letter* rather than on a mark. Ghunnah is easier than that: two letters, one behaviour, no exceptions.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Say the ordering decision out loud, because it is a real departure.** Tuhfat al-Atfal puts the rulings of the noon sākinah in **chapter 1** and does not reach *an-nūn wal-mīm al-mushaddadatān* until **chapter 2**. So the classical text teaches idghām bi-ghunnah, iqlāb and ikhfāʾ — three rules whose entire content is "hold a nasal for two counts" — *before it has ever defined the nasal or the two counts*. Most teachers quietly fix that ordering at the whiteboard. **This course fixes it on the page, and this lesson is where the fix happens.** Tell her: *"Everything in the next eleven lessons is this sound, plus a condition. Learn the sound now and the rest is bookkeeping."*

2. **Locate it physically.** The ghunnah comes from the **khayshum** (الخَيْشُوم), the nasal cavity — the fifth makhraj zone alongside the jawf, the throat, the tongue and the lips. It is the only zone with no moving part. What moves is the soft palate: it drops, and air that was going out of the mouth goes out of the nose instead. She does not need to control that consciously. She needs to *notice* it, and the pinch is how.

3. **Name the two letters, and only these two.** ن and م. Ghunnah is an inseparable attribute (*sifah lāzimah*) of both — you cannot remove it, only lengthen or shorten it. Its **most complete** form, *akmal al-ghunnah*, is on a **ن or م carrying a shaddah**. That is today's rule, and it is unconditional:

   > **Any نّ or مّ — any word, any position, joined or stopped on — is held for 2 counts of ghunnah.**

   No exceptions. No letter list to memorise. This is the simplest rule in the entire unit and she should be told that.

4. **Quote the matn once.** Al-Jamzūrī names the letters for exactly this reason:

   > وَغُنَّ مِيمًا ثُمَّ نُونًا شُدِّدَا ⁕ وَسَمِّ كُلًّا حَرْفَ غُنَّةٍ بَدَا
   > *"Nasalise a meem, then a noon, that are doubled — and call each one 'the letter of ghunnah', plainly."*

   See [[Tuhfat-al-Atfal]]. Read it aloud, translate it, move on. It is a memory hook, not a text to study.

5. **The two counts — and what a "count" actually is.** Two *ḥarakāt*, at the tempo she is already reciting in. The classical measure is the time taken to fold two fingers at a moderate pace, and this course teaches that as a learning aid. **The truth underneath it is relative:** two counts means twice her own short-vowel duration, so a slow tartīl ghunnah is genuinely longer in seconds than a fast one, and both are correct. Scholars differ on whether the finger convention, a measured half-second to second, or a purely relative definition is the right account — say so once, then use the fingers, because a beginner needs a ruler more than she needs the philosophy of rulers. Full discussion in [[Ghunnah]].

6. **Drill the pinch to independence.** This is the step most worth your session minutes. Have her run **إِنَّ · أَنَّ · ثُمَّ · ٱلنَّاس** with the nose pinched, then unpinched, then pinched again, until she can predict which it will be *before* she pinches. The goal is not the pinch. The goal is that she stops needing it.

7. **Show the counter-example, or she will over-generalise.** Write **ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ** (112:2) — the ayah she is memorising this week. Two shaddas, a doubled ل and a doubled ص. **Neither is a ghunnah.** They are held for two counts as any shadda is, but nothing goes through the nose; pinch and they are unaffected. Then write **ٱلنَّاسِ** beside them. *"Shadda means hold. Shadda on ن or م means hold **and** nasal. Those are two different instructions and only one of them is today."*

8. **The levels, said briefly and once — this is insurance against a mistake she will make in lesson 3-20.** Ghunnah is not on/off; it has ranks:

   | Rank | Where | Prolonged? |
   |---|---|---|
   | 1 — most complete | **نّ / مّ mushaddad** (today) | 2 counts |
   | 2 — merged | idghām with ghunnah, idghām shafawī | 2 counts |
   | 3 — concealed | ikhfāʾ, ikhfāʾ shafawī, iqlāb | 2 counts |
   | 4 — plain sākin | ن / م sākinah with no rule acting | inherent only |
   | 5 — vowelled | ن / م carrying a vowel | inherent only |

   Ranks 4 and 5 still *have* the nasal — it cannot be removed — it is simply **not held**. Say this now and point at it again in three weeks: when she reaches iẓhār and is told "no ghunnah", she will be tempted to switch the nose off entirely, and that is a different error from the one you fixed today.

9. **Heaviness, in one sentence.** The ghunnah of a doubled ن or م is normally light. It picks up a little weight when a heavy neighbour is beside it. Do not drill this today; it becomes systematic in lesson 3-24, where the ghunnah takes its weight directly from the letter that follows.

10. **Show the colour.** On a Quranly-family coloured muṣḥaf, every نّ and مّ is warm orange, `#FF7E1E`. Hand her a coloured page and have her find the orange before she reads it. In [cpfair/quran-tajweed](https://github.com/cpfair/quran-tajweed) the matching key is `ghunnah` — see [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]]. This is the first rule in the course with a colour of its own, and finding it on the page is a real skill worth ten seconds.

|---|---|---|
| بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ | 114:1 | *bi-rab-bi n-nāās* — the رّ is a plain hold, the نّ is nasal. One line, both behaviours |
| إِنَّ شَانِئَكَ | 108:3 | *in-na* — the pinch word |
| حَمَّالَةَ | 111:4 | *ḥam-mā-lah* — a مّ, and it is her hifz portion in five lessons' time |
| إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ | 110:3 | *in-na-hū* — mid-ayah, where it gets rushed |
| عَمَّ | 78:1 | reference example — outside your memorised sūrahs. One syllable, nothing to hide behind |
| ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ | 112:2 | the counter-example: two shaddas, no ghunnah in either |

**Teacher note on بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ.** Use this as the first example rather than إِنَّ, because it puts a non-nasal shadda and a nasal shadda four letters apart in a line she recites daily. If she holds both the same way, you have found the error immediately and without a diagnostic.

**Teacher note on عَمَّ.** It is outside the hifz set, and that is the point of including it — a word with no memorised context around it to carry her. When the ghunnah is the whole word, it cannot be smuggled past you.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the pinch, ten words.** Say each one twice: once with your nostrils held shut, once free. If the pinched version sounds the same as the free one, the nasal is not happening. Do the word again before moving on.

إِنَّ · أَنَّ · ثُمَّ · ٱلنَّاس · جَنَّة · أُمَّة · مُحَمَّد · إِنَّا · لَمَّا · هُمَّ

**Drill 2 — two counts, on your fingers.** Fold two fingers at a steady pace while you hold the nasal. Start the fold as the hold starts; the letter is released as the second finger lands. Do not speed up:

إِنَّ · ٱلنَّاس · حَمَّالَة · إِنَّهُۥ · عَمَّ · ٱلْجَنَّة · مِمَّا · إِنَّمَا

**Drill 3 — sort them.** Read the row, and after each word say out loud either "**ghunnah**" or "**hold only**". Do not read the next word until you have said one of the two:

ٱلنَّاس · ٱلصَّمَد · حَمَّالَة · ٱللَّه · إِنَّ · ٱلْحَقّ · ثُمَّ · ٱلضَّآلِّين · أُمَّة · رَبِّ

**Drill 4 — inside the ayah, not the word list.** Recite these lines whole, at your own pace, and hold every نّ and مّ properly *in context*. This is harder than the word list and it is where the rule actually has to survive:

- قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ (114:1)
- إِنَّ شَانِئَكَ هُوَ ٱلْأَبْتَرُ (108:3)
- وَٱمْرَأَتُهُۥ حَمَّالَةَ ٱلْحَطَبِ (111:4)
- إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ تَوَّابًۢا (110:3)

**Drill 5 — stopping on it.** Say **ٱلنَّاسِ** as though it ends the ayah and you are stopping. The final vowel goes; **the ghunnah does not.** Full two counts, then silence. Repeat with **ٱلْجِنَّةِ**. This is the drill for the error in the next section.

**Drill 6 — find them on the page.** Open any page of the muṣḥaf. Circle every نّ and مّ in the first three lines before reading a word. Then read those three lines. Sixty seconds, and it converts the rule from something you apply to something you see.

## Listen-for

**1. An oral *n* or *m* — no nasal at all.**
- *Listen for:* nothing, on its own. This one is invisible by ear to a beginner and to plenty of teachers. **Use the pinch; do not trust listening.**
- *Why it happens:* Uzbek, Russian, Turkish and English all have *n* and *m*, and in none of them is a doubled nasal held nasally for two counts. She is producing her native consonant, correctly, in the wrong language.
- *Correction cue:* "Pinch and say it." Physical, instant, self-verifying — she corrects herself and you say nothing.

**2. Under-holding — the nasal is there but short.**
- *Listen for:* a hum you can hear but could not count. This is the priority error of the lesson and it is far more common than a missing nasal.
- *Why it happens:* exactly the same weakness as the under-held shadda in 2-05. Diagnose it as that, out loud, so she sees it is one habit and not two problems.
- *Correction cue:* Do not describe the length. Tap twice on the table and have her fit the hum between the taps.

**3. Over-holding — four or six counts.**
- *Listen for:* a hum that has become a note. Usually appears the session after you fix error 2, and often in students with a musical ear.
- *Why it happens:* over-correction, plus the pleasant fact that a long ghunnah sounds impressive.
- *Correction cue:* "That was a madd, not a ghunnah." Give her two taps again — the same tool fixes both directions.

**4. Dropping it at waqf — stopping on ٱلنَّاسِ with a short نّ.**
- *Listen for:* the ghunnah shrinking at the end of an ayah, where the vowel is dropped.
- *Why it happens:* she associates the two counts with the vowel that follows, so when the vowel goes she assumes the hold goes with it. It does not — the shadda is still on the letter.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 5. Then have her recite an-Nās to the end of ayah 1 and stop, five times.

**5. Nasalising every shadda.**
- *Listen for:* a hum on ٱلصَّمَدُ or ٱلضَّآلِّينَ. This is the over-generalisation, and it usually appears the same day the rule is taught.
- *Why it happens:* she has bound "shadda" to "hum" instead of binding "ن or م" to "hum".
- *Correction cue:* Drill 3, out loud, with the label spoken before the word. Naming forces the eye onto the letter rather than the mark.

**6. Nasalising a plain, unvowelled ن or م and calling it done.**
- *Listen for:* a two-count hum on the noon of `عَنْهُ` or the meem of `أَلَمْ`, where no rule has yet been taught.
- *Why it happens:* enthusiasm, and the reasonable assumption that if some nasals are held, all are.
- *Correction cue:* "Only the ones with a shadda, today." Point at the levels table. What she is doing wrong now is the *right* answer to lessons 3-16 and 3-24 — tell her that, because it is true and it keeps the enthusiasm.

## Hifz for this session

**Session order is always revision before new material.** Manzil and Sabqi first, then Sabaq. See [[Hifz-Method]].

- **Manzil (old).** Rotate two surahs from the pool she has already promoted — al-Fātiḥa and an-Nās if the standard order has been followed.
- **Sabqi (recent).** al-Falaq 113, all five ayat, recited whole. Listen for `مِن شَرِّ` — she will meet that rule properly in lesson 3-24 and it is worth *not* correcting today beyond the ghunnah.
- **New Sabaq — al-Ikhlāṣ 112:1–2.**

> قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝ ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ

Two short ayat, and they were chosen for this lesson deliberately: **112:2 contains two shaddas and not one ghunnah.** The new hifz portion *is* the counter-example from the teaching sequence. Have her recite it and then tell you, unprompted, how many ghunnahs are in it. The answer is zero, and getting to zero confidently is the skill.

20–40 repetitions within the session: three reads from the page with full attention to pronunciation, then look away and recite, then find the exact point of failure and repeat *that*, alternating until it holds. Ten extra repetitions on any error you catch — count them separately.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **Two minutes — Drill 1, the pinch.** Every day. Stop when you can predict the result before pinching, then keep doing it anyway for the rest of the week.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 2.** Fingers folding, no exceptions, even on words you find easy.
3. **Three minutes — Drill 3.** Say the label out loud before each word. If you hesitate on one, mark it and bring it in.
4. **Four minutes — Drill 4.** Whole ayat, at reciting pace. Record one of them on your phone on day three and listen back on day four.
5. **Two minutes — Drill 5.** Stopping on ٱلنَّاسِ and ٱلْجِنَّةِ.
6. **The rest — hifz.** Manzil, then Sabqi (113), then 112:1–2. From the page, not from audio. Audio is for checking.

Bring to the next session: **one word from your own recitation where you are not sure whether the sound is coming out of your nose.** Not from the drills — from ṣalāh. We will pinch it together.
