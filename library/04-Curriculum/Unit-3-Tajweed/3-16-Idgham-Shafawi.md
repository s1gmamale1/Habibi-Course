# Lesson 3-16 — Idghām shafawī: two meems, one letter

## Objectives

- Student can merge a meem sākinah into a following meem, producing one doubled meem held on a 2-count ghunnah, with no trace of a break.
- Student can read the muṣḥaf's own signal for this rule — a bare meem at the end of one word, a shaddah'd meem at the start of the next.
- Student can distinguish idghām shafawī from a tanwīn or a noon merging into a meem, which look identical on the page and sound identical in the mouth.
- Student can find both instances in Sūrat Quraysh 106:4 unprompted.

## Hook

Write the ayah she recites in every second rakʿah and ask her to read it:

> **ٱلَّذِىٓ أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ** — Quraysh 106:4

Listen to `أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن`. She will almost certainly say **hum · min** — two meems, one at the end of a word and one at the start of the next, each pronounced.

Stop her there and say it: **there are two meems written and there is one meem spoken.** The first has no life of its own; it goes into the second, and what you hear is a single doubled meem, held for two counts, humming — *aṭʿama-humm-in jūʿ*.

Then say the part that will actually catch her attention: **she already knows how to do this.** She has been holding مّ for two counts since lesson 3-14. The only new thing today is that the doubled meem is **assembled out of two words** instead of arriving pre-built inside one.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-14** gave the doubled meem and its two-count nasal. `حَمَّالَةَ` (111:4) is that sound. Today's rule produces *the same sound*, from two words. Say that sentence early; it removes most of the difficulty.
- **Lesson 2-05** taught that a shadda is one letter written once and said twice, and that the first half is closed. Idghām shafawī is that construction with a space in the middle of the spelling.
- **Lesson 2-06** trained her to spot a shadda that came from an assimilation — the لـ of الـ going into a sun letter, `ٱلصَّمَدُ`, `ٱلنَّاسِ`. **This is the same phenomenon across a word boundary**, and it is worth naming as such: she has been reading assimilations correctly for a whole unit.
- **Lesson 3-15** put a meem sākinah before a ب. Today is the second of the three meem futures on the board.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Point at the chapter map on the board.** Second of three. Meem sākinah + **م** → merge. Meem sākinah + **ب** → conceal (last lesson). Meem sākinah + anything else → say it plainly (next lesson). Nothing else is coming.

2. **State the rule and its type in one breath.** A meem sākinah followed by **م** merges completely into it. One doubled meem results, carrying a **2-count ghunnah**. The merge is **kāmil** — complete — **always**. Nothing of the first meem survives except that its time is now part of the shaddah. There is no incomplete variant and no scholarly disagreement anywhere in this rule. Tell her that plainly; it is the cleanest rule in the unit and she deserves an easy one after `تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ`.

3. **Show her that the muṣḥaf already told her.** In `أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن` the first meem is **bare** — no sukūn printed on it — and the second carries a **shaddah**. That is the standing convention: *a bare letter at the end of a word, the same letter with a shaddah at the start of the next, means the first has gone into the second.* Have her look at the page and find the shaddah before she reads. **The shaddah is the instruction.**

4. **Give it its second name, because she will meet it.** This rule is also called **إِدْغَام المُتَمَاثِلَيْن الصَّغِير** — the merging of two identical letters — or **إِدْغَام مِثْلَيْه**. It is not a separate ruling; it is the *general* rule for two identical letters meeting, applied to the meem. The general theory arrives in lesson 3-26. Today she needs the name only so that a different book does not read like a different rule. See [[Idgham-Mutamathilayn]].

5. **Build it out loud, in three steps.** Do not define; assemble.
   - Say `هُمْ` and `مِّن` separately, with a gap.
   - Say them with the gap shrinking.
   - Say `هُم مِّن` as one word with a held meem: *humm-in*.
   Then write `حَمَّالَةَ` beside it. *"Same sound. One of them was born that way; the other was built."*

6. **Correct the course's own note, out loud.** Earlier course material recorded idghām shafawī as having **no clean example inside the memorised sūrahs**. **Checked against the pinned corpus, that is wrong** — Quraysh 106:4 contains **two**, `أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن` and `وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنْ`, both verified. Tell her this. Not for the drama, but because it is a habit worth modelling: a claim about the text is checked against the text, and when it fails, it is corrected in public. See [[Idgham-Shafawi]].

7. **Then show what 106:4 is actually carrying, because it is remarkable.** One ayah, eight words, five rulings:

   | Phrase | Rule | Lesson |
   |---|---|---|
   | أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن | **idghām shafawī** | today |
   | مِّن جُوعٍ | ikhfāʾ ḥaqīqī — ج | 3-23 |
   | وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنْ | **idghām shafawī** | today |
   | مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ | iẓhār ḥalqī — خ | 3-19 |
   | خَوْفٍۭ → بِسْمِ | iqlāb, on continuing into al-Māʿūn | 3-22 |

   **This ayah carries the whole nasal syllabus, and she already has it memorised.** Put it on a card today and return to it in lessons 3-19, 3-22, 3-23 and 3-25. By the revision lesson she should be able to name all five without the card.

8. **Now the trap, and give it real time — this is the hard part of the lesson.** On the page, three different rules produce *the same picture*: a word ending, then a meem with a shaddah.

   | What comes before the shaddah'd meem | Rule | Colour |
   |---|---|---|
   | a bare **meem** — `أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن` | **idghām shafawī** (today) | `#58B800` |
   | a **tanwīn** — `حَبْلٌ مِّن`, `بِحِجَارَةٍ مِّن` | idghām with ghunnah (3-20) | `#169777` |
   | a bare **noon** — `مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ` | idghām with ghunnah (3-20) | `#169777` |

   **The 2-count ghunnah is identical in all three. She will not mispronounce this. She will mis-*name* it** — and naming is what the colour-coded page and the oral exam test. Use `حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ` (111:5): rows two and three, side by side, in a sūrah she is memorising in four lessons' time.

9. **Colour.** Family B paints idghām shafawī **`#58B800`**, bright green; the cpfair key is `idghaam_shafawi` — see [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]]. Note that idghām *with ghunnah* is `#169777`, a teal-green. **These two greens are close.** Do not let her learn the rule by hue; teach it on the letter that comes before.

**Teacher note on 106:4.** Do not let her read it as "the ayah with the idghāms". Have her point at the two bare meems — `أَطْعَمَهُم`, `وَءَامَنَهُم` — with a finger, before she says anything. The rule is triggered by the *first* letter, and beginners look at the shaddah because the shaddah is what is decorated.

**Teacher note on the trap examples.** Read `أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن` and `حَبْلٌ مِّن` back to back and ask her which is which. If she cannot tell, that is the correct starting point — the sounds *are* the same. Cover the shaddah'd meem and ask again with only the first word visible. That is the only information the rule uses.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — assemble.** Say the left column with a gap, then the right column joined, with a two-count hold on the meem:

| Two words, apart | Joined |
|---|---|
| أَطْعَمَهُمْ · مِنْ | أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن |
| وَءَامَنَهُمْ · مِنْ | وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنْ |
| قُلُوبِهِمْ · مَرَضٌ | قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ |
| لَهُمْ · مَغْفِرَةٌ | لَهُم مَّغْفِرَةٌ |

**Drill 2 — born or built?** Read each word or phrase and say which it is. Both get the same two-count hum:

| Born doubled | Built from two words |
|---|---|
| حَمَّالَةَ | أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن |
| ثُمَّ | لَهُم مَّغْفِرَةٌ |
| أُمَّة | قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ |

**Drill 3 — two counts, on the fingers.** Fold two fingers on the merged meem. The word after it does not begin until the second finger lands:

أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن · وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنْ · لَهُم مَّغْفِرَةٌ · قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌ

**Drill 4 — name the rule.** Read each phrase, then say **"meem"**, **"tanwīn"** or **"noon"** — meaning: what was there *before* the shaddah'd meem. Do not say the rule's name; say the letter. The letter is the decision:

أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن · حَبْلٌ مِّن · مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ · وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنْ · هُدًى مِّن · لَهُم مَّغْفِرَةٌ · بِحِجَارَةٍ مِّن

**Drill 5 — the anchor ayah, cold.** Recite Quraysh 106:4 from memory and stop after each of the two merges. Then recite it whole, in one breath, with both merges intact.

**Drill 6 — cover and decide.** Have someone cover the second word of each pair in Drill 4 so only the first is visible. Name the rule from the first word alone. You should be right every time — the second word contributes nothing to the decision.

## Listen-for

**1. Pronouncing two separate meems — *hum-min*.**
- *Listen for:* two beats where there should be one long one, usually with a tiny gap at the word boundary.
- *Why it happens:* the words are printed apart, and Unit 2 taught her to read word by word. This is the default error and it will happen on the first read every time.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 1, left column then right. Then have her say `حَمَّالَةَ` and `هُم مِّن` back to back — the mouth does the same thing in both, and she can feel that.

**2. Omitting the ghunnah on the merged meem.**
- *Listen for:* a doubled meem that is held but not nasal. The pinch test settles it instantly.
- *Why it happens:* the merge is the new instruction, so attention goes to joining and the nasal is dropped. Same pattern as in 3-15.
- *Correction cue:* "Pinch and say it." She corrects herself.

**3. Failing to hold — releasing after one count.**
- *Listen for:* the merge happening correctly and finishing early. This is the error that survives longest, because it *sounds* right.
- *Why it happens:* it is the under-held shadda from lesson 2-05, arriving in new clothing. Diagnose it out loud as that.
- *Correction cue:* Fingers. Two folds. Do not let her run at speed until day three of the homework.

**4. Calling a tanwīn-plus-meem "idghām shafawī".**
- *Listen for:* the *naming*, not the sound. Ask her, do not listen for it — it is inaudible by construction.
- *Why it happens:* the page looks identical and the sound is identical. There is genuinely nothing to hear.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 4 and Drill 6. Cover the second word. The rule is decided entirely by what precedes.

**5. Merging a meem into a **noon**.**
- *Listen for:* `لَهُمْ نَصِيبٌ` coming out as a single held nasal.
- *Why it happens:* meem and noon are both nasals and both hum, so they feel like the same letter under load.
- *Correction cue:* "Two nasals is not the same as two identical letters." A meem before a noon is the *default* case — plain, lips closed, no hold — and its name arrives next lesson.

**6. Over-lengthening to four counts.**
- *Listen for:* the merged meem becoming a drone, usually because she is pleased with it.
- *Why it happens:* correcting error 3, past the target.
- *Correction cue:* Two taps on the table. Same tool, opposite direction.

## Hifz for this session

Revision first: **Manzil**, then **Sabqi**, then the new **Sabaq**. See [[Hifz-Method]].

- **Manzil.** Two surahs from the promoted pool.
- **Sabqi.** al-Falaq 113 and al-Ikhlāṣ 112, now complete — four ayat. If 112 has come back clean three sessions running, promote it to Manzil and say so out loud; the promotion is a real event and she should get to hear it.
- **New Sabaq — al-Masad 111:1.**

> تَبَّتْ يَدَآ أَبِى لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ

One ayah. It is short but it is not easy: **two doubled bāʾs**, `تَبَّتْ` and `وَتَبَّ`, and a doubled letter you cannot sustain is the kind she under-holds — that was error 3 in lesson 2-05 and it is the same muscle as today's. Two counts of *closure*, silence with the mouth shut, counted on the fingers during the silence.

Note for her, in passing: `لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ` is a tanwīn followed by a **و**, which is a merging rule she meets in lesson 3-20. Do not teach it today. Just say "there is a rule there, in four lessons" and let her carry the question.

20–40 repetitions; ten extra on any error caught, counted separately.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **Three minutes — Drill 1.** Apart, then joined. Every day.
2. **Two minutes — Drill 2.** Born or built. Say the label out loud.
3. **Three minutes — Drill 3.** Fingers folding on every merged meem.
4. **Three minutes — Drill 4.** Name the *preceding letter*, not the rule. This is the drill for the exam, and it is the one you will be tempted to skip because it feels like grammar rather than recitation. Do it anyway.
5. **Two minutes — Drill 5.** Quraysh 106:4 from memory, whole.
6. **The rest — hifz.** Manzil, Sabqi (113 + 112), then 111:1 from the page.

Bring to the next session: **Quraysh 106:4, recited from memory, and tell me how many rules are in it before you recite.** The answer is on the card. You do not have to name them all yet — just the number.
