# Lesson 3-16 — Ikhfāʾ shafawī: the lips touch but do not press

## Objectives

- Student can identify a meem sākinah followed by ب on sight, and can say why the sequence only ever occurs across a word boundary.
- Student can produce the light-contact lip posture — touching, not sealing — and can feel the difference between it and a fully closed meem.
- Student can hold the 2-count ghunnah *behind* the lips before releasing the ب, without either pressing shut or opening early.
- Student can read `تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ` correctly cold, from her own memorised al-Fīl.

## Hook

Write one phrase and ask her to read it:

> **تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ** — al-Fīl 105:4

She knows this ayah by heart. She will read it, and almost certainly she will do one of two things: press her lips shut and say a clean *tarmīhim*, then *biḥijāratin* — two separate words with a full stop between them — or she will lose the meem altogether and produce *tarmīhi-bḥijāratin*.

Both are wrong, and the sentence to say is this: **the lips are supposed to be somewhere in between, and stay there for two counts.**

Then demonstrate it, exaggerated: lips brought together so lightly they are barely touching, the nasal running behind them for a slow two, and only then the ب popping open. Ask her to put a finger against her own lips while you do it again — she should be able to see that they are together and hear that nothing is being pressed.

**Name the priority error now, because it is the one that will happen:** *she will press.* Every language she speaks presses its *m*. Two counts of light contact is not a sound she has ever needed before today.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-15** gave her the nasal itself and the two-count hold. Today's rule is that same hold, in a new place — behind lips that are not shut. Say it that way: *"you already own the sound; today it moves."*
- **Lesson 2-03** gave sukūn — a closed letter, said with no vowel after it. A meem sākinah is exactly that, and the whole of the meem chapter is about what happens to a closed meem depending on what stands next to it.
- **Lesson 2-05** taught that a shadda's first half *is* a sukūn. She has been producing closed meems since then without a name for the situation.
- From **Unit 2** she can already read `أَلَمْ تَرَ` and `لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ` correctly by instinct. Those are the *other* meem rule, and she is right about them — tell her so, and tell her the name is coming in lesson 3-18.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Frame the whole chapter first, in thirty seconds.** A meem sākinah has exactly three possible futures, decided entirely by the next letter:

   | Next letter | What happens | Lesson |
   |---|---|---|
   | **ب** | concealed, lips lightly touching, 2-count ghunnah | **today** |
   | **م** | merged into one doubled meem, 2-count ghunnah | 3-17 |
   | any of the other 26 | said plainly, lips fully closed, no hold | 3-18 |

   Two exceptions and a default. Draw it once and leave it on the board for four lessons. She should never be wondering how large this chapter is.

2. **State the rule.** Meem sākinah (مْ) followed by **ب** is *concealed* — **ikhfāʾ** — with a **2-count ghunnah**. *Shafawī* means **labial**, "of the lips", from شَفَة. It is named that way against the other ikhfāʾ she meets in lesson 3-24, *al-ikhfāʾ al-ḥaqīqī*, the "true" concealment, which hides a **noon** and happens on the tongue. Same operation, different letter, different place in the mouth.

3. **The condition has a structural fact worth pointing at.** In the Qurʾān, مْ + ب **always occurs across two words** — the sequence never arises inside a single word. So the meem is always the last letter of its word, and the ب always opens the next one. She never has to check mid-word for this rule. That is one less thing to scan for, and it is true without exception.

4. **Teach the posture with her hand, not with words.** Have her hold a fingertip lightly against her closed lips and try three things in sequence:
   - **Press** — say a hard *m*. She feels the pressure on her finger.
   - **Open** — say *b* with no meem at all. No contact.
   - **Touch** — bring the lips together until they are just in contact, hum, and hold. Almost no pressure on the finger.
   The third is the rule. Say the diagnostic aloud: **"If you can feel your lips tense, you are pressing."**

5. **Then add the timing.** Lips in the light-contact position, ghunnah runs for two counts — fold two fingers, exactly as in 3-15 — and only when the second finger lands does the ب release. Do not let her release early; the commonest execution fault is a correct posture held for half a count.

6. **Say the boundary out loud.** She is about to be told, in lesson 3-18, that a meem before *any other letter* has its lips **fully closed and no hold**. These two rules are lip-posture opposites and they differ by one letter. Flag that now, briefly, so the contrast is expected rather than confusing:

   | Rule | Lips | Ghunnah |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Ikhfāʾ shafawī** (ب) | **lightly touching** | 2 counts |
   | Iẓhār shafawī (26 letters) | **fully closed** | not prolonged |

7. **Flag the difference of opinion once, and do not drill it.** A **minority** of scholars hold that a meem sākinah before ب is **iẓhār**, not ikhfāʾ — their reasoning being that the meem and the ب already share the lips, so there is nothing to hide. **The jumhūr position is ikhfāʾ, and this course teaches ikhfāʾ.** Mention it in one sentence so that if she hears a teacher press the lips fully on `تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ` she recognises a known disagreement rather than concluding she was taught wrongly. Practically the two positions differ by very little: how much contact and how much hold, not whether a meem is there at all. Full discussion in [[Ikhfa-Shafawi]].

8. **Colour.** Family B paints ikhfāʾ shafawī **`#D500B7`**, magenta. The cpfair key is `ikhfa_shafawi` — see [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]]. Because the rule is rare, this colour is rare: finding a magenta meem on a page is itself a small event. Have her look for one.

9. **The forward pointer that will save you a lesson later.** In seven lessons she will meet [[Iqlab]], where a **noon** before ب becomes a concealed meem. **The lip posture is identical and the sound is nearly indistinguishable.** The two rules differ only in what was written before the rule applied — a م (today) or a ن (lesson 3-23). Say this now, in one sentence, and say it again then. Learners who meet the two rules cold, weeks apart, file them as one rule with a naming problem.

## Examples

| Text | Ref | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ | 105:4 | *tarmīhi-mm-biḥijārah* — the only one in your memorised sūrahs |
| وَمَا هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ | 2:8 | reference example — outside your memorised sūrahs. One instance, nothing else in the way |
| أَنۢبِئْهُم بِأَسْمَآئِهِمْ | 2:33 | reference example — outside your memorised sūrahs. Two lip-nasal rules, four letters apart |
| أَلَمْ تَرَ | 105:1 | the contrast: lips **shut**, no hold. Same sūrah, three words in |

**Teacher note on 105:4 — spend your time here.** `تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ` is the single in-set instance, and the ayah keeps giving: three words later it hands you `بِحِجَارَةٍ مِّن سِجِّيلٍ`, which is a merge with ghunnah (lesson 3-21). **One ayah, two lip-and-nasal rules, already in her memory.** Do not go looking for a better example; there isn't one.

**Teacher note on 2:33.** `أَنۢبِئْهُم بِ` contains an iqlāb — a noon turning into a concealed meem — and then, four letters later, an ikhfāʾ shafawī: a meem being concealed. **The mouth does the same thing twice, from two different written letters.** Save this for lesson 3-23 if you want the drama, or use it today as a preview. Either works; do not use it both times as though it were new.

**Teacher note on al-Fīl 105:1 vs 105:4.** Both are in one short sūrah she recites from memory. `أَلَمْ تَرَ` presses the lips shut; `تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ` does not. Put them side by side. The difference is the single letter that follows, and nothing else.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the finger test, three postures.** Fingertip resting against your lips. Say each one and report what you felt:

| Say | Lips should feel |
|---|---|
| أُمّ | pressed — this is a doubled meem, not today's rule |
| تَرْمِيهِم بِـ | barely touching |
| بِـ alone | closed then popped |

Repeat until you can hit the middle row on purpose rather than by luck.

**Drill 2 — build it from the pieces.** Say the left column, then the right:

| Two pieces, with a gap | Joined, with the rule |
|---|---|
| تَرْمِيهِمْ · بِحِجَارَةٍ | تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ |
| هُمْ · بِمُؤْمِنِينَ | هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ |
| أَنۢبِئْهُمْ · بِأَسْمَآئِهِمْ | أَنۢبِئْهُم بِأَسْمَآئِهِمْ |

The left column is deliberately wrong. Say it anyway — it makes the join audible.

**Drill 3 — two counts behind the lips.** Fold two fingers during the hold. Do not release the ب until the second finger has landed:

تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ · هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ · أَنۢبِئْهُم بِأَسْمَآئِهِمْ

Then do each one *wrong on purpose* — release after one count — so you can hear what you are avoiding.

**Drill 4 — is it a ب or not?** Read each pair. The first of each pair is today's rule; the second is not. Say the label before you read:

| Today (ب) | Not today |
|---|---|
| تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ | أَلَمْ تَرَ |
| هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ | لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ |
| أَنۢبِئْهُم بِأَسْمَآئِهِمْ | عَلَيْهِمْ طَيْرًا |

**Drill 5 — in the sūrah, whole.** Recite al-Fīl from memory, all five ayat, at your normal pace. The rule has to survive being surrounded by other words. If it only works when you are looking at it, it is not learned yet.

**Drill 6 — the mirror.** Do Drill 3 once in front of a mirror. You should be able to *see* your lips together and *see* that they are not compressed. Thirty seconds, once, and it settles the posture question for good.

## Listen-for

**1. Pressing the lips shut.**
- *Listen for:* a clean, fully-formed meem — *tarmīhim*, with a real stop before the ب. It sounds tidy, which is why it survives.
- *Why it happens:* every language she speaks has a pressed *m* and none has a lightly-held one. This is the default, not carelessness.
- *Correction cue:* Fingertip on the lips. "Less. Again. Less." Do not explain; adjust by feel.

**2. Leaving the lips apart — no meem at all.**
- *Listen for:* the meem vanishing, *tarmīhi-bḥijārah*. Usually appears the moment you fix error 1.
- *Why it happens:* over-correction. "Do not press" is heard as "do not close".
- *Correction cue:* "Touch. Not press, not open — touch." Back to Drill 1's three rows, in order.

**3. Omitting the ghunnah — the posture is right, the hold is not.**
- *Listen for:* a correct-looking mouth that releases straight into the ب.
- *Why it happens:* the posture is the new and interesting part, so all the attention goes there and the timing is dropped. Very common in the same session the posture is fixed.
- *Correction cue:* Fingers. Two folds, then release. Do not let her run the phrase without them until day three.

**4. Applying it to a meem before something other than ب.**
- *Listen for:* a soft, held meem in `أَلَمْ تَرَ` or `لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ`.
- *Why it happens:* new rules over-apply for about a week. Expected, not alarming.
- *Correction cue:* "What letter comes next?" Make her name it. The rule is decided by that letter and by nothing else.

**5. Breaking the two words apart.**
- *Listen for:* a micro-pause between `تَرْمِيهِم` and `بِحِجَارَةٍ`. Sometimes a breath.
- *Why it happens:* it is a word boundary, and Unit 2 trained her to read words as units. The rule requires the join.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 2, left column then right, twice. Then have her recite the ayah in one breath.

**6. Nasalising the ب.**
- *Listen for:* the hum continuing *through* the release, so the ب comes out muffled.
- *Why it happens:* she has learned to stop the nasal and open the lips as one movement, instead of stopping the nasal *then* opening.
- *Correction cue:* "Finish the hum. Then open." Two separate instructions, deliberately separated in time, then gradually brought back together.

## Hifz for this session

Revision first: **Manzil**, then **Sabqi**, then the new **Sabaq**. See [[Hifz-Method]].

- **Manzil.** Two surahs from the promoted pool.
- **Sabqi.** al-Falaq 113 whole, plus al-Ikhlāṣ 112:1–2 from last session. 112:2 is the two-shaddas-no-ghunnah ayah — check it again cold; it is exactly the kind of thing that decays in a week.
- **New Sabaq — al-Ikhlāṣ 112:3–4.**

> لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ۝ وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ

This portion is worth a comment while she memorises it. It has **three meem sākinahs** — `لَمْ`, `وَلَمْ`, `وَلَمْ` — and **not one of them is today's rule**, because each is followed by ي, not ب. All three are the default case, lips fully closed, no hold, and she will get its name in lesson 3-18. Have her read the portion and count the closed meems out loud. **Learning to see a rule and correctly conclude "not this one" is half the skill.**

Note also the `أَحَدٌۢ` at the end, with its small standing meem. She has read that mark for years. It is an iqlāb sign and it belongs to lesson 3-23 — if she asks, say "seven lessons away, and yes, it is related to what we did today."

20–40 repetitions; ten extra on any error caught, counted separately.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **One minute — Drill 1.** Fingertip on the lips, three postures. Every day, first thing, before anything else.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 2 and Drill 3.** Build from pieces, then hold two counts on the fingers.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 4.** Say the label before each phrase. If you hesitate, mark it.
4. **Three minutes — Drill 5.** al-Fīl from memory, whole, at pace.
5. **Once, on day two — Drill 6**, in front of a mirror.
6. **The rest — hifz.** Manzil, Sabqi (113 + 112:1–2), then 112:3–4 from the page.

Bring to the next session: **al-Fīl recited from memory, and tell me before you start where the one ikhfāʾ shafawī is.** If you can name it before reciting it, you have it.
