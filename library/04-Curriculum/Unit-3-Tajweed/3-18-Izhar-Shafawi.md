# Lesson 3-18 — Iẓhār shafawī: the twenty-six-letter default

## Objectives

- Student can state the meem sākinah rule in its complete form — two exceptions, twenty-six defaults — without reciting a letter list.
- Student can close the lips fully on a meem sākinah and release with no prolonged hold, and can do it before **و** and **ف** where the pull is strongest.
- Student can explain why iẓhār has **no colour** on a coloured muṣḥaf, and can read an uncoloured letter as an instruction rather than as an absence.
- Student can read al-Fātiḥa 1:7 with both `عَلَيْهِمْ` meems fully formed.

## Hook

Ask her for al-Fātiḥa, ayah 7 — the one she says seventeen times a day. Listen to the end:

> **صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ**

Nine times in ten, the second `عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا` comes out as *ʿalayhi-wwalā*. **The meem is gone.** Not shortened — gone. She has been dropping a letter out of the most-recited ayah in the language, every day, for as long as she has been praying.

Say so, plainly, and then say why it is not carelessness: **the wāw is made at the lips, exactly where the meem is made.** The mouth is already in position for the wāw, closing for the meem feels like extra work for no gain, and so the meem gets swallowed. It is the most predictable error in the entire meem chapter, and al-Jamzūrī named it in the matn twelve centuries ago.

Then the reframe that carries the lesson: **the last two lessons were the exceptions. Today is the rule.** Twenty-six letters out of twenty-eight. If she has been worrying that the meem chapter is complicated, it is not — she has already learned the hard two-thirds of it.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-16** put a meem sākinah before **ب**: lips lightly touching, two-count nasal.
- **Lesson 3-17** put it before **م**: merged into one doubled meem, two-count nasal.
- **Today is everything else.** The board diagram from lesson 3-16 is now complete; point at it and close it off.
- **Lesson 3-15** gave the levels of ghunnah, and rank 4 on that table — *sākin, plain, inherent nasal only, not prolonged* — is exactly what today's meem is. Go back to that table. It was put there for this lesson.
- **Unit 2** already taught her to read `أَلَمْ تَرَ` and `لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ` correctly. She does not need to learn today's rule so much as to stop losing it in two specific places.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Complete the map.** Meem sākinah, three futures, no fourth:

   | Next letter | Lips | Ghunnah | Rule |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **ب** (1 letter) | lightly touching | 2 counts | ikhfāʾ shafawī — 3-16 |
   | **م** (1 letter) | closed, doubled | 2 counts | idghām shafawī — 3-17 |
   | **the other 26** | **fully closed** | **not prolonged** | **iẓhār shafawī — today** |

   Say the count out loud: *twenty-six against two.* Iẓhār shafawī is the meem's normal state and by a wide margin the most frequent of the three.

2. **Do not give her a letter list.** The rule is "not ب and not م". A student who memorises twenty-six letters has memorised the wrong object; two exclusions is one item of memory, not twenty-six.

3. **State the rule.** The meem is pronounced **clearly** — *iẓhār* means making a thing plain. Lips **fully closed**, meem fully formed, released cleanly into the next letter, **with no prolonged ghunnah**.

   **In plain terms:** close your lips, say the meem, move on. No hum to hold, no lengthening — just a clean meem.

4. **Now the sentence that prevents next month's error.** "No prolonged ghunnah" does **not** mean "no nasal". A meem cannot exist without its nasal — it is an inseparable attribute of the letter. **What iẓhār removes is the hold, not the resonance.** Have her say `أَلَمْ` and pinch: the sound still blocks, because it is still a meem. She has not switched the nose off; she has stopped waiting on it. Point at the levels table in [[Ghunnah]] — this is level 4, not level zero.

5. **Then the two danger letters, and give them the middle of your session.** Al-Jamzūrī singles them out by name in [[Tuhfat-al-Atfal]]:

   > وَٱحْذَرْ لَدَى وَاوٍ وَفَا أَنْ تَخْتَفِي
   > *"And beware, at a wāw or a fāʾ, of letting it be concealed."*

   The reason is mechanical:
   - **و** is made at the **lips** — the same place as the meem. The mouth is already there. Closing and then rounding feels redundant, so the closure is skipped.
   - **ف** is made at the **lower lip against the upper teeth** — immediately adjacent. Same pull, slightly weaker.

   The result in both cases is a half-concealed meem that sounds like lesson 3-16's rule applied to the wrong letter. **The iẓhār must be strongest here, not weakest.**

   **In plain terms:** و and ف are the two that go wrong, because your lips are nearly in place for them already. Close fully for the meem first, then move.

6. **Both danger cases are already in her memory.** Do not hunt for examples:
   - `عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا` — al-Fātiḥa **1:7**
   - `كَيْدَهُمْ فِى تَضْلِيلٍ` — al-Fīl **105:2**

   Drill them by **exaggerating**: close the lips fully, pause a visible fraction of a second, then release into the و or ف. Once the closure is habitual, remove the pause. Do not skip the exaggeration stage — a correct meem at speed is not reachable directly from a missing one.

7. **The colour lesson, and this is the conceptual content of the session.** **Iẓhār shafawī has no colour in this course and no key in cpfair/quran-tajweed.** That is not an omission in the data.

   A coloured muṣḥaf marks **modifications** — the places where what you say differs from what is written. **Iẓhār is the absence of a modification.** The meem on the page is exactly the meem in the mouth, so there is nothing to annotate.

   Say the consequence out loud, because it is the whole point: **on a coloured page, the absence of colour is itself an instruction.** It means *"say precisely what is written"* — not *"no rule applies here"*. A student who reads black meems as "unmarked, therefore ignore" will swallow every one of them before a wāw.

   Hand her a coloured page. Have her find a **black** meem sākinah and say what the black is telling her. That is the exercise. See [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]] and [[Izhar-Shafawi]].

8. **Tell her the same argument is coming again.** On the noon side, in lesson 3-20, iẓhār ḥalqī is also uncoloured for exactly this reason. She will recognise the argument. Half of tajwīd literacy is knowing when the correct action is to do nothing.

9. **One structural point, once.** A meem sākinah before a **ن** — `وَيَمْنَعُونَ` (107:7), `لَهُمْ نَصِيبٌ` — is iẓhār. Meem and noon are both nasals, they both hum, and under load they feel like one letter. **They are not identical letters, so no merge applies.** Two nasals side by side, both said, neither held.

## Examples

| Text | Ref | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| أَلَمْ تَرَ | 105:1 | *a-lam · tara* — meem shut, released clean |
| عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا | 1:7 | **danger case.** The one she loses daily |
| كَيْدَهُمْ فِى تَضْلِيلٍ | 105:2 | **danger case.** Same fault, weaker pull |
| لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ | 109:6 | a comfortable one — use it to reset after the danger cases |
| لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ | 112:3 | three closed meems in five words, in her own hifz |
| عَلَيْهِمْ طَيْرًا | 105:3 | the ط is heavy; **the meem is not.** It does not catch |
| صَلَاتِهِمْ سَاهُونَ | 107:5 | mid-ayah, where the closure decays first |
| وَيَمْنَعُونَ ٱلْمَاعُونَ | 107:7 | meem before **ن**, inside one word. Two nasals, no merge |

**Teacher note on 112:3.** She memorised this two lessons ago and was told at the time to count the closed meems. Ask her again now, and this time give her the name. **A rule attached to a line she already owns is worth three rules attached to a word list.**

**Teacher note on 105:3.** `عَلَيْهِمْ طَيْرًا` is worth a minute. The ط is one of the heavy letters she learned in lesson 3-03, and a heavy letter next door pulls its neighbours. **The meem does not go heavy here.** Say the phrase both ways and let her hear the wrong one; a thickened meem is a common decoration that nobody has ever told her to stop.

**Teacher note on 107:7.** `وَيَمْنَعُونَ` puts a meem sākinah and a noon in direct contact inside one word. Use it after the meem-into-meem lesson has settled, because it is exactly the case a student over-merges the week after learning idghām shafawī.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the two danger cases, exaggerated.** Close the lips. **Hold the closure a beat.** Then release:

عَلَيْهِمْ · وَلَا  →  عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا
كَيْدَهُمْ · فِى  →  كَيْدَهُمْ فِى

Ten times each, with the pause. Then ten more with the pause removed but the closure kept.

**Drill 2 — the pinch, to prove the nasal is still there.** These are all iẓhār. Pinch your nose on each meem. **The sound should still block** — you have removed the *hold*, not the nose:

أَلَمْ تَرَ · لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ · لَمْ يَلِدْ · عَلَيْهِمْ طَيْرًا · صَلَاتِهِمْ سَاهُونَ

**Drill 3 — all three meem rules, mixed.** Read across, and after each phrase say **"conceal"**, **"merge"** or **"say it"**:

| | |
|---|---|
| تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ | أَلَمْ تَرَ |
| أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن | عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا |
| لَهُم مَّغْفِرَةٌ | كَيْدَهُمْ فِى تَضْلِيلٍ |
| هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ | لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ |

**Drill 4 — al-Fātiḥa 1:7, whole, five times.** Both `عَلَيْهِمْ` meems fully closed. This is the drill that actually matters; everything else in this lesson is preparation for it.

**Drill 5 — the black-letter hunt.** Open a coloured muṣḥaf page. Find three meem sākinahs that carry **no colour**. For each one, say aloud what the absence of colour is telling you. If your answer is "nothing happens here", correct yourself: it is *"say exactly what is written"*.

**Drill 6 — no thickening.** Read each phrase and keep the meem light even though a heavy letter follows:

عَلَيْهِمْ طَيْرًا · لَهُمْ صَلَاةٌ · عَلَيْهِمْ ظُلْمٌ · هُمْ قَوْمٌ

**Drill 7 — cold column.** No cue. Meem sākinah in every one; all are iẓhār:

أَلَمْ نَشْرَحْ · هُمْ فِيهَا · لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ · عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ · لَمْ يَكُنْ · أَمْ حَسِبْتُمْ · هُمْ يُرَآءُونَ · وَيَمْنَعُونَ · أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ · كُنتُمْ

## Listen-for

**1. The meem disappearing before و.**
- *Listen for:* `عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا` as *ʿalayhi-wwalā*. **This is the priority error of the lesson.** Check it in al-Fātiḥa, not in the drills — in the drills she will be watching for it.
- *Why it happens:* the wāw is a lip letter and the mouth is already there. It is a mechanical shortcut, not inattention.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 1 with the deliberate pause. Then straight into al-Fātiḥa 1:7 whole, before the closure fades.

**2. The meem half-swallowed before ف.**
- *Listen for:* `كَيْدَهُمْ فِى` with a soft, unfinished meem. Subtler than the wāw case and correspondingly easier to miss.
- *Why it happens:* the fāʾ's lower lip is immediately adjacent to the meem's closure.
- *Correction cue:* Same exaggeration. If she cannot hear the difference, have her record both and play them back.

**3. Adding a two-count ghunnah.**
- *Listen for:* a held nasal on a plain meem, especially in the session right after 3-16 and 3-17.
- *Why it happens:* she has spent two lessons being told to hold, and holds are satisfying.
- *Correction cue:* "What letter comes next? Is it ب? Is it م? Then say it and move on."

**4. Denasalising — over-correcting "no ghunnah" into "no nose".**
- *Listen for:* a dry, hard meem with no resonance. Often appears a week later, not on the day.
- *Why it happens:* "no ghunnah" was heard as "remove the nasal", which is not what it means.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 2. Pinch it. If it does not block, that is not a meem at all.

**5. Opening the lips before the meem is complete.**
- *Listen for:* a clipped half-meem, most often mid-ayah when she is tired.
- *Why it happens:* speed. The closure is the first casualty of tempo.
- *Correction cue:* Slow the whole line, not the meem. A meem that only works at half speed will come back with practice; one drilled in isolation will not.

**6. Merging a meem into a following ن.**
- *Listen for:* `وَيَمْنَعُونَ` with one long nasal instead of two letters.
- *Why it happens:* both are nasals; the ear groups them.
- *Correction cue:* "Same *kind* of letter is not the same letter." Say `وَيَمْنَعُونَ` slowly enough that both are audible, then rebuild.

**7. Thickening the meem before a heavy letter.**
- *Listen for:* a fat meem in `عَلَيْهِمْ طَيْرًا`.
- *Why it happens:* heaviness spreads if nobody stops it, and she has just spent lessons 3-03 to 3-08 learning to make letters heavy.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 6. Say the phrase both ways and ask her which was which.

## Hifz for this session

Revision first: **Manzil**, then **Sabqi**, then the new **Sabaq**. See [[Hifz-Method]].

- **Manzil.** Two surahs from the promoted pool. If al-Ikhlāṣ was promoted last session, it enters the rotation now.
- **Sabqi.** al-Falaq 113, al-Ikhlāṣ 112, and al-Masad 111:1 from last session. Check the two doubled bāʾs of `تَبَّتْ` and `وَتَبَّ` — silent holds decay fast.
- **New Sabaq — al-Masad 111:2–3.**

> مَآ أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُ مَالُهُۥ وَمَا كَسَبَ ۝ سَيَصْلَىٰ نَارًا ذَاتَ لَهَبٍ

Two short ayat. Point at `عَنْهُ` while she works on it: a **noon** sākinah before **ه**, and that is a rule she meets in two lessons — the noon-side iẓhār, with the same "no colour" argument you gave today. **Do not teach it now.** Just mark it: *"the meem side is finished; that word is the first thing on the other side."*

`نَارًا ذَاتَ` is a tanwīn before ذ, which lands in lesson 3-24. Leave it alone too. Two flags, no teaching.

20–40 repetitions; ten extra on any error caught, counted separately.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **Three minutes — Drill 1.** The two danger cases with the deliberate pause. Every day, all five days. This is the core of the homework.
2. **Two minutes — Drill 2.** Pinch each one. Confirm the nose is still working.
3. **Three minutes — Drill 3.** All three meem rules mixed, label spoken before you read.
4. **Three minutes — Drill 4.** al-Fātiḥa 1:7, five times, both meems closed.
5. **Once, on day two — Drill 5**, the black-letter hunt on a coloured page.
6. **Two minutes — Drill 7.** Cold column.
7. **The rest — hifz.** Manzil, Sabqi (113 + 112 + 111:1), then 111:2–3 from the page.

Bring to the next session: **al-Fātiḥa recited whole, and listen to yourself on ayah 7.** Tell me honestly whether the second meem was there. Next session is revision of the whole meem chapter — bring anything from these three lessons you are still unsure of.
