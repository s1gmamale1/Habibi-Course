# Lesson 3-19 — Revision and Q&A: the meem sākinah trio

No new rule today, and **no new Sabaq**. This session exists to make three lessons behave like one system, and to catch the errors that only appear once the rules are in the same room together.

## Objectives

- Student can decide, on any meem sākinah, which of the three rules applies, from the next letter alone, at reading speed.
- Student can produce the three lip postures on demand and name the one she is producing.
- Student can say what an uncoloured meem on a coloured page is instructing her to do.
- Student can name what is *not* a meem rule when the page makes it look like one.

## Hook

Put four phrases in front of her, in this order, and ask her to read all four before saying anything about any of them:

> **تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ** · **أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٍ** · **عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا** · **حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ**

Three of them are meem sākinah rules and one is not. Almost nobody gets the fourth right the first time, because on the page it is the same picture as the second.

**That is the shape of this session.** The individual rules are not the problem — she has had a week on each. What has not been tested is *choosing between them under time pressure*, and *not applying them where they do not belong*.

## What they already know

The whole chapter, in one table. Put it on the board before anything else and leave it there:

| Next letter | Lips | Ghunnah | Rule | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ب** — 1 letter | **lightly touching** | 2 counts | ikhfāʾ shafawī | `#D500B7` magenta |
| **م** — 1 letter | **closed, doubled** | 2 counts | idghām shafawī | `#58B800` green |
| **the other 26** | **fully closed** | not prolonged | iẓhār shafawī | **none** |

And underneath it, the thing that makes the table usable: **the decision is made by one letter, and it is the letter that comes *after* the meem.** Nothing else in the phrase is evidence.

She also has, from lesson 3-15, the levels of ghunnah — and she should be able to place all three rows on that scale without help: rows one and two are held nasals, row three is an inherent nasal that is not held.

## Teaching sequence

This session is drills and questions, not exposition. Three short pieces of framing, then get to the mixed work.

1. **Run the table once, out loud, in both directions.** You say a letter, she says the rule. Then you say a rule, she says which letters trigger it. Sixty seconds. If either direction is slow, that is where the session's time goes.

   **In plain terms:** a silent meem has exactly three futures: hide it before ب, merge it before another م, say it plainly before anything else. Three, no fourth.

2. **The three postures, side by side, with a fingertip on the lips.** Have her produce all three in sequence and name each one as she does it:
   - *touching* → ikhfāʾ shafawī
   - *closed and doubled, held* → idghām shafawī
   - *closed and released* → iẓhār shafawī

   This is the only place in the course where three lip states are in direct competition. Do it as a physical drill, not as a discussion.

3. **Say the "no colour" principle back to her, as a question.** *"You are looking at a coloured page. A meem sākinah is black. What is the page telling you?"* The answer is **"say exactly what is written"** — not "nothing happens here". If she gives the second answer, spend five minutes on it, because it is the belief that produces the `عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا` error. See [[Izhar-Shafawi]].

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the mixed column, cold.** Read down. After each phrase say **"conceal"**, **"merge"**, **"say it"** or **"not a meem rule"**. Do not pause to think; if you have to think, mark it and come back:

| | |
|---|---|
| 1. أَلَمْ تَرَ | 8. حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ |
| 2. تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ | 9. صَلَاتِهِمْ سَاهُونَ |
| 3. عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا | 10. أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٍ |
| 4. لَهُم مَّغْفِرَةٌ | 11. عَلَيْهِمْ طَيْرًا |
| 5. كَيْدَهُمْ فِى تَضْلِيلٍ | 12. هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ |
| 6. وَيَمْنَعُونَ ٱلْمَاعُونَ | 13. بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ |
| 7. لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ | 14. وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ |

Answers, for the teacher: 1 say · 2 conceal · 3 say · 4 merge · 5 say · 6 say · 7 say · 8 not a meem rule · 9 say · 10 merge · 11 say · 12 conceal · 13 not a meem rule · 14 merge.

**Drill 2 — cover the second word.** Someone covers everything after the meem. Name the rule from what is left. **You should fail every time** — and that is the point of the drill. The rule is decided by the letter you cannot see. It teaches the eye to look forward, which is the single habit this chapter needs.

Then uncover, one letter at a time, and stop the moment you know.

**Drill 3 — the three postures, called at random.** Someone says "touching", "doubled", "closed". You produce the posture on the word `هُمْ` and hold it until told to release. Twenty repetitions, random order, fingertip on the lips.

**Drill 4 — the two danger cases, still.** They do not stop being the priority because the lesson is over:

عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا · كَيْدَهُمْ فِى تَضْلِيلٍ

Ten each, exaggerated closure. Then al-Fātiḥa 1:7 whole, three times.

**Drill 5 — whole sūrahs, listening only for meems.** Recite al-Fīl and Quraysh from memory. Do not recite for beauty; recite as an inspection. Say the rule name out loud *after* each meem sākinah, mid-recitation. It will sound absurd. Do it anyway — twice.

**Drill 6 — the pinch sweep.** Go down Drill 1 again, pinching your nose on every meem. Rows that should block: all of them. Rows where the block should *last*: 2, 4, 10, 12, 14. Anything else that lasts is an added ghunnah.

**Drill 7 — write it.** On a blank sheet, from memory, reproduce the three-row table: letter, lips, ghunnah, colour. If the colour row has three entries instead of two, you have not understood the "no colour" principle.

## Troubleshooting Q&A

These are the questions that actually get asked at this point in the chapter, with the answers that work.

**"Ikhfāʾ shafawī and idghām shafawī both hum for two counts. Why are they two rules?"**
Because they do different things to the lips and they start from different pictures. In ikhfāʾ the lips *touch* and the meem stays a meem, hidden. In idghām the lips *close* and two meems become one. On the page: ikhfāʾ shows a bare meem then a **ب**; idghām shows a bare meem then a **meem with a shaddah**. And they are coloured differently, which tells you the tradition considers them distinct.

**"Why does `عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا` need such a big closure? It sounds fine to me."**
It sounds fine because you have heard yourself do it wrong for years and your ear has calibrated to it. Record yourself and compare with a reciter. This is the one place in the chapter where self-diagnosis fails, and it is why the exaggeration drill exists.

**"If iẓhār has no colour, how do I know I have not missed a rule?"**
You do not know from the colour — you know from the letter. Colour marks modifications only. **Black means: what is written is what is said.** That is a positive instruction, not silence. If you find yourself reading black text as "no information", you have inverted the system.

**"On the page, `أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن` and `حَبْلٌ مِّن` look the same to me."**
They look the same *after* the shaddah'd meem. They differ *before* it: one is a bare meem, one is a tanwīn. Cover the shaddah'd meem and the ambiguity disappears entirely. The colours also differ — `#58B800` against `#169777` — but those two greens are close enough that you should not rely on them. **Read the letter, not the hue.**

**"`حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍ` — you said it is not a meem rule, but it has meems in it."**
It has meems being *merged into*. A meem sākinah rule needs a meem sākinah — a closed meem — as the letter the rule acts on. Here the letters being acted on are a tanwīn and a noon. **The rule is named after the letter that changes, not the letter it changes into.** You meet these in lesson 3-21; today you only need to be able to say "not mine".

**"Do I hold the ghunnah when I stop on a word ending in a meem sākinah?"**
If you stop, the next letter is gone, and with it the rule. There is no ikhfāʾ or idghām across a stop. What remains is a plain closed meem. This becomes systematic in lessons 3-34 and 3-35; for now: **no next letter, no rule.**

**"My teacher presses the lips fully on `تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ`. Is one of you wrong?"**
Neither. A minority of scholars read a meem before ب as iẓhār rather than ikhfāʾ, on the grounds that the meem and the ب already share the lips. The jumhūr — and this course — read it as ikhfāʾ. The two positions differ by how much contact and how much hold, not by whether a meem is there. Recognise it as a known difference, learn one, and be consistent.

**"Is the meem in `أَنْعَمْتَ` covered by this chapter?"**
No — that is a **noon** sākinah before ع, which is the next chapter. Read the letters, not the shapes.

## Hifz for this session

**Revision lessons pause new rules; they never pause hifz.** No new Sabaq today — Sabqi and Manzil only. See [[Hifz-Method]].

- **Manzil.** Run the promoted pool wider than usual today, since the session has no new Sabaq to make room for. Three surahs rather than two.
- **Sabqi.** Everything recent, recited whole and unaided:
  - al-Falaq **113**, five ayat
  - al-Ikhlāṣ **112**, four ayat
  - al-Masad **111:1–3**
- **Promotion check.** If al-Falaq has come back clean three consecutive sessions, promote it to Manzil. Three clean recitations is this course's own threshold, not a transmitted one — adjust it from what you observe. If her promoted surahs keep decaying, raise it to four or five. See [[Hifz-Method]] §3.

Use the extra time to run **111:1–3 against the meem rules**: `مَآ أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُ مَالُهُۥ` has no meem sākinah at all, and being able to say that confidently is worth as much as finding one.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days. No new rule material — this week is consolidation and hifz.**

1. **Four minutes — Drill 1**, the mixed column, cold, once through. Then again with the labels said before reading rather than after.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 4.** The two danger cases and al-Fātiḥa 1:7. Every single day.
3. **Three minutes — Drill 5.** al-Fīl and Quraysh from memory, naming meem rules mid-recitation.
4. **Once, on day one — Drill 7.** Write the table from memory. Then check it and write it again on day five without looking at your first attempt.
5. **The rest — hifz.** Manzil (three surahs), then Sabqi: 113, 112, 111:1–3, all unaided.

Bring to the next session: **the row of Drill 1 you got wrong**, and one meem from your own ṣalāh that you are not confident about. Next session opens the noon chapter — six throat letters and, again, no colour.
