# Lesson 3-20 — Idghām maʿal ghunnah: يَنْمُو, and the four words that break it

## Objectives

- Student can name the four letters of يَنْمُو and merge a noon sākinah or tanwīn into each with a 2-count nasal.
- Student can state the word-boundary condition, and can name all four exception words on sight.
- Student can explain why ي and و carry no shaddah after a merged noon, and can keep the nasal anyway.
- Student can find both instances in `حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ` — the ayah she is memorising today.

## Hook

Write the four exception words on the board, with no rule attached to them yet, and ask her to read all four:

> **ٱلدُّنْيَا · بُنْيَان · صِنْوَان · قِنْوَان**

She will read the first one perfectly — `ٱلدُّنْيَا` is one of the most frequent words in the Qurʾān and she has said it a thousand times. Good. **Now teach the rule**, get her merging noons into ي, and then put `ٱلدُّنْيَا` back in front of her.

Roughly half of students, within ten minutes of learning the rule, will read it as ***ad-duyyā***.

That is the shape of this lesson, and you should say so out loud when it happens: **the rule you just learned has broken a word you already knew.** She was right before the lesson and wrong after it. The exception is not a footnote — it is the reason the rule has a boundary condition at all, and it is the classic trap of the entire noon chapter.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-19** gave the four-way map of the noon chapter and the arithmetic: **6 + 4 + 2 + 1 + 15 = 28**. Today is the **4**. Point at the map; she is filling in a box, not learning something free-standing.
- **Lesson 3-19** also gave the *distance* explanation: the throat letters were too far from the noon to assimilate. **Today's four are the opposite end of the scale** — near or identical — so the noon does not survive at all.
- **Lesson 3-16** taught idghām shafawī: a meem sākinah merging into a meem, producing one doubled letter on a 2-count nasal. **Today's ن-into-ن is exactly that operation on the other nasal**, and today's ن-into-م is the same operation with the letters crossing over. She has done this before.
- **Lesson 3-16 also taught the naming trap**: a tanwīn or a noon before a shaddah'd meem is *this* rule, not idghām shafawī. She was told the name would arrive today. It has.
- **Lesson 2-01** gave tanwīn. Every rule in this chapter applies to it.

## Teaching sequence

1. **State the rule and its condition together.** A **noon sākinah at the end of a word**, or a **tanwīn**, followed by one of four letters at the **start of the next word**, **merges into that letter**, which becomes doubled, while a **2-count nasal is retained**.

   The noon does not vanish silently. It is absorbed, and its nasal resonance is what you hear for those two counts.

2. **The four letters — يَنْمُو.** ي · ن · م · و, gathered in the word *yanmū*, "it grows". Four letters, one word, no sentence needed. Say the word, write it, and have her recover the letters from it three times.

3. **Say the word-boundary condition as part of the rule, not as a caveat.** "**At the end of a word**" is load-bearing. When these same four letters follow a noon sākinah *inside* one word, the rule is **cancelled**. Tanwīn always sits at the end of a word, so for tanwīn the condition is automatically met and she never has to check.

4. **Now kāmil and nāqiṣ — the technical heart of the lesson.** The four letters do **not** behave alike:

   | Letters | Type | What happens | Shaddah on the page? |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **ن · م** | **kāmil** — complete | letter *and* nasal both merge; the noon is entirely gone | **yes** |
   | **ي · و** | **nāqiṣ** — incomplete | the noon's *body* merges; its **nasal survives** | **no** |

   In `مِّن نِّعْمَةٍ` the ن carries a shaddah: two noons became one doubled noon, and the nasal you hear belongs to *that* doubled noon.

   In `مَن يَقُولُ` the ي carries **no shaddah**: the nasal you hear is the **departed noon's**. It is a leftover, not a property of the yāʾ. That is what *incomplete* means.

   **Have her look at the page and confirm it herself.** The missing shaddah on ي and و is not a printing quirk — it is a ruling written into the orthography. This is the second time this chapter that the muṣḥaf has been quietly telling her the answer; the first was the printed sukūn in lesson 3-19.

5. **Flag the disagreement once, and do not drill it.** A **minority** of scholars hold that the idghām of ي and و is *kāmil* like the others. **This course teaches nāqiṣ**, following the jumhūr and following the Madinah muṣḥaf's own orthography. Say it in one sentence and move on. Full discussion in [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]].

6. **Duration.** Two counts, on the fingers, exactly as in lesson 3-14. This is level 2 on the ghunnah scale — the same length as the doubled meem of `حَمَّالَةَ`, which she memorised last session.

7. **Now the exception, and give it a third of your session.** **الإِظْهَار المُطْلَق** — *iẓhār muṭlaq*, "absolute clarification", also called *iẓhār wājib*. When a noon sākinah meets ي ن م و **inside the same word**, there is **no idghām**. The noon is pronounced clearly, exactly as in lesson 3-19 — but it is called *muṭlaq* because the trigger is **position**, not the throat.

   **The reason is worth giving, because it makes the exception memorable rather than arbitrary:** merging inside a word would erase the boundary between a genuine root letter and an assimilated one. `ٱلدُّنْيَا` would become *ad-duyyā*, and the word would no longer be recoverable from its own sound.

8. **There are exactly four such words in the entire Qurʾān.** Not "a few". Four.

   | Word | Reference |
   |---|---|
   | **ٱلدُّنْيَا** | 2:85, and around 115 further places |
   | **بُنْيَان** | 61:4 — `بُنْيَٰنٌ مَّرْصُوصٌ` |
   | **صِنْوَان** | 13:4 — `صِنْوَانٌ وَغَيْرُ صِنْوَانٍ` |
   | **قِنْوَان** | 6:99 — `قِنْوَانٌ دَانِيَةٌ` |

   All four are **reference examples — outside your memorised sūrahs.** None occurs in al-Fātiḥa or 105–114. Say that; she cannot check these against her own memory and should know it.

   **Teach `ٱلدُّنْيَا` first and spend the most time on it.** It is high-frequency, it was learned early by ear, and *ad-duyyā* is a genuinely easy slip. The other three are effectively vocabulary items.

9. **`بُنْيَٰنٌ مَّرْصُوصٌ` is the best single drill in the set.** One phrase, both behaviours, four letters apart: the **نْ + ي** inside `بُنْيَٰن` gets **no** merge, and the **tanwīn + م** across the boundary gets a **full** one. If she can read that phrase correctly, she has the rule and the exception at once.

10. **Colour, and one honest warning.** Family B paints idghām with ghunnah **`#169777`**, teal-green; the cpfair key is `idghaam_ghunnah` — see [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]]. **Iẓhār muṭlaq, being iẓhār, has no colour and no key.** On a coloured page the noon of `ٱلدُّنْيَا` is plain black — which, as she learned in lessons 3-17 and 3-19, is exactly the signal that nothing happens to it. The system is consistent; make her state why.

Teach the rule from the material she is memorising the same hour. That coincidence was arranged; use it.

**Teacher note on 106:4, again.** The anchor ayah now carries a fourth rule for her: `جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم`. Add it to the card. She has met idghām shafawī and iẓhār ḥalqī in it already; two more are still to come.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — يَنْمُو, cold.** Say the word, then the four letters, then the word again. Then, given each letter, say whether it is kāmil or nāqiṣ:

يَنْمُو → ي · ن · م · و
ي → nāqiṣ · ن → kāmil · م → kāmil · و → nāqiṣ

**Drill 2 — assemble the merge.** Left column apart, right column joined with a two-count nasal on the fingers:

| Apart | Joined |
|---|---|
| حَبْلٌ · مِنْ | حَبْلٌ مِّن |
| مِنْ · مَسَدٍ | مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ |
| بِحِجَارَةٍ · مِنْ | بِحِجَارَةٍ مِّن |
| جُوعٍ · وَءَامَنَهُمْ | جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم |
| مَنْ · يَقُولُ | مَن يَقُولُ |
| مِنْ · نِعْمَةٍ | مِّن نِّعْمَةٍ |

**Drill 3 — shaddah or no shaddah.** Look at the second word only. Say "**kāmil**" if it carries a shaddah, "**nāqiṣ**" if it does not. Then read the phrase:

مِّن نِّعْمَةٍ · مَن يَقُولُ · حَبْلٌ مِّن · جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم · مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ · لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ

**Drill 4 — the nasal on ي and و, where nothing reminds you.** These have no shaddah to prompt you and the nasal is the *only* trace of the noon. Pinch your nose on each; it must block for two counts:

مَن يَقُولُ · جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم · لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ · مِن وَلِىٍّ

**Drill 5 — the four exception words.** Say each one, then say **"one word — no merge"**:

ٱلدُّنْيَا · بُنْيَان · صِنْوَان · قِنْوَان

Then, in reverse: someone says a word, you say whether it merges. Mix in `مَن يَقُولُ` and `مِّن نِّعْمَةٍ`, which do.

**Drill 6 — the double-duty phrase.** Read it ten times. Say what happens at each of the two points before you read:

**بُنْيَٰنٌ مَّرْصُوصٌ** — نْ + ي inside the word: **nothing.** Tanwīn + م across the boundary: **full merge, two counts.**

**Drill 7 — the boundary question, asked every time.** For each of these, say "**one word**" or "**two words**" *before* deciding the rule:

ٱلدُّنْيَا · مَن يَقُولُ · صِنْوَان · مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ · قِنْوَان · جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم · بُنْيَان · بِحِجَارَةٍ مِّن

**Drill 8 — the hifz line, whole.** `فِى جِيدِهَا حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ` — five words, two merges, at reciting pace, ten times.

## Listen-for

**1. Pronouncing the noon before merging it — *min-nyaqūl*.**
- *Listen for:* the noon audible as its own letter, then the merged letter after it. Three beats where there should be two.
- *Why it happens:* the noon is written, and Unit 2 trained her to say what is written. **This is the single most common fault in the rule.**
- *Correction cue:* Drill 2, apart then joined. Then have her say the joined form with the first word's final letter deliberately deleted.

**2. Dropping the nasal on ي and و.**
- *Listen for:* `مَن يَقُولُ` as a clean *ma-yaqūl*, with no hum at all.
- *Why it happens:* there is no shaddah on the page to remind her, and she has just been told the merge is "incomplete", which sounds like "less of everything".
- *Correction cue:* "The missing shaddah means the merge is incomplete, **not** that the nasal is gone." Drill 4, with the pinch.

**3. Merging inside a word — *ad-duyyā*, *buyyān*.**
- *Listen for:* `ٱلدُّنْيَا`, specifically, and specifically in the twenty minutes after you teach the rule. **This is the priority error of the lesson.**
- *Why it happens:* a new rule over-applies, and this rule's condition is invisible unless you are looking for word boundaries.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 7. Make her say "one word" or "two words" out loud **before** the rule. The boundary question has to be asked first or it does not get asked at all.

**4. Failing to hold the shaddah on ن and م.**
- *Listen for:* `مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ` released after one count.
- *Why it happens:* the under-held shadda from lesson 2-05, again. It will keep returning; keep naming it.
- *Correction cue:* Fingers. Two folds.

**5. Calling the whole thing "idghām shafawī".**
- *Listen for:* the naming, not the sound. Ask.
- *Why it happens:* `أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن` and `حَبْلٌ مِّن` are the same picture from the shaddah onward.
- *Correction cue:* Cover the second word. **What changed is the letter before.** A meem changing is a meem rule; a noon or tanwīn changing is this one.

**6. Applying the rule to a noon with a vowel on it.**
- *Listen for:* care over `نَمْلَة` or `مَنَامًا`.
- *Why it happens:* over-application.
- *Correction cue:* "The noon must be closed and it must be at the end of its word."

## Hifz for this session

Revision first: **Manzil**, then **Sabqi**, then the new **Sabaq**. See [[Hifz-Method]].

- **Manzil.** Two surahs from the promoted pool.
- **Sabqi.** al-Ikhlāṣ 112 and al-Masad 111:1–4. When she reaches `أَبِى لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ` in 111:1 — **stop her.** That is today's rule, nāqiṣ, in material she memorised four lessons ago and was told at the time to leave alone. Collect the flag.
- **New Sabaq — al-Masad 111:5.**

> فِى جِيدِهَا حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ

**This is the best line in the Qurʾān for today's rule and she is memorising it today.** Two merges back to back: `حَبْلٌ مِّن` (tanwīn + م) and `مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ` (noon + م). Both kāmil, both two counts, and the second one's shaddah is printed right there.

Have her memorise the ayah first, in the ordinary way, and only then run the rule over it. Hifz work is hifz work; do not turn the Sabaq into a tajwīd drill until the words are secure.

Note also the `ۭ` on `مَّسَدٍۭ` — the small standing meem. That mark is an iqlāb sign and it fires when she runs al-Masad into al-Ikhlāṣ. **Two lessons away.** Flag it, do not teach it.

With this ayah, **al-Masad is complete.** Five ayat, done.

20–40 repetitions; ten extra on any error caught, counted separately.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **One minute — Drill 1.** يَنْمُو, and kāmil-or-nāqiṣ for each letter.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 2.** Apart, then joined, fingers folding.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 4.** The pinch, on ي and و only. This is where the nasal dies first.
4. **Three minutes — Drill 5 and Drill 7.** The four exception words, and the boundary question asked out loud before every phrase. **Every day.**
5. **Two minutes — Drill 6.** `بُنْيَٰنٌ مَّرْصُوصٌ`, ten times.
6. **Three minutes — Drill 8.** `فِى جِيدِهَا حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ` at pace.
7. **The rest — hifz.** Manzil, Sabqi (112 + 111:1–4), then 111:5. al-Masad closes this week.

Bring to the next session: **say `ٱلدُّنْيَا` for me, cold, before we start.** If it survives a week of merging drills, the exception has taken.
