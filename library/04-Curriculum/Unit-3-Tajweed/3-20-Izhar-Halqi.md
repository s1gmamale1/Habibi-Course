# Lesson 3-20 — Iẓhār ḥalqī: the noon that nothing happens to

## Objectives

- Student can name the six throat letters from the mnemonic, in makhraj order, without a list.
- Student can pronounce a noon sākinah or tanwīn plainly before them — full noon, no extra count, no held nasal — and can do it without denasalising.
- Student can state that the rule applies to a **tanwīn** exactly as to a written noon, and can show why.
- Student can read the muṣḥaf's own orthographic signal: a printed sukūn on the noon means iẓhār; a bare noon does not.

## Hook

Take her own hifz and turn it against her. Ask for Quraysh, ayah 4:

> **ٱلَّذِىٓ أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ**

Listen to the two `مِن`s. Most students, at this point in the course, do one of two things: they treat both identically, or — and this is the more interesting failure — they have just spent five lessons holding two-count nasals, so they **hold both**, and `مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ` comes out as *minnn khawf*.

Say it plainly: **same word, twice, two lines apart, and the two are pronounced in opposite ways.** In `مِّن جُوعٍ` the noon is hidden behind a two-count nasal. In `مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ` the noon is simply said — a clean *n*, no hold, and straight into the خ.

Then the sentence that frames the whole chapter: **the noon does not have one behaviour. It has four, and which one you get is decided entirely by the letter that comes next.** Today is the easiest of the four, and it is the one where the answer is *do nothing*.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-15** gave the ghunnah and, crucially, the **levels table**. Go back to it now. Today's noon is **level 4** — the nasal is inherent and cannot be removed, but it is **not prolonged**. She was warned in 3-15 that this distinction was coming; this is where it lands.
- **Lessons 3-16 to 3-19** ran the whole meem chapter. The noon chapter has the same shape — a small number of futures decided by the next letter — but four branches instead of three. Tell her the structure is familiar before you tell her the content is new.
- **Lesson 3-18** taught that iẓhār has **no colour**, and why. **The identical argument applies today**, on the other letter. She should be able to state it back to you before you say it. Ask her to.
- **Lesson 2-01 and 2-02** gave tanwīn and taught her that a tanwīn *is* a noon in disguise — a noon sound with no noon written. Today that becomes load-bearing.
- **Lesson 3-01** gave the makhārij and the throat as a zone. The six letters of today's rule are exactly the throat.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Frame the chapter, and give her the whole map now.** Do not reveal the noon rules one at a time as mysteries. Draw all four today:

   | Next letter | Count | What happens | Lesson |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **6** throat letters | 6 | nothing — say the noon plainly | **today** |
   | **4** — ي ن م و | 4 | merge, with a 2-count nasal | 3-21 |
   | **2** — ل ر | 2 | merge, no nasal at all | 3-22 |
   | **1** — ب | 1 | becomes a hidden meem, 2 counts | 3-23 |
   | **the remaining 15** | 15 | concealed, 2-count nasal | 3-24 / 3-25 |

   **6 + 4 + 2 + 1 + 15 = 28.** Say the arithmetic out loud and let it land. **Every letter of the alphabet is accounted for exactly once. Nothing is left over and nothing is counted twice.** This is not a list of rules to remember; it is a partition of the alphabet, and she will be shown it again in lesson 3-26 as proof that the system is complete.

2. **State the rule.** *Iẓhār* is making a thing plain. A **noon sākinah (نْ)** or a **tanwīn (ـً ـٌ ـٍ)** followed by one of the **six throat letters** is pronounced **clearly from its own makhraj** — no merging, **no prolongation of the nasal**. The noon is simply itself. Nothing happens to it.

3. **Say the tanwīn part explicitly, and do not assume it.** Tanwīn behaves exactly as a written noon sākinah, because that is what it is: a noon sound at the end of a word, written as a doubled vowel instead of a letter. **Every rule in this chapter says "noon sākinah *or tanwīn*", and it means it.** Three of today's in-set examples are tanwīn, not noons. Students who learn the chapter on written noons alone get half of it.

4. **The six letters, in makhraj order.** Deepest throat outward:

   **ء · ه · ع · ح · غ · خ**

   Al-Jamzūrī lists them in exactly that order:

   > هَمْزٌ فَهَاءٌ ثُمَّ عَيْنٌ حَاءُ ⁕ مُهْمَلَتَانِ ثُمَّ غَيْنٌ خَاءُ

   The teaching mnemonic is a sentence, and a sentence is what she should memorise:

   > **أَخِي هَاكَ عِلْمًا حَازَهُ غَيْرُ خَاسِرِ**
   > *"My brother, here is knowledge that a non-loser has acquired."*

   First letters: **أ ه ع ح غ خ**. Recovering six letters from a sentence is fast; recovering six unordered letters from memory is not.

5. **Give the reason, because the reason makes all four rules one idea.** The noon's makhraj is the **tip of the tongue against the gum ridge**. The throat letters are as far from that as a letter in this language can get. **There is nothing for the noon to assimilate toward, so it stays whole.** Every one of the four noon rules is a consequence of distance:

   | Distance from the noon | What happens | Rule |
   |---|---|---|
   | furthest — the throat | nothing | **iẓhār ḥalqī** |
   | nearest, or identical | full merge | idghām — 3-21, 3-22 |
   | the lips (ب) | becomes a meem | iqlāb — 3-23 |
   | everything in between | a half-way state | ikhfāʾ — 3-24 |

   Teach the chapter from this table, not from four unrelated lists. A student who understands the gradient can reconstruct any rule she forgets.

6. **Duration — and the trap.** **No prolongation.** The noon's own nasal is still there; it is a permanent attribute of the letter and cannot be removed. It is simply **not extended**. Level 4 in [[Ghunnah]], not level zero. Have her pinch her nose on `مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ`: the noon should still block. If it does not, she has over-corrected into a dry, denasalised *n*, which is a different error from the one you started the lesson fixing.

7. **The orthography is already telling her, and nobody has ever pointed at it.** Look at the Quraysh ayah again, closely:

   - `مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ` — the noon carries a **printed sukūn**
   - `مِّن جُوعٍ` — the noon is **bare**

   **The muṣḥaf marks the sukūn only when the noon is genuinely pronounced in its written place.** When the noon is concealed or converted, the sukūn is omitted. This is a standing convention across the Uthmānī text, and it is free information she has been looking past for years. Have her check three more noons on any open page and predict the rule from the sukūn alone before reading.

8. **No colour — the same argument as lesson 3-18.** **Iẓhār ḥalqī has no colour in this course and no key in cpfair/quran-tajweed.** Not an oversight: a coloured muṣḥaf marks **modifications**, and iẓhār is the *absence* of a modification. Nothing to annotate.

   So say it again, in the same words as last chapter, because repetition across two chapters is what makes a principle stick: **the absence of colour is itself a signal.** Black-on-white means *"pronounce exactly what is written"*. It does not mean *"no rule here"*. **Half of tajwīd literacy is knowing when to do nothing.** See [[Izhar-Halqi]] and [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]].

9. **Show her how much of it is already in her memory.** Five of the six throat letters have an instance inside the memorised sūrahs:

   | Letter | Where | |
   |---|---|---|
   | **ء** | `كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ` 112:4 · `غَاسِقٍ إِذَا` 113:3 · `حَاسِدٍ إِذَا` 113:5 | tanwīn |
   | **ه** | `عَنْهُ مَالُهُۥ` 111:2 | inside one word |
   | **ع** | `ٱلَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ` 1:7 | inside one word |
   | **ح** | `وَٱنْحَرْ` 108:2 | inside one word |
   | **خ** | `مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ` 106:4 | across a boundary |
   | **غ** | — | **no in-set instance** |

   For غ use `مِّنْ غِلٍّ` (7:43) and label it: **reference example — outside your memorised sūrahs.** Say the gap out loud rather than papering over it; she should know which of her examples she can check against her own memory and which she cannot.

10. **One condition to state and then stop worrying about.** Iẓhār ḥalqī applies **inside a word or across a word boundary**, indifferently. `أَنْعَمْتَ` and `وَٱنْحَرْ` are single words; `مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ` is two. Unlike the merging rules coming in lessons 3-21 and 3-22, there is no boundary condition here at all.

| Text | Ref | Letter | What to say |
|---|---|---|---|
| مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ | 106:4 | خ | *min · khawf*. Clean noon, no hold |
| ٱلَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ | 1:7 | ع | inside one word; the noon does not soften |
| عَنْهُ مَالُهُۥ | 111:2 | ه | her Sabqi from two lessons ago |
| وَٱنْحَرْ | 108:2 | ح | one word, and short enough to have nowhere to hide |
| كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ | 112:4 | ء | **tanwīn**, not a written noon |
| غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ | 113:3 | ء | tanwīn again |
| حَاسِدٍ إِذَا حَسَدَ | 113:5 | ء | tanwīn again — and it closes al-Falaq |
| مِّنْ غِلٍّ | 7:43 | غ | reference example — outside your memorised sūrahs |

**Teacher note on `وَٱنْحَرْ`.** Al-Kawthar's second ayah is three words long and she has had it memorised for years. The noon sits mid-word with a ح immediately after — a throat letter that is genuinely awkward to reach from a closed noon. **This is where a helper vowel appears**: *wa-n-a-ḥar*. Listen for it specifically; it is the second-commonest error in the lesson and it is easiest to catch here.

**Teacher note on the three tanwīn examples.** All three are `ٍ إِذَا` or `ًا أَحَدٌ` — a tanwīn running into a hamzah. Use them as a block, and ask her afterwards which of them had a written noon. The answer is none, and if she has to think about it, the tanwīn point has not landed.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the mnemonic, cold.** Say the sentence, then unpack it letter by letter, then say the six letters alone:

**أَخِي هَاكَ عِلْمًا حَازَهُ غَيْرُ خَاسِرِ** → ء · ه · ع · ح · غ · خ

Do it until the sentence is faster than the list. Then do it backwards, from the letters to the sentence.

**Drill 2 — one noon, six throats.** Say each. The noon is identical in all six; only what follows changes:

مِنْ أَ · مِنْ هَـ · مِنْ عَـ · مِنْ حَـ · مِنْ غَـ · مِنْ خَـ

Then the same with a tanwīn in place of the noon:

عَلِيمًا أَ · عَلِيمًا هَـ · عَلِيمًا عَـ · عَلِيمًا حَـ · عَلِيمًا غَـ · عَلِيمًا خَـ

**Drill 3 — the pinch, to prove the nose is still on.** Every one of these is iẓhār. Pinch on the noon: **the sound should still block, briefly.** If nothing blocks, you have denasalised:

مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ · أَنْعَمْتَ · عَنْهُ · وَٱنْحَرْ · مِّنْ غِلٍّ · كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ

**Drill 4 — the contrast pair, and this is the drill of the lesson.** Read them back to back, ten times:

**مِّن جُوعٍ** — hold two counts, tongue never touches
**مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ** — no hold, tongue touches, clean noon

The first belongs to lesson 3-24 and you are not learning it today. You are learning to **not** do it here.

**Drill 5 — read the sukūn.** Open any page. Find five noon sākinahs. For each one, look at whether a **sukūn is printed** on it, and say "iẓhār" or "something else" *before* you look at the letter that follows. Then check yourself against the letter. You should be right nearly every time.

**Drill 6 — no helper vowels.** These all put a noon straight into a throat letter with no comfortable gap. Say each as one word, with nothing inserted:

وَٱنْحَرْ · أَنْعَمْتَ · يَنْهَوْنَ · مِنْ حَيْثُ · مَنْ عَمِلَ · إِنْ هُوَ

**Drill 7 — in the sūrahs, whole.** Recite al-Kawthar, al-Falaq and al-Fātiḥa from memory. Name the iẓhār out loud as you pass it. Three sūrahs, five instances, no page.

## Listen-for

**1. Holding the noon — turning iẓhār into a concealment.**
- *Listen for:* `مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ` as *minnn khawf*. **This is the priority error**, and the reason is entirely your fault as a teacher: she has spent five lessons being trained to hold nasals.
- *Why it happens:* the most recent instruction wins. It is a scheduling artefact, not a misunderstanding.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 4, the contrast pair, back to back. Do not explain — alternate. The ear sorts it out faster than the intellect.

**2. Inserting a vowel between the noon and the throat letter.**
- *Listen for:* *min-a-khawf*, *wa-n-a-ḥar*. Three syllables where there are two.
- *Why it happens:* the throat letters are physically far from the noon, and the mouth wants a running start. It is the cluster-breaking habit from lesson 2-03 returning under new conditions — diagnose it as that, out loud.
- *Correction cue:* Go back to the clap test from 2-03. Fix the sukūn, then return.

**3. Weakening or swallowing the noon.**
- *Listen for:* a noon that is present but barely audible, especially inside a word.
- *Why it happens:* "nothing happens to it" is heard as "it does not matter".
- *Correction cue:* "Nothing happens to it *means it is fully pronounced*." Say `أَنْعَمْتَ` with the noon deliberately over-articulated, then normally.

**4. Denasalising — a hard, dry *n* with no resonance.**
- *Listen for:* nothing, by ear. **Use the pinch.**
- *Why it happens:* over-correcting error 1. "No ghunnah" is heard as "no nose". It usually appears a week after the lesson, not during it.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 3, and the levels table from 3-15. Level 4, not level zero.

**5. Missing the rule on a tanwīn.**
- *Listen for:* `كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ` or `غَاسِقٍ إِذَا` treated as though no noon were present.
- *Why it happens:* there is no noon on the page. If she learned the rule as "when you see a نْ", tanwīn is invisible to it.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 2's second row. Say the rule name with the phrase "**or tanwīn**" in it, every time, for a month.

**6. Applying it before a throat letter that follows a *vowelled* noon.**
- *Listen for:* care being taken over `نَعْبُدُ` or `مَنَعَ`, where the noon carries a vowel.
- *Why it happens:* over-application in the week after a new rule.
- *Correction cue:* "The noon must be **closed**. A noon with a vowel on it is not in this chapter at all."

20–40 repetitions; ten extra on any error caught, counted separately.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **Two minutes — Drill 1.** The mnemonic, both directions. Every day until the sentence is automatic.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 4.** The contrast pair, `مِّن جُوعٍ` against `مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ`. **This is the most important three minutes of the week.**
3. **Two minutes — Drill 3.** Pinch each one. Confirm the noon still blocks.
4. **Three minutes — Drill 6.** No helper vowels. Slowly, then at pace.
5. **Once, on day three — Drill 5.** The sukūn hunt on an open page.
6. **Three minutes — Drill 7.** al-Kawthar, al-Falaq and al-Fātiḥa from memory, naming the iẓhār as you pass it.
7. **The rest — hifz.** Manzil, Sabqi (112 + 111:1–3), then 111:4 from the page.

Bring to the next session: **Quraysh 106:4, and tell me which noon is held and which is not, before you recite it.** Next session is the first of the merging rules, and one of its examples is the ayah you are about to memorise.
