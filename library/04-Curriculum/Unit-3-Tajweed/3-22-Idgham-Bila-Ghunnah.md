# Lesson 3-22 — Idghām bilā ghunnah: the merge with nothing left over

## Objectives

- Student can merge a noon sākinah or tanwīn completely into a following ل or ر, with **no nasal at all**.
- Student can hold the resulting doubled letter for its ordinary shadda duration, no longer.
- Student can apply the rāʾ rules from lesson 3-10 to a rāʾ that arrived by merging.
- Student can read `وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ` and `فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ` correctly cold, from her own hifz.

## Hook

Ask her to read one line she has known by heart for weeks:

> **وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ** — al-Ikhlāṣ 112:4

She learned this ayah in lesson 3-16. Since then she has spent five sessions holding two-count nasals on almost everything. Listen carefully to `يَكُن لَّهُۥ`.

There will be a hum in it. *yakun-nn-lahū*, or a nasal smeared across the لّ.

Say it: **this merge has no nasal. None. Not a short one — none at all.** The noon is gone, completely, and what remains is a doubled lām held exactly as long as any other shadda. Say the phrase correctly beside her wrong version: *wa-lam yakul-lahū*.

**This is the one merge in the whole chapter where the answer to "how long is the nasal?" is "there isn't one."** And she is going to import a nasal here out of pure habit, every time, for about two weeks. Tell her that now so that when it happens she recognises it rather than getting discouraged.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-20's map.** Six, four, **two**, one, fifteen. Today is the **two**. Third box filled.
- **Lesson 3-21** taught merging with the nasal kept, and taught the word-boundary condition. **Today is the same operation with the nasal removed.** Frame it as a subtraction from last week, not as a new rule.
- **Lesson 3-20's distance argument.** ل and ر are the tongue's two closest neighbours to the noon: all three are articulated at the gum ridge with the tip of the tongue. **The makhārij are so close that the merge is total** — there is no residual position left for a nasal to sound from. That is *why* the nasal goes, and giving the reason is what stops her treating it as an arbitrary exception.
- **Lesson 3-10** gave the rāʾ rules — fatḥa or ḍamma makes it heavy, kasra makes it light. That lesson is a prerequisite today, and you will use it in the last five minutes.
- **Lesson 2-05** gave the ordinary shadda hold. That is the timing today, unchanged.

## Teaching sequence

1. **State the rule.** A **noon sākinah at the end of a word**, or a **tanwīn**, followed by **ل** or **ر**, merges **completely** into it. The nasal is **entirely dropped**. What you hear is a doubled ل or ر, held for the ordinary duration of a shadda, and nothing more.

2. **Say "always kāmil" and mean it.** Both the noon's articulation and its nasal go. **There is no incomplete variant and no scholarly disagreement about this rule anywhere.** After the kāmil/nāqiṣ split of last lesson and the ikhfāʾ/iẓhār minority view of lesson 3-16, she deserves to be told when something is simply settled. This is one of them.

   **In plain terms:** before ل and ر the nūn vanishes completely — and unlike last lesson there is no hum left behind. Nothing at all.

3. **Put the whole merge family in one table, because the contrast is the lesson.**

   | | Letters | Nasal? | Type |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | idghām with ghunnah — 3-21 | **ن · م** | **yes**, 2 counts | kāmil |
   | idghām with ghunnah — 3-21 | **ي · و** | **yes**, 2 counts | **nāqiṣ** |
   | **today** | **ل · ر** | **no** | kāmil |

   Six letters merge. Four keep the nasal, two do not. That is the entire content of two lessons.

4. **The word boundary — same condition, one fewer thing to memorise.** Like last lesson, this rule needs the noon to be at the end of its word. **Unlike last lesson, there is no in-word exception to learn**: the sequence نْ + ل or نْ + ر simply does not arise inside a single Qurʾānic word. Nothing to memorise here. Say so; it is a relief and she has earned one.

5. **Duration.** **Zero counts of nasal.** The doubled ل or ر is held exactly as long as any other shadda — the hold she learned in lesson 2-05. Use the fingers if she needs them, but the count is the shadda count, not a ghunnah count. Do not let her fold two fingers "for the ghunnah" here; the gesture will keep the nasal alive.

   **In plain terms:** no nasal, no counting. The doubled ل or ر is held like any other shadda and that is the end of it.

6. **Both examples she needs are already in her memory, which is unusual.** Most rules in this chapter need a reference example; this one does not:

   - `وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ` — al-Ikhlāṣ **112:4** — a **written noon**
   - `فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ` — al-Māʿūn **107:4** — a **tanwīn**

   One of each, one sūrah apart, both memorised. Teach the entire ل half of the rule from these two and do not open a reference until you reach ر.

7. **For ر there is no in-set example — say so and label it.** Use `مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ` (2:5) and mark it clearly: **reference example — outside your memorised sūrahs.** She should always know which of her examples she can verify against her own memory. `مِّن لَّدُنْهُ` (18:2) is a useful second reference, and it has a bonus: the نْ *inside* `لَّدُنْهُ` is a throat-letter iẓhār from lesson 3-20. One short phrase, two noon rules, opposite treatments.

8. **The rāʾ, and give this the last block of the session.** **The merged ر is still a rāʾ and still takes the rāʾ rules from lesson 3-10.** In `مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ` the rāʾ carries a fatḥa, so it is **heavy** — *mir-rab-bihim*, with a full, thick rāʾ. Students who are concentrating on deleting the nasal routinely produce a thin, apologetic rāʾ, because all their attention is upstream. Say the phrase both ways and let her hear it.

9. **Colour — and an honest warning about it.** Family B paints idghām bilā ghunnah **`#169200`**; the cpfair key is `idghaam_no_ghunnah` — see [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]].

   **This green is almost indistinguishable from the `#169777` of last lesson's rule at small type sizes.** That is a deliberate signal — the two are the same operation — but it is a genuine accessibility hazard: a reader who cannot separate the hues cannot tell "hold a two-count nasal" from "hold none", which is the entire difference between the two rules.

   **Do not let her learn this pair by colour.** Teach it on the letter: ي ن م و hum, ل ر do not. Say the hazard out loud rather than hiding it; a student who trusts the colours and then meets a badly printed page needs to know the colours were never the primary evidence. See [[Idgham-Bila-Ghunnah]].

## Examples

| Text | Ref | Letter | What to say |
|---|---|---|---|
| وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ | 112:4 | ل | *wa-lam yakul-lahū*. Written noon, in your hifz |
| فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ | 107:4 | ل | *fa-wayl-lil-muṣallīn*. Tanwīn, in your hifz |
| مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ | 2:5 | ر | reference example — outside your memorised sūrahs. **Heavy rāʾ** |
| مِّن لَّدُنْهُ | 18:2 | ل | reference example. Two noon rules in three syllables |
| هُدًى لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ | 2:2 | ل | reference example. Tanwīn + ل |
| حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ | 111:5 | م | **the contrast.** Same merge, nasal kept |

**Teacher note on 112:4.** She has had this ayah since lesson 3-16 and has recited it dozens of times with a nasal in it that should not be there. **Fix it in the hifz, not only in the drill.** Have her recite al-Ikhlāṣ whole, three times, immediately after the correction — otherwise the drill version becomes correct and the memorised version stays wrong. This is a real failure mode and it is worth three minutes.

**Teacher note on 107:4.** `فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ` is a tanwīn merge and it is easy to misread as "the ل is doubled because of the article". It is not: the لّ is doubled because a tanwīn went into it. Cover `فَوَيْلٌ` and the shaddah has no explanation. Uncover it and it does.

**Teacher note on `مِّن لَّدُنْهُ`.** Worth ten seconds even though it is a reference: the first noon merges completely into the ل with no nasal; the second noon, four letters later, is pronounced plainly before the ه. **Two noons, one word apart, two different rules.** It is the cheapest demonstration available that the rule is decided letter by letter.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — assemble, with no hum.** Left column apart, right column joined. Say the right column with your nose pinched — **it should make no difference at all**:

| Apart | Joined |
|---|---|
| يَكُنْ · لَهُۥ | يَكُن لَّهُۥ |
| فَوَيْلٌ · لِلْمُصَلِّينَ | فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ |
| مِنْ · رَبِّهِمْ | مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ |
| مِنْ · لَدُنْهُ | مِّن لَّدُنْهُ |
| هُدًى · لِلْمُتَّقِينَ | هُدًى لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ |

**Drill 2 — the pinch, inverted.** In every previous lesson the pinch proved a nasal was present. **Today it proves one is absent.** Pinch and say each phrase. If the sound changes, you are humming:

يَكُن لَّهُۥ · فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ · مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ · هُدًى لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ

**Drill 3 — hum or no hum.** Read across, and say **"hum"** or **"no hum"** before each phrase:

| Hum | No hum |
|---|---|
| حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ | وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ |
| مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ | فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ |
| جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم | مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ |
| مَن يَقُولُ | مِّن لَّدُنْهُ |

Then shuffle the two columns together and do it again without the layout helping you.

**Drill 4 — the six merging letters, called at random.** Someone says a letter — **ي, ن, م, و, ل, ر**. You say "**hum, two counts**" or "**no hum**". Twenty repetitions. This is the drill that has to be automatic before lesson 3-26.

**Drill 5 — the rāʾ keeps its own rules.** Read each and say whether the merged rāʾ is heavy or light *before* you say it:

مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ — fatḥa, **heavy**
مِّن رِّزْقٍ — kasra, **light**
مِّن رَّسُولٍ — fatḥa, **heavy**

**Drill 6 — the hold, not the hum.** The doubled ل or ر must still be held for a full shadda. Say each one twice: once released early, once held properly, and hear the difference:

يَكُن لَّهُۥ · فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ · مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ

**Drill 7 — in the sūrahs, whole.** Recite al-Ikhlāṣ and al-Māʿūn from memory. The two instances must be right *in the recitation*, not only in the drill.

## Listen-for

**1. Adding a nasal, carried over from يَنْمُو.**
- *Listen for:* a hum before the لّ or رّ. **This is the priority error by a wide margin**, and it is a scheduling artefact: she spent last week being trained to hum on every merge.
- *Why it happens:* the most recently practised instruction wins under load.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 2 — pinch and repeat. The pinch is the fastest correction available and she can run it alone.

**2. Not fully doubling the ل or ر.**
- *Listen for:* the shadda released after one count, so the phrase sounds hurried.
- *Why it happens:* she has been told "no ghunnah" and hears it as "nothing to hold". The nasal goes; **the hold does not.**
- *Correction cue:* Drill 6, both ways. Then the ordinary shadda drill from lesson 2-05 on `كُلُّ` and `رَبِّ`, to reconnect the timing to something she already owns.

**3. Pronouncing the noon before merging — *min-rabbihim*.**
- *Listen for:* the noon audible as its own letter.
- *Why it happens:* the same fault as in lesson 3-21, and for the same reason: the noon is written.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 1, apart then joined. Then delete the noon deliberately: "say *mir*, then *rabbihim*, then join them."

**4. A thin rāʾ where it should be heavy.**
- *Listen for:* `مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ` with a light, English-sounding *r*.
- *Why it happens:* all her attention is on suppressing the nasal, and the rāʾ rules from lesson 3-10 fall out of memory two letters later.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 5. Make her name heavy or light **before** reading. Naming is what keeps the rule alive under load.

**5. Reading the doubled ل of `لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ` as the article.**
- *Listen for:* the *naming*, not the sound — ask her why the ل is doubled.
- *Why it happens:* she spent lesson 3-13 on lām shamsiyyah, and a doubled ل at the start of a word has an obvious story attached to it.
- *Correction cue:* Cover the preceding word. If the shaddah has no explanation without it, the shaddah came from the merge.

**6. Missing the rule on a tanwīn.**
- *Listen for:* `فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ` read as two separate words.
- *Why it happens:* no noon on the page.
- *Correction cue:* Same as lesson 3-20: say the rule with "**or tanwīn**" in it, out loud, every time.

## Hifz for this session

Revision first: **Manzil**, then **Sabqi**, then the new **Sabaq**. See [[Hifz-Method]].

- **Manzil.** Two surahs from the promoted pool.
- **Sabqi.** al-Masad **111**, now complete — five ayat, recited whole and unaided. Also al-Ikhlāṣ 112 if it has not yet promoted. **When she reaches 112:4, listen for the hum.** That is today's rule inside her own memorised text, and it is where the correction has to actually take.
- **New Sabaq — an-Naṣr 110:1.**

> إِذَا جَآءَ نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلْفَتْحُ

One ayah, and it is a change of texture: after al-Masad's short, hard lines, an-Naṣr's ayat are longer and smoother. Expect the first day to be slower than she anticipates.

Nothing in this ayah is today's rule — `نَصْرُ` has a vowelled noon, not a closed one. **Ask her to confirm that herself.** Being able to look at a line and say "no noon rule here" is the skill, and she has now had four lessons' worth of the chapter.

20–40 repetitions; ten extra on any error caught, counted separately.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **Three minutes — Drill 1 and Drill 2.** Assemble, then pinch. The pinch every single day; it is the self-correction tool for this rule.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 3.** Hum or no hum, said out loud before each phrase.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 4.** The six letters, called at random. Get someone to call them if you can; if not, write them on six slips and draw.
4. **Two minutes — Drill 5.** The rāʾ rules on a merged rāʾ.
5. **Two minutes — Drill 6.** The hold, both ways.
6. **Three minutes — Drill 7.** al-Ikhlāṣ and al-Māʿūn from memory, listening for the two instances.
7. **The rest — hifz.** Manzil, Sabqi (111 whole + 112), then 110:1.

Bring to the next session: **al-Ikhlāṣ, recited from memory, with 112:4 clean.** If the hum is still there after a week, we will drill it before we start anything new.
