# Lesson 3-22 — Iqlāb: one letter, and a mark she has read for years

## Objectives

- Student can state the rule in one sentence — noon or tanwīn, before **ب** only, becomes a concealed meem held for two counts.
- Student can recognise the small standing meem `ـۢ` on the page and can say that it is an instruction, not a letter.
- Student can produce the light-contact lip posture and can say why iqlāb and ikhfāʾ shafawī sound alike but are two rules.
- Student can point to the five iqlāb signs inside her own memorised sūrahs and explain when each one fires.

## Hook

Do not start with the rule. Start with a mark she has been looking at for years and never asked about.

Write the last word of al-Ikhlāṣ:

> **أَحَدٌۢ**

Ask her what the little shape above the tanwīn is. She will not know. Almost nobody does — it is not in the reading primers, it is not explained in the mushaf legend she has, and she has recited past it several thousand times.

Then write the last words of the four sūrahs beside it:

> **مَّأْكُولٍۭ** (105:5) · **خَوْفٍۭ** (106:4) · **تَوَّابًۢا** (110:3) · **مَّسَدٍۭ** (111:5)

**Every sūrah she has memorised in the last month ends with this mark.**

Then the payoff: it is a **small standing meem**, and it is telling her that when she runs one sūrah into the next — `أَحَدٌۢ` straight into `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ` — **the noon sound at the end of the first word turns into a meem.** She has almost certainly been doing it correctly by imitation for years. Today it gets a name and a reason.

Then name the error, because it is coming: **she will press her lips.** Every student does, and it converts the rule into a plain meem.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-19's map.** Six, four, two, **one**, fifteen. Today is the **one**. Fourth box filled; one left.
- **Lesson 3-15** gave her the exact lip posture this rule needs — lips touching without pressure, nasal running behind them for two counts — on a *meem* before ب. **Today is the same posture starting from a noon.** She does not need to learn a new sound. She needs to learn a new road to it.
- **Lesson 3-15 also warned her this was coming**, in one sentence, seven lessons ago. Collect that flag; it makes the course feel like a plan rather than a sequence.
- **Lesson 3-14** gave the nasal and the two counts. Unchanged.
- **Lesson 2-01** gave tanwīn, and **lesson 2-02** taught that a tanwīn disappears when you stop. That fact does real work today.

## Teaching sequence

1. **State the rule.** *Iqlāb* is turning a thing over — converting it. A **noon sākinah or tanwīn** followed by **ب** is **converted into a hidden meem** (*mīm mukhfāh*), pronounced with a **2-count nasal**. Inside a word or across a word boundary; there is no boundary condition.

   The noon is not said. **And the meem is not fully said either.** What is produced is a nasal held at the lips, on its way into the ب.

2. **One letter. Say the number.** ب, and nothing else. Iqlāb is the narrowest of the four noon rules — **one letter against ikhfāʾ's fifteen.** There is nothing to memorise; if the next letter is a bāʾ, this is the rule.

3. **Give the reason, in one breath.** The ب is a lip letter. Reaching it from the noon's gum-ridge position while still holding a nasal is mechanically awkward, and the nearest nasal the lips can make is a meem. **So the tongue gives up its position and the nasal moves to the lips.** The rule is a shortcut the mouth takes on its own; the tradition wrote it down rather than inventing it.

4. **The posture — and this is where the session's minutes go.** Fingertip against the lips, exactly as in lesson 3-15. **The lips come together lightly, without pressure.** They must not:
   - **press shut** — that produces a clear, fully-formed meem, which is a different ruling;
   - **stay apart** — that leaves the original noon audible.

   They rest against each other just enough to close the mouth, the nasal runs for two counts, and only then is the ب released. **The diagnostic, unchanged from lesson 3-15: if you can feel your lips tense, you are pressing.**

5. **Now the pairing with ikhfāʾ shafawī — teach them adjacent, deliberately.**

   | | Written letter | Becomes | Colour |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **Iqlāb** (today) | **ن** or tanwīn before ب | a concealed meem | `#26BFFD` |
   | Ikhfāʾ shafawī — 3-15 | **م** before ب | a concealed meem | `#D500B7` |

   **Both end at the same place. One of them travelled there.** The sounds are near-identical; the rules differ in **origin, not in output**. Teach the *cause*, never the sound — a student who tries to tell these apart by ear is being asked to do something impossible.

   `أَنۢبِئْهُم بِأَسْمَآئِهِمْ` (2:33) puts both in one phrase, four letters apart: an iqlāb, then an ikhfāʾ shafawī. **The mouth does the same thing twice, from two different written letters.** It is the ideal drill for the pair.

6. **How the muṣḥaf marks it.** A **small standing meem** — `ـۢ` — printed **above the noon**, or **in place of one stroke of the tanwīn**:
   - `أَنۢبِئْهُم`, `مِّنۢ بَعْدِهِۦ` — above the noon
   - `خَوْفٍۭ`, `أَحَدٌۢ`, `مَّسَدٍۭ` — replacing a stroke of the tanwīn

   **The small meem is not a vowel and is never pronounced as a full meem.** It is a pronunciation instruction: *"here, the noon has become a nasal at the lips."* Learners who read it as a written letter produce *an-m-baʾ*, with both letters in it.

7. **And the orthography again tells her, if she looks.** The Uthmānī text **omits the sukūn** on an iqlāb noon — `أَنۢبِئْهُم`, not `أَنْۢبِئْهُم` — for exactly the reason it omitted it on the concealed noon of `مِّن جُوعٍ`: **the noon is not being pronounced in its written place.** Compare `مِّنْ` in lesson 3-19, which keeps its sukūn. Three lessons now have turned on this one convention. Point at it each time until she starts checking it unprompted.

8. **Where it lives in her own memory — an unexpected answer.** Earlier course material assumed iqlāb had no clean example in the hifz set. **Checked against the corpus, that is half right:**

   - **There is no ayah-internal iqlāb anywhere** in al-Fātiḥa or sūrahs 105–114. Confirmed by a full scan. The nearest candidate, `تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ` (105:4), is a **meem** before ب — lesson 3-15's rule, not this one.
   - **But five ayat in the hifz set carry the iqlāb sign**, and it is real:

   | Ref | Word | Fires into |
   |---|---|---|
   | 105:5 | `كَعَصْفٍ مَّأْكُولٍۭ` | `بِسْمِ` of Quraysh |
   | 106:4 | `خَوْفٍۭ` | `بِسْمِ` of al-Māʿūn |
   | 110:3 | `تَوَّابًۢا` | `بِسْمِ` of al-Masad |
   | 111:5 | `مَّسَدٍۭ` | `بِسْمِ` of al-Ikhlāṣ |
   | 112:4 | `أَحَدٌۢ` | `بِسْمِ` of al-Falaq |

   When a reciter runs one sūrah into the next through the basmalah, the **final tanwīn meets the ب of `بِسْمِ`** — and iqlāb applies. The muṣḥaf prints the small meem there precisely because reciting the sūrahs consecutively is the normal case.

   **Pedagogically this is the best possible example**, because she already does it in ṣalāh every day without knowing what she is doing, and it explains a mark she has been staring at for years.

9. **State the condition on it, because it is itself a lesson.** **The rule only fires on continuation.** If she **stops** at the end of the sūrah, the tanwīn is dropped — she learned that in lesson 2-02 — and **there is no iqlāb at all.** Nothing to convert. Have her say `أَحَدٌۢ` twice: once stopping, once running into `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ`. Two different endings from the same written word.

10. **For iqlāb inside a single ayah, use a reference example and label it.** There is none in her memorised sūrahs:
    - `أَنۢبِئْهُم` — al-Baqarah **2:33**, inside one word
    - `مِّنۢ بَعْدِهِۦ` — Āl ʿImrān **3:160**, across a word boundary
    - `لَيُنۢبَذَنَّ` — al-Humazah **104:4**, one sūrah before the hifz set

    All three are **reference examples — outside your memorised sūrahs.** Say the label each time. `لَيُنۢبَذَنَّ` is the one to lean on if she wants to add a sūrah: al-Humazah sits directly before al-Fīl and is short.

11. **Colour.** Family B paints iqlāb **`#26BFFD`**, sky blue; the cpfair key is `iqlab` — see [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]]. It is a distinctive colour and a rare one. Have her find the blue at the end of al-Ikhlāṣ on a coloured page.

**Teacher note on the stop.** Do not skip step 9. A student who learns "`أَحَدٌۢ` has an iqlāb" without the continuation condition will produce a lip-nasal at the end of al-Ikhlāṣ when she stops there — which is wrong, and which sounds odd enough that it will get corrected by someone else later, confusingly.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the three postures, again.** Fingertip on the lips. Say each and report what you felt:

| Say | Lips |
|---|---|
| أُمّ | **pressed** — a doubled meem, not this rule |
| أَنۢبِـ | **barely touching** |
| بِـ alone | closed, then popped |

**Drill 2 — two counts behind the lips.** Fold two fingers during the hold. The ب does not open until the second finger lands:

أَنۢبِئْهُم · مِّنۢ بَعْدِهِۦ · لَيُنۢبَذَنَّ

**Drill 3 — the surah joins, out loud.** Recite the end of each sūrah straight into the basmalah of the next, without a break. **This is the drill that matters, because it is what she does in ṣalāh:**

- `... كَعَصْفٍ مَّأْكُولٍۭ` → `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ`
- `... مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ` → `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ`
- `... كَانَ تَوَّابًۢا` → `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ`
- `... حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ` → `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ`
- `... كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ` → `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ`

**Drill 4 — stop or continue.** Say each ending twice: once **stopping**, once **continuing** into the basmalah. Name what happened each time:

أَحَدٌۢ · مَّسَدٍۭ · تَوَّابًۢا

Stopping: the tanwīn is dropped, no iqlāb. Continuing: iqlāb, two counts.

**Drill 5 — noon or meem?** For each phrase, say which letter was **written** before the rule applied. Do not try to hear it — look:

أَنۢبِئْهُم · تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ · مِّنۢ بَعْدِهِۦ · هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ · لَيُنۢبَذَنَّ · أَحَدٌۢ بِسْمِ

**Drill 6 — the pair, in one phrase.** `أَنۢبِئْهُم بِأَسْمَآئِهِمْ` — ten times. Name the two rules as you pass them: **iqlāb**, then **ikhfāʾ shafawī**. Same posture twice, four letters apart.

**Drill 7 — find the mark.** Open any page of the muṣḥaf. Find three small standing meems. For each, say what letter is written underneath it and what letter is coming next. The answer is always: a noon or a tanwīn, and a ب.

## Listen-for

**1. Pressing the lips fully shut.**
- *Listen for:* a clean, fully-formed meem — *am-baʾ* with a real meem in it. **Priority error**, identical to lesson 3-15's, and it will come back even if it was fixed then.
- *Why it happens:* every language she speaks presses its *m*.
- *Correction cue:* Fingertip on the lips. "Less. Again. Less." Do not explain; adjust by feel.

**2. Omitting the nasal — releasing straight into the ب.**
- *Listen for:* *ambaʾ* with no hold at all.
- *Why it happens:* the posture is the interesting new part and the timing gets dropped. Same pattern as every rule in this chapter.
- *Correction cue:* Fingers, two folds. Do not let her run at speed until day three.

**3. Pronouncing the original noon — *an-baʾ*.**
- *Listen for:* a genuine *n* before the ب.
- *Why it happens:* the noon is written on the page, and the small meem is small.
- *Correction cue:* "The letter on the page is not the letter in your mouth. The mark above it is the letter in your mouth."

**4. Reading the small meem as a written letter.**
- *Listen for:* *an-m-baʾ* — a noon **and** a meem, both audible.
- *Why it happens:* it looks like a letter and it is printed at letter height.
- *Correction cue:* "It is an instruction, not a letter. It is telling you what to do with the noon, not adding something to it."

**5. Applying it at a stop.**
- *Listen for:* a lip-nasal at the end of al-Ikhlāṣ when she stops there.
- *Why it happens:* she has bound the rule to the *mark* rather than to the *sequence*, and the mark is still printed whether she stops or not.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 4. "No tanwīn, no rule. You dropped the tanwīn when you stopped."

**6. Confusing it with ikhfāʾ shafawī.**
- *Listen for:* the naming. **Do not listen for the sound — the sound is genuinely the same.** Ask her which letter was written.
- *Why it happens:* it is not a mistake of the ear; the ear is right. Only the page distinguishes them.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 5. Look, do not listen.

1. **One minute — Drill 1.** Three postures, fingertip on the lips.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 3.** The sūrah joins. **Every day.** This is the drill that puts the rule where she actually uses it.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 4.** Stop or continue, said out loud.
4. **Two minutes — Drill 2 and Drill 6.** Two counts on the fingers; then `أَنۢبِئْهُم بِأَسْمَآئِهِمْ` ten times.
5. **Two minutes — Drill 5.** Noon or meem — look, do not listen.
6. **Once, on day two — Drill 7.** Find three small meems on an open page.
7. **The rest — hifz.** Manzil (run consecutively), Sabqi (111 + 112 + 110:1), then 110:2.

Bring to the next session: **recite al-Fīl straight into Quraysh, without stopping.** Tell me where the iqlāb was before you start. Next session is the last box on the map — fifteen letters, and it is the one you will meet most often.
