# Lesson 3-24 — Ikhfāʾ ḥaqīqī I: the tongue never arrives

## Objectives

- Student can recite al-Jamzūrī's fifteen-word couplet and recover the fifteen letters from it.
- Student can produce the concealed noon — tongue never touching the gum ridge, nasal running for two counts — and can feel that her tongue did not arrive.
- Student can state the two boundaries: touching the makhraj gives iẓhār, doubling the next letter gives idghām, and ikhfāʾ is neither.
- Student can name the rule in `مِّن جُوعٍ` and complete the five-rule card for Quraysh 106:4.

## Hook

Go back to the ayah that has been on the card since lesson 3-17.

> **ٱلَّذِىٓ أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ** — Quraysh 106:4

Four of its five rules now have names. **`مِّن جُوعٍ` is the last one, and today it gets its name.**

Ask her to read the two `مِن`s back to back. By now she should get `مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ` right — plain noon, no hold. Listen to what she does with `مِّن جُوعٍ`. There are two likely failures and they are opposite:

- **She touches.** The tongue goes to the gum ridge, a real noon comes out, and the phrase becomes an iẓhār. *min · jūʿ*.
- **She doubles.** The ج gets a shadda that is not there. *mij-jūʿ*.

Say the rule as the space between those two errors: **the noon is neither said nor deleted. It is hidden.** The tongue **leaves the gum ridge alone** and moves straight into position for the ج, while the nasal runs for two counts. Nothing touches. Nothing doubles.

Then the practical point: **this is the rule she will meet most often.** Fifteen letters out of twenty-eight — more than half the alphabet. Whatever else she gets wrong in this chapter, this is the one that will be wrong most frequently.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-20's map.** Six, four, two, one, **fifteen**. This is the last box. After today, the noon chapter is closed and every letter of the alphabet has a home.
- **Lesson 3-16** taught *ikhfāʾ shafawī*, "labial concealment" — a **meem** hidden at the lips. Today's is *ikhfāʾ ḥaqīqī*, the **"true"** concealment, because it is a **noon** that is genuinely concealed. Same idea, different letter, different place in the mouth. The naming is a contrast, not a hierarchy — tell her that, because "true" sounds like a value judgement on the other one.
- **Lesson 3-15** gave the nasal and the two counts. Level 3 on the levels table.
- **Lesson 3-03** gave the **isti'lā** letters — **خص ضغط قظ** — the heavy ones. That lesson is a prerequisite today and you will use it in the last fifteen minutes.
- **Lesson 3-20** gave the orthographic tell: a printed sukūn means the noon is pronounced in its written place. **Today's noons carry no sukūn.** She should be able to predict the rule from the page before reading it.

## Teaching sequence

1. **State the rule.** A **noon sākinah or tanwīn** followed by any of the **fifteen remaining letters** is pronounced in a state **between iẓhār and idghām** — **concealed**, with a **2-count nasal**, and **no shaddah** on the following letter. Inside a word or across a boundary; no boundary condition.

2. **Say the mechanism physically, because the definition is abstract and the mechanism is not.** **The tongue never touches the noon's makhraj.** It leaves the gum ridge alone and instead moves into position for the letter that is coming, while the nasal sound runs. The noon is neither pronounced nor deleted — it is *hidden*.

   Have her do it slowly with `أَنتُمْ`: start the nasal, and while it is running, put her tongue where the **ت** goes. Not to the noon's place first. Straight to the ت's place, and hum on the way.

3. **Define it by its two boundaries — this is how she will actually diagnose herself.**

   | If she… | She has produced | Rule |
   |---|---|---|
   | touches the gum ridge | a real noon | iẓhār — 3-20 |
   | doubles the following letter | a merge | idghām — 3-21 |
   | does neither, and hums for two | **ikhfāʾ** | **today** |

   Two named errors, one target between them. Say all three out loud every time you correct her.

4. **The fifteen letters.** Everything that is not one of the six throat letters, the four of يَنْمُو, the two of ل ر, or ب:

   **ص · ذ · ث · ك · ج · ش · ق · س · د · ط · ز · ف · ت · ض · ظ**

   **Do not have her memorise fifteen loose letters.** Al-Jamzūrī's mnemonic is a **fifteen-word couplet**, and the **first letter of each word** is one of the fifteen:

   > صِفْ ذَا ثَنَا كَمْ جَادَ شَخْصٌ قَدْ سَمَا ⁕ دُمْ طَيِّبًا زِدْ فِي تُقًى ضَعْ ظَالِمَا
   > *"Describe one of praise — how generously a person of standing gave! Remain good, increase in piety, put aside one who wrongs."*

   **Learn the couplet as language, not as a letter list.** Recovering fifteen letters from a remembered sentence is fast; recovering fifteen unordered letters from memory is not. Have her recite the couplet, then unpack it word by word, then recite it again.

   *(Some printings give the second half as `دَامَ طَيِّبًا`. Same letters, same rule — if she meets that version, it is not a different mnemonic.)*

5. **Then give her the shortcut she will actually use at speed.** She does not need to scan fifteen letters. She needs to ask, in order:
   - Is it a **throat letter**? → iẓhār.
   - Is it in **يَنْمُو**? → merge, with nasal.
   - Is it **ل** or **ر**? → merge, no nasal.
   - Is it **ب**? → iqlāb.
   - **Otherwise → ikhfāʾ.**

   **Ikhfāʾ is the default.** Thirteen letters to check against, and everything else falls through to today's rule. That is a much smaller memory load than fifteen, and it is the same answer. Teach the couplet for the exam and the fall-through for reading.

6. **No shaddah — and the muṣḥaf shows it.** This is the whole difference between ikhfāʾ and idghām, and it is printed on the page: in `مِّن جُوعٍ` the ج carries **no shaddah**, while in `مِّن نِّعْمَةٍ` (lesson 3-21) the ن does. **A learner who doubles the following letter has produced idghām by mistake.** Put the two phrases side by side and have her point at the shaddah that is missing.

7. **Duration.** Two counts, on the fingers. Level 3 on the ghunnah scale from lesson 3-15 — the same length as everything else in this chapter that holds.

8. **Now heaviness, introduced but not yet drilled — the systematic version is next lesson.** The nasal of ikhfāʾ is **not fixed in weight**. It takes the heaviness of the letter it is running into:

   - **Heavy** before the isti'lā letters, which among the fifteen are **ص ض ط ظ ق**: `عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ` (107:5)
   - **Light** before the other ten: `وَلَآ أَنتُمْ` (109:3)

   Say the two phrases back to back and let her hear that the *hum itself* changes colour. **This is the earliest place in the syllabus where tafkhīm and tarqīq stop being a property of letters and become a property of a sound in context.** It is worth slowing down for, and it gets a full lesson next session. Today: introduce it, demonstrate it twice, move on. See [[Istila]].

9. **Colour.** Family B paints ikhfāʾ ḥaqīqī **`#9400A8`**, violet; the cpfair key is `ikhfa` — see [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]]. **Because the rule covers fifteen letters, this is the colour she will meet most often on a coloured page.** Hand her a page and have her count the violets in one line. There will be more than she expects.

10. **Close the map.** Six, four, two, one, fifteen. **Twenty-eight.** Every letter of the alphabet decides the noon's fate, exactly one way, with nothing left over and nothing counted twice. Say it now, and say it again in lesson 3-26 with the arithmetic on the board — it is the cleanest available proof that the system is complete.

**Eight words. Five rulings. All of them in a sūrah she recites from memory.** Have her name all five off the card today, and off no card in lesson 3-26.

**Teacher note on 114:4.** `مِن شَرِّ ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ ٱلْخَنَّاسِ` is unusually generous: a concealed noon at the start and a **doubled** noon four words later in `ٱلْخَنَّاسِ`. Two nasals, both two counts, and completely different operations. Ask her to say which is which and why. If she says "they sound the same", she is nearly right — the *lengths* are the same; the tongue position is not.

**Teacher note on 109:3.** `أَنتُمْ` is the counter-example to the boundary conditions she has just spent three lessons on. Idghām needs a word boundary; **ikhfāʾ does not.** She will over-apply the boundary rule for about a week; `أَنتُمْ` is the cheapest correction.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the couplet.** Recite it. Then unpack it, word by word, naming the letter. Then recite it again:

> صِفْ ذَا ثَنَا كَمْ جَادَ شَخْصٌ قَدْ سَمَا ⁕ دُمْ طَيِّبًا زِدْ فِي تُقًى ضَعْ ظَالِمَا

ص · ذ · ث · ك · ج · ش · ق · س · د · ط · ز · ف · ت · ض · ظ

**Drill 2 — the fall-through, out loud.** Someone calls a letter at random from the whole alphabet. You answer with **one** of: "throat", "yanmū", "lām-rāʾ", "bāʾ", "**ikhfāʾ**". Thirty repetitions. Speed matters more than elegance here.

**Drill 3 — the three outcomes, on one syllable.** Say `مِنْ` followed by each letter, three ways, so you can hear the two errors you are avoiding:

| | Say it as |
|---|---|
| **touching** | *min · jūʿ* — wrong, that is iẓhār |
| **doubling** | *mij-jūʿ* — wrong, that is idghām |
| **concealed** | the target: nasal for two, tongue goes straight to the ج |

Do all three on: مِّن جُوعٍ · مِن شَرِّ · مِّن سِجِّيلٍ · مِن قَبْلُ

**Drill 4 — the tongue check.** Say `أَنتُمْ` very slowly and notice **where your tongue is** during the hum. It should already be at the **ت**. If it went to the noon's place first and then moved, you produced two letters instead of one concealed one.

**Drill 5 — two counts, no shaddah.** Fold two fingers on the nasal. Then check: is the following letter doubled? It must not be:

مِّن جُوعٍ · مِن شَرِّ · نَارًا ذَاتَ · وَلَآ أَنتُمْ · عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ · مِّن سِجِّيلٍ

**Drill 6 — the contrast pair, still.** From lesson 3-20, and it does not retire:

**مِّن جُوعٍ** — hold, conceal
**مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ** — no hold, say it plainly

Ten times, alternating. Then look at the page: one noon has a printed sukūn, one does not.

**Drill 7 — heavy and light, two examples only.** Say them back to back and listen to the hum, not the letters:

**عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ** — heavy · **وَلَآ أَنتُمْ** — light

That is all for today. The full treatment is next lesson.

**Drill 8 — in the sūrahs.** Recite al-Falaq and an-Nās from memory. Five instances of `مِن شَرِّ` between them. Every one gets two counts and no doubled ش.

## Listen-for

**1. Touching the noon's makhraj — producing iẓhār.**
- *Listen for:* a clean, audible *n* before the following letter.
- *Why it happens:* the noon is written and she has been told for two units to say what is written. It is also, physically, the easiest thing to do.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 4. "Where was your tongue?" Make her answer. She usually knows.

**2. Doubling the following letter — producing idghām.**
- *Listen for:* a shadda that is not on the page. *mij-jūʿ*, *miš-šarr*.
- *Why it happens:* three of the last four lessons were merging rules. Over-application on schedule.
- *Correction cue:* Point at the page. "Where is the shaddah?" There isn't one. **The absence of the shaddah is the instruction.**

**3. Dropping the nasal entirely on ف and ك.**
- *Listen for:* no hum at all before these two specifically. **These are the two the ear forgives most easily and therefore the two that decay first.**
- *Why it happens:* ف and ك are far from the noon and the join is comfortable without a nasal, so nothing forces the issue.
- *Correction cue:* Drill them separately, weekly, after the rest of the rule is solid. Pinch on each.

**4. A thin nasal before ص ض ط ظ ق.**
- *Listen for:* `عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ` with a light, forward hum.
- *Why it happens:* she has not yet been taught the weight rule systematically — that is next lesson. Flag it today; do not treat it as a fault yet.
- *Correction cue:* Say it heavy for her, twice. Note it for next session.

**5. Missing the rule on a tanwīn.**
- *Listen for:* `نَارًا ذَاتَ` read with no hold.
- *Why it happens:* no noon on the page. The same fault as in lessons 3-20 and 3-22.
- *Correction cue:* Say the rule with "**or tanwīn**" in it, every time.

**6. Looking for a word boundary that is not required.**
- *Listen for:* hesitation on `أَنتُمْ` or `كُنتُمْ`, or an iẓhār produced there deliberately.
- *Why it happens:* lessons 3-21 and 3-22 both had a boundary condition and she has generalised it.
- *Correction cue:* "Merging needs a boundary. Concealing does not." `أَنتُمْ` and `أَنفُسَهُمْ` back to back.

**7. Adding a vowel — *min-a-jūʿ*.**
- *Listen for:* three beats instead of two.
- *Why it happens:* the cluster-breaking habit from lesson 2-03, which returns whenever a join is hard.
- *Correction cue:* Slow the whole phrase rather than the noon, and rebuild.

The longest ayah in the sūrah and the last one — **an-Naṣr completes today.** Three things in it she now owns:

- `إِنَّهُۥ` — doubled noon, two counts of nasal. Lesson 3-15, and it was one of that lesson's examples.
- `تَوَّابًۢا` — a doubled wāw, and then **one of the five iqlāb signs** from last session. When she runs an-Naṣr into al-Masad, that tanwīn becomes a lip-nasal. She was told last week this was coming; collect it.
- `وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ` — a hamzat al-waṣl and a sākin rāʾ. Lesson 2-09 and lesson 3-11 respectively. Nothing new, but it is a dense word and it will need repetitions.

Memorise first, analyse second. Do not let the tajwīd commentary eat the repetition count.

20–40 repetitions; ten extra on any error caught, counted separately.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **Three minutes — Drill 1.** The couplet, then the letters, then the couplet. Every day. By Friday it should be faster than thinking.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 2.** The fall-through, called at random. Write the letters on slips if nobody can call them.
3. **Three minutes — Drill 3.** All three outcomes on each phrase. **Say the wrong ones deliberately** — you cannot avoid an error you have never heard yourself make.
4. **Two minutes — Drill 5.** Two counts, and check for a shaddah that should not be there.
5. **Two minutes — Drill 6.** `مِّن جُوعٍ` against `مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ`.
6. **Three minutes — Drill 8.** al-Falaq and an-Nās from memory.
7. **The rest — hifz.** Manzil, Sabqi (112 + 111 + 110:1–2), then 110:3. **an-Naṣr closes this week.**

Bring to the next session: **the couplet, cold, and the fifteen letters out of it.** Next session takes the same rule and puts weight on it — heavy and light, and the three degrees of concealment.
