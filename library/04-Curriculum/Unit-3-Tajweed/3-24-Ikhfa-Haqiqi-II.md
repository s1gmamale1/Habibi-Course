# Lesson 3-24 — Ikhfāʾ ḥaqīqī II: the hum takes its colour from what follows

## Objectives

- Student can say whether an ikhfāʾ nasal is heavy or light before she says the phrase, from the following letter alone.
- Student can produce the difference audibly on `عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ` against `وَلَآ أَنتُمْ`.
- Student can read all fifteen letters cold, with the nasal intact on ف and ك.
- Student can place a given ikhfāʾ on the aʿlā / awsaṭ / adnā scale and can say why the scale is descriptive, not three rulings.

## Hook

Two phrases, both from sūrahs she has memorised, both the same rule:

> **عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ** (107:5) · **وَلَآ أَنتُمْ** (109:3)

Ask her to read both, then ask a question she will not expect: **"Were those two hums the same sound?"**

Almost every student says yes. They are not. Say them yourself, slowly, back to back, and let the difference sit in the air: the first hum is **thick and back**, the second is **thin and forward**. Same rule. Same two counts. Same tongue behaviour. **Different weight** — and the weight was decided by a letter that had not been pronounced yet.

Then the sentence that makes this lesson worth its own session: **up to now, heavy and light have been properties of letters.** ص is heavy, ت is light; she learned that in lesson 3-03 and has been applying it letter by letter ever since. **Today heaviness stops being a property of a letter and becomes a property of a sound in context.** The nasal has no weight of its own. It borrows one.

Name the error to come: **she will flatten everything.** Fifteen letters, one hum, no variation — because that is what she has been practising for a week and it was correct last lesson.

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-23** gave the rule, the fifteen letters, the couplet, and the mechanism. Everything today is refinement of a rule she can already produce.
- **Lesson 3-03** gave the **isti'lā** letters — **خص ضغط قظ**, the seven that raise the tongue. Five of them are among today's fifteen: **ص ض ط ظ ق**. (خ and غ are throat letters, so they belong to lesson 3-19's rule instead.)
- **Lesson 3-06** gave tafkhīm and tarqīq in general, and taught that the alif inherits its weight from what precedes it. **Today's nasal inherits from what follows.** Same idea, opposite direction — say that out loud; it is the cleanest bridge available.
- **Lesson 3-19** gave the *distance* explanation of the whole chapter. Today's three degrees are that same gradient, measured more finely.

## Teaching sequence

1. **State the weight rule.** The nasal of ikhfāʾ **adopts the heaviness of the letter it is running into.**

   | | Letters | Nasal |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Heavy** — mufakhkham | **ص · ض · ط · ظ · ق** | thick, back |
   | **Light** — muraqqaq | the other ten: **ذ ث ك ج ش س د ز ف ت** | thin, forward |

   Five heavy, ten light. And the five heavy ones she already knows by another name: they are the isti'lā letters from lesson 3-03, minus the two that live in the throat.

2. **Give the mechanism, not just the list.** She does not need to memorise five more letters. **The tongue is already moving toward the next letter while the nasal runs** — that was the whole mechanism of lesson 3-23. If the next letter is one that raises the back of the tongue, **the tongue is already raised while she is humming.** The heaviness is not applied to the nasal; it is a physical consequence of where the tongue has gone. Have her feel it: hum toward a ص, and notice that the back of her tongue is already up.

   Once she has felt that, the rule stops needing to be remembered.

3. **Drill the pair before anything else.** `عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ` / `وَلَآ أَنتُمْ`, ten times, alternating. Both are in her hifz. Do not move on until she can hear it herself — if she cannot hear it, she cannot check herself at home, and the rest of the lesson is decoration.

4. **Then the three degrees of concealment.** The nearer the following letter's makhraj is to the noon's, the closer the ikhfāʾ sits to a merge:

   | Degree | Letters | Character |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Aʿlā** — closest to idghām | **ط · د · ت** | makhraj almost on the noon's own |
   | **Awsaṭ** — middle | the remaining eleven | the default sound |
   | **Adnā** — closest to iẓhār | **ق · ك** | furthest makhraj; the noon is barely veiled |

5. **Say clearly what this classification is for, because it is easy to misuse.** **These are not three different rulings.** All three are a 2-count nasal with no shaddah. The scale is **descriptive**, and its use is **diagnostic**:

   > A student whose `مِن قَبْلُ` sounds like iẓhār and whose `أَنتُمْ` sounds like idghām is **not making two errors**. She is exaggerating a real gradient.

   That single sentence will save you from mis-correcting a student at least twice this month. Tell her too — a learner who has been told her `مِن قَبْلُ` sounds "too plain" needs to know the letter is genuinely near that end of the scale.

6. **Now run all fifteen letters, systematically.** This is the consolidation the lesson exists for. Take them in couplet order and give each one a phrase. Six of the fifteen have an instance inside her memorised sūrahs; nine do not, and each of those must be labelled **reference example — outside your memorised sūrahs**:

   | Letter | Phrase | Ref | In her hifz? | Weight |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | **ص** | عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ | 107:5 | **yes** | heavy |
   | **ذ** | نَارًا ذَاتَ لَهَبٍ | 111:3 | **yes** | light |
   | **ث** | مِن ثَمَرَةٍ | 2:25 | reference | light |
   | **ك** | مِّن كُلِّ أَمْرٍ | 97:4 | reference | light — *adnā* |
   | **ج** | مِّن جُوعٍ | 106:4 | **yes** | light |
   | **ش** | مِن شَرِّ | 113:2 | **yes** | light |
   | **ق** | مِن قَبْلُ | 2:25 | reference | heavy — *adnā* |
   | **س** | مِّن سِجِّيلٍ | 105:4 | **yes** | light |
   | **د** | مِّن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ | 2:23 | reference | light — *aʿlā* |
   | **ط** | مِّن طِينٍ | 6:2 | reference | heavy — *aʿlā* |
   | **ز** | إِنَّآ أَنزَلْنَٰهُ | 97:1 | reference | light |
   | **ف** | أَنفُسَهُمْ | 2:9 | reference | light |
   | **ت** | وَلَآ أَنتُمْ | 109:3 | **yes** | light — *aʿlā* |
   | **ض** | مِّن ضَعْفٍ | 30:54 | reference | heavy |
   | **ظ** | ظِلًّا ظَلِيلًا | 4:57 | reference | heavy |

   Work down the table once with her, then have her work down it alone naming **weight** before reading each row.

7. **Flag the two letters that decay first, and give them their own minute.** **ف and ك.** The join into them is comfortable without a nasal, so nothing in the mouth forces the issue and nothing in the ear complains. `أَنفُسَهُمْ` and `مِّن كُلِّ أَمْرٍ` will lose their hum before any other row in the table — not this week, but in a month. **Put them in the weekly maintenance list now**, before they decay, rather than rediscovering them later.

8. **Close the loop on ق and ك.** They are *adnā* — nearest to iẓhār — **and ق is heavy while ك is light.** The two facts are independent and students routinely fuse them. `مِن قَبْلُ` and `مِّن كُلِّ أَمْرٍ` back to back settles it: same degree, opposite weight.

9. **Colour, one honest note.** Everything on today's table is the same violet, `#9400A8` — see [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]]. **The colour does not tell her the weight and does not tell her the degree.** The page marks *that* a concealment happens, not *how* it sounds. This is the general limit of colour-coding and it is worth saying once: the colours locate rules; they do not execute them.

10. **Prepare next session.** Lesson 3-25 puts all four noon rules in one drill and proves the partition — **6 + 4 + 2 + 1 + 15 = 28**. Tell her the arithmetic is coming and ask her to work out for herself, before then, why it *has* to add to 28.

| إِنَّآ أَنزَلْنَٰهُ | 97:1 | ز | light | awsaṭ |

Everything above marked with a reference outside 1 and 105–114 is a **reference example — outside your memorised sūrahs**: 2:25, 97:4, 6:2, 30:54, 4:57, 2:9, 97:1.

**Teacher note on `ظِلًّا ظَلِيلًا`.** A tanwīn running into a ظ, and the ظ is one of the heaviest letters in the language. It is the clearest heavy nasal available and it is worth using even though it is a reference. Say it and then say `وَلَآ أَنتُمْ` — the two extremes of the weight scale, back to back.

**Teacher note on `إِنَّآ أَنزَلْنَٰهُ`.** Al-Qadr 97:1 gives a **doubled noon** and a **concealed noon** two words apart. Both hum for two counts. In the first the tongue is firmly on the gum ridge for the whole hold; in the second it never goes there at all. **Same duration, opposite tongue.** If she can do both in one breath, the chapter is working.

**Teacher note on the reference-heavy table.** Nine of fifteen letters have no in-set instance, and that is simply what the hifz set contains. **Do not hide the imbalance.** Say which rows she can check against her own memory and which she cannot, and lean the drilling on the six she can.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the weight pair.** Ten times, alternating, listening to the hum and not the letters:

**عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ** — heavy · **وَلَآ أَنتُمْ** — light

**Drill 2 — call the weight first.** Someone reads a letter from the fifteen. You say "**heavy**" or "**light**" before you are shown a phrase. Then read the phrase. Twenty repetitions, random order.

**Drill 3 — the full table, top to bottom.** Work down all fifteen rows from the teaching sequence. For each: say the letter, say the weight, then read the phrase. Once slowly. Then once at pace.

**Drill 4 — the two extremes.** Back to back, five times:

**ظِلًّا ظَلِيلًا** (heaviest available) · **وَلَآ أَنتُمْ** (lightest available)

If these two sound the same, nothing in this lesson has landed yet. Start again at Drill 1.

**Drill 5 — the decay list.** ف and ك, with the pinch, every time:

أَنفُسَهُمْ · مِّن كُلِّ أَمْرٍ · مِن فَضْلِهِۦ · مِّن كُلِّ مَكَانٍ

The nose must block for two counts on every one. Put this drill in your weekly maintenance permanently.

**Drill 6 — same degree, opposite weight.** Five times:

**مِن قَبْلُ** — adnā, **heavy** · **مِّن كُلِّ أَمْرٍ** — adnā, **light**

**Drill 7 — same length, opposite tongue.** Say each pair. Both hum for two counts; only one puts the tongue on the gum ridge:

| Tongue on the ridge | Tongue never arrives |
|---|---|
| إِنَّ | أَنتُمْ |
| ٱلنَّاسِ | مِن شَرِّ |
| ٱلْخَنَّاسِ | مِّن جُوعٍ |
| إِنَّآ | أَنزَلْنَٰهُ |

**Drill 8 — the whole chapter, mixed.** Read down and name the rule. Four rules in play; do not think, answer:

| | |
|---|---|
| 1. مِّن جُوعٍ | 7. مِّن طِينٍ |
| 2. مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ | 8. وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ |
| 3. حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ | 9. عَنْهُ مَالُهُۥ |
| 4. أَنۢبِئْهُم | 10. فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ |
| 5. عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ | 11. مَن يَقُولُ |
| 6. كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ | 12. أَنفُسَهُمْ |

For the teacher: 1 ikhfāʾ · 2 iẓhār · 3 idghām w/ ghunnah ×2 · 4 iqlāb · 5 ikhfāʾ (heavy) · 6 iẓhār · 7 ikhfāʾ (heavy) · 8 idghām no ghunnah · 9 iẓhār · 10 idghām no ghunnah · 11 idghām w/ ghunnah (nāqiṣ) · 12 ikhfāʾ (light).

**Drill 9 — in the sūrahs.** Recite al-Māʿūn, al-Kāfirūn and al-Falaq from memory. Name every noon rule as you pass it, out loud, mid-recitation. It will sound ridiculous. It is the single best preparation for next session.

## Listen-for

**1. One flat hum for all fifteen letters.**
- *Listen for:* `عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ` and `وَلَآ أَنتُمْ` sounding identical. **Priority error of the lesson**, and it is a direct consequence of last lesson doing its job.
- *Why it happens:* she spent a week building one reliable nasal. Now she is being asked to vary it.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 1 and Drill 4. Alternate; do not explain. If she cannot hear the difference in Drill 4, record both and play them back.

**2. Heaviness leaking onto light letters.**
- *Listen for:* a thick hum before ت, س, ش — usually in the session right after you fix error 1.
- *Why it happens:* over-correction, and heavy sounds impressive.
- *Correction cue:* "Ten of the fifteen are light. Heavy is the exception, not the reward."

**3. The nasal disappearing on ف and ك.**
- *Listen for:* no hum at all in `أَنفُسَهُمْ`, `مِّن كُلِّ`. **It will not happen today.** It happens in a month, quietly.
- *Why it happens:* the join is comfortable without it and the ear does not object.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 5, with the pinch, on the weekly maintenance list permanently.

**4. Turning `مِن قَبْلُ` into a full iẓhār.**
- *Listen for:* an audible noon before ق.
- *Why it happens:* ق is *adnā* — genuinely close to iẓhār — so she is exaggerating a real gradient, not inventing an error. **Correct it gently and say so.**
- *Correction cue:* "Nearly plain is not plain. The tongue still does not touch."

**5. Turning `أَنتُمْ` into a merge.**
- *Listen for:* a doubled ت.
- *Why it happens:* ت is *aʿlā* — genuinely close to idghām. Same phenomenon as error 4, at the other end.
- *Correction cue:* "Where is the shaddah?" Look at the page.

**6. Treating the three degrees as three rules.**
- *Listen for:* three different durations, or a question like "how long is the adnā one?".
- *Why it happens:* a table with three rows looks like three rulings.
- *Correction cue:* "All three are two counts and no shaddah. The scale describes the *colour* of the sound, not the ruling."

**7. Losing the weight rule inside a full recitation.**
- *Listen for:* correct weight in the drill, flat weight in the sūrah. Very common, and the drill will not reveal it.
- *Correction cue:* Drill 9. Naming rules mid-recitation is what moves a rule from the exercise into the text.

## Hifz for this session

Revision first: **Manzil**, then **Sabqi**, then the new **Sabaq**. See [[Hifz-Method]].

- **Manzil.** Two surahs, run consecutively through the basmalah.
- **Sabqi.** an-Naṣr **110**, now complete — three ayat. al-Masad 111 and al-Ikhlāṣ 112 if either is still in the recent window. Promote whatever has come back clean three sessions running.
- **New Sabaq — al-Kāfirūn 109:1–3.**

> قُلْ يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلْكَـٰفِرُونَ ۝ لَآ أَعْبُدُ مَا تَعْبُدُونَ ۝ وَلَآ أَنتُمْ عَـٰبِدُونَ مَآ أَعْبُدُ

**Three ayat in one session — more than usual, and deliberately so.** al-Kāfirūn is the most repetitive sūrah in the hifz set: ayat 3 and 5 are word-for-word identical, and 2 and 4 differ by very little. The repetition is a gift for memorisation and a trap for accuracy — **the commonest error in this sūrah is not forgetting a line but reciting the right line in the wrong place.**

Two things to point at:

- `وَلَآ أَنتُمْ` in 109:3 is **today's example**, the light nasal, and she will meet it again in 109:5. Two identical instances in one sūrah.
- `يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا` and `عَـٰبِدُونَ` carry long madds she has had since lesson 2-07. Nothing new, but they set the sūrah's pace.

Give this Sabaq the full 40 repetitions rather than 20. The repetitiveness makes it feel easier than it is.

Ten extra repetitions on any error caught, counted separately.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **Two minutes — Drill 1.** The weight pair, alternating. Every day.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 3.** The full fifteen-row table, weight named before each phrase.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 5.** ف and ك with the pinch. **This one never comes off the list.**
4. **Two minutes — Drill 4 and Drill 6.** The two extremes; then same degree, opposite weight.
5. **Two minutes — Drill 7.** Same length, opposite tongue.
6. **Four minutes — Drill 8.** The mixed column, cold, naming the rule.
7. **Three minutes — Drill 9.** al-Māʿūn, al-Kāfirūn and al-Falaq from memory, naming rules as you pass them.
8. **The rest — hifz.** Manzil, Sabqi (110 + whatever is recent), then 109:1–3 with 40 repetitions.

Bring to the next session: **the four noon rules, their letter counts, and the total.** Next session is revision — the whole quartet, mixed, and the proof that nothing has been left out.
