# Lesson 3-26 — Revision and Q&A: the noon quartet

No new rule today, and **no new Sabaq**. Six lessons went into this chapter one rule at a time. This session puts them in the same room and shows her that they are not six things.

## Objectives

- Student can name the rule on any noon sākinah or tanwīn, at reading speed, from the following letter alone.
- Student can reproduce the partition — 6 + 4 + 2 + 1 + 15 = 28 — and can say what it proves.
- Student can read Quraysh 106:4 and name all five of its rulings with no card.
- Student can say, for each of the four rules, whether there is a nasal and how long it lasts.

## Hook

Write the arithmetic on the board and nothing else:

> **6 + 4 + 2 + 1 + 15 = 28**

Ask her what it is. She will get it — the noon rules and the alphabet — and getting it is the point.

Then say what it proves, because this is the idea the session is built around:

**Every letter of the Arabic alphabet appears exactly once in that sum. Nothing is left over. Nothing is counted twice.** The four noon rules are not four rules that happen to cover a lot of ground; they are a **partition**. Whatever letter follows a closed noon, there is exactly one rule, and she can always name it — not because she has memorised enough cases, but because the cases are exhaustive by construction.

That is worth more than any individual rule in the chapter, and it is the cleanest evidence available that the system is complete rather than merely long.

## What they already know

The whole chapter, in one table. Put it on the board first and leave it up:

| Letters | Count | Nasal | Rule | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ء ه ع ح غ خ** | **6** | not prolonged | **iẓhār ḥalqī** | **none** |
| **ي ن م و** | **4** | **2 counts** | **idghām with ghunnah** | `#169777` |
| **ل ر** | **2** | **none** | **idghām without ghunnah** | `#169200` |
| **ب** | **1** | **2 counts** | **iqlāb** | `#26BFFD` |
| **ص ذ ث ك ج ش ق س د ط ز ف ت ض ظ** | **15** | **2 counts** | **ikhfāʾ ḥaqīqī** | `#9400A8` |
| | **28** | | | |

And underneath it, the four facts that the table does not show and that the exam will ask for:

1. **Tanwīn counts as a noon sākinah, everywhere, in every one of the four rules.**
2. **The two merges need a word boundary.** Iẓhār, iqlāb and ikhfāʾ do not.
3. **The four exception words** — ٱلدُّنْيَا · بُنْيَان · صِنْوَان · قِنْوَان — are iẓhār muṭlaq: the merge is cancelled inside a word.
4. **ي and و merge incompletely.** No shaddah on the page, and the nasal survives anyway.

## Teaching sequence

Framing only. The session is drills and questions.

1. **Run the table both ways, sixty seconds.** You say a letter, she says the rule. You say a rule, she says the letters and the count. Wherever she is slow is where the session's minutes go.

2. **The gold contrast — 106:4, and give it real time.** Sūrat Quraysh, ayah 4, contains **ikhfāʾ and iẓhār in the same ayah, on the same word:**

   > ٱلَّذِىٓ أَطْعَمَهُم **مِّن جُوعٍ** وَءَامَنَهُم **مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ**

   - `مِّن جُوعٍ` — ج. **Concealed.** Tongue never touches, two-count nasal, violet on the page.
   - `مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ` — خ. **Plain.** Tongue touches, no hold, no colour at all.

   **Same word, `مِن`. Same position in the sentence. Opposite treatment, decided entirely by the next letter.** This is the single best drill in the whole nasal syllabus, and it is inside a sūrah she recites from memory.

   Then point at the orthography one more time: the muṣḥaf prints the sukūn on `مِّنْ` and leaves it bare on `مِّن`. **The page told her the answer before any colour was applied.**

3. **Then show her the whole ayah, all five rulings, with no card.** She has had this on a card since lesson 3-17. Take the card away today:

   | Phrase | Rule | Nasal | Chapter |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن | idghām shafawī | 2 counts | **meem** |
   | مِّن جُوعٍ | ikhfāʾ ḥaqīqī | 2 counts, light | noon |
   | جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم | idghām with ghunnah, nāqiṣ | 2 counts | noon |
   | مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ | iẓhār ḥalqī | not prolonged | noon |
   | خَوْفٍۭ → بِسْمِ | iqlāb, on continuing | 2 counts | noon |

   **Eight words. Five rulings. One of them from the meem chapter.** If she can name all five unprompted, the two chapters are joined.

   Then count what that actually amounts to, on the board, against the table above: **106:4 contains four of the five noon rulings.** Iẓhār, ikhfāʾ, idghām with ghunnah, iqlāb — **every family except idghām without ghunnah.** And the one it is missing is the one she can supply from memory in four words: `وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ` (112:4).

   So: **two ayāt, one from Quraysh and one from al-Ikhlāṣ, exhaust the entire noon chapter.** Both memorised. If she wants a single thing to carry out of six lessons, it is those two lines and the ability to name everything in them.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the partition, from memory.** On a blank sheet, write the five rows: letters, count, nasal, rule name. Then add them. **If the total is not 28, find the missing letter before you look at anything.** Finding your own gap is the exercise.

**Drill 2 — the gold contrast.** Ten times, alternating, then the whole ayah in one breath:

**مِّن جُوعٍ** — conceal, two counts · **مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ** — say it, no hold

**Drill 2b — the whole chapter in two ayāt.** Recite Quraysh 106:4, then al-Ikhlāṣ 112:4, both from memory. Name every ruling in each. Between them you should produce **all five noon rules and two of the three meem rules** — 112:4 alone gives you an iẓhār shafawī on `وَلَمْ`, a merge without ghunnah on `يَكُن لَّهُۥ`, a throat iẓhār on `كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ`, and an iqlāb on the join out. Only ikhfāʾ shafawī is missing from the pair, and that one lives in al-Fīl 105:4. Do this before Drill 3; if it goes cleanly, the mixed column will too.

**Drill 3 — the mixed column, cold.** Read down. Name the rule. Do not pause; if you have to think, mark the row and come back:

| | |
|---|---|
| 1. عَنْهُ مَالُهُۥ | 10. مِن شَرِّ |
| 2. حَبْلٌ مِّن مَّسَدٍۭ | 11. مِّنۢ بَعْدِهِۦ |
| 3. وَلَآ أَنتُمْ | 12. وَٱنْحَرْ |
| 4. وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ | 13. جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم |

**Drill 4 — the nasal question, only.** Go down Drill 3 again and answer one question per row: **"two counts", "no hold", or "no nasal at all".** Three possible answers, eighteen rows. This is the fastest diagnostic in the chapter.

**Drill 5 — one word, five futures.** Say `مِنْ` followed by each, and name the rule:

مِنْ + خَـ · مِنْ + مَـ · مِنْ + لَـ · مِنْ + بَـ · مِنْ + جَـ

**Drill 6 — the exception words, cold.** After six weeks of merging drills:

ٱلدُّنْيَا · بُنْيَان · صِنْوَان · قِنْوَان

Then `بُنْيَٰنٌ مَّرْصُوصٌ`, which does both.

**Drill 7 — tanwīn only.** Every rule applies to tanwīn. Read these and name the rule; not one of them has a written noon:

كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ · حَبْلٌ مِّن · فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ · نَارًا ذَاتَ · أَحَدٌۢ بِسْمِ · لَهَبٍ وَتَبَّ · ظِلًّا ظَلِيلًا · غَاسِقٍ إِذَا

**Drill 8 — the boundary question.** For each, say "**needs a word boundary**" or "**does not**", then name the rule:

أَنتُمْ · حَبْلٌ مِّن · وَٱنْحَرْ · يَكُن لَّهُۥ · أَنۢبِئْهُم · ٱلدُّنْيَا

**Drill 9 — whole sūrahs, naming as you go.** Recite al-Fīl, Quraysh, al-Māʿūn and al-Kāfirūn from memory, naming every noon and meem rule out loud as you pass it. Four sūrahs. **This is the exercise that converts a table into recitation**, and it is the closest thing in the chapter to the final assessment.

**Drill 10 — the colour page.** On a coloured muṣḥaf, find one instance of each of the four colours — `#169777`, `#169200`, `#26BFFD`, `#9400A8` — and one **uncoloured** noon. Say what the uncoloured one is instructing you to do.

## Troubleshooting Q&A

The questions that actually get asked at the end of this chapter, with the answers that work.

**"Why does 6 + 4 + 2 + 1 + 15 have to equal 28?"**
Because the rules are defined by exclusion. Iẓhār takes the six throat letters. The merges take six more. Iqlāb takes one. **Ikhfāʾ is defined as "everything else"** — its fifteen letters are not a chosen list, they are the remainder. So the sum cannot come out to anything but 28. That is why you can always name the rule: there is nowhere for a letter to hide.

**"Idghām with ghunnah and ikhfāʾ both hum for two counts. How do I hear the difference?"**
You hear it in the following letter, not in the hum. **Idghām doubles it; ikhfāʾ does not.** `مِّن نِّعْمَةٍ` has a shaddah on the ن; `مِّن جُوعٍ` has none on the ج. And you see it before you hear it: the shaddah is printed.

**"The two greens look the same to me."**
They nearly are — `#169777` and `#169200` — and that is a real defect of the palette, not a failure of your eyes. **Read the letter, not the hue.** ي ن م و hum; ل ر do not. If a page is badly printed or you are reading on a phone at night, the letter is the only reliable evidence.

**"Iqlāb and ikhfāʾ shafawī sound identical."**
They are identical, or near enough that no one can reliably tell them apart by ear. **The difference is on the page:** a noon or tanwīn before ب is iqlāb; a meem before ب is ikhfāʾ shafawī. Look, do not listen. This is the one place in the chapter where the ear is not the instrument.

**"If I stop at the end of al-Ikhlāṣ, is `أَحَدٌۢ` still an iqlāb?"**
No. Stopping drops the tanwīn, and with it the noon sound the rule acts on. **No tanwīn, no rule** — just a plain *aḥad*. The small meem stays printed because the page cannot know whether you will stop.

**"`ٱلدُّنْيَا` has a noon before a ي. Why doesn't it merge?"**
Because merging needs a word boundary and this is one word. If it merged you would get *ad-duyyā*, and the root would be unrecoverable from the sound. There are exactly four such words in the whole Qurʾān and you know all four.

**"`مَن يَقُولُ` has no shaddah on the ي. Does that mean there is no merge?"**
There is a merge, but an incomplete one — *nāqiṣ*. The noon's body goes into the ي; **its nasal stays behind.** The missing shaddah is the muṣḥaf telling you the merge is incomplete. It is not telling you the nasal is gone. This is the most-dropped nasal in the chapter.

**"Do I have to memorise the fifteen ikhfāʾ letters?"**
For the exam, yes — learn the couplet, not the letters. For reading, no: check throat, يَنْمُو, ل/ر, ب, and **everything that falls through is ikhfāʾ.** Four checks instead of fifteen.

**"Why does iẓhār have no colour when it is a rule like any other?"**
Because colour marks **modifications**, and iẓhār is the absence of one. The noon on the page is the noon in your mouth. **Black means "say exactly what is written"** — a positive instruction. You met the same argument on the meem side in lesson 3-18.

**"My ikhfāʾ hum before ق sounds almost like a plain noon. Am I doing it wrong?"**
Probably not. ق and ك are *adnā* — the furthest of the fifteen from the noon's makhraj — so the concealment is genuinely lightest there. The test is the tongue: **it still must not touch the gum ridge.** If it does not, you are correct even if it sounds nearly plain.

**"Is a noon with a vowel on it ever in this chapter?"**
No. Every rule here needs a **closed** noon or a tanwīn. `نَصْرُ`, `نَعْبُدُ`, `مَنَعَ` — nothing applies.

## Hifz for this session

**Revision lessons pause new rules; they never pause hifz.** No new Sabaq today — Sabqi and Manzil only. See [[Hifz-Method]].

- **Manzil.** Widen the rotation today, since there is no new Sabaq competing for time. Three or four surahs, run **consecutively through the basmalah** so the iqlāb joins get their maintenance.
- **Sabqi.** Everything recent, whole and unaided:
  - an-Naṣr **110**, three ayat
  - al-Kāfirūn **109:1–3**
  - al-Masad **111** and al-Ikhlāṣ **112**, if either is still in the recent window
- **Promotion check.** Anything clean three consecutive sessions goes to Manzil. See [[Hifz-Method]] §3 — the three-session threshold is this course's own default, not a transmitted number, and it should be adjusted from what you actually observe.

**Use the spare time for Drill 9 on her own hifz.** Four sūrahs, naming rules mid-recitation. That is revision of the rules and revision of the memorisation in the same ten minutes, which is exactly what a revision session is for.

Halfway note, worth saying out loud: **she now has six of the eleven items in the hifz scope** — al-Fātiḥa, 114, 113, 112, 111, 110 — plus half of 109. The chapter and the memorisation have been moving together, and they are both past the middle.

5. **Two minutes — Drill 7.** Tanwīn only.
6. **Four minutes — Drill 9.** al-Fīl, Quraysh, al-Māʿūn and al-Kāfirūn from memory, naming as you go.
7. **Once, on day three — Drill 10**, on a coloured page.
8. **The rest — hifz.** Manzil (widened, consecutive), then Sabqi: 110, 109:1–3, and anything still recent.
