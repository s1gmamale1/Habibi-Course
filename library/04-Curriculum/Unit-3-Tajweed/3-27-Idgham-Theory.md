# Lesson 3-27 — Idghām theory: the rule the last six lessons were instances of

This is the whole of stage **3.5**, and it is a single lesson by design. **Its revision folded into lesson 3-26**, which had already covered the noon-sākinah merges this lesson generalises — so there is no separate revision session, and the next lesson opens the madd chapter.

## Objectives

- Student can classify any two adjacent letters as mutamāthilān, mutajānisān, mutaqāribān or mutabāʿidān, and can say which of the four produce a merge.
- Student can state why a **madd letter never merges**, and can point at the instance in an-Nās.
- Student can produce the heavy ت of `أَحَطتُ` — the signature error of the whole idghām syllabus.
- Student can say why almost everything in this lesson is a **reference example** and what that means for how she studies it.

## Hook

Two phrases. She has one of them memorised and does not know there is a rule in it:

> **مَا عَبَدتُّمْ** — al-Kāfirūn 109:4
> **ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ** — an-Nās 114:5

Ask her to read both. Almost certainly she reads the first as *ʿabad-tum*, carefully separating the د and the ت. It is **not** *ʿabad-tum*. It is ***ʿabat-tum*** — the د vanishes into the ت, which is doubled, and there is a shaddah printed on the page to say so.

Then the second. Two yāʾs in direct contact — the ى ending `ٱلَّذِى` and the ي opening `يُوَسْوِسُ` — **identical letters, touching, and nothing happens.** No merge at all.

Say the frame: **for six lessons she has been merging letters. Today she finds out that the merging was never about noons and meems.** It is a general rule about what happens when two letters meet, and the noon and meem cases were simply the frequent ones, taught first because they are everywhere. Today is the theory underneath, plus the small number of remaining cases — **and one of them is in the sūrah she is memorising this hour.**

## What they already know

- **Lesson 3-17** taught idghām shafawī — م into م — and told her at the time that it is "the general rule for two identical letters, applied to the meem", with the general theory promised for this lesson. Collect that flag.
- **Lesson 3-21** taught ن into ن and ن into م. Both are the same general rule.
- **Lesson 3-22** taught ن into ل and ر. She will see it again today from the other side, and will be told it was never a special case.
- **Lesson 3-26** proved the noon chapter is a partition. Today's classification is a different partition — of *letter pairs* rather than of the alphabet — and it is worth naming the similarity.
- **Lesson 2-07** gave the madd letters. That lesson does real work today.
- **Lesson 3-03** gave isti'lā and iṭbāq. Those two attributes are the entire content of the `أَحَطتُ` case.

## Teaching sequence

1. **Give the classification first — it is short, and everything else hangs off it.** Idghām is classified by **how related the two letters are**:

   | Type | Makhraj | Ṣifāt | Merges in Ḥafṣ? |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **Mutamāthilān** المُتَمَاثِلَان — "the two alike" | **same** | **same** | **yes, always** |
   | **Mutajānisān** المُتَجَانِسَان — "the two of one kind" | **same** | different | yes — **7 pairings** |
   | **Mutaqāribān** المُتَقَارِبَان — "the two near each other" | close | close | yes — specific pairings |
   | **Mutabāʿidān** المُتَبَاعِدَان — "the two far apart" | far | far | **no — always iẓhār** |

   **The fourth row is why most letter sequences in the Qurʾān have no rule at all.** Two distant letters simply cannot merge. Say that; it stops her scanning every word for a hidden idghām.

   **In plain terms:** same place and same character means identical; same place, different character means alike; near-enough places means close. That is the whole classification.

2. **Then the second classification, by vowel state, and close two of its three rows immediately.**

   | Type | Definition | In Ḥafṣ |
   |---|---|---|
   | **Ṣaghīr** الصَّغِير | first **sākin**, second **vowelled** | idghām wherever the pair permits |
   | **Kabīr** الكَبِير | **both** vowelled | almost none — two words |
   | **Muṭlaq** المُطْلَق | first **vowelled**, second **sākin** | **never** |

   **Everything in this course is ṣaghīr.** That is not a simplification of the syllabus — it is what Ḥafṣ actually transmits. Say it plainly so she does not go looking for the other two.

3. **Mutamāthilayn — the easy one, and she already does it.** Two identical letters, the first sākin: **always kāmil.** Two identical letters have nothing to distinguish them, so nothing survives the merge. The second takes a shaddah and is held.

   - `ٱضْرِب بِّعَصَاكَ` (26:63) — ب into ب
   - `قَد دَّخَلُوا` (5:61) — د into د
   - `بَل لَّا يَخَافُونَ` (74:53) — ل into ل
   - `أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن` (106:4) — م into م, **which is lesson 3-17**
   - `مِّن نِّعْمَةٍ` (16:53) — ن into ن, **which is lesson 3-21**

   The last two are the point. **She has been doing mutamāthilayn since lesson 3-17 under a different name.**

4. **The exception, and it is inside her hifz.** **If the first of the two identical letters is a madd letter — or a leen letter — there is no idghām.** The letter is a vowel-carrier, not a consonant to be absorbed; merging it would destroy the prolongation.

   **`ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ` — an-Nās 114:5.** Two yāʾs in sequence, and **nothing happens.** Point at it directly: *"two yāʾs, no merge — because the first one is a madd."* Also `قَالُوا۟ وَهُمْ`, `فِى يَوْمٍ`, on the same principle.

   **This is the one part of today's lesson she can check against her own memory.** Lean on it.

5. **Mutajānisayn — same makhraj, different ṣifāt. A closed list of seven.**

   | Pair | Type | Example |
   |---|---|---|
   | **د → ت** | kāmil | `قَد تَّبَيَّنَ` 2:256 · **`عَبَدتُّمْ` 109:4** |
   | **ت → د** | kāmil | `أَثْقَلَت دَّعَوَا` 7:189 |
   | **ت → ط** | kāmil | `وَقَالَت طَّآئِفَةٌ` 3:72 |
   | **ط → ت** | **nāqiṣ** | `أَحَطتُ` 27:22 · `بَسَطتَ` 5:28 · `فَرَّطتُمْ` 12:80 |
   | **ذ → ظ** | kāmil | `إِذ ظَّلَمُوٓا۟` 4:64 |
   | **ث → ذ** | kāmil | `يَلْهَث ذَّٰلِكَ` 7:176 — **the only occurrence** |
   | **ب → م** | kāmil | `ٱرْكَب مَّعَنَا` 11:42 — **the only occurrence** |

   **Three makhraj groups account for all seven**: the gum-ridge plosives (د ت ط), the tongue-and-teeth fricatives (ذ ظ ث), and the lips (ب م). Teach it as three groups, not seven rows.

6. **ط → ت is nāqiṣ, and this is the real content of the lesson — give it your best ten minutes.**

   Every pairing above is complete **except** ط → ت. When a sākin **ط** merges into a **ت**, the ط's **iṭbāq** — the tongue's rise to the roof — and its **isti'lā** **survive**. The letter becomes a ت; **its heaviness does not go with it.**

   So `أَحَطتُ` is **not** *aḥattu*. It is a ت pronounced with the tongue still raised — a heavy, hollow ت that sounds unlike any other ت in the Qurʾān. Same in `بَسَطتَ` and `فَرَّطتُمْ`.

   **This is the classic error in the whole idghām syllabus.** A learner told "the ط merges" flattens it, and the word appears once — so the mistake, once made, is almost never corrected. Say it heavy, say it flat, and make her tell you which was which.

   Then the orthography, one more time: **the ت in `أَحَطتُ` carries no shaddah**, exactly as ي and و carry none after a merged noon in lesson 3-21. **A missing shaddah on a merged letter is the muṣḥaf's standing signal for nāqiṣ.** That convention has now appeared in three separate lessons; she should be able to state it herself.

   **In plain terms:** in ط → ت the ط does not vanish completely — its heaviness stays behind. That leftover is what nāqiṣ means.

7. **Mutaqāribayn — close but not identical. Very few cases.**

   | Pair | Type | Example | In her hifz? |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **ل → ر** | kāmil | `قُل رَّبِّ` 23:118 · `بَل رَّفَعَهُ` 4:158 | **no** |
   | **ق → ك** | **nāqiṣ** per most; kāmil per some | `نَخْلُقكُّم` 77:20 — **the only occurrence** | **no** |
   | **ن → ل, ر** | kāmil | `وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ` 112:4 · `فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ` 107:4 | yes — but see below |

   **Every genuinely new case on this page is a reference example — outside your memorised sūrahs.** There is **no ل → ر and no ق → ك anywhere** in al-Fātiḥa or sūrahs 105–114. Say the gap out loud; she cannot check either row against her own memory.

   **The third row is not new.** It is lesson 3-22, seen from the theory side. Say so explicitly: *"it was never a special case."* It is what happens when three letters share the gum ridge and two of them are near enough to absorb the third. And **ل → ر is the mirror of it** — the same two letters, merging the other way.

   **A note on counting, and it is worth understanding rather than memorising.** Search the cpfair dataset for `idghaam_mutaqaribayn` inside the hifz set and you get **zero hits** — even though row three is sitting right there in al-Ikhlāṣ and al-Māʿūn. The reason is that `وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ` and `فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ` are annotated as **`idghaam_no_ghunnah`**, under the noon chapter where they are taught, not under the phonetic relationship they belong to. **The dataset is not wrong and neither is the classification** — they answer different questions, which is exactly the palette inconsistency in step 11. Both counts are true at once: *zero annotated mutaqāribayn in her hifz, two mutaqāribayn merges in her hifz.*

   Teach the relationship from `وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ`, which is in her hifz, and then generalise to `قُل رَّبِّ`, which is not.

8. **`نَخْلُقكُّم` — two transmitted readings, and say so clearly.** Al-Mursalāt 77:20 is the **only** place where a sākin ق meets a ك. Ḥafṣ transmits **both**:
   - **nāqiṣ** — the ق's body merges into the ك, but its **isti'lā remains**, so the ك is heavy. **This is the more common teaching, and what this course teaches.**
   - **kāmil** — the ق disappears entirely and a plain doubled ك is heard.

   **Both are valid; neither is an error.** This is one of the few places where she will hear two reciters differ and needs to know it is not a mistake. Note also that the pattern is identical to ط → ت: a heavy letter merges, and the argument is over whether the heaviness travels. In both cases the missing shaddah is the tell.

9. **`ٱرْكَب مَّعَنَا` — a disagreement about the label, not the sound.** Some classify it as mutajānisayn (ب and م are both labial); others as mutaqāribayn (within the lips, the two points are argued to be distinct). **Both camps merge the ب into the م and hold the shaddah with a nasal.** This course files it under mutajānisayn. **Pick one and be consistent** — a learner who meets both labels in two books will assume they are two different rules.

10. **Idghām kabīr — mention it, close it, and warn her about recordings.** When **both** letters carry a vowel, Ḥafṣ merges in exactly **two words**:
    - **`تَأْمَ۫نَّا`** — Yūsuf 12:11 (originally تَأْمَنُنَا), read with **ishmām** — lips round silently on the merged ن — or **rawm / ikhtilās**, a fraction of the vowel sounded. Both transmitted.
    - **`مَكَّنِّى`** — al-Kahf 18:95.

    **Idghām kabīr as a *system* belongs to Abū ʿAmr al-Baṣrī, not to Ḥafṣ.** Say this explicitly: a learner who listens to Abū ʿAmr recitations will hear merges that are simply not available in the reading she is being taught, and will conclude she has been taught wrongly.

11. **Colour, and what it does not cover.**
    - **Mutamāthilayn has no colour and no cpfair key.** Its two frequent cases are already annotated under their own names — `idghaam_shafawi` and `idghaam_ghunnah` — so there is no separate category for the general rule. `ٱضْرِب بِّعَصَاكَ` shows nothing but the shaddah. **The shaddah is the annotation**, and that generalises: a shaddah on the first letter of a word, where the previous word ended in the same letter, *is* an idghām mark regardless of colour.
    - **Mutajānisayn and mutaqāribayn share one grey, `#A1A1A1`.** Deliberately: both are rare, structurally similar, and not worth two colours a reader must separate under pressure. **Grey reads as "something merged here — look it up"**, which is the correct instruction for a rule with fewer than thirty occurrences in the whole text.
    - And an inconsistency worth admitting: **ن → ل / ر is coloured `#169200` as idghām bilā ghunnah, not grey**, even though it is a mutaqāribayn merge. The palette classifies by **where the rule is taught**, not by **what the phonetics are**. Both classifications are correct; they answer different questions. See [[cpfair-quran-tajweed]].

12. **Say the study strategy out loud, because this lesson is different in kind.** Almost every example today is a **reference example — outside your memorised sūrahs**, and several are **the only occurrence in the entire Qurʾān**. **This is not a rule to drill into reflex. It is a rule to recognise.** Learn the classification, learn `أَحَطتُ`, learn the two in her hifz, and treat the rest as a lookup — which is exactly what the grey colour is telling her to do.

**Teacher note on `عَبَدتُّمْ`.** It is the **only mutajānisayn in the hifz set**, it is in the sūrah she is memorising this hour, and **nobody notices it**. That combination makes it the best teaching moment in the lesson. *`mā ʿabattum`*, not *`mā ʿabad-tum`*. Have her say it wrong once, deliberately, then right, then recite the ayah whole.

**Teacher note on `ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ`.** an-Nās 114:5 is the earliest sūrah she memorised. The exception is therefore checkable from memory rather than from a reference, which is rare in this lesson. Use it as the anchor for the whole madd-letter principle.

**Teacher note on `أَحَطتُ`.** Do not let this become a footnote because the word appears once. **It is the single most-flattened letter in the tradition**, and the reason it stays flattened is precisely that the word is rare enough that nobody hears it often enough to correct. Give it its own two minutes and its own line in the homework.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — classify, out loud.** For each pair of adjacent letters, say **"alike"**, **"one kind"**, **"near"** or **"far"**, then say whether it merges:

ب · ب → alike, merges
د · ت → one kind, merges
ل · ر → near, merges
م · ك → **far, no merge**
ن · ل → near, merges
س · ق → **far, no merge**

**Drill 2 — the two in your hifz.** Ten times each, then in the whole ayah:

**عَبَدتُّمْ** — *ʿabattum*, one doubled ت
**ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ** — two yāʾs, no merge, the madd intact

**Drill 3 — the heavy ت.** Say each one twice: once flat (wrong), once with the tongue still raised (right). Name which is which:

أَحَطتُ · بَسَطتَ · فَرَّطتُمْ

If the two versions sound the same to you, the tongue is not rising. Say a plain ط first, feel where the tongue goes, then keep it there and say the ت.

**Drill 4 — kāmil or nāqiṣ, from the page.** Look at the second letter. **Shaddah = kāmil. No shaddah = nāqiṣ.** Say which before you read:

قَد تَّبَيَّنَ · أَحَطتُ · وَقَالَت طَّآئِفَةٌ · فَرَّطتُمْ · إِذ ظَّلَمُوٓا۟ · نَخْلُقكُّم · يَلْهَث ذَّٰلِكَ

**Drill 5 — the seven mutajānisayn pairings, by group.** Recite them as three groups, not seven items:

- gum-ridge plosives: **د → ت · ت → د · ت → ط · ط → ت**
- tongue-and-teeth: **ذ → ظ · ث → ذ**
- lips: **ب → م**

**Drill 6 — the madd exception.** Say each and confirm nothing merges:

ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ · قَالُوا۟ وَهُمْ · فِى يَوْمٍ

**Drill 7 — old rules, new names.** Read each and give **both** names — the one you learned before, and the theory name:

| Phrase | Then | Now |
|---|---|---|
| أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن | idghām shafawī | mutamāthilayn |
| مِّن نِّعْمَةٍ | idghām with ghunnah | mutamāthilayn |
| وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ | idghām without ghunnah | mutaqāribayn |
| فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ | idghām without ghunnah | mutaqāribayn |

**Drill 8 — the shaddah as annotation.** On any page, find a word that begins with a shaddah'd letter where the **previous word ended in the same letter**. Say the rule name. There is no colour on it; the shaddah is the whole marking.

**Drill 9 — al-Kāfirūn, whole.** From memory, with `عَبَدتُّمْ` merged. Then an-Nās, whole, with `ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ` **not** merged. Two sūrahs, one rule and its exception.

## Listen-for

**1. Flattening the ط in `أَحَطتُ`, `بَسَطتَ`, `فَرَّطتُمْ`.**
- *Listen for:* an ordinary light ت. **The signature error of the entire idghām syllabus.**
- *Why it happens:* "the ط merges" is heard as "the ط is gone". The body goes; the heaviness does not.
- *Correction cue:* Have her say a plain ط, hold the tongue where it is, and say the ت from that position. Physical, not explanatory.

**2. Pronouncing the merged letter separately — *qad tabayyana*, *ʿabad-tum*.**
- *Listen for:* both letters audible, usually with a micro-gap.
- *Why it happens:* both letters are written, and the words are printed apart.
- *Correction cue:* Delete the first letter deliberately: "say *ʿabat*, then *tum*, then join." Drill 2.

**3. Reading a missing shaddah as "no idghām".**
- *Listen for:* `أَحَطتُ` or `نَخْلُقكُّم` read with two separate letters.
- *Why it happens:* she has correctly learned that a shaddah marks a merge, and reasoned backwards.
- *Correction cue:* "**No shaddah means the merge is incomplete, not absent.**" Same convention as ي and و in lesson 3-21. Say the two cases side by side.

**4. Merging after a madd letter.**
- *Listen for:* `ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ` collapsing into one long yāʾ.
- *Why it happens:* two identical letters are touching, and she has just been taught that identical letters merge.
- *Correction cue:* "The first one is not a consonant. It is a vowel wearing a consonant's shape." Drill 6.

**5. Not holding the resulting shaddah.**
- *Listen for:* the merge happening and finishing early. The under-held shadda from lesson 2-05, still.
- *Correction cue:* Fingers, two folds.

**6. Importing Abū ʿAmr's kabīr merges.**
- *Listen for:* merges in places Ḥafṣ does not merge, usually in a student who listens to a lot of recitation.
- *Why it happens:* she is imitating a reciter in a different reading and has no way to know.
- *Correction cue:* Name the reading. "That is Abū ʿAmr. In Ḥafṣ there are two kabīr merges and you know both."

**7. Treating the ب → م and ق → ك disagreements as errors.**
- *Listen for:* a question shaped like "which one is right?".
- *Correction cue:* "Both are transmitted. Pick the one you were taught, be consistent, and recognise the other when you hear it." This is the same answer she got about ي/و in lesson 3-21 and about the ب of ikhfāʾ shafawī in lesson 3-16 — **and by now she should notice that it is the same answer.**

## Hifz for this session

Revision first: **Manzil**, then **Sabqi**, then the new **Sabaq**. See [[Hifz-Method]].

- **Manzil.** Two or three surahs, run consecutively through the basmalah.
- **Sabqi.** an-Naṣr 110 and al-Kāfirūn 109:1–3.
- **New Sabaq — al-Kāfirūn 109:4–6.**

> وَلَآ أَنَا۠ عَابِدٌ مَّا عَبَدتُّمْ ۝ وَلَآ أَنتُمْ عَـٰبِدُونَ مَآ أَعْبُدُ ۝ لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ وَلِىَ دِينِ

**Three ayat, and today's rule is in the first of them.** `عَبَدتُّمْ` — the د into the ت, the only mutajānisayn in her hifz. Teach the ayah as memorisation first, then run the rule over it, then have her recite the sūrah whole with the merge intact.

Three other things worth flagging as she works:

- `عَابِدٌ مَّا` — a tanwīn into a م: **idghām with ghunnah**, lesson 3-21, two counts.
- `وَلَآ أَنتُمْ` in 109:5 — the **light ikhfāʾ** from lesson 3-25, and this is its second appearance in the sūrah.
- `لَكُمْ دِينُكُمْ` — **iẓhār shafawī**, lesson 3-18, lips fully closed.

**With this portion, al-Kāfirūn is complete — and so is your slice of the hifz strand.** Seven of the eleven items in scope are now memorised: al-Fātiḥa, 114, 113, 112, 111, 110, 109. Say that out loud; it is a real milestone and she has been working toward it for thirteen sessions.

Also worth saying: **al-Kāfirūn's repetitiveness is a trap for accuracy, not for recall.** Ayat 3 and 5 are word-for-word identical. Test the *order*, not the words — ask her to start at ayah 5 and finish, then start at ayah 3 and finish, and see whether she can tell them apart from the inside.

20–40 repetitions; ten extra on any error caught, counted separately.

## Homework

**15–20 min/day, five days.**

1. **Two minutes — Drill 1.** Classify pairs, out loud. Alike, one kind, near, far.
2. **Three minutes — Drill 2.** The two in your hifz, ten times each, then in the whole ayah.
3. **Two minutes — Drill 3.** The heavy ت. **Every day** — this one does not stick from a single session.
4. **Two minutes — Drill 4.** Kāmil or nāqiṣ, read off the page before you speak.
5. **Two minutes — Drill 5 and Drill 6.** The seven pairings by group; then the madd exception.
6. **Two minutes — Drill 7.** Old rules, new names.
7. **Three minutes — Drill 9.** al-Kāfirūn and an-Nās from memory.
8. **The rest — hifz.** Manzil, Sabqi (110 + 109:1–3), then 109:4–6. **al-Kāfirūn closes this week.**

Bring to the next session: **`أَحَطتُ`, and tell me whether the ت was heavy.** Next session opens the madd chapter — the last of the rule families, and the one that decides how a recitation actually sounds.
