# Lesson 3-28 — The Madd Family, and Madd Ṭabīʿī

> **Opening lesson of Unit 3.6.** Six lessons: five rules and a capstone. This one lays the
> measuring stick that the other five are measured against, so do not rush it because the
> content looks familiar.

## Objectives

- Student can name the **three madd letters** and state the vowel each one requires.
- Student can produce a **clean, level 2 counts** — not 1, not 4 — and can hear when her own
  madd has slipped.
- Student can say what turns a natural madd into a lengthened one: **a hamzah, or a sukūn**.
- Student can explain, in her own words, that a ḥarakah is a **proportion of her own tempo**,
  and that the finger is only a way of learning it.

## Hook — corrective

Ask her to read this, slowly:

> **مَٰلِكِ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ** (1:4)

She has recited al-Fātiḥah hundreds of times. Nine times out of ten the dagger alif on
مَٰلِكِ comes out short — somewhere between one count and one and a half — because it is not
written on the line and the eye does not weigh it.

Say nothing yet. Ask her to read it again, and this time **you** read مَٰلِكِ alongside her at
a true two counts. She will hear the gap immediately, and she will hear that she is the one
who is short.

> **"You have been reading this every day for years, and you have been reading it a little bit
> fast. Not wrong — short. Today we fix the length, and once the length is right, the next five
> lessons are just multiples of it."**

That is the frame for the whole unit: **everything in madd is a multiple of what you do
today.** If two counts are wrong, four and six are wrong as well, and no amount of work on the
harder rules will repair them.

## What they already know

- The three long vowels and their two counts, from 2-07 — where they were taught as a *reading*
  fact, not a *named rule*.
- Madd meeting a shaddah (2-08), which she read at six counts without being told why.
- That a long vowel **grows when you stop on it** (2-12 §3, item three) — observed, unnamed,
  and deliberately left that way until 3-31.
- All of Unit 3.1–3.5: nūn sākinah, mīm sākinah, lām, rāʾ, qalqalah. **Madd is the last of the
  six families**, and she should be told that: the map is nearly complete.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. Where madd sits on the map

Draw the six families for her on one sheet, and put a tick against the five she already holds:

| Family | Status |
|---|---|
| Nūn sākinah & tanwīn | ✓ done |
| Mīm sākinah | ✓ done |
| Lām — shamsiyyah, qamariyyah, jalālah | ✓ done |
| Rāʾ — tafkhīm, tarqīq | ✓ done |
| Qalqalah | ✓ done |
| **Madd** | **starts today** |

Adults need to see the end of the road. Say it plainly: **this is the last family, it has five
rules, and you already do four of them by ear.**

### 2. The three madd letters

| Letter | Requires | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **ا** | always sākinah, always after a **fatḥah** | قَالُوا۟ |
| **و** | sākinah, after a **ḍammah** | صُدُورِ |
| **ي** | sākinah, after a **kasrah** | ٱلدِّينِ |

Two mnemonics; teach whichever she takes to. They are gathered in **وَاي**, and in the single
word **نُوحِيهَا** (11:49), which carries all three.

**The vowel must match.** A wāw after a fatḥah is not a madd — it is a *līn* letter, which she
met in 2-08 and which returns at 3-31. Make her say the pairing out loud: *fatḥah–alif,
ḍammah–wāw, kasrah–yāʾ.*

### 3. The dagger alif is not a smaller alif

This is where her two counts are actually leaking. Put these three in front of her:

> **ذَٰلِكَ · ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · هَٰذَا**

The superscript stroke ٰ is a **full alif**, written above the line for orthographic reasons
that have nothing to do with length. It gets exactly the same two counts as an alif on the
line. Have her read ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ (1:3) and hold both madds equally — the ā of
ar-Raḥmān and the ī of ar-Raḥīm are the **same length**. If they are not, one of them is wrong.

### 4. What a ḥarakah actually is — say this out loud

She is about to be given numbers: 2, 4, 5, 6. She will ask what a count *is*. Do not dodge it.

> **The scholars differ.** Three positions are current: (a) the time it takes to fold or extend
> one finger; (b) a measured half-second or so; (c) purely **relative** to the reciter's own
> tempo, with no absolute value at all.

**Teach the finger as a learning aid, and say that it is one.** Fold the index finger for one,
the middle finger for two — it externalises the count and stops her guessing, and for the next
six lessons it is the most useful tool she has.

**Then tell her the truth:** the relative definition is the real one. A ḥarakah is a proportion
of *her* recitation speed. Her two counts in slow tartīl are physically longer than her two
counts in ḥadr, and both are correct. What must never change inside a single session is the
**ratio** between 2, 4 and 6.

The practical consequence, which is what she needs: **do not chase a stopwatch. Keep your
tempo steady and keep the proportions honest.** A reciter whose madds are all consistently
slightly long is fine. A reciter whose 2-count is sometimes 1 and sometimes 3 is not.

### 5. The condition — and the whole of Unit 3.6 in one sentence

A madd letter that is followed by **neither a hamzah nor a sukūn** is *ṭabīʿī*, natural, two
counts, and needs no cause. That is why it is also called **al-madd al-aṣlī**, the original
madd — it is the baseline state.

> **The moment a hamzah or a sukūn lands after a madd letter, the madd gets longer.** That
> sentence is Unit 3.6. Five rules, and every one of them is the same sentence with the
> details filled in:

| What follows the madd letter | Rule | Counts | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| nothing special | **ṭabīʿī** | 2 | today |
| hamzah, **same word** | muttaṣil | 4–5 | 3-29 |
| hamzah, **next word** | munfaṣil | 4–5 | 3-30 |
| sukūn **only because you stopped** | ʿāriḍ lis-sukūn | 2/4/6 | 3-31 |
| sukūn that is **always there** | lāzim | 6 | 3-32 |

Write that table on a card and leave it with her. She will use it for six weeks.

### 6. Reading the sign: the maddah `~`

The mushaf tells her. A **maddah** ~ printed over a madd letter — آ, ـَٰٓ, ـُوٓ, ـِيٓ — means
*this madd is longer than two.* No maddah, no lengthening.

So the eye rule is: **madd letter with no ~ above it → 2 counts, do not think about it.** That
one habit removes most of the anxiety from the next five lessons, because it turns four of them
into "look for the wiggle."

### 7. Madd ṭabīʿī ḥarfī — the short letters of the muqaṭṭaʿāt

Touch this lightly today; it comes back in full at 3-32.

In the disconnected letters at the head of certain sūrahs, a letter whose **spelt name is two
letters long**, ending in a madd letter with no sukūn after it, takes a plain two counts.

**The five: ح ي ط ه ر — mnemonic حَيٌّ طَهُرْ.**

- **طه** (20:1) — "Ṭā-Hā", both two counts.
- **حمٓ** (40:1) — the ḥāʾ is two; the mīm has a maddah and is six.

And the one that catches everyone: **ا (alif) gets no madd at all.** Its name is spelt with
three letters but does not end in a madd letter. In الٓر the alif is bare, the lām is six, the
rāʾ is two.

## Examples

Work these in order. She reads, you hold up two fingers and fold them with her.

| # | Text | Ref | What it drills |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | قَالُوا۟ | 2:25 | alif madd — the reference case |
| 2 | صُدُورِ ٱلنَّاسِ | 114:5 | wāw madd, then alif madd, in one phrase |
| 3 | مَٰلِكِ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ | 1:4 | dagger alif + yāʾ madd, equal length |
| 4 | ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ | 1:3 | dagger alif alone — the leak |
| 5 | إِنَّآ أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ ٱلْكَوْثَرَ | 108:1 | today's hifz; the dagger alif of أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ is plain 2 |
| 6 | طه | 20:1 | ḥarfī — two counts on a letter-name |

**Note on item 5, and do not let it pass:** إِنَّآ carries a maddah `~`. That one is **not**
two counts, and she will meet it properly at 3-30. Today the honest answer is: *"That wiggle
means longer. I am not telling you how much longer until lesson 3-30. The word after it —
أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ — has a plain dagger alif and that one is two."* Naming the boundary of what she
knows is better than fudging it.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the finger metronome.** Set your own comfortable tartīl tempo by tapping four
beats. Now read each of the six examples, folding a finger per count. Every alif, wāw and yāʾ
madd gets exactly two folds. If a madd ends before the second fold, say the word again.

**Drill 2 — the pair test.** I give you two words. Tell me whether their madds are the same
length, and if not, which is longer:
ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ / ٱلرَّحِيمِ · مَٰلِكِ / ٱلدِّينِ · قَالُوا۟ / صُدُورِ.
The answer is *the same* every time. Your job is to make it true, not to guess it.

**Drill 3 — hunt the dagger.** Take one page of the muṣḥaf and circle every superscript alif on
it. Read the page aloud giving each circled mark a full two counts. Count how many you
circled — the number will surprise you, and that is the point.

**Drill 4 — the shortening test.** Read قَالُوا۟ correctly, then deliberately read it at one
count so you hear قَلُّوا. Do the same with كَاتِب and كَتَب. **Say the wrong one on purpose,
once.** You cannot avoid an error you have never heard yourself make.

**Drill 5 — no-maddah sweep.** On the same page, find five madd letters that have **no** ~ over
them. Read each at two counts and move on without thinking. This drill is about speed of
recognition, not accuracy — the ~-free case is meant to become invisible.

**Drill 6 — tempo swap.** Read Sūrat al-Kawthar twice: once slow, once fast. Your madds are
physically different lengths in the two readings, and both are correct. What must be identical
is that every ṭabīʿī madd in one reading matches every other ṭabīʿī madd in that same reading.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** a madd that is *level*. It starts, it holds at a steady pitch and volume, and it
stops. It does not swell, sag, or trail off.

- **The mistake:** one and a half counts on a dagger alif — مَٰلِكِ, ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ, هَٰذَا.
  **Why it happens:** the mark is small and sits off the line, so it does not read as a letter.
  This is a visual problem, not a listening problem.
  **Say:** *"Cover the word. What did you just say? Now say ٱلرَّحِيمِ. Same length. Again —
  both of them, back to back."*

- **The mistake:** the madd sags in volume across its two counts, so the second count is
  half-voiced.
  **Why it happens:** she is running low on breath support, usually because she took the breath
  in the wrong place.
  **Say:** *"Hold the sound at the same strength all the way to the end. If you can't, you
  breathed too late — go back and breathe before the phrase."*

- **The mistake:** over-correction — four counts on a plain madd, because she is now thinking
  about length.
  **Why it happens:** a predictable and temporary consequence of today's lesson. Expect it for
  a week.
  **Say:** *"Two. You gave me four. A natural madd that gets stretched is no longer natural —
  it's just a different mistake."*

- **The mistake:** madds of inconsistent length within one āyah.
  **Why it happens:** she is measuring each one separately instead of holding a tempo.
  **Say:** *"Tap the beat first, then read. The madd fits the beat; the beat does not fit the
  madd."*

- **The mistake:** giving ح or ط six counts in the muqaṭṭaʿāt, or giving alif any madd at all.
  **Why it happens:** she has heard reciters do the six-count letters and has generalised.
  **Say:** *"حَيٌّ طَهُرْ — those five are two. Everything else in the openings waits until
  3-32."*

## Hifz for this session

**New Sabaq: Sūrat al-Kawthar (108), all three āyāt.**

The whole surah in one session — it is the shortest in the Qurʾān and she can hold it.

Session order is **Manzil, then Sabqi, then new Sabaq**, never the reverse ([[Hifz-Method]]
§2). Old material decays when it is skipped, and it is what gets skipped when the mind is
tired.

Method for the new portion: three reads from the page with full attention to pronunciation, then
look away and recite; on failure, find the exact point of failure and repeat *that*; alternate
look / no-look until it holds. Target **20–40 repetitions** inside the session.

**Ten extra repetitions after any caught error**, as its own step on the checklist.

Tajweed to insist on today, since it is what the lesson is about: **the dagger alif of
أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ is exactly two counts.** إِنَّآ is longer and she does not yet know by how much;
tell her it is coming and let it be approximately long for now.

## Homework

1. **Kawthar**, daily: Manzil rotation first, then Sabqi, then Kawthar to 20–40 repetitions.
   Log the ten-after-an-error repetitions on their own line.
2. **The card.** Copy the five-row table from §5 onto a card by hand. Keep it in the muṣḥaf.
   Handwriting it is part of the homework; a photograph is not.
3. **The dagger hunt** (Drill 3) on the page you are given. Bring the circled page.
4. Read Sūrat an-Nās (114) aloud once a day, giving **every** madd letter exactly two counts,
   with the finger fold. Five āyāt. You already know it by heart — that is why it is safe to
   spend all your attention on length.
5. Listen to al-Fātiḥah once in Ḥuṣarī's recitation and count his madd on مَٰلِكِ with your
   finger. Come back and tell me the number you got.
