# Lesson 3-29 — Madd Wājib Muttaṣil

## Objectives

- Student can identify a **madd letter followed by a hamzah inside the same word**, on sight,
  from the maddah `~` plus the position of the hamzah.
- Student can hold **4 counts** cleanly and land the hamzah crisply at the end of it.
- Student can say why this madd is **wājib** — obligatory for every reciter, with no
  two-count option anywhere.
- Student can state the consistency rule: muttaṣil is held at **one length for the whole
  session**, and never shorter than munfaṣil.

## Hook — corrective

Give her 110:1, which she can already read:

> **إِذَا جَآءَ نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلْفَتْحُ**

She will almost certainly read **جَآءَ** at two counts — and then, because the hamzah is hard
work after a long vowel, she will either swallow the hamzah or turn the whole word into a
smeared *jaa*.

Read it back to her twice: once her way, once at four counts with a clean hamzah landing on the
end. Then say:

> **"There is a wiggle over that alif. You read straight past it. That mark is the single
> most reliable instruction in the whole muṣḥaf, and from today you never read past one
> again."**

The corrective value here is high because she has been reading جَآءَ short since Unit 2 and
nothing has ever contradicted her.

## What they already know

- The three madd letters and a clean 2 counts (3-28).
- The maddah `~` means *longer than two* (3-28 §6) — today she learns her first actual number.
- The five-row map from 3-28 §5. Point at the second row.
- Hamzah as a letter with its own makhraj, from Phase 1 — including that it is a **stop**, an
  articulated closure, not a vowel.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. The definition, in her hand

> **A madd letter followed by a hamzah, in the same word.**

Write جَآءَ large. Put a finger over the hamzah. What is left is جَا — a plain two-count madd.
Now uncover the hamzah:

> **"The hamzah is the cause. It is heavy, it is hard to say, and the tongue needs room to get
> to it. The madd is the room."**

That is not a mnemonic — it is close to the actual reason the classical sources give: the
hamzah is the deepest and most effortful letter, and the extra length is the approach to it.

### 2. Why *wājib*

The word means **obligatory**, and here it is meant literally: **every qāriʾ in every ṭarīq
lengthens this beyond two counts.** There is no reading in which جَآءَ is two.

Contrast it with what is coming next lesson, because the contrast is the point:

| | muttaṣil | munfaṣil (3-30) |
|---|---|---|
| Where the hamzah is | **same word** | next word |
| Can the two be separated? | **never** | yes — stop on the first word and the cause is gone |
| Ruling | **wājib** — obligatory | **jāʾiz** — permitted, reciters differ |
| Shortest correct length | **4** | 2, in another ṭarīq |

**Because cause and madd live in one word, nothing can ever separate them.** That is the whole
argument, and it is worth giving her, because it makes the ruling follow from the spelling
instead of being a fact to memorise.

   **In plain terms:** the hamzah is stuck to the madd inside one word, so it can never get away. That is why nobody shortens this one — every reciter lengthens it.

### 3. The length: 4 or 5

**4 or 5 ḥarakāt**, via ṭarīq ash-Shāṭibiyyah — 4 in ḥadr and tadwīr, 5 in slow tartīl.

**This course pins 4–5**, matching the Ḥuṣarī recitation used throughout. Have her choose one —
recommend **4** while learning, because it is audible without being ponderous — and then hold it.

Two consistency rules, both of which she must be able to state back:

1. **One length for the whole session.** Four in one āyah and five in the next is the actual
   error. Neither number is.
2. **Muttaṣil ≥ munfaṣil.** Whatever she does next lesson, her separated madd must never be
   longer than her connected one. She does not know why yet; tell her the rule now and the
   reason at 3-30 §3.

### 4. Landing the hamzah

Half of this lesson is length; the other half is the letter at the end of it, and the second
half is what she will actually get wrong in three weeks' time.

After four counts of open vowel, the throat is relaxed and the hamzah wants to disappear. Drill
the shape explicitly:

> **hold — hold — hold — hold — *close*.**

The hamzah is a clean glottal closure and release. It arrives **at full volume**, not as an
afterthought. Have her say جَآءَ with an exaggerated, almost percussive hamzah four or five
times, then dial it back to normal. Overshooting first is the fastest route to normal.

### 5. Spotting it on the page — two questions, in order

1. **Is there a `~` over a madd letter?** If no, it is two counts, move on.
2. **Where is the hamzah that caused it?** Same word → **muttaṣil, 4**. Next word → munfaṣil,
   which is next lesson.

That is the entire visual procedure and it will serve her for both rules. Practise it as a
*silent* scan before she reads — eyes only, no sound — because doing it mid-recitation is too
late.

**In plain terms:** two questions, in order. Is there a squiggle over the long vowel? Then where is the hamzah — same word means four, next word means the other rule.

### 6. The stop that buys a sixth count

Say this once, clearly, and do not drill it hard today.

If she **stops on the word-final hamzah itself** — as in ٱلسَّمَآءِ or ٱلشِّتَآءِ — the sukūn
of stopping stacks onto the hamzah, and **6 counts becomes permitted**. Some name this *madd
muttaṣil ʿāriḍ*. It is the same mechanism she will meet properly at 3-31.

Her practical instruction for now: **join → 4. Stop on the hamzah → 4 is still fine, 6 is also
fine.** She does not have to use the six. She does have to not be confused when she hears it.

### 7. Where it is *not*

Two traps worth pre-empting, because both cost her marks later:

- **A hamzah before the madd letter is irrelevant.** إِيَّاكَ, ءَامَنُوا۟ — the hamzah comes
  first. That is a different rule ([[Madd-Badal]]), and it is 2 counts.
- **A dagger alif still counts.** لِلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ (2:30) is muttaṣil. The alif is superscript
  and the maddah sits on top of it; it is a full madd letter and the hamzah follows it in the
  same word. She will miss this one.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — cover the hamzah.** I show you a muttaṣil word with the hamzah covered by my thumb.
Read what you see: two counts. I lift my thumb. Read it again: four counts and a clean hamzah.
Ten words, alternating.

**Drill 2 — the ladder.** Take جَآءَ and read it four times: at 2, at 4, at 5, at 6. Say which
one is wrong and why. *(2 is wrong — it is the only one that is never permitted. 6 is only
available if you stop on the hamzah.)*

**Drill 3 — the percussive hamzah.** Say جَآءَ · سُوٓءًا · ٱلسَّمَآءِ with the hamzah far too
hard, five times each. Then say each one normally. Listen for whether the hamzah is still
fully there when you stop pushing.

**Drill 4 — silent scan.** Here is a page. **Do not read it aloud.** With a pencil, mark every
`~` you can find. Then, for each one, write M if the hamzah is in the same word and N if it is
in the next. Now read the page and apply only the M's — the N's you read at 2 counts for now.

**Drill 5 — consistency check.** Read āyāt 1–6 of a page containing three or more muttaṣil.
I am timing nothing except whether all three are the **same length as each other**. One
inconsistency and we start the page again.

**Drill 6 — the Badal trap.** Read these back to back and tell me which is 4 and which is 2:
جَآءَ / ءَامَنُوا۟ · ٱلسَّمَآءِ / إِيمَان · سُوٓءًا / أُوتُوا۟. *(The first of each pair.)*
The question you are answering is always: **does the hamzah come before or after the madd
letter?**

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** four steady counts, and then a hamzah you can hear as a separate event.

- **The mistake:** two counts on جَآءَ, ٱلسَّمَآءِ, يُرَآءُونَ.
  **Why it happens:** she has read these words for years without the rule, and the maddah has
  never meant anything to her before. Habit, not ignorance.
  **Say:** *"Point at the wiggle. That mark has been there the whole time. It means four."*

- **The mistake:** the hamzah is swallowed — *jaa*, *as-samaa*.
  **Why it happens:** the throat relaxes across a long vowel and cannot re-close in time. It is
  a physical timing failure, not carelessness.
  **Say:** *"Hold the aaa, and then shut it. I want to hear the door close."*

- **The mistake:** the madd swells or rises in pitch before the hamzah lands.
  **Why it happens:** she is bracing for the hamzah and the effort leaks backwards into the
  vowel.
  **Say:** *"Flat. All four counts at the same pitch, then the hamzah. Don't announce it."*

- **The mistake:** 4 counts in one āyah, 5 in the next.
  **Why it happens:** she is producing each madd from scratch rather than from a held tempo.
  This is the most common error of the whole unit and it will persist.
  **Say:** *"Both are correct. Both in one page is not. Pick four, and give me four every time
  today."*

- **The mistake:** lengthening ءَامَنُوا۟ or إِيَّاكَ.
  **Why it happens:** over-generalisation from today's lesson — she has learned "hamzah plus
  madd means long" without the order.
  **Say:** *"Which came first, the hamzah or the alif? Hamzah first is not this rule."*

## Hifz for this session

**New Sabaq: al-Māʿūn 107:1–107:2.**

> أَرَءَيْتَ ٱلَّذِى يُكَذِّبُ بِٱلدِّينِ · فَذَٰلِكَ ٱلَّذِى يَدُعُّ ٱلْيَتِيمَ

Two āyāt. Manzil rotation first, then Sabqi — al-Kawthar from 3-28 is squarely in the fragile
window and gets reviewed every single session — then the new portion at 20–40 repetitions.

**Ten extra repetitions after any caught error**, on its own line of the checklist.

Two tajweed points to insist on inside the new portion, both of which are revision:
- **ٱلَّذِى** — the final yāʾ is a plain two-count [[Madd-Tabii]], not longer.
- **يَدُعُّ** — the shaddah is held; she learned this in 2-06 and it slips under memorisation
  load.

Tell her that **al-Māʿūn contains today's rule** in يُرَآءُونَ (107:6), which she will reach in
two lessons. Preview it once so that when she memorises it, the four counts are already there.

## Homework

1. **Hifz.** 107:1–2 to 20–40 repetitions daily. Sabqi = al-Kawthar, every session. Manzil =
   the two-surah rotation.
2. **Drill 4 (silent scan)** on one fresh page: mark every `~`, label each M or N. Bring the
   marked page. **You are not asked to read it correctly — you are asked to see it correctly.**
3. Read Sūrat al-Māʿūn from the page (not from memory) once a day and give **يُرَآءُونَ** four
   counts every time, even though you have not formally reached it.
4. Write out five muttaṣil words you find anywhere in the last juzʾ, with the sūrah and āyah
   number for each. If you cannot find five, bring three and we will look together.
5. Say اللهم آمين at the end of al-Fātiḥah and notice that **ءَامِين** is *not* a muttaṣil.
   Be ready to tell me why. *(The hamzah is before the madd, not after.)*
