# Lesson 3-30 — Madd Jāʾiz Munfaṣil

> **This is the lesson where she meets scholarly disagreement head-on for the first time in the
> madd family.** §4 is not optional and it is not an aside. Teach it.

## Objectives

- Student can identify a madd letter at the **end of one word** followed by a hamzah at the
  **start of the next**, and give it 4–5 counts.
- Student can state that the madd **disappears if she stops on the first word** — because the
  cause is on the other side of a boundary she just closed.
- Student can say that **4–5 and 2 are both correct Ḥafṣ**, name the two ṭuruq, and recognise
  the 2-count reading when she hears it without concluding the reciter is wrong.
- Student holds munfaṣil **≤ muttaṣil** and constant across a session.

## Hook — corrective

Give her 2:4 and let her read it:

> **وَٱلَّذِينَ يُؤْمِنُونَ بِمَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكَ**

She will read **بِمَآ** at two counts — or, more likely now that 3-29 has made her
maddah-aware, she will spot the `~`, hesitate, and guess.

Then give her the one she has been reciting since lesson 3-28:

> **إِنَّآ أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ ٱلْكَوْثَرَ** (108:1)

> **"Two lessons ago I told you that wiggle meant longer and that I would not tell you how
> much longer yet. You have been reciting al-Kawthar every day since. Today you find out —
> and you find out that it is four, and that if you stop on إِنَّآ it is two, and that some
> excellent reciters give it two even when they join."**

The corrective is deliberately about her own hifz. She has been reciting a rule she could not
name for two weeks; naming it now is the reward.

## What they already know

- Madd ṭabīʿī at 2 (3-28) and muttaṣil at 4–5 (3-29).
- The two-question visual procedure from 3-29 §5: *is there a `~`?* then *where is the
  hamzah?* Today she completes the second question's other branch.
- The consistency rule she was given without a reason: **munfaṣil ≤ muttaṣil.** §5 supplies the
  reason.
- From Unit 2: how to stop on a word, and that stopping changes what the word does. That fact
  is load-bearing here for the first time in Unit 3.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. The definition, and the whole difference from last week

> **A madd letter at the end of one word, followed by a hamzah at the start of the next.**

Write the two side by side, big:

> **جَآءَ**  ·  **بِمَآ أُنزِلَ**

Same `~`. Same hamzah. Same 4 counts. **One difference: the space.** In جَآءَ nothing can ever
get between the madd and its cause. In بِمَآ أُنزِلَ there is a word boundary, and a word
boundary is a place a reciter is allowed to stop.

### 2. Jāʾiz — "permitted", and why the word is honest

*Muttaṣil* is **wājib** because cause and madd are welded together. *Munfaṣil* is **jāʾiz**
because they are not: the boundary is real, the reciters differ across it, and **more than one
length is genuinely correct.**

This is a different kind of word from *wājib*. Do not soften it into "you may do what you
like": within a given ṭarīq the length is fixed. What *jāʾiz* means is that **the transmitted
readings differ**, not that the reciter improvises.

### 3. The madd that can vanish

This is the mechanical fact that separates munfaṣil from everything else in the family:

> **Stop on the first word and the rule disappears.** The hamzah that caused it is in the next
> word, and you are not going there. What is left is a plain 2-count [[Madd-Tabii]].

| Reading | إِنَّآ أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ |
|---|---|
| Joining onward | إِنَّآ at **4 counts**, then أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ |
| Stopping on إِنَّآ | إِنَّا at **2 counts**, full stop |

Have her produce both, back to back, three times. She should be able to hear that the second
one is not a shortened version of the first — it is a different rule, correctly applied.

**This never happens with muttaṣil**, and saying so out loud is the cleanest way to fix the
distinction: ٱلْأَسْمَآءَ is four counts whether you join or stop, because the hamzah is inside
the word and goes wherever the word goes.

### 4. The disagreement — say all of this, do not compress it

| Ṭarīq | Munfaṣil length | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **ash-Shāṭibiyyah** | **4 or 5 ḥarakāt** | what this course teaches |
| aṭ-Ṭayyibah — *Ḥafṣ bi-qaṣr al-munfaṣil* | **2 ḥarakāt** | equally valid Ḥafṣ |

**Teach 4–5. Name the 2 out loud, in the room, today.**

The reason is practical and she needs it stated as a prediction about her own future: **she is
going to hear reciters give it two.** Many widely-broadcast reciters read with qaṣr
al-munfaṣil. A student who has only ever been told "munfaṣil is four" and then hears a famous
qāriʾ give it two draws one of two wrong conclusions — that the qāriʾ is sloppy, or that her
teacher is. Both are corrosive, and both are avoidable with ninety seconds today.

> **"There are two roads. We are walking the Shāṭibiyyah road, so you give it four. The other
> road gives it two, it is not a mistake, and when you hear it you will now know what you are
> hearing."**

Notice what does **not** vary: muttaṣil. Nobody shortens muttaṣil. That asymmetry is itself the
best evidence for her that these are transmitted readings and not preferences.

### 5. Why munfaṣil can never exceed muttaṣil

Now the reason she was owed last lesson.

Muttaṣil is the **stronger** cause: the hamzah is inside the word, inseparable, obligatory for
everyone. Munfaṣil is the **weaker** cause: separable, and disputed. A weaker cause producing a
*longer* madd than a stronger one is incoherent, and no transmitted reading does it.

**Practical rule for her mark sheet:** munfaṣil ≤ muttaṣil, always, and both constant within a
session. If she settles on 4 for muttaṣil, munfaṣil is 4 or (in the other ṭarīq) 2 — never 5.

### 6. Why the eye misses it

The failure is visual and it has a precise cause: **the words are printed apart, so the eye
reads a boundary and the tongue stops lengthening.** She reads بِمَآ, closes it, and only then
sees أُنزِلَ.

The fix is a reading habit, not a rule: **look one word ahead.** The `~` is on the *first*
word; the reason for it is on the *second*. She has to have both in view before she starts the
first.

Drill 3 below is entirely about this and is the most valuable drill in the lesson.

### 7. يَٰٓأَيُّهَا — the one she will always miss

> **يَٰٓأَيُّهَا** (2:21, 66:6, and everywhere)

It is printed as one unbroken shape and it is **two words**: the vocative يَا plus أَيُّهَا.
The madd is on the يَا, the hamzah begins أَيُّهَا, and it is a full munfaṣil at 4 counts.

Nothing about the printing helps her. This one is memorised, and it is worth memorising because
it occurs constantly. Have her say it at four counts five times before she leaves.

### 8. Madd ṣilah kubrā — one sentence of preview

If [[Madd-Silah]] has already been taught, connect it now; if not, plant it: when the pronoun
hāʾ carries an implied wāw or yāʾ and the next word begins with a hamzah, that is a munfaṣil
too, and takes the same 4–5. Same rule, invisible madd letter. Do not drill it today.

## Examples

| # | Text | Ref | What it drills |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | بِمَآ أُنزِلَ | 2:4 | the reference case |
| 2 | إِنَّآ أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ | 108:1 | **her own hifz** — and the join/stop pair |
| 3 | يَٰٓأَيُّهَا | 2:21 | printed as one word, actually two |
| 4 | قُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ | 66:6 | wāw madd, so she cannot pattern-match alif |
| 5 | نُوحِيهَآ إِلَيْكَ | 11:49 | three madds in one word, only the last is 4 |
| 6 | عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ ٱلْمِسْكِينِ | 107:3 | **contrast** — today's hifz, and *not* munfaṣil |

**Item 6 earns its place by being negative.** عَلَىٰ ends in a madd; طَعَامِ begins with a ṭāʾ,
not a hamzah. No hamzah, no munfaṣil, 2 counts. She is memorising this āyah today, and a
learner fresh from this lesson will over-apply the rule to exactly this shape.

Item 5 is worth unpacking on the board: **نُوحِيهَآ إِلَيْكَ** carries a wāw madd (2), a yāʾ
madd (2), and then a final alif madd before a hamzah in the next word (4). Three madds, two
lengths, one word. If she can read that correctly she has the rule.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — join it, stop it.** Every item, twice: once joined to what follows, once stopping
on the first word.
إِنَّآ أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ / إِنَّآ · بِمَآ أُنزِلَ / بِمَآ · نُوحِيهَآ إِلَيْكَ / نُوحِيهَآ.
**Four counts, then two counts.** Say them back to back so you hear that the second is not a
mistake.

**Drill 2 — M or N.** I show you ten `~` words. You say **M** (muttaṣil, same word) or **N**
(munfaṣil, next word) before you read anything. Wrong answers cost nothing; guessing after you
have started reading costs everything.

**Drill 3 — look one word ahead.** I cover the *second* word with my thumb. You read the first
word and tell me how long its madd is. You cannot answer — that is the point. I lift my thumb;
now answer. Do this fifteen times until your eye starts travelling ahead of your voice on its
own.

**Drill 4 — the two roads.** Read Sūrat al-Kawthar twice: once giving إِنَّآ four counts, once
giving it two. Both readings are correct Ḥafṣ. Say which ṭarīq each one belongs to.
*(Four: Shāṭibiyyah. Two: Ṭayyibah.)* Then pick four and keep it.

**Drill 5 — the ceiling.** Read a passage with both muttaṣil and munfaṣil in it. I am checking
one thing: **is your munfaṣil ever longer than your muttaṣil?** If it is once, we do the
passage again.

**Drill 6 — the false alarm.** Here are six pairs where a word ends in a madd letter. In three
of them the next word starts with a hamzah; in three it does not. Read all six at the right
length. عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ · بِمَآ أُنزِلَ · فِى دِينِ · قُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ · إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ ·
نُوحِيهَآ إِلَيْكَ.

**Drill 7 — the vocative.** يَٰٓأَيُّهَا, five times, four counts each. Then find three
occurrences of it in the muṣḥaf and read the whole āyah each time.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** the length surviving the space. The madd should not shorten just because the
word ended.

- **The mistake:** 2 counts on بِمَآ, إِنَّآ, يَٰٓأَيُّهَا while joining.
  **Why it happens:** the eye reached the word boundary and the tongue obeyed it. Purely a
  scanning failure, and telling her to "remember the rule" will not fix it.
  **Say:** *"Where is the hamzah? Point at it. Now go back and start the word again, looking
  at the hamzah while you say the alif."*

- **The mistake:** keeping the 4 counts when she **stops** on the first word.
  **Why it happens:** over-application — she has just learned the rule and is applying it to the
  word rather than to the situation.
  **Say:** *"You stopped. The hamzah never happened. What is left is two."*

- **The mistake:** munfaṣil longer than muttaṣil — five here, four there.
  **Why it happens:** the new rule gets attention and attention makes it longer.
  **Say:** *"The connected one is the strong one. It can never be the shorter of the two."*

- **The mistake:** missing يَٰٓأَيُّهَا entirely.
  **Why it happens:** it looks like one word. Nothing on the page corrects her.
  **Say:** *"Cover أَيُّهَا. What is left? يَا. That is a word, and this is two of them."*

- **The mistake:** applying it to عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ or فِى دِينِ — a madd at a word end with no
  hamzah after it.
  **Why it happens:** she has abbreviated the rule to "madd at the end of a word."
  **Say:** *"Two conditions, not one. Madd letter, **and** a hamzah. Where is the hamzah?"*

- **The mistake:** hesitating audibly while she works out which rule applies.
  **Why it happens:** she is deciding at the moment of utterance instead of during the scan.
  This is a real error and it costs points at the checkpoint as non-fluency.
  **Say:** *"Decide before you open your mouth. Read the line silently first, then aloud."*

## Hifz for this session

**New Sabaq: al-Māʿūn 107:3–107:4.**

> وَلَا يَحُضُّ عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ ٱلْمِسْكِينِ · فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ

Manzil, then Sabqi — al-Kawthar and 107:1–2 — then the new portion, 20–40 repetitions, with the
**ten-after-an-error** step logged separately ([[Hifz-Method]] §2).

Three things to hold her to inside the new portion:

- **عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ** is the contrast case from §Examples. Two counts. She has just been taught
  the four-count rule and she will over-apply it here today of all days.
- **يَحُضُّ** and **لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ** — shaddah held, from 2-06.
- **فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ** is the classic **waqf qabīḥ**: the meaning is completed only by
  107:5. Tell her now that she must not stop there deliberately, and that the reason gets its
  own treatment at 3-34. Memorising 107:4 and 107:5 as one breath from the start prevents a
  habit she would otherwise have to break.

## Homework

1. **Hifz.** 107:3–4 to 20–40 repetitions daily; Sabqi = al-Kawthar + 107:1–2; Manzil rotation
   first. Recite 107:4 and 107:5 **joined**, every time.
2. **Drill 3, self-administered.** Cover the second word with a bookmark. Twenty times, on a
   page of your choosing. This is the drill that transfers.
3. Read Sūrat al-Kawthar aloud once a day, and once a week read it with the two-count munfaṣil
   so that the other ṭarīq stays in your ear as *a reading*, not as an error.
4. Find and write down **five munfaṣil** and **three muttaṣil** from anywhere in the last juzʾ,
   labelled M or N with references.
5. Listen to any recitation of Sūrat al-Baqarah's opening page and write down whether that
   reciter is giving munfaṣil four counts or two. One sentence. Bring the answer.
