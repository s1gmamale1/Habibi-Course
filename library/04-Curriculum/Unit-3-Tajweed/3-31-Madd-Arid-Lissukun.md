# Lesson 3.30 — Madd ʿĀriḍ lis-Sukūn

> ## ⚠ Read this before you teach — the ordering point
>
> **Madd ʿāriḍ lis-sukūn is defined by what happens when you stop.** It does not exist in
> joined reading at all: in waṣl the same word carries a plain two-count [[Madd-Tabii]]. So the
> rule has a hard dependency on waqf.
>
> The classical arrangement puts madd **before** waqf — al-Jazariyyah treats madd in chapter 11
> and waqf in chapter 12 — and an earlier draft of this course's own syllabus inherited the same
> inversion, with madd at 3.18 and waqf at 3.25. Both put the consequence before its cause.
>
> **This course fixed it by teaching practical stopping in Unit 2.** Lesson 2-12 introduced it,
> 2-13 drilled it, 2-14 applied it on a real muṣḥaf page. She has been stopping correctly, and
> letting long vowels stretch at stops, since before Unit 3 began — she was told at the time
> that it had a name and a number of counts and that both were coming.
>
> **So today teaches her nothing new about stopping.** It gives a habit she has had for months
> its name, its counts, and its place on the madd map. The prerequisites are `3-28` and `2-12`,
> and they are deliberate — do not reorder this lesson back before the waqf material, and do not
> re-teach stopping from scratch here. 3-34 and 3-35 then complete the waqf taxonomy on top of
> the same habit.

## Objectives

- Student can name the stretch she has been producing since 2-12 and state that it exists
  **only at a stop**.
- Student can produce **2, 4 or 6** counts on demand, and can say that all three are correct.
- Student holds **one** of the three for the whole session, and can say why switching is the
  actual error.
- Student can distinguish ʿāriḍ from [[Madd-Lazim]] (next lesson) by the one question: **is the
  sukūn there when I join?**

## Hook — corrective

Do not open with a correction of her stopping — she stops well. Open by catching her out on
**consistency**, which is where she is genuinely weak and does not know it.

Ask her to read 1:2 through 1:5, stopping at the end of every āyah:

> ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ ۝ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ ۝ مَٰلِكِ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ ۝
> إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ ۝

Now tell her what she just did, and be specific: her ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ was probably long, her
ٱلرَّحِيمِ shorter, her ٱلدِّينِ shorter still, and نَسْتَعِينُ long again because it is the
end of a familiar phrase. **Four stops, three different lengths.**

> **"You have been doing this rule correctly since Unit 2 — you just weren't doing it the same
> way twice. Today it gets a name, a number, and a rule that says you have to pick one."**

That is the whole corrective. The stretch is right; the wandering is wrong.

## What they already know

- **Everything mechanical.** 2-12 §3 item three: *a long vowel before a stop stretches; let it;
  don't measure it.* 2-13 drilled it, 2-14 applied it across a page.
- That the final ḥarakah drops at a stop (2-12), which is what creates the sukūn in the first
  place — she may never have connected the two halves, and connecting them is §2 below.
- Madd ṭabīʿī at exactly 2 (3-28), which is what this same word does when she joins.
- Muttaṣil and munfaṣil at 4–5 (3-29, 3-30), and the discipline of holding one length.
- **She does not yet know the waqf taxonomy or the signs.** That is 3-34. Today needs only
  "āyah ends are always a safe stop", which she has had since 2-12.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. Name it, and give her the credit

> **A madd letter, followed by one letter, followed by a stop.**

That is the shape. She already produces it. Say so:

> **"This has a name — al-madd al-ʿāriḍ lis-sukūn, the madd that happens because of a
> *temporary* sukūn. ʿĀriḍ means temporary, incidental, something that showed up. The sukūn
> showed up because you stopped. Join onward and it goes away, and so does the madd."**

For an adult learner this framing matters: she is not being taught a new skill, she is being
handed the vocabulary for a skill she has. Say it in those words.

### 2. Where the sukūn comes from — join the two halves

She learned in 2-12 that the **final ḥarakah drops** at a stop. She learned separately that the
**long vowel stretches**. She may never have been told these are cause and effect.

Put it on the board as a two-step:

> نَسْتَعِينُ — join → *nastaʿīnu*, the yāʾ is a plain **2**.
> نَسْتَعِينْ — stop → the ḍammah drops, the **nūn goes sākin**, and now a madd letter is
> followed by a sukūn. **That is a cause.** The yāʾ lengthens.

The rule follows from something she already does. Make her state the chain back to you before
moving on.

### 3. The three lengths

**2, 4 or 6 ḥarakāt. All three are correct.**

| Count | Name | Character |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | ishbāʿ / ṭūl | full — slow tartīl |
| 4 | tawassuṭ | middle |
| 2 | qaṣr | short — ḥadr |

This is genuinely **the reciter's choice**, in a way that muttaṣil is not. Nobody is wrong.

**But: hold one choice for the whole session.** The error is not a length; the error is
*switching*. What she did in the Hook — four stops, three lengths — is the thing being fixed.

**Practical recommendation for this course: use 4 while learning.** It is audible without being
ponderous, and it matches the tawassuṭ she hears on the Ḥuṣarī recordings the course uses.

Then say the sentence that makes it stick: **"If someone recorded you reading a whole page and
played back only the stops, they should all sound the same."**

### 4. The rule that separates it from everything else in the family

One question, and she should be able to answer it in a second:

> **Is the sukūn there when I join?**

| Answer | Rule | Counts |
|---|---|---|
| **No — only when I stop** | ʿāriḍ lis-sukūn | 2 / 4 / 6, my choice, held constant |
| **Yes — always** | [[Madd-Lazim]] (next lesson) | **6**, always, no choice |

That is the entire distinction between today's lesson and next week's, and it is worth putting
on the card from 3-28 §5.

### 5. Stacking — what else happens at the same stop

At a stop, several rules land on the same word at once. She has met them all; today they are
assembled.

- **Qalqalah kubrā.** If the final letter is a qalqalah letter, do **both** — the madd and the
  bounce. Do not let the madd swallow the bounce or the bounce cut the madd short.
- **Madd līn.** If the letter before the last is a **wāw or yāʾ sākinah after a fatḥah** — a
  *līn* letter, not a true madd letter — the same 2/4/6 choice applies, and the choice must
  match the one she is using for ʿāriḍ. مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ (106:4) is the case, and she reaches it in
  her hifz in three lessons.
- **Muttaṣil at a stop.** Stopping on a word-final hamzah after a madd gives muttaṣil its
  optional sixth count — 3-29 §6, and it is this same mechanism.
- **The contrast case.** أَحَدٌ (112:1) has **no madd letter** before the dāl. Stopping on it
  gives qalqalah kubrā and nothing else. No madd. Say this explicitly; a student who has just
  learned "stopping makes things long" will lengthen a word that has nothing to lengthen.

### 6. The one place she will apply it wrongly

**In waṣl.** Reading ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ مَٰلِكِ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ straight through and
still stretching ٱلرَّحِيمِ.

There is no sukūn there. She did not stop. The word carries a plain two-count madd and nothing
else. Demonstrate both readings of 1:3→1:4 back to back — joined and stopped — and make her
name which rule is running in each.

Item 6 is the best teaching item in the list: it is the last word of a surah she is memorising

will end on a madd ʿāriḍ.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the same stop every time.** Read al-Fātiḥah stopping at every āyah end. I am
counting **only** whether all seven stops are the same length as each other. They will not be,
the first time. Read it again.

**Drill 2 — pick a number and defend it.** Read Sūrat al-Māʿūn three times: all stops at 2, all
stops at 4, all stops at 6. Then tell me which you are going to use from now on, and use it.

**Drill 3 — join it, stop it.** Same as Drill 2 of lesson 2-12, now with the counts attached.
ٱلرَّحِيمِ / ٱلرَّحِيمْ · ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ / ٱلْعَٰلَمِينْ · ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ / ٱلْمُفْلِحُونْ.
Two counts, then four. Back to back so you hear the difference is caused by the stop.

**Drill 4 — the waṣl trap.** Read 1:3 into 1:4 **joined**, with no stop:
ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ مَٰلِكِ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ. Every madd in that line is **two**. If you
stretched ٱلرَّحِيمِ, you invented a stop that was not there.

**Drill 5 — stop on my signal.** Read on. When I raise my hand, stop at the end of the word you
are on and apply everything: drop the ḥarakah, stretch the madd to your chosen count, and add
the qalqalah if the letter calls for it. This is 2-12's Drill 4 with the counts added.

**Drill 6 — madd or nothing.** I give you ten words to stop on. Five end in a madd letter plus
one letter; five do not. Stretch only the five that qualify.
نَسْتَعِينُ · أَحَدٌ · ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ · ٱلْفَلَقِ · ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · وَتَبَّ · سَاهُونَ ·
ٱلْحَطَبِ · ٱلْمَاعُونَ · ٱلْكَوْثَرَ.

**Drill 7 — the līn pair.** مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ and مِّن جُوعٍ, stopped. The first has a līn wāw
before the fāʾ; give it the same count you give your ʿāriḍ. The second does not. Say which is
which before you read.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** stops that are all the same shape. Same length, same clean release, no vowel
tail.

- **The mistake:** stops of three different lengths on one page.
  **Why it happens:** she is producing each stop as an isolated event, and familiar phrases get
  longer stops than unfamiliar ones. Almost nobody notices this in themselves.
  **Say:** *"Your ʿālamīn was long and your dīn was short. Pick four and give me four every
  time. Tap it if you have to."*

- **The mistake:** lengthening in waṣl — a stretch on ٱلرَّحِيمِ while joining onward.
  **Why it happens:** she has attached the rule to the *word* rather than to the *stop*.
  **Say:** *"Did you stop? No. Then there is no sukūn and there is no madd. Two."*

- **The mistake:** the madd is held but the final letter is not released cleanly, so the stop
  sounds smeared or breathy.
  **Why it happens:** she is running out of air at the end of the madd, usually because she
  planned her breath for the joined length, not the stopped one.
  **Say:** *"Breathe earlier. The stop is longer than the join — plan for the longer one."*

- **The mistake:** madd swallows the qalqalah, or qalqalah cuts the madd short.
  **Why it happens:** two rules competing for the end of one word, both new-ish.
  **Say:** *"Both. Hold the long vowel all the way, then bounce. In that order, and don't
  hurry."*

- **The mistake:** stretching أَحَدٌ, ٱلْفَلَقِ, وَتَبَّ at a stop.
  **Why it happens:** over-generalisation from today. There is no madd letter to stretch.
  **Say:** *"What is the letter before the last one? A dāl has nothing to hold."*

- **The mistake:** confusing it with lāzim and giving a flat 6 everywhere.
  **Why it happens:** she has heard six-count stops on recordings and copied them without the
  condition.
  **Say:** *"Six is allowed here. It is also allowed to be two. Next week you meet the one that
  is six and has no choice — this is not that one."*

## Hifz for this session

**New Sabaq: al-Māʿūn 107:5–107:7 — the surah completes today.**

> ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ سَاهُونَ · ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ يُرَآءُونَ ·
> وَيَمْنَعُونَ ٱلْمَاعُونَ

Manzil, then Sabqi (al-Kawthar, 107:1–4), then the new portion at 20–40 repetitions, with the
**ten-after-an-error** step logged on its own line.

Three points to hold her to, and all three are today's lesson or last week's:

- **سَاهُونَ, ٱلْمَاعُونَ** — madd ʿāriḍ at the stops. Whatever count she chose in §3, that is
  the count here, every time.
- **يُرَآءُونَ** — the muttaṣil from 3-29, which she has been previewing for two lessons.
  **Four counts.** This is the payoff of that preview.
- **107:4 joined to 107:5.** فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ must not be left hanging; the meaning is
  completed by ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ سَاهُونَ. She was told this at 3-30; now she has
  both āyāt and can actually do it.

**Al-Māʿūn is now complete.** Three of eleven items done. Say the count out loud — visible
progress is the cheapest motivation available.

## Homework

1. **Hifz.** 107:5–7 to 20–40 repetitions daily. From tomorrow, recite **al-Māʿūn whole**, and
   recite 107:4 → 107:5 without a breath between them.
2. **Drill 1** on al-Fātiḥah, once a day, out loud, stopping at every āyah end. One length.
   You are allowed to tap your finger.
3. Write at the top of your madd card: **ʿāriḍ = 2/4/6, my choice, same every time.** Under it
   write the number you chose today.
4. Read one full page of the muṣḥaf stopping only at āyah ends. Count how many of your stops
   landed on a madd letter. Bring the number.
5. **The waṣl test.** Read 1:2 through 1:7 with **no stops at all**, in as few breaths as you
   can. Every madd in it is two counts except ٱلضَّآلِّينَ, which you have been reading long
   since Phase 1. Next week you find out what that one is.
