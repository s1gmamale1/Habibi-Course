# Lesson 3-32 — Madd Lāzim and Its Four Subtypes

> **The last new madd rule in the course.** After today she has all five, and 3-33 assembles
> them. Budget the full session: the subtypes are more taxonomy than any single earlier lesson.

**In plain terms:** two questions give you all four kinds: is it a whole word or a spelt-out letter, and is the sukūn a shadda or a plain one.

## Objectives

- Student can state that lāzim is **6 counts, always, with no alternative**, and say what makes
  it different from ʿāriḍ: **the sukūn is permanent**.
- Student can recognise **kalimī muthaqqal** — madd letter then shaddah in the same word — on
  sight, and produce a true six on ٱلضَّآلِّينَ.
- Student can name **kalimī mukhaffaf**, say it occurs in **one word, in two places only**
  (Yūnus 10:51 and 10:91), and read it.
- Student can read the **muqaṭṭaʿāt** using the three-tier rule: نَقَصَ عَسَلُكُمْ = 6,
  حَيٌّ طَهُرْ = 2, alif = nothing, with ʿayn as the flagged exception.

## Hook — corrective

Have her read the last line of al-Fātiḥah:

> **وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ** (1:7)

She has been reading this with a long madd since Phase 1 lesson **1-14**, and 2-08 drilled the
madd-meets-shadda shape without naming it. So she will produce something long — and it will
almost certainly be **four**, not six. Four is the commonest error in the whole of al-Fātiḥah,
and it is the error of someone who is trying.

Count it with her on your fingers, out loud, while she holds it. Then hold a real six against
it.

> **"You have been reading this correctly in kind and short in degree for months. This one is
> not a choice. It is six, it is always six, and it is the only madd in the language that has
> no second option."**

The corrective is precise: not *wrong*, **short**. Say it that way.

## What they already know

- 2-08, "Madd Meets Shadda" — the shape, unnamed, drilled as a reading fact.
- Lesson 1-14, where ٱلضَّآلِّينَ was read with a six-count madd as a piece of al-Fātiḥah.
- Madd ṭabīʿī ḥarfī from 3-28 §7: the five short letters حَيٌّ طَهُرْ at 2, and alif at nothing.
  **Today supplies the other eight letters**, so half the muqaṭṭaʿāt rule is already hers.
- Madd ʿāriḍ (3-31) and its 2/4/6 choice — the thing lāzim is defined against.
- Shaddah as a doubled letter: a sākin letter plus a voweled one (2-05, 2-06). §2 depends on
  her seeing the sukūn hiding inside the shaddah.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. The definition, and the one question

> **A madd letter followed by a sukūn that is *permanent* — there whether you join or stop, in
> every reading, always.**

Because the cause never goes away, neither does the madd: **6 ḥarakāt, obligatory, no
alternative length.** It is the only madd in the family with a single non-negotiable count.

Put it against last week's rule with the same question she was given at 3-31 §4:

> **Is the sukūn there when I join?**

| Answer | Rule | Counts |
|---|---|---|
| No — only when I stop | [[Madd-Arid-Lissukun]] | 2 / 4 / 6, her choice |
| **Yes — always** | **lāzim** | **6, no choice** |

*Lāzim* means **necessary, inseparable**. The name is the rule.

   **In plain terms:** the sukūn is printed and permanent, so it is there whether you stop or not. That is what makes this one six counts with no choice attached.

### 2. Kalimī muthaqqal — the one she meets constantly

**A madd letter followed, in the same word, by a letter carrying a shaddah.**

The shaddah is where the sukūn is hiding, and she has to see it:

> **A shaddah is two letters — a sākin one and a voweled one.** ٱلضَّآلِّينَ is
> ḍād–alif–**lām sākinah**–lām–yāʾ–nūn. The alif madd is followed by a sākin lām. That sukūn is
> written into the word. It is there when you join and there when you stop. Six.

Write the word out letter by letter with the hidden sukūn made explicit. This is the single
most useful three minutes in the lesson, because it converts a memorised fact into something
she can derive.

The four to work today:

| Word | Ref | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ٱلضَّآلِّينَ | 1:7 | the milestone word |
| ٱلصَّآخَّةُ | 80:33 | |
| ٱلْحَآقَّةُ | 69:1 | |
| دَآبَّةٍ | 2:164 | mid-āyah, not at a familiar surah end — the harder recognition |

**Getting ٱلضَّآلِّينَ to a true six is a milestone**, and it is worth marking as one. It is
the first six-count madd almost every learner ever produces deliberately.

### 3. Kalimī mukhaffaf — two places in the whole Qurʾān

**A madd letter followed, in the same word, by an *original sukūn with no shaddah*.**

It occurs in **one word, in two places**:

| Ref | Text |
|---|---|
| **Yūnus 10:51** | ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ وَقَدْ كُنتُم بِهِۦ تَسْتَعْجِلُونَ |
| **Yūnus 10:91** | ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ وَقَدْ عَصَيْتَ قَبْلُ |

Both verified verbatim against the pinned [[Tanzil]] corpus. **There is no third place.**

> **A flagged double classification, and she should hear it.** The same word is also
> **[[Madd-Farq]]**, because its opening madd arises from an interrogative hamzah entering on a
> hamzat al-waṣl. Both labels are correct; both are taught by reputable teachers. **The
> recitation is identical either way — six counts.** Teach it as farq (the cause) and mention
> lāzim mukhaffaf (the form), or the reverse — but never let her think there are two different
> sounds. The disagreement is about the name on the box, not what is in it.

Practical framing: she will meet this word twice in her life as a reciter, and both times it is
in Sūrat Yūnus. It is on the syllabus because the *category* is on the syllabus, and because a
rule quiz will ask for it.

### 4 & 5. Ḥarfī — the muqaṭṭaʿāt, as one three-tier rule

She already has two of the three tiers. Assemble the whole thing on one line.

A letter of the disconnected openings whose **spelt name is three letters**, the middle one a
madd letter, takes **6**.

**The eight: ن ق ص ع س ل ك م — mnemonic نَقَصَ عَسَلُكُمْ.**

| Tier | Letters | Counts |
|---|---|---|
| Long | نَقَصَ عَسَلُكُمْ (ن ق ص ع س ل ك م) | **6** — ʿayn excepted, below |
| Short | حَيٌّ طَهُرْ (ح ي ط ه ر) | **2** — [[Madd-Tabii]], from 3-28 |
| None | ا (alif) | **no madd at all** |

**Muthaqqal or mukhaffaf** is a distinction *within* the six-count tier, and it is about what
happens after the madd, not about length:

- **Muthaqqal** — the letter's final sākin merges (idghām) into what follows: **الٓمٓ** (2:1),
  where the lām's closing mīm merges into the mīm; **طسٓمٓ**.
- **Mukhaffaf** — it does not: **نٓ** (68:1), **قٓ**, **صٓ**.

Both are six. Teach the split because the quiz asks for it and because the idghām in الٓمٓ is a
real production requirement — "lām-mīm" must not come out as two separate units.

> **ع is the exception inside the exception.** In **كٓهيعٓصٓ** (19:1) and **عٓسٓقٓ** (42:2) the
> ʿayn's madd is a **līn** madd, not a true madd letter, so it takes **4 or 6** — not the flat
> six of its neighbours. Sources transmit both counts; either is correct, **held consistently**.
> See [[Madd-Leen]].

**كٓهيعٓصٓ is the whole rule in one word** and it is the best single exercise in the lesson:

> **kāf 6 · hā 2 · yā 2 · ʿayn 4–6 · ṣād 6**

Have her read it slowly, with fingers, five times. If she can do كٓهيعٓصٓ she can do every
sūrah opening in the muṣḥaf.

### 6. The four subtypes on one card

|  | In a **word** (kalimī) | In a **letter-name** (ḥarfī) |
|---|---|---|
| **Heavy** — muthaqqal (a shaddah follows) | ٱلضَّآلِّينَ · ٱلصَّآخَّةُ · ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · دَآبَّةٍ | الٓمٓ · طسٓمٓ |
| **Light** — mukhaffaf (plain sukūn) | ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ **only** | نٓ · قٓ · صٓ |

Four boxes. **All four are six counts.** The grid is for naming, not for length — say that
explicitly or she will suspect the boxes have different numbers in them.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — spell out the shaddah.** Take ٱلضَّآلِّينَ, ٱلْحَآقَّةُ, دَآبَّةٍ and write each
one letter by letter, marking the hidden sukūn inside every shaddah. Then read each word. You
should be able to point at the sukūn that causes the six.

**Drill 2 — four against six.** Read ٱلضَّآلِّينَ at four counts, then at six, back to back,
three times. The four is what you have been doing. Learn to hear the gap; you cannot fix a
length you cannot hear.

**Drill 3 — al-Fātiḥah, one requirement.** Read the whole surah. I am marking **one** thing:
whether ٱلضَّآلِّينَ is six. Everything else, I say nothing about today.

**Drill 4 — ʿāriḍ or lāzim.** Ten words. For each, tell me whether the sukūn is there when you
join. Then give the count.
نَسْتَعِينُ · ٱلضَّآلِّينَ · ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ · ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · سَاهُونَ · دَآبَّةٍ ·
ٱلْمَاعُونَ · ٱلصَّآخَّةُ · ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · ٱلضَّآلِّينَ again.
*(Answer shape: the ʿāriḍ ones take your chosen 2/4/6; the lāzim ones take 6 with no choice.)*

**Drill 5 — كٓهيعٓصٓ, five times.** Fingers up. kāf six, hā two, yā two, ʿayn four, ṣād six.
Then do الٓمٓ, and make the mīm of the lām actually merge — one continuous unit, not "lām, mīm".

**Drill 6 — the three-tier sort.** Here are the opening letters of eight sūrahs. Sort every
letter in them into 6 / 2 / none before you read a syllable:
الٓمٓ (2:1) · الٓر (10:1) · طه (20:1) · يسٓ (36:1) · حمٓ (40:1) · صٓ (38:1) · قٓ (50:1) ·
طسٓمٓ (28:1).

**Drill 7 — the two Yūnus places.** Open the muṣḥaf to Sūrat Yūnus and find 10:51 and 10:91.
Read the word in both. Say out loud: *"This word is here twice, and nowhere else."* Rare things
are learned by being located, not by being described.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** a six that is genuinely six — and a shaddah that is still fully doubled when it
arrives at the end of it.

- **The mistake:** four counts on ٱلضَّآلِّينَ.
  **Why it happens:** the commonest error in al-Fātiḥah anywhere in the world. Four *feels*
  long, and nothing in her experience has contradicted it.
  **Say:** *"Count with me. One, two, three, four, five, six. Now again, and I'll count out
  loud with you until it stops needing me."*

- **The mistake:** the shaddah arrives single — ٱلضَّآلِّينَ ending as *ad-dāalīn* with one
  lām.
  **Why it happens:** all the attention went into the madd and there was nothing left for the
  letter after it. Very common the week this rule is taught.
  **Say:** *"Six, and then a double lām. Hold the lām. I want to hear two of them."*

- **The mistake:** الٓمٓ read as two separate units, "lām · mīm".
  **Why it happens:** she has not been told there is an idghām in it, and the letter names look
  like a list.
  **Say:** *"The lām's mīm and the next mīm are one sound. Run them together —
  lāāāāmmmmmīīīīm — one breath, no seam."*

- **The mistake:** ʿayn given a flat six alongside its neighbours in كٓهيعٓصٓ.
  **Why it happens:** the neighbours are six and pattern-matching is cheap.
  **Say:** *"That one is a līn, not a madd. Four or six — pick one and use it in both places
  it occurs."*

- **The mistake:** giving ح, ط or ر six counts, or giving alif any madd.
  **Why it happens:** the six-count tier is more memorable than the two-count tier.
  **Say:** *"حَيٌّ طَهُرْ. Say the mnemonic. Is that letter in it?"*

- **The mistake:** treating ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ as ordinary and reading it short.
  **Why it happens:** it is unfamiliar and looks like nothing else on the page.
  **Say:** *"Six. Same as ٱلضَّآلِّينَ. Rare does not mean short."*

4. **Find the openings.** Go through the muṣḥaf and write down every sūrah that begins with

   the word aloud in both places. That is the whole of madd lāzim kalimī mukhaffaf, done.
