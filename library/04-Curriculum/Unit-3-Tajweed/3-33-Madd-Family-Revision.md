# Lesson 3.32 — Revision and Q&A: the Madd Family Capstone

> **No new rules today, and no new Sabaq.** Revision lessons pause new *rules*; they never pause
> hifz revision ([[Hifz-Method]]). This session exists because five madd rules were taught in
> five weeks and the failure mode of that pace is not forgetting — it is **confusing**.

## Objectives

- Student can produce the **five lengths** on demand and say which of them are choices and which
  are not.
- Student can sort any madd she meets using **two questions**: *is there a `~`?* and *what comes
  after the madd letter?*
- Student's madd lengths are **internally consistent** across a full page — the single thing
  Unit 3.6 will be assessed on.
- Student can ask her own questions and get straight answers, including "the scholars differ"
  where that is the true answer.

## Hook — corrective

Hand her a fresh page of the muṣḥaf and ask for one page, read aloud, stops at āyah ends.

Say nothing while she reads. Mark only madd, and mark it on **four columns**: ṭabīʿī, muttaṣil,
munfaṣil, ʿāriḍ. Note the length she actually gave, not whether it was "right".

Then show her the sheet.

The result will almost always be the same shape: the rules are **known** and the lengths
**wander**. A 3-count ṭabīʿī here, a 4-count munfaṣil there, a 5-count muttaṣil in the next
āyah, and a set of stops ranging from 2 to 6 with no pattern.

> **"You know all five rules. Look at the sheet — you named every one of them correctly as you
> read. What you don't yet have is a *tempo*. Today is not about knowing. It is about
> steadiness."**

This is the honest diagnosis at this point in the course, and it reframes the session from
"revision because you might have forgotten" — which is mildly insulting to an adult — to
"calibration", which is what it actually is.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. The whole family on one sheet

Rebuild the card with her, from memory, before showing her the original. What she cannot
reconstruct is what needs the session.

| Cause | Rule | Counts | Choice? |
|---|---|---|---|
| nothing after the madd letter | ṭabīʿī | **2** | no |
| hamzah, **same word** | muttaṣil | **4–5** | length fixed by ṭarīq; **wājib** |
| hamzah, **next word** | munfaṣil | **4–5** | ṭarīq differs — 2 also correct Ḥafṣ |
| sukūn **only at a stop** | ʿāriḍ lis-sukūn | **2 / 4 / 6** | **yes** — hers, held constant |
| sukūn **always there** | lāzim | **6** | **no** |

Two rules that govern the whole table:

1. **Munfaṣil ≤ muttaṣil.** Always. The stronger cause never produces the shorter madd.
2. **One length per session.** Whatever she picks, it holds for the whole reading.

### 2. The two-question sort

This is the procedure, and it should be automatic by the end of the session.

> **Question 1 — is there a `~` over the madd letter?**
> No → **2 counts**, do not think about it further.
> Yes → question 2.
>
> **Question 2 — what is after the madd letter?**
> hamzah, same word → **muttaṣil, 4**
> hamzah, next word → **munfaṣil, 4**
> shaddah or written sukūn → **lāzim, 6**
> nothing, but I am stopping here → **ʿāriḍ, my number**

Run it fifteen times on real words at speed. The target is a decision in under a second, made
**before** she starts saying the word.

### 3. The four that everyone confuses — do these as pairs

Confusion in this family is never random. It is always one of these four pairs, so drill the
pairs rather than the rules.

**Pair A — muttaṣil vs munfaṣil.** *Where is the hamzah?* Same word or next word.
> جَآءَ / بِمَآ أُنزِلَ

**Pair B — ʿāriḍ vs lāzim.** *Is the sukūn there when I join?*
> نَسْتَعِينُ / ٱلضَّآلِّينَ

**Pair C — muttaṣil vs badal.** *Which came first, the hamzah or the madd letter?*
> جَآءَ / ءَامَنُوا۟ — hamzah after = 4; hamzah before = 2.

**Pair D — ʿāriḍ vs līn.** *Is the letter before the last a true madd letter, or a wāw/yāʾ
sākinah after a fatḥah?*
> ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ / مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ — same 2/4/6 choice, different condition.

### 4. The disagreements, restated in one place

She has met three. Collect them, because a scattered set of caveats reads as vagueness while a
collected set reads as scholarship.

| Point | The disagreement | What she does |
|---|---|---|
| **Munfaṣil length** | 4–5 via ash-Shāṭibiyyah; **2** via aṭ-Ṭayyibah (*Ḥafṣ bi-qaṣr al-munfaṣil*) | Reads 4. Recognises 2 as correct when she hears it. |
| **What a ḥarakah is** | finger-fold · a measured ~half-second · **purely relative to tempo** | Uses the finger as a learning aid. Knows the relative definition is the true one. |
| **ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ** | madd farq (by cause) or madd lāzim kalimī mukhaffaf (by form) | Six counts either way. The names differ; the sound does not. |
| **ʿayn in the muqaṭṭaʿāt** | 4 or 6, both transmitted | Picks one and uses it in both places it occurs. |

The lesson to draw for her, out loud: **a disagreement is not a gap in her knowledge.** Being
able to say "there are two positions and here is the one I read by" is a *higher* standard than
being able to say "it is four", not a lower one.

### 5. Q&A — the actual point of the session

Give this at least fifteen minutes and do not fill the silence.

Questions that reliably come up at this point, with short answers ready:

- **"How long is a count, really?"** — See §4 row two. Answer it honestly; do not invent a
  number.
- **"Why is munfaṣil four here and two on that recording?"** — Two ṭuruq. Neither reciter is
  wrong.
- **"What if I stop somewhere with a madd and I'm not sure?"** — If you stopped and there is a
  madd letter one place from the end, it is ʿāriḍ, and it takes your number. That covers nearly
  every case.
- **"Am I allowed to use six for ʿāriḍ if I use four for muttaṣil?"** — Yes. ʿĀriḍ is the one
  genuine free choice in the family. The only constraint is munfaṣil ≤ muttaṣil.
- **"What happens if I get a length wrong in the exam?"** — A madd shortened enough to change
  the word is **Jali**. A madd audibly present but short of its count is **Khafi**. See
  [[Assessment]] §1 — and note which one most of her errors are.

**Write down any question you cannot answer**, tell her you are writing it down, and bring the
answer next session. That behaviour is worth more to an adult student than a confident guess,
and she will test it at least once.

### 6. Self-assessment against the checkpoint

Show her the actual criterion she will be measured against, now rather than at the end:

> **Test 3 of the Final Checkpoint is a binary oral rule quiz, pass at ≥90%, and it covers all
> six families.** Today's material is one of the six.

Run ten quiz-format questions at the end of the session and give her the raw score. If it is
below nine, she knows what the next fortnight's homework is, and she found out from a number
rather than from being told.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — name it before you read it.** I point at a word. You say the rule and the number
**before** you make any sound. Twenty words, timed. Under two seconds each.

**Drill 2 — the four pairs.** Read each pair from §3 back to back, five times: جَآءَ /
بِمَآ أُنزِلَ · نَسْتَعِينُ / ٱلضَّآلِّينَ · جَآءَ / ءَامَنُوا۟ · ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ /
مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ. Say the distinguishing question out loud each time.

**Drill 3 — the consistency page.** One page, read aloud, stops at āyah ends. I mark **only**
whether same-rule madds match each other. Your target is zero variation. This is the drill that
Unit 3.6 is actually assessed on.

**Drill 4 — al-Fātiḥah, fully labelled.** Write out al-Fātiḥah and mark every madd in it with
its rule name and count. Then read it and hit every number. Al-Fātiḥah contains ṭabīʿī, ʿāriḍ
and lāzim — three of the five — and you recite it seventeen times a day.

**Drill 5 — the length ladder, on one word.** Take ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ at a stop and give it 2, then
4, then 6. All three are correct. Now pick one and give me the same one on ten different stops
in a row.

**Drill 6 — teach it back.** Explain the difference between madd ʿāriḍ and madd lāzim to me as
if I had never heard of either. Two minutes, no notes. If you can teach it you have it; if you
cannot, we have found today's real gap.

**Drill 7 — mock quiz.** Ten binary questions in the Final Checkpoint's own format:
*How many counts is madd lāzim? · Is munfaṣil wājib or jāʾiz? · Name the two places ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ
occurs. · Which is longer, munfaṣil or muttaṣil? · What are the eight six-count letters? · Does
ʿāriḍ exist when you join? · Which madd has no alternative length? · What does the maddah sign
mean? · Which letters are حَيٌّ طَهُرْ and what do they take? · What is a ḥarakah?*

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** the *same* length twice. Almost every deduction available in this unit is a
consistency deduction, not a knowledge deduction.

- **The mistake:** correct rule, wandering length.
  **Why it happens:** she is producing each madd as a separate act of will rather than from a
  held tempo. This is the dominant error at this stage and it is worth saying so.
  **Say:** *"Right rule, three different lengths. Set the beat first. The madd fits the beat."*

- **The mistake:** hesitation while she classifies mid-word.
  **Why it happens:** the sort in §2 is not yet automatic, so it is running out loud.
  **Say:** *"Scan the line silently first. Decide, then read. Deciding while you speak costs
  you a mark for fluency even when the answer is right."*

- **The mistake:** munfaṣil creeping above muttaṣil.
  **Why it happens:** whichever rule got taught most recently gets the most length.
  **Say:** *"Connected is the strong one. It can never be shorter."*

- **The mistake:** over-correction — a 3-count ṭabīʿī everywhere, because she is now
  length-conscious.
  **Why it happens:** four weeks of being told madds are too short.
  **Say:** *"Two is a number, not a minimum. A natural madd that gets stretched is a different
  error, not a smaller one."*

- **The mistake:** giving a confident wrong answer in Q&A rather than saying she is not sure.
  **Why it happens:** adult learners guess to avoid looking unprepared, and guessing hides
  exactly the gap the session exists to find.
  **Say:** *"'I don't know' is a correct answer here and it costs you nothing. It is the only
  answer I can actually do something with."*

## Hifz for this session

**No new Sabaq.** Revision lessons pause new *rules* and new *portions*; they never pause hifz
review ([[Hifz-Method]]).

Instead, use the session's hifz slot for a **consolidation pass**:

- **Manzil** — the normal two-surah rotation.
- **Sabqi** — al-Kawthar, al-Māʿūn, Quraysh 106:1–2. Recite each **whole and unaided**.
- **Promotion check.** Under [[Hifz-Method]] §3 this course promotes Sabqi → Manzil after
  **three consecutive clean recitations** — flagged there as the course's own default, since no
  source gives a number. Apply it explicitly today: al-Kawthar and probably al-Māʿūn. **Write
  the promotion date down.**
- **Tally.** Say the number out loud: **al-Fātiḥah + an-Nās, al-Falaq, al-Ikhlāṣ, al-Masad,
  an-Naṣr, al-Kāfirūn, al-Kawthar, al-Māʿūn — plus Quraysh in progress.** Four lessons and
  nineteen āyāt remain to complete all eleven.

Recite the four newest items with **today's madd calibration applied**. The point of the
session transfers directly: her hifz recitation is where inconsistent lengths are most
audible, because the words are automatic and the lengths are not.
