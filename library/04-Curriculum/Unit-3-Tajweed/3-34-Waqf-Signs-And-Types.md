# Lesson 3.33 — Waqf Signs and the Types of Stopping

> **Opening lesson of Unit 3.7.** She has stopped competently since 2-12; what she has never had
> is the *classification* — which stops are good ones, and what the little letters above the
> line are telling her. That is this lesson and the next.

## Objectives

- Student can read the **six waqf signs** this course teaches and say, for each, whether it is an
  instruction or advice.
- Student can classify a stop as **tām, kāfī, ḥasan or qabīḥ**, and say where she may restart
  from in each case.
- Student can state that **stopping at an āyah end is always permitted**, and that **running out
  of breath is not a sin** — but that where she *restarts* is never excused.
- Student knows the sign set **depends on which muṣḥaf she is holding**, and can find and read
  the legend in her own copy.

## Hook — corrective

Give her 6:36 and ask her to read it as she normally would, breathing where she likes:

> **إِنَّمَا يَسْتَجِيبُ ٱلَّذِينَ يَسْمَعُونَ وَٱلْمَوْتَىٰ يَبْعَثُهُمُ ٱللَّهُ**

She will read straight through. Now translate what she just said, word for word: *"only those
who listen respond — and the dead — God will raise them."* Joined without a break, the first
half reads as though the dead are among those who respond.

Then point at the small **م** printed after يَسْمَعُونَ.

> **"That letter has been on the page the whole time. It is not decoration and it is not an
> abbreviation of a word. It means **stop** — and it is there because someone worked out, a
> long time ago, that a reciter who does not stop here says something the āyah does not
> say."**

She has been reading past these marks for the entire course. Today they become readable.

## What they already know

- **How to stop**, mechanically and well, since 2-12; drilled at 2-13, applied on a page at
  2-14. Nothing about the physical act of stopping is new today.
- **Plan the breath before you read** (2-12 §1), and **never stop mid-word** (2-12 §2).
- **Āyah ends are always safe.** She has been given this as her working rule since Unit 2, and
  §4 today explains why it is true and where its one limit is.
- **Madd ʿāriḍ** (3-31) — the rule that only exists at a stop, so every stop she chooses today
  carries a length decision with it.
- **[[Sakt]]** from 3-25: a pause of about two counts with **no breath**. Today its muṣḥaf sign
  joins the set.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. First: check her muṣḥaf, before you teach any sign

**Do this before §2, not after.** The waqf signs are **not part of the revealed text.** They are
a later scholarly aid — chiefly the work of Shaykh Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī Khalaf al-Ḥusaynī
"al-Hamadhānī" (d. 1357 AH) — and **different printing traditions use different sets.**

| Tradition | Character of the set |
|---|---|
| **Madinah (King Fahd)** | the set taught below |
| **Indo-Pak (Tāj / Persian)** | partly different — **ط** for waqf muṭlaq, **ز** for mujawwaz, plus the **ع** rukūʿ marker |
| **Turkish / Ottoman** | a further variant again |

**Teach the set that matches the muṣḥaf she actually holds.** For Uzbek and wider Central Asian
learners the **Indo-Pak set is very likely what is in their hands**, not the Madinah set. If her
copy is Indo-Pak, teach ط and ز from her own page and treat the table below as the second set she
will meet, not the first.

Then have her **find the legend in her own muṣḥaf** — most editions print one, usually on the
first or last page. Read it together. This is a rehearsal of the skill 3-36 §5 makes explicit:
**read the legend of the book in front of you.**

### 2. The six signs this course teaches

| Sign | Arabic name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **م** | الوقف اللازم | **Must stop.** Joining would corrupt the meaning. |
| **لا** | لا وقف فيه | **Do not stop.** If it falls at an āyah end you may stop, but must resume from before it. |
| **ج** | الوقف الجائز | Stopping and joining are **equally permissible**. |
| **صلى** | الوصل أولى | Both permitted; **joining is better**. |
| **قلى** | الوقف أولى | Both permitted; **stopping is better**. |
| **س / سكتة** | السكتة | **Brief pause, no breath** — [[Sakt]], from 3-25. |

Six is enough to read fluently and safely. Anything else on the page: treat it as *"a stop is
discussed here"* and stop at the āyah end instead.

**How to read them honestly** — and this distinction is the real content of §2:

1. **م and لا are instructions.** Follow them.
2. **ج, صلى, قلى are advice.** They record a scholar's judgement about meaning. They are not
   binding, and reciters do differ. Say this plainly; she will meet recordings that ignore them.
3. **The rosette ۝ outranks all of it for a beginner.** Short of breath? Aim for the next
   rosette rather than guess.
4. **س is not a stop at all.** No breath is taken.

### 3. Explicitly excluded from this course, and why

Say what is being left out. A student who later meets an unexplained mark concludes her teacher
did not know it.

- **∴ … ∴ muʿānaqah** (المعانقة) — three dots at **two** places: stop at **one** of them, never
  both. Excluded because it needs the tām/kāfī framework in §5 to be secure first.
- **ق** (قيل عليه الوقف) — "it has been said one may stop here", but joining is better. Excluded
  because learners reliably confuse it with **قلى**.

Also outside this course's set: ص (murakhkhaṣ), صل (qad yūṣal), ز (mujawwaz), ك (kadhālik),
وقفة (a longer saktah).

Tell her: *"You will see these. When you do, stop at the āyah end and ask me."*

### 4. Four types of waqf by **cause**

| Type | Arabic | What it is | Ruling |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ikhtiyārī** | الاختياري | Chosen deliberately | The one classified for quality in §5 |
| **Iḍṭirārī** | الاضطراري | Compelled — breath, cough, sneeze | Permitted **anywhere**; **resume from before** the stopping point |
| **Ikhtibārī** | الاختباري | For testing or teaching — stopping on a word to show its waqf form | Teaching contexts only; resume from that word or earlier |
| **Intiẓārī** | الانتظاري | "Waiting" — gathering variant readings of a word | Specialist qirāʾāt study only |

**The line that matters to her, said in these words: running out of breath is not a sin.** Stop,
breathe, go back a word or two, carry on. She has been told this since 2-12; now it has a name
and a ruling, and the name is worth having because it separates *the stop* (permitted anywhere)
from *the restart* (never excused).

Note that **ikhtibārī is what you and she do in a lesson** every time you ask her to stop on a
word to show what it becomes. Name it when you do it.

### 5. Four types of waqf by **quality** — Ibn al-Jazarī's scheme

> **وَبَعْدَ تَجْوِيدِكَ لِلْحُرُوفِ ⁕ لَا بُدَّ مِنْ مَعْرِفَةِ الْوُقُوفِ**
> *"After mastering the letters, knowledge of the stops is indispensable."*

| Type | Arabic | Meaning | Restart from the next word? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tām** | التام | Complete; no grammatical or semantic link forward | **Excellent** |
| **Kāfī** | الكافي | Complete; linked in *meaning* but not grammar | **Good** |
| **Ḥasan** | الحسن | Understandable alone, but grammatically attached forward | Stopping is good; **restarting from the next word is not** — unless the stop was an āyah end |
| **Qabīḥ** | القبيح | Incomplete, or the stop **distorts** the meaning | **Impermissible** deliberately; if forced, go back |

Work one example of each, in order — 2:5 (tām), 2:3 (kāfī), 1:2 (ḥasan), 107:4 (qabīḥ).

**ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ** (1:2) is the clearest ḥasan: stopping there is fine, but restarting from
رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ is not, because the phrase is grammatically hanging off what came before.
Have her stop there and then restart correctly — from ٱلْحَمْدُ, not from رَبِّ.

### 6. The qabīḥ trap — and it is in her own hifz

> **فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ** (107:4)

She has been reciting al-Māʿūn since 3-30, and she was told then not to stop there. Now she gets
the reason.

The phrase is a complete sentence — *"so woe to those who pray"* — and it lands on an **āyah
end**. Every rule of thumb she has says that is a safe stop. And it is the one place a rule of
thumb fails: the meaning is completed only by 107:5, ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ سَاهُونَ.
Stopped alone, the āyah says the opposite of what it means.

So: **stopping deliberately at a place that reverses the sense is qabīḥ even at an āyah end.**
That is the one limit on "āyah ends are always safe", and 107:4 is the example she should carry
for it, because it is hers.

The other one to give her, because it is the most serious in the Qurʾān:
**لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ** (2:255). Stopping after لَآ إِلَٰهَ alone is a negation of the
creed. **Never stop mid-negation.**

### 7. Al-Ibtidāʾ — where you **start**, and why it is stricter

| | |
|---|---|
| **The rule** | A start must **always** be at a place where the meaning is complete and correct. |
| **Why stricter than waqf** | **There is no such thing as a compelled start.** You always choose where to begin. An ugly stop can be forced on you by your lungs; an ugly start cannot. |
| **In practice** | Stopped somewhere qabīḥ, or forced to stop? **Go back** to a suitable earlier point and resume there. |

And starting invokes [[Hamzat-Wasl]] — she needs the starting vowel the instant she resumes. She
had this at 2-09; **3-36 gives it in full**. Say the connection out loud today: *stopping and
starting are one skill with two halves.*

sakt protects a meaning; in 75:27 it exists purely to stop a merger the general rules would
otherwise force. Same mark, same execution, different reason.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — read your own legend.** Open your muṣḥaf to its legend page. Read every sign in it
aloud with its meaning. Circle the six from §2. Anything left over, we discuss.

**Drill 2 — sign hunt.** One page. Find and circle every waqf sign on it. Label each **I** for
instruction (م, لا) or **A** for advice (ج, صلى, قلى). Then read the page obeying the I's and
following the A's.

**Drill 3 — obey and disobey.** Read 6:36 twice: once stopping at the **م**, once joining
through it. Tell me in your own words what the second reading says. This is the only time you
will be asked to break a waqf lāzim, and you are doing it to hear why it exists.

**Drill 4 — sort the stop.** I give you ten stopping places. Name each one tām, kāfī, ḥasan or
qabīḥ, and tell me where you would restart from.

**Drill 5 — the restart.** I stop you mid-phrase, at random, without warning. You restart —
correctly. Not from where I stopped you; from a place where the meaning begins. Ten times.
Expect to get the first few wrong.

**Drill 6 — the two traps.** Read 107:4 into 107:5 without a breath, five times. Then read 2:255
from ٱللَّهُ through هُوَ without a breath, five times. Both of these you will be doing for the
rest of your life; build them in now.

**Drill 7 — sakt, not stop.** عِوَجَا ۜ قَيِّمًا and مَنْ ۜ رَاقٍ. Two counts of silence,
**breath held**. If you breathed, you made it a waqf. Do it again.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** stops that were **chosen**. You should be able to tell, from the outside, that
she decided where to stop before she got there.

- **The mistake:** reading straight past a **م**.
  **Why it happens:** the marks have been invisible to her for the whole course, and invisibility
  is a hard habit to break in one session.
  **Say:** *"Go back. What is above the line after that word? Now read it again."*

- **The mistake:** stopping at **لا**.
  **Why it happens:** it looks like an instruction *to* stop — لا is a word she knows, and the
  mark's meaning is the opposite of what the eye suggests.
  **Say:** *"لا means no. No stop. Read on."*

- **The mistake:** stopping at 107:4 and restarting from 107:5.
  **Why it happens:** it is an āyah end, and her rule of thumb says āyah ends are safe. She is
  applying a rule she was correctly given.
  **Say:** *"That's the exception, and it's the one you recite every day. What does the āyah mean
  on its own? Now join it."*

- **The mistake:** restarting from wherever she happened to stop.
  **Why it happens:** she is treating the restart as a continuation rather than as a start. This
  is the single most common ibtidāʾ error and it survives long after the waqf errors are gone.
  **Say:** *"You're not continuing, you're starting. Go back to where the meaning begins."*

- **The mistake:** breathing during a sakt.
  **Why it happens:** two counts of silence feels exactly like a short stop, and the breath comes
  automatically.
  **Say:** *"Hold your breath. Silence, then continue, same breath. Again."*

- **The mistake:** treating ج / صلى / قلى as binding and becoming rigid.
  **Why it happens:** over-correction from today's lesson on م and لا.
  **Say:** *"Those three are advice from a scholar, not an instruction from the text. You are
  allowed to weigh them."*
