# Lesson 3-35 — How Words Change When You Stop

> Ibn al-Jazarī gives these three topics one chapter — **معرفة المقطوع والموصول وحكم تاء
> التأنيث** — and they belong together for one reason: **all three are about what the muṣḥaf's
> spelling tells you about where you may stop and what you say when you get there.**

## Objectives

- Student can apply the **nine changes** a word undergoes at a stop, without prompting.
- Student can distinguish **tāʾ marbūṭah ة** from an **open tāʾ ت** in the printed word and stop
  on each correctly — as a **hāʾ** and as a **tāʾ** respectively.
- Student can say what **rawm** and **ishmām** are and when each is available.
- Student can read **maqṭūʿ** (written apart) and **mawṣūl** (written joined) and say why the
  spelling constrains where she may stop.

## Hook — corrective

Ask her to stop on each of these two, one after the other:

> **سُنَّةَ ٱللَّهِ** (48:23)  ·  **سُنَّتَ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ** (35:43)

Same word. Same meaning. She will stop on both the same way, almost certainly as *sunnah*.

> **"Look at the two tāʾs. One is round with two dots — ة. One is open — ت. They are the same
> word, spelt two ways, in two different āyāt, and they stop differently: *sunnah* and
> *sunnat*. The muṣḥaf is not being inconsistent. It is telling you something, and today you
> learn to hear what."**

That is the whole lesson in one pair: **the spelling is instruction.** She has been taught for
two units to read what is written; today she learns that the *shape of the writing itself*
carries rules.

## What they already know

- **Three of the nine changes**, from 2-12 §3: the final ḥarakah drops, ة becomes a silent hāʾ,
  and a long vowel before the stop stretches. She has been doing all three since Unit 2.
- **The exception already given once**: ذَاتَ in 111:3 is written with an open tāʾ, so it stops
  as *dhāt*. 2-12 gave it in passing; today it becomes a category.
- **Madd ʿāriḍ** (3-31) and **madd līn** — changes 5 and 6 below are those rules, seen from the
  waqf side.
- **Qalqalah kubrā** — change 7.
- **Waqf types and signs** (3-34), which tell her *where* to stop. Today is *what happens* once
  she does.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. The nine changes

Stopping is not just silence. **The last word physically changes shape.** A learner who stops
without applying these produces a word that does not exist in Arabic.

| # | Change | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Final ḥarakah drops** | The last letter becomes sākin | ٱلرَّحِيمِ → ٱلرَّحِيمْ |
| 2 | **Tāʾ marbūṭah → sākin hāʾ** | ة becomes هْ | ٱلْجَنَّةِ → ٱلْجَنَّهْ |
| 3 | **Tanwīn fatḥ → alif** | 2 counts — [[Madd-Iwad]] | أَفْوَاجًا → أَفْوَاجَا |
| 4 | **Tanwīn ḍamm / kasr → dropped** | Plain sukūn, no madd | أَحَدٌ → أَحَدْ |
| 5 | **Madd ʿāriḍ appears** | A preceding madd letter takes 2/4/6 | نَسْتَعِينُ → نَسْتَعِينْ |
| 6 | **Madd līn appears** | A preceding līn letter takes 2/4/6 | خَوْفٍۭ → خَوْفْ |
| 7 | **Qalqalah kubrā** | If the final letter is a qalqalah letter | أَحَدٌ → أَحَدْ, bounced |
| 8 | **Madd ṣilah drops** | The connective madd on the hāʾ vanishes | لَّهُۥ → لَهْ |
| 9 | **Shaddah retained** | A final shaddah is held, with emphasis | وَتَبَّ → وَتَبّْ |

**Teach it as one table, not nine rules.** Four separate madd rules she already knows — ʿiwaḍ,
ʿāriḍ, līn, and the disappearance of ṣilah — are *consequences* of this single table. Framed
this way, half of them stop being new material and become observations.

Point out the arithmetic to her: **she already does 1, 2, 5, 7 and (unknowingly) 3.** Today adds
4, 6, 8, 9 — four items, not nine.

### 2. Change 3 is not an addition

أَفْوَاجًا → *afwājā*. She is not adding an alif. **Look at the page: the alif is already
printed there.** All that happened is that the tanwīn above it went quiet, leaving the alif to
be read as what it is. That framing costs ten seconds and saves her a rule; 2-12 §3 already gave
it to her, and repeating it now is deliberate.

### 3. The tāʾ marbūṭah, and the open-tāʾ exception

**ة is read as a تـ when you join, and as a هْ when you stop.** The two dots exist for the
joined reading. Stop, and you ignore them.

| Joined | Stopped |
|---|---|
| ٱلْمُوقَدَةُ (104:6) | ٱلْمُوقَدَهْ |
| رِحْلَةَ ٱلشِّتَآءِ (106:2) | رِحْلَهْ, if she stopped there |
| حَمَّالَةَ ٱلْحَطَبِ (111:4) | joined straight through — the ة is a *t* |

**Where the muṣḥaf writes an open tāʾ**, it stops as a plain sākin **تْ**, not as a hāʾ:

> **رَحْمَتَ · نِعْمَتَ · سُنَّتَ · ٱمْرَأَتَ · كَلِمَتَ · شَجَرَتَ · لَعْنَتَ · فِطْرَتَ ·
> ذَاتَ**

This is **spelling, not grammar**. There is no rule to derive; there is a shape on the page to
obey. And the same word can appear both ways — سُنَّةَ ٱللَّهِ (48:23) against
سُنَّتَ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ (35:43) — which is exactly why she must look rather than remember.

> **Say this out loud, because it is the transferable lesson: read what is written. The muṣḥaf's
> orthography is transmitted, not typeset. Where it looks irregular, the irregularity is the
> information.**

**In plain terms:** the round tāʾ goes soft when you stop — a breath, not a *t*. The open tāʾ, written flat, keeps its *t*.

### 4. Rawm and ishmām

Two optional refinements at a stop. Teach them as *available*, not as required.

| Term | Arabic | What you do | Applies to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rawm** | الروم | Pronounce about **one third** of the final vowel — audible, very soft | **ḍammah and kasrah only**, never fatḥah |
| **Ishmām** | الإشمام | **Round the lips** immediately after the sukūn, silently — visible, inaudible | **ḍammah only** |

Demonstrate both on ٱلرَّحِيمِ (rawm, kasrah) and on نَسْتَعِينُ (ishmām, ḍammah). Have her
watch your mouth for the ishmām — the whole point is that it is seen and not heard.

**Neither is required of her at the Final Checkpoint**, and she should know that. They are here
because she will see reciters do them and because ishmām is the mechanism behind the one
Ḥafṣ-specific word she must eventually know: **لَا تَأْمَ۫نَّا** (Yūsuf 12:11), the **only**
ishmām in Ḥafṣ. See [[Hafs-Special-Words]].

   **In plain terms:** rawm is saying a sliver of the vowel out loud; ishmām is shaping it with your lips and making no sound at all. One is heard, one is only seen.

### 5. Al-Maqṭūʿ wa'l-Mawṣūl — separated and joined

Ibn al-Jazarī pairs this with the tāʾ chapter, and the reason is the same: **the muṣḥaf's
spelling constrains where you may stop.**

Some word pairs are written **apart** (maqṭūʿ) and some are written **joined** (mawṣūl), and the
Uthmānī spelling is not free variation — it is fixed and transmitted.

| Written **apart** — maqṭūʿ | Written **joined** — mawṣūl |
|---|---|
| مِّن مَّا مَلَكَتْ (30:28) | وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَٰهُمْ (2:3) |
| فِى مَآ ءَاتَىٰكُم (5:48) | فِيمَا كَانُوا۟ (2:113) |
| أَن لَّآ أَقُولَ (7:105) | أَلَّا تَعْبُدُوٓا۟ (11:2) |
| مَالِ هَٰذَا ٱلْكِتَٰبِ (18:49) | — |

**The rule she needs, in one line:**

> **Written apart → the two are separate words, so a stop between them is possible.
> Written joined → it is one word, and you never stop inside a word.**

That is the practical consequence, and it connects straight back to 2-12 §2: *never stop in the
middle of a word*. Whether something *is* one word is decided by the page, not by grammar.

**مَالِ هَٰذَا ٱلْكِتَٰبِ** (18:49) is the memorable one: مَا and لِ, written with a gap. It
looks like a typographic accident and it is not.

Keep this section short and concrete. She needs the principle plus four examples, not the
classical list of every case.

### 6. Putting it together — one word, everything at once

Take the last word of Sūrat Quraysh, which she completed last session:

> **مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ** (106:4)

Stopping on it runs three of the nine changes at once: the **tanwīn kasr drops** (change 4), the
**līn wāw appears** with her chosen 2/4/6 (change 6), and the fāʾ closes on a clean sukūn
(change 1). Have her produce it slowly, naming each change as it happens, then at speed.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — join it, stop it, name the change.** For each word, read it joined, then stopped,
then tell me which numbered change you applied.
ٱلرَّحِيمِ · ٱلْمُوقَدَةُ · أَفْوَاجًا · أَحَدٌ · نَسْتَعِينُ · خَوْفٍۭ · لَّهُۥ · وَتَبَّ.

**Drill 2 — the two tāʾs.** I show you twelve words ending in a tāʾ. Before reading, say
**round** or **open**. Then stop on each: hāʾ for round, tāʾ for open. Look, do not remember.

**Drill 3 — the sunnah pair.** Read 48:23 and 35:43, stopping on the word in each.
*sunnah* / *sunnat*. Five times, alternating. Then tell me what made the difference.

**Drill 4 — the ة hunt, level two.** 2-12's Drill 3 asked you to find eight words ending in ة.
Now find eight ending in an **open** tāʾ. They are rarer, and hunting for them is how you learn
to see them.

**Drill 5 — watch my mouth.** I stop on نَسْتَعِينُ twice: once plainly, once with ishmām. Tell
me which was which. You cannot hear it — you have to look. Then you do it and I will watch.

**Drill 6 — apart or joined.** Six items. Say whether they are written apart or joined, and
whether a stop is possible inside them: مِّن مَّا · وَمِمَّا · فِى مَآ · فِيمَا · أَن لَّآ ·
أَلَّا. Then read 18:49 and find مَالِ.

**Drill 7 — the full stop.** Read one page. At every stop, apply **every** change that word
calls for. I will interrupt and ask you to name them. Slowly at first — accuracy before pace.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** a stopped word that is a *word*. If what she produced is not a possible Arabic
form, one of the nine did not fire.

- **The mistake:** ة read as a **t** at a stop — *al-mūqadat*, *raḥmat*.
  **Why it happens:** the two dots are right there, and Unit 2 spent a whole lesson teaching her
  to read the dots. She may also have heard the *-at* form in loanwords in her own language.
  **Say:** *"Cover the two dots with your fingernail. What letter is it now? Say that."*

- **The mistake:** stopping on an **open** tāʾ as a hāʾ — *raḥmah* for رَحْمَتَ.
  **Why it happens:** brand-new over-correction from change 2, taught fifteen minutes earlier.
  **Say:** *"Look at the shape. Is it round or open? Open stays a t."*

- **The mistake:** the final ḥarakah still audible — *nastaʿīnu*, *aḥadun*.
  **Why it happens:** the vowel is printed and she is obeying the page. Give her credit for this
  when you correct it; it is obedience, not carelessness.
  **Say:** *"The page is written for joining. When you stop, the last mark is not yours."*

- **The mistake:** the ṣilah madd kept at a stop — *lahū* instead of *lah*.
  **Why it happens:** the little wāw is printed after the hāʾ and looks like a letter.
  **Say:** *"That mark only exists to carry you to the next word. You're not going there."*

- **The mistake:** a final shaddah released single — *wa-tab* for وَتَبَّ.
  **Why it happens:** stopping feels like ending and ending feels like relaxing.
  **Say:** *"Hold the bāʾ. Two of them, and then stop. Lean on it."*

- **The mistake:** stopping inside a mawṣūl word — a breath between مِمَّا's two halves.
  **Why it happens:** she knows the grammar says two words and she is trusting the grammar over
  the page.
  **Say:** *"Is there a space? No. Then it is one word, and you never stop inside a word."*

- **The mistake:** attempting rawm or ishmām and producing a full vowel.
  **Why it happens:** a third of a vowel is genuinely hard, and she is overshooting.
  **Say:** *"That was the whole vowel. Try a third of it — or leave it out entirely. Neither is
  required of you."*

## Hifz for this session

**New Sabaq: al-Fīl 105:1–105:2.**

> أَلَمْ تَرَ كَيْفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِأَصْحَٰبِ ٱلْفِيلِ ·
> أَلَمْ يَجْعَلْ كَيْدَهُمْ فِى تَضْلِيلٍ

Manzil first, then Sabqi — al-Kawthar, al-Māʿūn, Quraysh — then the new portion at 20–40
repetitions, **ten more after any caught error**.

**The last surah of the set begins today.** Three more āyāt after this and all eleven items are
in place. Say that to her.

Points to hold her to:

- **105:1 is the longest āyah in the set** and it is the breath-plan sentence she first met at
  2-12. Plan the breath before she recites, not during.
- **ٱلْفِيلِ** and **تَضْلِيلٍ** — madd ʿāriḍ at both āyah ends (3-31). Her number, both times.
- **أَلَمْ تَرَ** opens 105:1 and **أَلَمْ يَجْعَل** opens 105:2. Nearly identical openings are
  the classic place for a transposition error — and transposition is scored on the
  **memorisation-fidelity axis** at the Final Checkpoint, separately from tajweed
  ([[Hifz-Method]] §6). Drill the two openings back to back today.

## Homework

1. **Hifz.** 105:1–2 to 20–40 repetitions daily. Sabqi = al-Kawthar, al-Māʿūn, Quraysh, all
   whole. Manzil rotation first. Drill أَلَمْ تَرَ / أَلَمْ يَجْعَل as a pair.
2. **Drill 4.** Find eight words in the muṣḥaf written with an **open** tāʾ. Write each with its
   reference. Read each one stopped.
3. Copy the nine-change table by hand onto a card. Nine rows. By hand.
4. **Drill 7** on one page a day: stop at āyah ends and apply every change. Mark on the page any
   stop you were unsure about and bring the page.
5. Recite Sūrat Quraysh and stop deliberately on **مِّنْ خَوْفٍۭ** with the līn at your chosen
   count. Then recite al-Masad and stop on **وَتَبَّ** with the shaddah held. Two stops, two
   different rules, both from surahs you already hold.
