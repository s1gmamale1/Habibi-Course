# Lesson 3-36 — Hamzat al-Waṣl in Full, Silent Letters, and How to Read Any Legend

> **Three topics, one skill: reading marks rather than remembering them.** The first two are
> classical; §6 is the one this course adds, and it is what end goal 4 actually requires.

## Objectives

- Student can start on **any** word beginning with **ٱ** and produce the correct vowel, using the
  verb / noun / particle branches rather than by memory.
- Student never pronounces a waṣl hamzah **when joining**.
- Student can read the **round zero ○** and the **oval zero ٘** and say what each does at a join
  and at a stop.
- Student can pick up **any coloured muṣḥaf or app**, find its legend, and read it — rather than
  assuming a palette she has memorised.

## Hook — corrective

Two corrections, one after the other, and they pull in opposite directions.

**First**, ask her to read 110:3 joined:

> **فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ**

Many learners produce *wa-istaghfirhu* — an audible glottal stop in the middle of the phrase.
It is especially common in learners with Persian, Urdu or Turkic speech habits. **The hamzah is
not there when you join.** *Wastaghfirhu.*

**Second**, stop her and ask her to *start* from ٱدْخُلُوا۟ (16:32). She will say *idkhulū*.

> **"So one of them you are pronouncing when you shouldn't, and the other you are pronouncing
> wrong when you should. Both are the same letter. Today it stops being a guess."**

Two errors in ninety seconds, from opposite directions, on one letter. That is a good hook
because it demonstrates that the letter has a *rule*, not a habit.

## What they already know

- **2-09** gave her the working version: the article starts with a fatḥah, and the hamzah drops
  when joining. She has been resuming from stops with it since 2-12. Today is the **full**
  branch structure, not a first pass.
- **3-34 §7**: an ibtidāʾ must be at a place where the meaning is complete — and every ibtidāʾ
  on a **ٱ** word invokes today's rule. Stopping and starting are one skill.
- **3-35**: the muṣḥaf's spelling is instruction. Silent letters are the same principle again:
  a letter written and not read, with a mark saying when.
- **[[Madd-Farq]]** or [[Madd-Lazim]] from 3-32: ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ, which is an interrogative hamzah
  landing on a waṣl hamzah. §5 closes that loop.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. The two hamzahs

| | **Hamzat al-qaṭʿ** | **Hamzat al-waṣl** |
|---|---|---|
| Written | أ إ ؤ ئ ء | **ٱ** — alif with a small **ص** (for صِلَة) |
| Pronounced when **starting** on the word | yes | **yes** |
| Pronounced when **joining** from the previous word | **yes** | **no — dropped entirely** |
| Examples | أَنْعَمْتَ · إِيَّاكَ · أَحَدٌ | ٱلْحَمْدُ · ٱدْخُلُوا۟ · ٱبْن |

**Why it exists, in one sentence:** the waṣl hamzah is there to give a sākin letter something to
start on. Once there is a previous word to lean on, it is not needed and disappears.

Her own hifz supplies the cleanest examples: **وَٱنْحَرْ** (108:2) is *wanḥar*, and
**وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ** (110:3) is *wastaghfirhu*. Never *wa-inḥar*, never *wa-istaghfirhu*.

### 2. Starting on a waṣl hamzah — the three branches

This is the whole rule, and it is the part 2-09 only sketched.

#### A. Verbs — أفعال

| Case | Vowel | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Past tense of 5- and 6-letter verbs | **kasrah** | اِنْطَلَقَ · اِسْتَغْفَرَ |
| Command of 5- and 6-letter verbs | **kasrah** | اِنْطَلِقْ · اِسْتَغْفِرْ · اِسْتَقِمْ |
| Command of a 3-letter verb, third letter of the **present tense** has an **original ḍammah** | **ḍammah** | اُدْخُلُوا (يَدْخُلُ) · اُنظُرْ (يَنظُرُ) · اُعْبُدُوا (يَعْبُدُ) |
| Command of a 3-letter verb, third letter has **fatḥah or kasrah** | **kasrah** | اِضْرِبْ (يَضْرِبُ) · اِذْهَبْ (يَذْهَبُ) · اِجْعَلْ (يَجْعَلُ) |
| **Exception** — that ḍammah is **ʿāriḍ**, arising from a deleted weak letter | **kasrah** | اِمْشُوا (38:6) · اِبْنُوا · اِقْضُوا |

**The trick to teach, and it is the whole branch:** *say the present tense in your head first.*
يَدْخُلُ has a ḍammah on the khāʾ → start *udkhulū*. يَذْهَبُ has a fatḥah on the hāʾ → start
*idhhab*. Make her do this out loud, slowly, six or seven times, until the lookup becomes
internal.

#### B. Nouns — أسماء

**The seven samāʿī ("heard") nouns that occur in the Qurʾān all take kasrah:**

> **اِسْم · اِبْن · اِبْنَة · اِمْرُؤ · اِمْرَأَة · اِثْنَان · اِثْنَتَان**

Classical Arabic lists ten; اِسْت, اِيْمُن and اِبْنُم do not occur in the Qurʾān, so seven is
the working number. **Memorise the seven.** There is nothing to derive here — that is what
*samāʿī*, "heard", means.

#### C. Particles — حروف

**The definite article ال always takes fatḥah.** اَلْحَمْدُ · اَلرَّحْمَٰن · اَلنَّاس ·
اَلَّذِينَ.

One item, no exceptions, and it is by far the most frequent case. Say so: **most of the time
the answer is "a".**

### 3. Waṣl hamzah followed by a sākin hamzah

When she **starts** on a word whose waṣl hamzah is immediately followed by a **sākin hamzah**,
the second hamzah turns into a madd letter matching the first vowel:

| Written | Started as | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| ٱئْتُوا | اِيتُوا | 2:23 |
| ٱئْتِ | اِيتِ | 10:15 |
| ٱؤْتُمِنَ | اُوتُمِنَ | 2:283 |
| ٱئْذَن | اِيذَن | 9:49 |

Four words. Show her all four, have her start on each, and move on. This is a small closed set
and it does not deserve a large share of the session.

### 4. Silent letters — three sources of silence

Some letters are **written but not recited**. In Uthmānī orthography this is never an archaism
to be ignored: **the muṣḥaf marks each case, and the mark tells you *when* the letter is
silent.**

**1. Hamzat al-waṣl** — silent whenever you join. §1 above. The most frequent silent letter in
the Qurʾān.

**2. The small round zero ○** — الصفر المستدير — **never** pronounced, joining or stopping. Pure
spelling.
- **أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ** (2:5) — the wāw is never sounded: *ulāʾika*.
- **لَّٰكِنَّا۠** (18:38) when joining — *lākinna huwa*.

**3. The small oval / vertical zero ٘** — الصفر المستطيل القائم — silent **only when joining**;
pronounced, 2 counts, when stopping.

| Word | Ref | Joining | Stopping |
|---|---|---|---|
| سَلَٰسِلَا۟ | 76:4 | *salāsila* | *salāsilā* |
| ٱلظُّنُونَا۠ | 33:10 | *aẓ-ẓunūna* | *aẓ-ẓunūnā* |
| وَثَمُودَا۟ | 53:51 | *thamūda* | *thamūdā* |

**أَنَا۠ — learn this one by heart.** It occurs some sixty times. Final alif **written, silent
when joining, 2 counts at a stop**. *ana aqallu* when joining (18:39) — but *anā* if she stops
on it. A learner who reads *anā* mid-sentence is adding a madd that is not there.

> **The contrast that matters:** a letter carrying **no mark at all** is *not* silent — it is
> merged (mudgham) or hidden (mukhfā), and it is still doing acoustic work. The absence of a
> sukūn is itself information. Do not confuse a bare letter with a zeroed one.

### 5. Closing the loop on ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ

Interrogative hamzah landing on a hamzat al-waṣl: **ءَآلذَّكَرَيْنِ · ءَآللَّهُ ·
ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ** — six counts, or *tas-hīl*. She met the word at 3-32 as madd lāzim kalimī
mukhaffaf; now she can see the other half of why it is spelt the way it is. Two names, one
sound, six counts. One minute; do not re-teach it.

### 6. How to read any legend — the section that matters most

This is the course's own addition, and it is what **end goal 4** actually requires: not "know
this palette", but **"read whatever is in front of you."**

**The problem, stated plainly:** coloured muṣḥafs do not agree with each other.

| Source | Red means | Blue means |
|---|---|---|
| **The Quranly app** — this course's default, **Family B** | **qalqalah** | **madd** |
| The printed **Dar al-Maʿrifah** muṣḥaf | **madd** | — |

Both are in wide use. Both are internally consistent. **A learner who has memorised one palette
and picks up the other will misread every red letter on the page** — she will bounce her madds
and stretch her qalqalahs.

This course's default is **Family B**, matching Quranly, verified by direct observation of the
app ([[Verification-Log]]): red = qalqalah, blue = madd, and the rest as below.

| Family B colour | Rule |
|---|---|
| `#DD0008` red | [[Qalqalah-Kubra]] · [[Qalqalah-Sughra]] |
| `#537FFF` / `#4050FF` / `#2144C1` blue band | [[Madd-Tabii]] 2 · madd farʿī 4–5 · [[Madd-Lazim]] 6 |
| `#FF7E1E` orange | [[Ghunnah]] |
| `#9400A8` violet | [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]] |
| `#26BFFD` sky blue | [[Iqlab]] |
| `#169777` teal-green | [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]] |
| `#169200` green | [[Idgham-Bila-Ghunnah]] |
| `#AAAAAA` grey | [[Hamzat-Wasl]] · [[Silent-Letters]] · [[Lam-Shamsiyyah]] |

**Notice that grey is today's colour.** The silent things share a colour, and that is a real
piece of information about how the palette thinks.

**The procedure to teach her — four steps, and she should be able to say them back:**

1. **Find the legend.** Muṣḥafs print it on the first or last page; apps put it in settings or
   behind a "?" icon. If there is no legend, the book is not usable for rule identification and
   she should not guess.
2. **Look up red first.** Red is the colour the traditions disagree about, so it is the fastest
   test of which family she is holding.
3. **Check one word she already knows.** Open to al-Fātiḥah and look at **ٱلضَّآلِّينَ**. If it
   is coloured and the legend says that colour is madd, she is in a madd-coloured palette.
   Cross-check with a qalqalah letter — the dāl of أَحَدٌ.
4. **Read the whole legend before you read the page.** Two minutes, once per book.

> **"You are not learning a set of colours. You are learning to ask a book what its colours
> mean. Every muṣḥaf you ever pick up will answer that question if you look, and the ones that
> won't answer it are the ones you shouldn't trust."**

Do this live, in the session, with whatever coloured muṣḥaf or app she actually has. If she has
two, do it with both and let her find the contradiction herself.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — which vowel?** I point at twenty **ٱ** words. You say **a**, **i** or **u** before
reading, and you say which branch you used: article, verb, or one of the seven nouns.

**Drill 2 — present tense first.** Ten 3-letter commands. For each, say the present tense out
loud, point at its third letter, then start the command. اُدْخُلْ (يَدْخُلُ) · اِذْهَبْ
(يَذْهَبُ) · اُنظُرْ (يَنظُرُ) · اِضْرِبْ (يَضْرِبُ) · اُعْبُدْ (يَعْبُدُ) · اِجْعَلْ
(يَجْعَلُ).

**Drill 3 — join it, start it.** Each word twice: once joined from the word before, once started
on. وَٱنْحَرْ / ٱنْحَرْ · وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ / ٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ · بِٱلْحَمْدِ / ٱلْحَمْدُ. The hamzah
appears and disappears. That is the rule doing its job.

**Drill 4 — the seven nouns.** Recite them: اِسْم, اِبْن, اِبْنَة, اِمْرُؤ, اِمْرَأَة,
اِثْنَان, اِثْنَتَان. All kasrah. Ten times, until it is a list you own.

**Drill 5 — stop-and-start.** I stop you at random. You restart from a place where the meaning
begins (3-34 §7), and if that word starts with **ٱ**, you get the vowel right first time.
Fifteen times.

**Drill 6 — zeros.** Ten marked letters, mixed round and oval. Say **never** or **only at a
stop** for each, then read the word both ways where it applies.

**Drill 7 — أَنَا۠.** Find five occurrences. Read each joined onward — no madd. Then stop on one
deliberately: two counts.

**Drill 8 — the legend, live.** Open your coloured muṣḥaf or app. Find the legend. Read it aloud
to me. Tell me what red is in *this* book. Now open al-Fātiḥah and check ٱلضَّآلِّينَ and the
dāl of أَحَدٌ against what you just read. **This is the drill you will actually use for the rest
of your life.**

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** silence where the page says silence — and a clean, correctly-vowelled start when
she resumes.

- **The mistake:** pronouncing the waṣl hamzah when joining — *wa-istaghfirhu*, *wa-inḥar*.
  **Why it happens:** the alif is visible and her first language may not permit the consonant
  cluster that results from dropping it.
  **Say:** *"Run the two words together with no bump. wastaghfirhu. Say it as one word."*

- **The mistake:** starting ٱلْحَمْدُ with a kasrah — *il-ḥamdu*.
  **Why it happens:** kasrah is the default in most branches, and the article is the exception
  that is also the most common case.
  **Say:** *"Is it ٱل? Then it's an a. Always."*

- **The mistake:** starting ٱدْخُلُوا۟ with a kasrah.
  **Why it happens:** she has not run the present-tense lookup, because the lookup takes a
  second and she is reading in real time.
  **Say:** *"يَدْخُلُ. What's on the khāʾ? A ḍammah. So: udkhulū."*

- **The mistake:** pronouncing the wāw of أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ.
  **Why it happens:** it is a letter, it is written, and nothing in her reading experience says
  a written letter can be pure decoration.
  **Say:** *"Look at the little circle. That's a zero. There is no wāw. ulāʾika."*

- **The mistake:** reading *anā* in the middle of a sentence.
  **Why it happens:** *anā* is the form she knows from spoken Arabic and from the stop form.
  **Say:** *"Are you stopping on it? No. Then it's ana, short, straight into the next word."*

- **The mistake:** treating an unmarked merged or hidden letter as if it were silent.
  **Why it happens:** over-generalisation from today — "letters can be silent" becomes "letters
  I can't hear clearly are silent".
  **Say:** *"Is there a zero over it? No? Then it's doing work. Hidden is not silent."*

- **The mistake:** naming a colour from memory instead of from the legend.
  **Why it happens:** she has been reading one app for months and the palette feels like a fact
  about the rules rather than a fact about the app.
  **Say:** *"Don't tell me what red means. Show me where the book says what red means."*

Points to hold her to:
