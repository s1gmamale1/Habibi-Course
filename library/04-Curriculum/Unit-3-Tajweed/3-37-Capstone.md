# Lesson 3-37 — Capstone: All Families, All Colours, Mock Oral Quiz

> **The last lesson of Unit 3.** Nothing new is taught. This session exists to find out what she
> actually holds, one week before the Final Checkpoint does it for real — and to make sure that
> what the Checkpoint finds is not a surprise to either of you.

## Objectives

- Student can identify **every rule she has been taught**, by family, on unseen text.
- Student can read a **coloured** page in a palette she has not used, having read its legend
  first.
- Student can recite **all eleven hifz items**, unaided, in reverse muṣḥaf order.
- Student and teacher both know, in writing, **which items will be flagged** at the Final
  Checkpoint and what the fortnight before it is for.

## Hook — corrective

No warm-up, no explanation. Hand her an unseen plain page and say: *"Read."*

Mark it silently on the Final Checkpoint's own rubric ([[Assessment]] §1): Jali −2, Khafi −1,
self-correction after prompt −1, examiner corrects −2. Log every deduction **per letter and per
rule**, not per passage.

Then show her the sheet before you say anything about it.

> **"That is exactly what happens in the Final Checkpoint, and this is exactly the sheet. The
> difference is that today the result is information and in two weeks it is a result. Look at
> the columns — that is what the next fortnight is for."**

The corrective is the *format*, not any individual error. An adult learner who meets the marking
sheet for the first time on the day of the exam is being examined on the sheet as well as on the
recitation.

## What they already know

Everything. That is the point of the session. Six families:

| 2 | Mīm sākinah — [[Izhar-Shafawi]], [[Ikhfa-Shafawi]], [[Idgham-Shafawi]] | 3.2 | green |

Plus the sifāt and makhārij from Phase 1, which are still live and still marked.

## Teaching sequence

### 1. Rebuild the map from memory

Blank sheet. She writes the six families and every rule under each, **before** you show her
anything. Then compare against the table above.

What is missing from her sheet is the fortnight's homework, and it is far more informative than
anything you could ask her. Typical gaps at this point: the rāʾ family (least colour support,
fewest visual cues), and the distinction between the three idghām types in family 1.

   **In plain terms:** six families is the whole map. If you can draw it from memory, you know what you are looking at on any page you open.

### 2. Read an unseen plain page — the full-attention pass

One page. Plain Uthmani, no colour, no annotation. She plans her stops first — that is a skill
this course taught and it is assessed, not excluded.

Mark **everything**: makhraj, ṣifāt, all six families, waqf placement, ibtidāʾ, pace.

**Give her the per-column totals, not the total.** A result of "−9" is unactionable. A result of

> −2 rāʾ-tafkhīm (×2) · −1 madd-ʿāriḍ-consistency (×3) · −2 waṣl-hamzah-start

says: *her rāʾ rules and her stop lengths need the fortnight; everything else is ready.* That is
a revision plan, and it costs one extra column on the sheet ([[Assessment]] §2).

### 3. Read a coloured page — in a palette she has not used

**Do not use the app she has been reading all year.** The whole point of 3-36 §6 is that she can
read a palette she has not memorised.

1. Give her a coloured muṣḥaf or app she has not used.
2. She finds the legend and reads it aloud.
3. She states what **red** means **in this book**.
4. Only then does she read the page, naming each coloured span and what it demands.

**If she names a colour from memory instead of from the legend, stop her.** That is the failure
mode the section exists to prevent, and it is better found today.

Two scoring axes, kept separate, exactly as at the Checkpoint: **identification** (does she know
what the colour is) and **execution** (can she do it). A student who names ikhfāʾ correctly and
then produces a plain nūn has one problem, not two.

### 4. Mock oral quiz — twenty questions, binary

Run it in the Checkpoint's own format: **binary correct/incorrect, no partial credit, pass at
≥90%.** That means **two wrong out of twenty**, and she should be told the arithmetic before she
starts.

Cover all six families. A workable set:

1. What are the four rules of nūn sākinah and tanwīn? Name the fifth.
2. Which letters cause iẓhār ḥalqī?
3. What does iqlāb turn the nūn into, and how long is the ghunnah?
4. Name the three rules of mīm sākinah.
5. Which letter causes ikhfāʾ shafawī?
6. How do you tell lām shamsiyyah from lām qamariyyah **without** reading it aloud?
7. When is the lām of the name of Allah heavy, and when light?
8. Name the five qalqalah letters.
9. What is the difference between qalqalah ṣughrā and kubrā?
10. When is a rāʾ light?
11. How many counts is madd ṭabīʿī?
12. Is muttaṣil wājib or jāʾiz? Why?
13. How many counts is munfaṣil in ṭarīq ash-Shāṭibiyyah? And in ṭarīq aṭ-Ṭayyibah?
14. How many counts is madd lāzim, and what is the alternative? *(Six. There is none.)*
15. Name the two places ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ occurs.
16. What are the eight six-count letters of the muqaṭṭaʿāt?
17. What does **م** mean above the line? What does **لا** mean?
18. What is the difference between waqf tām and waqf ḥasan?
19. What vowel do you start ٱدْخُلُوا۟ with, and how do you know?
20. What happens to a tāʾ marbūṭah when you stop on it? And to an open tāʾ?

**Give her the raw score immediately.** If it is below 18, the fortnight has a clear job and she
found out from a number.

### 5. Hifz — all eleven, in reverse muṣḥaf order, unaided

See §"Hifz for this session" below. Run it as a full rehearsal, not a spot check.

### 6. Write the flag list, together, in front of her

End the session by writing — on paper, both of you looking at it — a list of every item that
will be flagged at the Checkpoint, with the drill attached to each.

Three columns: **what · which lesson · which drill.** Nothing else. No encouragement, no
summary paragraph. She keeps the sheet, works it for a fortnight, and brings it back on the day.

Then say the outcome structure out loud, because it removes the fear that distorts performance:

> **"There are three outcomes and none of them is a restart. Pass, pass with a homework flag, or
> revise and retest the part that failed. Failing something at the Checkpoint costs you two or
> three sessions on that one thing. It does not cost you this course."**

**Item 2 is the course's single hardest line** and it is worth ending on. Four requirements land
on one word: the lām merges into the ḍād, the ḍād is heavy and long, the madd is a full six, and
the doubled lām after it must still arrive doubled. It has been in front of her since Phase 1
lesson 1-14. Ask for it last, and if it is right, say so.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the blank map.** Six families, every rule under each, from memory, in five minutes.
No notes.

**Drill 2 — name every rule on the line.** I point at a line of unseen text. You name every rule
in it, in order, **before reading it aloud.** Ten lines.

**Drill 3 — the legend, cold.** A coloured muṣḥaf you have not used. Find the legend, read it,
tell me what red is here. Then read the page.

**Drill 4 — twenty questions.** §4, run straight, scored, no discussion until the end.

**Drill 5 — the eleven, backwards.** al-Fātiḥah, then 114 down to 105, unaided. No prompts, no
starting syllables. I mark two axes separately: tajweed, and whether the words are right.

**Drill 6 — the hardest line.** وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ, ten times. All four requirements, every
time.

**Drill 7 — teach it back.** Pick any one of the six families. Explain it to me in three minutes
as though I had never heard of it. You choose the family; I will choose the next one.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** rules that hold up **under load**. Everything in this session is something she
can do in isolation. The capstone asks whether she can do all of it at once, on unseen text, at
pace — which is a different skill.

- **The mistake:** rules degrade as the page goes on — clean in the first two āyāt, sloppy by
  the eighth.
  **Why it happens:** sustained precision is its own competency and it is the one the ijāzah
  exam actually tests. She has practised rules; she has practised endurance much less.
  **Say:** *"You had it for three lines. Read the whole page again and I'll mark only the second
  half."*

- **The mistake:** correct identification, wrong execution — she names ikhfāʾ and produces a
  clear nūn.
  **Why it happens:** knowledge and production are separate, which is exactly why the Checkpoint
  scores them separately.
  **Say:** *"You know it. Now do it. Say the rule and then perform it, twice."*

- **The mistake:** madd lengths wandering under load, even though 3-33 fixed them.
  **Why it happens:** consistency is the first thing to go when attention is divided across six
  families.
  **Say:** *"Set the tempo before you start. Everything else fits inside it."*

- **The mistake:** naming a colour from memory instead of reading the legend.
  **Why it happens:** the palette she uses daily feels like a fact about tajweed rather than a
  fact about one app.
  **Say:** *"Show me where the book says that."*

- **The mistake:** hifz recall failing on the surahs learned earliest, not the newest.
  **Why it happens:** Manzil rotation slipped while the new Sabaq took the attention. It is the
  predictable failure of the last six weeks and it is a scheduling problem, not a memory problem.
  **Say:** *"Which two surahs did you revise yesterday? That's the gap, and it's in the
  timetable, not in your head."*

- **The mistake:** she apologises for errors instead of noting them.
  **Why it happens:** the session feels like a test. It is a diagnostic, and the distinction is
  worth insisting on.
  **Say:** *"Don't apologise, write it down. Every one of these has a drill and a lesson number
  attached to it."*

## Hifz for this session

**No new Sabaq. The set is complete — this session confirms it.**

**The full rehearsal: al-Fātiḥah, then 114 → 105, unaided, in one sitting.**

- **an-Nās · al-Falaq · al-Ikhlāṣ · al-Masad · an-Naṣr · al-Kāfirūn · al-Kawthar · al-Māʿūn ·
  Quraysh · al-Fīl** — plus **al-Fātiḥah** first. **Eleven items.**

**Reverse muṣḥaf order, which is the order she learned them in** ([[Hifz-Method]] §4), and the
order the Final Checkpoint will ask for.

**Unaided means unaided:** no prompt, no starting syllable, no "look at the page for a second".
If she cannot start a surah, note it and move to the next one. That is a data point, not a
failure of the session.

**Score two axes, separately, exactly as [[Hifz-Method]] §6 requires:**

| Axis | What it records |
|---|---|
| **Tajweed** | Jali / Khafi, on the standard table |
| **Memorisation fidelity** | Words **omitted, added, or transposed** |

Keeping them apart is what makes the result actionable. Clean recall with rough recitation and
clean recitation with shaky recall need opposite remedies, and one combined number cannot tell
you which you are looking at. Word omission or addition is itself meaning-changing, so it scores
as Jali **on its own axis**.

**Fail granularity:** more than **3 examiner corrections in one surah** fails **that surah
alone** — not the test, and not the set. Say this to her before she starts, because a student who
believes one bad surah sinks the whole recitation will recite defensively and produce more
errors, not fewer.

**Also check the Manzil schedule, not just the recall.** By now every one of the eleven should be
promoted or close to it. The two-surahs-per-day rotation covers the whole pool in five to six
days ([[Hifz-Method]] §3). If the earliest surahs are the weakest, the rotation slipped — fix the
timetable, not the memorisation.

## Homework

**This is the fortnight before the Final Checkpoint. The homework is the flag list from §6.**

1. **The flag list.** Every item on it, its drill, every day. It is a short list on purpose.
2. **One unseen plain page a day**, read aloud, stops planned first. Mark your own errors in
   pencil on the page. Bring all fourteen pages.
3. **The eleven, daily.** al-Fātiḥah then 114 → 105, unaided, once through. Then the Manzil
   rotation on top.
4. **Twenty questions, weekly.** Get someone to ask you §4's list. Write your score. Twice, a
   week apart. If the second score is not higher than the first, tell me before the Checkpoint,
   not after it.
5. **One coloured page a day, from a palette you did not use the day before.** Read the legend
   first, every time. This is a two-minute habit and it is the whole of end goal 4.
6. Read **وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ** correctly, ten times a day, for fourteen days. It is one line and
   it carries four rules; it is the best value in the entire course.
