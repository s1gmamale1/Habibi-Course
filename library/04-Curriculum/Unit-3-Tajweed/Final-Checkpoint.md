# Final Checkpoint — Four Tests, One per End Goal

**Four tests, and each one exists because one of the course's four end goals exists.** Nothing is
tested here that is not an end goal, and no end goal goes untested. If a test cannot be traced to
a goal, it does not belong in this document.

| Test | End goal it measures |
|---|---|
| **1. Unseen muṣḥaf page** | Read any page of the Qurʾān correctly, unaided |
| **2. Hifz recitation** | Hold al-Fātiḥah and the last ten surahs from memory |
| **3. Oral rule quiz** | Know the rules by name and be able to state them |
| **4. Colour-muṣḥaf reading** | Read and use any coloured muṣḥaf or app |

The scoring model, the error taxonomy, and the reasoning behind the three outcomes are in
[[Assessment]]. This note specifies how they are applied here, test by test.

**Run all four in one sitting where possible**, in the order given, with breaks. Test 1 is the
most demanding and goes first while she is fresh. Test 3 is the least performance-dependent and
can be split off to another day if needed.

## 0a. How long to allow, and whether to split it

**Allow 75–90 minutes, and it is legitimate to run it across two sittings.** This is not
transmitted from anywhere — it is derived from what the four tests actually contain: a full
muṣḥaf page read cold, eleven surahs from memory, twenty quiz items, and a coloured page with
its legend, each marked per cause as §0b requires.

**The tests are independent** (§5), so splitting them costs nothing — Test 2 does not depend on
Test 1 having just happened. If she is tiring by Test 3, stop and finish another day. **A
fatigued Test 4 measures fatigue, not whether she can read a legend.**

Nothing about the result depends on how long she takes. There is no time limit on any test.

## 0. The scoring model, restated

| Error | Deduction |
|---|---|
| **Laḥn Jali** — major, clear: wrong letter, wrong makhraj, wrong lāzim ṣifah, a word that is not Arabic | **−2** |
| **Laḥn Khafi** — minor, hidden: quality degraded, meaning intact | **−1** |
| **Self-correction after prompt** — she fixes it once alerted | **−1** |
| **Examiner has to correct** — she cannot fix it even when alerted | **−2** |
| Non-fluency — hesitant, not flowing | **−0.5** |

The general test: *if a native listener with no tajweed training would notice, it is Jali.*

**The self-correction line is the most informative number on the sheet.** A student who fixes her
own error once prompted has a working internal monitor and needs practice. A student who cannot
fix it even when told something is wrong has not formed the category and needs re-teaching. Those
are different problems with different remedies, which is why they carry different costs.

Marking convention: **Jali in red, Khafi in yellow. Mark all Jali first.**

## 0b. Errors are logged per letter and per rule — this is not optional

A result of **"−11 on the page"** is unactionable and the checkpoint was wasted. A result of

> −2 rāʾ-tafkhīm (×2) · −2 waṣl-hamzah-start · −1 madd-ʿāriḍ-consistency (×4) · −1 ghunnah-length (×3)

says: *her rāʾ rules and her starting vowels are Jali-level and need re-teaching; her stop
lengths and ghunnah durations are Khafi and need a fortnight of drills.* That is a plan, and it
costs one extra column on the mark sheet ([[Assessment]] §2).

**Use a mark sheet with a row per item and a column per cause.** Columns for this checkpoint:

> makhraj/ṣifah (per letter) · iẓhār ḥalqī · idghām (which type) · iqlāb · ikhfāʾ ḥaqīqī ·
> mīm sākinah (which rule) · lām shamsiyyah/qamariyyah · lām jalālah · rāʾ tafkhīm/tarqīq ·
> qalqalah ṣughrā/kubrā · madd 2 · madd muttaṣil · madd munfaṣil · madd ʿāriḍ (length) ·
> madd ʿāriḍ (**consistency**) · madd lāzim · waqf placement · waqf word-changes ·
> hamzat waṣl (join) · hamzat waṣl (start) · silent letters · pace

**Every deduction gets a tick in exactly one column.** The column totals are the result that
matters; the number across the bottom is only a number.

**Carry the sheet forward from Checkpoints 1 and 2.** A cause that cost a deduction at Checkpoint
1, again at Checkpoint 2, and again here is **fossilising**, and it goes to the front of the
queue regardless of the totals. See [[Common-Mistakes]].

## 1. Test 1 — Unseen muṣḥaf page

**End goal: read any page of the Qurʾān correctly, unaided.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Material** | One **full page**, plain Uthmani, **no colour**, no annotation, no transliteration |
| **Unseen** | Genuinely never read. Not al-Fātiḥah, not surahs 105–114, not any page used in Unit 2 or 3. Take a page from the middle of the muṣḥaf |
| **Aid** | None. No prompting, no starting syllable, no pointing |
| **Preparation** | She may **plan her stops** before reading. Planning is a taught skill and is being assessed, not excluded |

**A full page, not a line.** Sustaining precision across extended recitation is itself a
competency, and it is what an ijāzah examination actually tests. A student who is clean for four
lines and degrades over the next twenty has a real, nameable weakness, and a short passage would
hide it.

She should be told before she begins that **she will not know the words**, and that this is
correct and expected.

### Pass condition

| Result | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Pass** | **Zero uncorrected Jali**, and Khafi **≤ 5** |
| **Pass-with-homework-flag** | Zero uncorrected Jali, Khafi **6–10** |
| **Revise-and-retest** | Any uncorrected Jali, **or** Khafi **> 10** |

"Uncorrected" means: she did not fix it, and could not fix it when prompted. **A Jali error she
self-corrected is a −1, not a fail.**

## 2. Test 2 — Hifz recitation

**End goal: hold al-Fātiḥah and the last ten surahs from memory.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Material** | **al-Fātiḥah, then 114 → 105.** Eleven items |
| **Order** | Reverse muṣḥaf order — the order she learned them in ([[Hifz-Method]] §4) |
| **Aid** | **Unaided.** No prompt, no starting syllable, no glance at the page |

### Two axes, scored separately — the design decision that makes this test worth running

| Axis | What it records |
|---|---|
| **Tajweed** | Jali / Khafi, on the §0 table |
| **Memorisation fidelity** | Words **omitted, added, or transposed** |

**These are never combined into one number.** A student with solid memorisation and rough tajweed
needs recitation practice; a student with clean tajweed who cannot recall the text needs more
repetitions. One combined score hides which of the two you are looking at, and the two remedies
are opposite.

Word omission or addition is itself meaning-changing, so **it scores as Jali on its own axis** —
not on the tajweed axis. Do not double-count it.

### Fail granularity

> **More than 3 examiner corrections in one surah fails that surah alone** — not the test, not
> the set.

Tell her this **before** she starts. A student who believes one shaky surah sinks the whole
recitation recites defensively, and defensive recitation produces more errors, not fewer.

A failed surah means: that surah returns to Sabqi, gets the full repetition protocol, and is
re-tested on its own. The other ten stand.

### Waqf inside the hifz recitation is in scope

She chooses her stops. Two specific items to watch, both taught in Unit 3.7:

- **A deliberate stop at 107:4** (فَوَيْلٌ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ) without joining to 107:5 is a **waqf
  qabīḥ** and scores as an error, notwithstanding that it is an āyah end.
- **Restarting from the wrong place** after a breath — ibtidāʾ is stricter than waqf, because
  there is no such thing as a compelled start.

## 3. Test 3 — Oral rule quiz

**End goal: know the rules by name and be able to state them.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Format** | **Binary** — correct or incorrect per rule named and explained. No partial credit |
| **Scope** | **All six families**, plus waqf and hamzat al-waṣl |
| **Why binary** | This is knowledge, not performance. The Jali/Khafi scale does not apply: a rule is either stated correctly or it is not |
| **Pass** | **≥ 90%** |

At twenty questions that is **two wrong**. Tell her the arithmetic before she starts.

**Coverage requirement — every family must appear, and the quiz must include at least one item
from each of these:**

1. Nūn sākinah & tanwīn — the four rules, and their letters
2. Mīm sākinah — the three rules
3. Lām — shamsiyyah/qamariyyah, and lām jalālah heavy vs light
4. Rāʾ — when heavy, when light
5. Qalqalah — the five letters, ṣughrā vs kubrā
6. **Madd** — the five lengths, including **munfaṣil's two ṭuruq** and **lāzim's absence of an
   alternative**
7. Waqf — the six signs, and tām / kāfī / ḥasan / qabīḥ
8. Hamzat al-waṣl — the starting vowel, and how she derives it

**Questions about disagreement are fair and should be included.** "How many counts is munfaṣil?"
has a correct full answer — *four or five by ash-Shāṭibiyyah, two by aṭ-Ṭayyibah, and I read
four* — and a student who can give it knows more than one who says "four". Mark the fuller answer
correct and the bare "four" correct as well; mark "there is only one length" incorrect.

## 4. Test 4 — Colour-muṣḥaf reading

**End goal: read and use any coloured muṣḥaf or app.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Material** | A coloured page — **in a palette she has not been using daily** |
| **Procedure** | She finds and reads the **legend first**, states what red means in *this* book, then reads the page |
| **Two axes** | **Identification** (what is this coloured span?) and **execution** (can she do it?) — scored separately |
| **Pass** | **≥ 90%** of colour-to-rule identifications correct, **and zero Jali on the coloured spans themselves** |

### The legend step is part of the test, not a preliminary

**If she names a colour from memory rather than from the legend, that item is marked incorrect
even if the answer happens to be right.**

The reason is that the palettes genuinely disagree. This course's default is **Family B**,
matching the Quranly app: **red = qalqalah, blue = madd**. The printed **Dar al-Maʿrifah** muṣḥaf
uses **red = madd**. A student who has memorised one palette and picks up the other will bounce
every madd and stretch every qalqalah on the page. **Reading the legend is the competency**;
knowing one palette is not.

Use a palette she has not been reading daily. If the only coloured resource she owns is the one
she uses, source a second for the examination.

### Recommended item: al-Fātiḥah 1:7

[[Assessment]] §4 names **1:7** as the recommended item, and it is a good one — a single line
carrying several families at once:

| In 1:7 | Rule |
|---|---|
| أَنْعَمْتَ | **iẓhār ḥalqī** — nūn sākinah before ʿayn |
| عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ | **iẓhār shafawī** — mīm sākinah before ghayn |
| ٱلضَّآلِّينَ | **lām shamsiyyah** — the lām merges into the ḍād |
| ٱلضَّآلِّينَ | **madd lāzim kalimī muthaqqal** — six counts, no alternative |
| ٱلَّذِينَ · ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ | lām shamsiyyah and lām qamariyyah, side by side |

> ### ⚠ Correction to [[Assessment]] §4, flagged rather than silently applied
>
> [[Assessment]] §4 lists **madd munfaṣil** among the rules 1:7 carries. **It does not.** There
> is no madd munfaṣil anywhere in al-Fātiḥah in the Ḥafṣ reading: munfaṣil requires a word-final
> madd letter followed by a **hamzat qaṭʿ** beginning the next word, and no such sequence occurs
> in the sūrah. In وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ the alif of وَلَا meets a **hamzat waṣl**, not a qaṭʿ, and
> is dropped for the meeting of two sākins.
>
> **1:7 remains the recommended item** — it carries four families in one line, which is what the
> recommendation is for. But **a munfaṣil item must be supplied separately.** Use
> **إِنَّآ أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ** (108:1): it is a clean munfaṣil, it is coloured in every palette, and
> she has held it in memory since lesson 3-28.
>
> This note has not edited [[Assessment]]; the correction is recorded here and in
> [[Verification-Log]] for the owner to apply at the source.

### A minimum item set for Test 4

Whatever page is used, the identification items must between them cover: **ghunnah · ikhfāʾ ·
idghām · iqlāb · lām shamsiyyah · qalqalah · madd 2 · madd 4–5 · madd 6 · a silent letter or waṣl
hamzah**. Ten spans is a workable size; at ten, ≥90% means **at most one wrong**.

## 5. Outcomes — three, and none of them is a restart

| Outcome | Meaning | Next |
|---|---|---|
| **Pass** | All four tests passed. | Course complete. See §7 on what the record may and may not claim |
| **Pass-with-homework-flag** | Passed, with named drills attached to a following block of sessions. The flag is carried **in writing** and cleared explicitly, never quietly forgotten | Complete, with the flag list |
| **Revise-and-retest** | Repeat **only the lessons named by the failed columns**, then re-test **only the failed test** | Typically 2–4 sessions |

**A failed Final Checkpoint never triggers a restart of Unit 3, and never a restart of the
course.** This is a design commitment ([[Assessment]] §5), not a kindness. The column totals
already identify exactly which rules failed; re-teaching material she demonstrably holds burns
sessions and, for an adult learner, is the most reliable way to lose her.

**The tests are independent.** Failing Test 2 on one surah says nothing about Test 1. Re-test
only what failed — and within Test 2, only the surah that failed.

## 7. What this result is and is not evidence of

Test 3, and the identification half of Test 4, are evidence about **knowledge**, and they stand
on their own.

Tests 1, 2 and the execution half of Test 4 are evidence about **production**, and production
evidence is only as good as the ear that scored it. In this course a qualified teacher is
present in the room, so those scores mean something. **In any asynchronous or self-scored version
of the same rubric they do not, and must not be reported as if they did.** See [[Methodology]].

**No result here is an ijāzah**, and any completion record must say so in as many words. An
ijāzah is a transmitted licence with a chain, granted by an authorised teacher after a full
recitation; this is a course checkpoint. The two are not the same thing and the record must not
be capable of being mistaken for one.

Reported to the student, the result should be a sentence and a list, in that order:
*"You can read the Qurʾān, and you hold the eleven. These three things need work."* The numbers
are for the mark sheet.
