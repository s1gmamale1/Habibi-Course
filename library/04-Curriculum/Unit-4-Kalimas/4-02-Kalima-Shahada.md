# Lesson 4.2 — Kalima Shahāda

> **`needs-review` for the same reason as [[4-01-Kalima-Tayyiba]]:** this is not Qurʾān,
> so the Arabic cannot be verified against the pinned corpus and is kept out of
> `examples:`. Check the wording against a printed Hanafi primer before teaching — in
> particular whether your primer prints **أَنْ لَا** or **أَن لَّا**, which changes how
> §2 below looks on the page (but not how it sounds). Log the check in
> [[Verification-Log]].

## Objectives

- Student can recite Kalima Shahāda from memory with correct tajweed, in one or two
  planned breaths.
- Student can name the rules in it, **and can say which ones she already met in 4-01**.
- Student can produce the two contrasting nūn rules in this text: idghām into a lām, and
  iẓhār before a throat letter.
- Student can carry the [[Lam-Jalalah]] rule from 4-01 without being reminded of it.

## Hook

Write the Ṭayyiba on one line and the Shahāda underneath it, and let her see it before you
say anything:

> لَا إِلٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ مُحَمَّدٌ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ
> أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ

> **"This is yesterday's kalima with a frame around it."**

The heart of the Shahāda is the Ṭayyiba. What is added is *"I bear witness that…"* at the
front and *"…His servant and His messenger"* at the back. Everything she fixed in 4-01 —
the four-count *lā*, the heavy lāms, the join over the waṣl hamzah — **transfers
unchanged**, and it should transfer today with no re-teaching.

So this lesson is short on new rules and long on new *joins*. Say it:

> **"You are not learning a new sentence. You are learning what happens at the two ends of
> a sentence you already fixed."**

## The text

**أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ**

*ashhadu an lā ilāha illa-llāhu wa ashhadu anna muḥammadan ʿabduhū wa rasūluh*

> "I bear witness that there is no god except Allah, and I bear witness that Muḥammad is
> His servant and His messenger."

**Shahāda** (شَهَادَة) means *testimony*, *witnessing* — the Word of Testimony. Note what
changed in the second half: in the Ṭayyiba Muḥammad is named *rasūlu-llāh*, "the Messenger
of Allah"; here he is *ʿabduhū wa rasūluh*, "**His servant** and His messenger" — servant
first. That ordering is the point of the sentence, and it is worth one sentence of
explanation before you start correcting sounds.

## Rules occurring in it

### Carried over from 4-01 — do not re-teach, only check

The middle of this kalima is the Ṭayyiba's opening, unchanged:

| Where | Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| لَا إِلٰهَ | [[Madd-Munfasil]] | 4 counts on *lā* |
| إِلٰهَ | [[Madd-Tabii]] | 2 counts on the dagger alif |
| إِلَّا اللَّهُ | [[Hamzat-Wasl]] | the alif of the name is silent — *illa-llāhu* |
| إِلَّا اللَّهُ | [[Lam-Jalalah]] | preceded by fatḥa → **heavy** |

Run these four as a checklist, not a lesson. If any of them has decayed overnight, that is
information about how much repetition 4-01 needs, and you should log it — but fix it in
thirty seconds and move on to what is new.

### 1. أَشْهَدُ — the shīn before the hāʾ

No named rule fires here, and that is worth saying out loud, because a student who has
just been taught to hunt for rules will invent one.

What there *is*: a **shīn sākinah** running straight into a **hāʾ**. Both are whispered
letters ([[Hams]] — the breath keeps flowing through them, no vibration), so the whole
sequence is breathy and easy to smudge. The failure mode is *ash-hadu* collapsing into
*ash-adu* with the hāʾ lost, or a vowel sprouting between the two: *ashi-hadu*.

Two clean letters, no vowel between them, both on breath.

### 2. أَنْ لَا — [[Idgham-Bila-Ghunnah]]

**The first new rule of the lesson, and the important one.**

**أَنْ** ends in a **nūn sākinah**. The next word begins with **ل**. Nūn sākinah followed
by ل or ر → idghām *without* ghunnah: the nūn disappears completely into the lām, no nasal
residue.

*an lā* becomes **al-lā** — a doubled lām, held, and nothing in the nose.

She met this rule yesterday in *muḥammadur rasūl*, with a rāʾ. This is the same rule with
a lām, and it is harder, because the result sounds like a word she knows (*allā*) and she
will second-guess it.

If your primer prints **أَن لَّا** — with the sukūn dropped from the nūn and a shadda
written on the lām — that spelling *is* the rule, made visible. Same sound either way.

### 3. أَنَّ — [[Ghunnah]]

**نّ** — a nūn mushaddadah. **2 counts of ghunnah**, held in the nose.

Pair it in her ear with **مُحَمَّدٌ** from 4-01: mīm mushaddadah, also 2 counts of
ghunnah. The two nasal letters, doubled, behave identically. That is the whole of the
ghunnah rule for doubled letters, and this kalima contains both.

### 4. مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ — [[Izhar-Halqi]]

**The second new rule, and the contrast that makes this lesson worth teaching.**

**مُحَمَّدًا** ends in tanwīn fatḥ. The next word begins with **ع** — a throat letter.
Nūn sākinah or tanwīn before a throat letter (ء ه ع ح غ خ) → **iẓhār**: the nūn is
pronounced **clearly and fully**, with no merging, no hiding, no nasalisation carried into
the next letter.

*muḥammadan ʿabduhū* — the **n** is completely audible.

Now put it beside §2 in the same sentence:

| | Sound | What happens to the nūn |
|---|---|---|
| أَنْ لَا | *al-lā* | **gone** — merged into the lām |
| مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ | *muḥammadan ʿabduhū* | **fully pronounced** |

Same nūn sound, opposite treatments, thirty syllables apart, decided entirely by the
letter that follows. That comparison is the pedagogical payload of 4-02 — do not let the
lesson end without her being able to state it herself.

### 5. عَبْدُهُ — [[Qalqalah-Sughra]]

**بْ** — a bāʾ with sukūn, in the middle of a word. Bāʾ is one of the five qalqalah
letters (ق ط ب ج د). A qalqalah letter with sukūn in the middle of a word gets the
**lesser** qalqalah: a small, clean bounce, no vowel added.

*ʿab-duhū* — you should hear a light echo off the bāʾ, not *ʿabu-duhū*.

Contrast with the qalqalah kubrā she met at a stop in 4-01 §10: same letter family, but
mid-word it is *small*. Small does not mean absent.

### 6. عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ — [[Madd-Silah]]

Optional, and flag it as such — this is a Qurʾānic-recitation convention being applied to
a non-Qurʾānic text, which is exactly what this unit is for, but she should know that is
what is happening.

The **هُ** of *ʿabduhū* is a pronoun-hāʾ (hāʾ al-ḍamīr): it stands between two voweled
letters and is followed by a consonant (wāw), so it takes **ṣilah ṣughrā** — stretched to
**2 counts**, as if a small wāw were written after it: *ʿabduhū wa rasūluh*.

If your primer or your teacher's tradition does not apply ṣilah outside the Qurʾān, say so
and read it short. Either way, say *which* you are doing and be consistent.

### 7. وَرَسُولُهُ — [[Ra-Tafkhim]], [[Madd-Tabii]], and the stop

**رَ** with fatḥa → **heavy**, as in 4-01. ḍamma + wāw → 2 counts.

At the end, you stop: the final hāʾ takes a sukūn and its ḍamma is not pronounced —
*rasūluh*. There is **no madd ʿāriḍ** here, because the letter before the hāʾ is a lām,
not a madd letter. Compare with the end of the Ṭayyiba, where there *was* one. Same-looking
ending, different rule. She should be able to say why.

**In plain terms:** nothing here is new. Every rule in this sentence you already met in Unit 3; the only new thing is saying them all correctly without stopping to think.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the transfer check.** Recite only the middle: *lā ilāha illa-llāh*. Four
counts, heavy lām, no glottal stop. If it is still right from yesterday, we spend today on
new material. If it is not, we spend today on yesterday. Your recitation decides.

**Drill 2 — the nūn pair.** Say these two, back to back, ten times:
> *an lā* … *muḥammadan ʿabduhū*

Then tell me, in English, why the n vanishes in the first and stays in the second. Say the
letter names. Not "because it sounds better."

**Drill 3 — no nose.** *al-lā*, holding the lām, with your fingers pinching your nose.
Nothing should change. If you hear a hum, the nūn is still in there.

**Drill 4 — the two doubled nasals.** *anna* … *muḥammad* — both doubled, both two counts
in the nose, both with the pinch test giving an obvious change. Alternate them.

**Drill 5 — the bounce.** *ʿab-duhū*, twenty times, listening for a bounce off the bāʾ
with **no vowel after it**. Then the same word inside the phrase, where it is much harder.

**Drill 6 — the breath plan.** This kalima is long enough that you must decide where you
breathe **before** you start. Two options: one breath for the whole thing, or a stop after
*illa-llāh*. Pick one, mark it with a pencil, and use the same one every time. Do not
decide mid-sentence — that is how a stop lands in the middle of *muḥammadan ʿabduhū* and
takes the iẓhār with it. See [[Waqf-Types]].

**Drill 7 — the recitation pass.** Whole kalima, your breath plan, half speed, then full
speed. Name the one rule that broke at full speed.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** a clean *ash-hadu* at both ends, *allā* with no hum, a fully audible **n**
before *ʿabduhū*, and the middle of the sentence sounding exactly as it did in 4-01.

- **The mistake:** *ashadu* — the hāʾ swallowed, or *ashi-hadu* with a vowel inserted.
  **Why it happens:** shīn sākinah into hāʾ is two whispered letters in a row with no
  voicing to hold onto; the mouth either drops one or props them apart with a vowel.
  **Say:** *"Two letters, no vowel between them. sh — then h. Breathe both, don't sing
  them."*

- **The mistake:** *an lā*, with the nūn fully pronounced.
  **Why it happens:** the nūn is written, and — more powerfully — this is how it is
  recited around her by people who never learned the rule. As in 4-01, she is copying a
  room.
  **Say:** *"The n goes into the lām and dies there. al-lā. Hold the l, and nothing in the
  nose."*

- **The mistake:** *muḥammadaʿ ʿabduhū* — the tanwīn's nūn dropped, or nasalised into the
  ʿayn.
  **Why it happens:** she has just spent ten minutes deleting a nūn in *an lā*, and the
  rule generalises in her head. Overcorrection, not ignorance — which means repeating the
  iẓhār rule at her will not fix it. The **contrast drill** will.
  **Say:** *"That one you keep. ʿayn is a throat letter — the n stays, out loud, before
  it."*

- **The mistake:** *ʿabu-duhū* — a small vowel after the bāʾ.
  **Why it happens:** qalqalah is taught as "bounce", and a bounce with the voice still on
  produces a vowel. The bounce has to be a release, not a note.
  **Say:** *"Cut the sound. The bounce is the letter leaving, not a new letter arriving."*

- **The mistake:** a breath taken in the middle of *muḥammadan ʿabduhū*.
  **Why it happens:** no breath plan, and this is the point in the sentence where the air
  runs out.
  **Say:** *"Breathe after llāh, or don't breathe at all. Never in the last four words."*

## Homework

1. The whole kalima, five times after each ṣalāh, on your chosen breath plan. Same plan
   every time.
2. The nūn pair from Drill 2 — *an lā* / *muḥammadan ʿabduhū* — twenty times a day, out
   loud, alternating. This is the drill for this lesson.
3. Add today's rule-stops to the card you started in 4-01. Mark the four that are carried
   over from the Ṭayyiba with a tick — you should end the unit with most of the card
   ticked.
4. Recite the Ṭayyiba and the Shahāda back to back once a day and notice that the middle
   is the same words. If they come out differently, tell me which one is right.
