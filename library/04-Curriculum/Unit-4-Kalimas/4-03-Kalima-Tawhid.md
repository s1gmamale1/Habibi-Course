# Lesson 4.3 — Kalima Tawḥīd

> **`needs-review`.** Not Qurʾān, therefore not verifiable against the pinned corpus and
> deliberately absent from `examples:` — see [[4-01-Kalima-Tayyiba]] for the full reason.
> This kalima in particular varies between primers: some print **يُحْيِي وَيُمِيتُ**
> before *wa huwa ḥayyun*, some after; some omit **أَبَدًا أَبَدًا**'s repetition. **Check
> your primer and match it**, then log it in [[Verification-Log]]. Where the wording
> differs, the rule analysis below still applies phrase by phrase.

## Objectives

- Student can recite Kalima Tawḥīd from memory with correct tajweed on a planned breath
  scheme.
- Student can name the rules in it, and can find the **two lām rules** — sun and moon —
  without being told which is which.
- Student can distinguish [[Ra-Tafkhim]] from [[Ra-Tarqiq]] in *sharīka* and *qadīr*.
- Student can hold a rule correctly across a long sentence, not only in isolation.

## Hook

Two kalimas in, she is now expecting a short sentence. Show her this one and let the
length land.

> **"The first two you could hold in one breath. This one you cannot. So today the rules
> are not the hard part — keeping them alive to the end of the sentence is."**

That is the honest difficulty of 4-03. Every individual rule in it is one she can already
do. What is new is **duration**: four rules deep into a long sentence, accuracy starts
leaking, and the leak is always in the same places — madd length and heaviness. Name that
before it happens, so that when it happens she recognises it rather than feeling caught
out.

## The text

**لَا إِلٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ، لَهُ الْمُلْكُ وَلَهُ الْحَمْدُ يُحْيِي وَيُمِيتُ، وَهُوَ حَيٌّ لَا يَمُوتُ أَبَدًا أَبَدًا، ذُو الْجَلَالِ وَالْإِكْرَامِ، بِيَدِهِ الْخَيْرُ، وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ**

*lā ilāha illa-llāhu waḥdahū lā sharīka lah, lahu-l-mulku wa lahu-l-ḥamdu yuḥyī wa yumīt,
wa huwa ḥayyul lā yamūtu abadan abadā, dhu-l-jalāli wa-l-ikrām, bi yadihi-l-khayr, wa huwa
ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr*

> "There is no god except Allah, alone, with no partner. His is the dominion and His is
> the praise. He gives life and gives death. He is Living and does not die, ever, ever.
> Owner of majesty and honour. In His hand is all good. And He has power over
> everything."

**Tawḥīd** (تَوْحِيد) means *making one*, *declaring oneness* — the Word of Oneness. It
opens with the Ṭayyiba and then keeps going, which by now she will expect.

## Rules occurring in it

### The opening — checked, not taught

**لَا إِلٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ** is the Ṭayyiba's opening for the third lesson running.
[[Madd-Munfasil]] at 4, [[Madd-Tabii]] at 2, silent [[Hamzat-Wasl]], [[Lam-Jalalah]]
**heavy** after the fatḥa. Check in ten seconds. If it is right three days in a row, it is
hers.

### 1. وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ — [[Ra-Tarqiq]]

**وَحْدَهُ** — the **حْ** is a sākin ḥāʾ. It is *not* a qalqalah letter, and the common
error is to bounce it as if it were: *waḥa-dahū*. Ḥāʾ is a whispered letter ([[Hams]]);
it is cut off on breath, not on a bounce.

**شَرِيكَ** — and here is the first genuinely new rule of the unit: **the rāʾ carries a
kasra**, and a rāʾ with kasra is **light** (muraqqaq). Thin, forward, nothing filling the
back of the mouth: *sha-rī-ka*.

Every rāʾ she has met in Unit 4 so far — *rasūl*, *wa rasūluh* — was heavy. This is the
first light one. Put them side by side out loud: *rasūl* (heavy) … *sharīka* (light). See
[[Ra-Tafkhim]] and [[Ra-Tarqiq]] for the full conditions; the working rule for this unit
is **fatḥa or ḍamma → heavy, kasra → light.**

Then ياء + كَ: ḍamma-less, a plain natural madd on the yāʾ, 2 counts.

### 2. لَهُ الْمُلْكُ وَلَهُ الْحَمْدُ — [[Lam-Qamariyyah]] ×2, and [[Izhar-Shafawi]]

Three rules in six words.

**الْمُلْكُ** — the definite article's lām before a **mīm**. Mīm is a *moon* letter, so
the lām is **pronounced with a clear sukūn**: *al-mulk*, the l audible. That is
[[Lam-Qamariyyah]].

**الْحَمْدُ** — same again, before **ḥāʾ**, also a moon letter: *al-ḥamd*.

And inside *al-ḥamdu*: **مْ** sākinah followed by **د**. A mīm sākinah before any letter
other than bāʾ or mīm is pronounced **clearly**, with the lips fully closed and no
nasalisation running on — [[Izhar-Shafawi]]. *al-ḥam-du*, lips shut, then the dāl.

This is the rule students most often lose in fast recitation, because the lips get lazy
and the mīm turns into a hum. Watch her lips, not her sound.

### 3. يُحْيِي وَيُمِيتُ — two natural madds and one sākin ḥāʾ

**يُحْيِي** — ḥāʾ sākinah again (no bounce, breath only), then a natural madd on the final
yāʾ: 2 counts. **وَيُمِيتُ** — natural madd on the yāʾ: 2 counts.

Nothing exotic. Its function in this lesson is **rhythm maintenance**: this is the point
where the sentence has gone on long enough that madds start shrinking. Two 2-counts here,
both full. If they are not, that is the leak you warned her about in the hook.

### 4. حَيٌّ لَا — [[Idgham-Bila-Ghunnah]], and a shadda that is *not* a ghunnah

**حَيٌّ** ends in tanwīn ḍamm; the next word begins with **ل** → idghām **without**
ghunnah, third occurrence in the unit: *ḥayyul lā*, the nūn gone into the lām, nothing in
the nose.

But look at the word itself: **يّ**, a doubled yāʾ. She has now met doubled mīm
(*muḥammad*) and doubled nūn (*anna*), both of which carry **2 counts of [[Ghunnah]]**.
This one does **not**. Ghunnah on a doubled letter belongs to **mīm and nūn only** — the
two nasal letters. A doubled yāʾ is simply held, in the mouth, with no nose at all.

If she hums the yāʾ, she has over-generalised the ghunnah rule, and the fix is the pinch
test.

### 5. أَبَدًا أَبَدًا — [[Izhar-Halqi]] and [[Madd-Iwad]]

**أَبَدًا أَبَدًا** — tanwīn fatḥ followed by a **hamzah**, a throat letter → **iẓhār**,
the nūn fully pronounced: *abadan abadā*. Same rule as *muḥammadan ʿabduhū* in 4-02, and
she should name it before you do.

Then the second **أَبَدًا** is followed by a pause. **A tanwīn fatḥ, when you stop on it,
becomes a plain alif of 2 counts** — that is [[Madd-Iwad]], the "madd of compensation".
*abadā*, not *abadan*.

So the same two words, printed identically, are pronounced differently: **the first keeps
its n because you keep going; the second loses it because you stop.** That is the neatest
illustration of waqf changing pronunciation anywhere in this unit — see
[[Waqf-Word-Changes]].

### 6. ذُو الْجَلَالِ وَالْإِكْرَامِ — [[Lam-Qamariyyah]] ×2, [[Ra-Tafkhim]]

**ذُو** — natural madd on the wāw, then the article's alif is a waṣl hamzah and is
swallowed: *dhu-l-jalāl*, no glottal stop.

**الْجَلَالِ** — jīm is a moon letter → clear lām. **وَالْإِكْرَامِ** — hamzah is a moon
letter → clear lām.

**وَالْإِكْرَامِ** also has **كْ** sākinah — kāf is *not* a qalqalah letter, so no bounce
— and then **رَا**, a rāʾ with fatḥa → **heavy**, with a natural madd. Put it against
*sharīka* from §1: two rāʾs in one kalima, one light, one heavy, and she should be able to
say why for each.

### 7. بِيَدِهِ الْخَيْرُ — [[Lam-Qamariyyah]], [[Leen]], [[Ra-Tafkhim]]

**بِيَدِهِ** — the pronoun-hāʾ here is followed by a waṣl hamzah, so **no ṣilah**: it stays
short. (Contrast *ʿabduhū wa* in 4-02, where the next letter was a consonant and the ṣilah
applied. The condition is the *next* letter, every time.)

**الْخَيْرُ** — khāʾ is a moon letter → clear lām. **خَيْ** is a **līn** letter: a yāʾ
sākinah after a fatḥa, pronounced flat and soft, **not** stretched while you are joining
([[Leen]]). And **رُ** with ḍamma → **heavy**.

### 8. عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ — the closing four

[[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]], [[Madd-Leen]], [[Ra-Tarqiq]] and [[Madd-Arid-Lissukun]], in six words.

**عَلَىٰ** — dagger alif, natural madd, 2 counts.

**شَيْءٍ** — another **līn** (yāʾ sākinah after fatḥa). While joining it stays short; **if
you stop on it**, it lengthens to 2, 4 or 6 counts — [[Madd-Leen]].

**شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ** — tanwīn kasr followed by **ق**. Qāf is one of the fifteen ikhfāʾ
letters, so the nūn sound is neither pronounced clearly nor merged: it is **hidden**, with
**2 counts of ghunnah**, the tongue already moving towards the qāf: *shayʾin qadīr*.
That is [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]] — the first ikhfāʾ in the unit.

**قَدِيرٌ** — the qāf is an elevated letter and is **heavy** ([[Tafkhim]]). And the final
rāʾ: at a stop it becomes sākin, and a sākin rāʾ **preceded by a kasra is light**
([[Ra-Tarqiq]]). *qadīr* ends thin. This is the one almost everybody gets wrong, because
the heavy qāf at the front of the word pulls the whole word backwards.

And because the yāʾ before it is a madd letter and the rāʾ is made sākin by stopping, the
last syllable is a **madd ʿāriḍ lis-sukūn**: 2, 4 or 6 counts — *qadīīīr*, ending light.

**Heavy qāf, long madd, light rāʾ, all in one word.** That is the assessment item for this
lesson.

**In plain terms:** this one is mostly lāms. Watch whether the lām after *al-* is spoken or swallowed, and let the rest follow the habits you already have.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the rule-spotting pass.** Silent read, finger on each rule, name it out loud.
There are around fifteen stops. Do not recite yet. When you cannot name one, mark it — the
marks are the lesson plan.

**Drill 2 — the two rāʾs.** *sharīka* … *wa-l-ikrām* … *qadīr*. Light, heavy, light. Say
them as a set, ten times, and then tell me the vowel that decided each one.

**Drill 3 — the sun and the moon.** Read only these: *al-mulk, al-ḥamd, al-jalāl,
al-ikrām, al-khayr*. Every single lām in this kalima is a **moon** lām — clear, audible.
There is not one sun lām in the whole sentence. Say each with the l deliberately loud, then
at normal volume.

**Drill 4 — the doubled letters.** *muḥam-mad* (hum), *an-na* (hum), *ḥay-yun* (no hum).
Pinch your nose for all three. The third one should not change.

**Drill 5 — abadan / abadā.** Say it as a pair: *abadan abadā*. n, then no n. Twenty times.
Then explain to me why, in English.

**Drill 6 — the last word.** *qadīr*, on its own, fifty times if necessary. Heavy q. Long
ī. **Thin r.** When you can do it alone, do the phrase *ʿalā kulli shayʾin qadīr*, where
the ikhfāʾ before it makes it much harder.

**Drill 7 — the breath plan.** Mark your stops before you recite. The natural scheme is
four breaths: after *lā sharīka lah* · after *yuḥyī wa yumīt* · after *abadan abadā* ·
after *wa-l-ikrām* · then to the end. Use commas already in the text — do not invent stops
mid-phrase. See [[Waqf-Types]].

**Drill 8 — the recitation pass.** Whole kalima, your breath plan, half speed, then full.
Then tell me which rule collapsed last — because in a sentence this long, one always does,
and knowing which one is your own diagnostic skill developing.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** every lām of the article audible, madds still full length in the second
half, a light rāʾ in *sharīka*, and a thin *qadīr* at the very end.

- **The mistake:** the madds shrink after the halfway point — *yuḥyī wa yumīt* clipped,
  *dhū* short.
  **Why it happens:** length is the first thing to go when attention is divided, and by the
  middle of this sentence she is managing rule, meaning, memory and breath at once. This is
  the same failure documented at phrase level in [[2-11-Phrases]], now at sentence length.
  **Say:** *"Halve the speed of the whole thing. Not just the madd — everything."*

- **The mistake:** *qadīr* said with a heavy, rolled final r.
  **Why it happens:** the qāf is heavy and the mouth does not reset in one syllable; and
  most recitation she has heard around her rolls it. Two causes, both physical.
  **Say:** *"Heavy at the front, thin at the end. Smile on the r."* The smile actually
  works — it forces the tongue forward.

- **The mistake:** a hum on *ḥayyun*.
  **Why it happens:** over-generalising the doubled-letter ghunnah from *muḥammad* and
  *anna*. This is a *good* mistake — it means the ghunnah rule has been learned. It just
  needs a boundary.
  **Say:** *"Only m and n go in the nose. Every other shadda is held in the mouth."*

- **The mistake:** *abadan abadan* — the n kept at the stop.
  **Why it happens:** the tanwīn is written and she reads what is written. The waqf rule
  that overrides it is one of very few places where the page is *not* the final authority,
  and that takes time to accept.
  **Say:** *"When you stop, the two lines become an alif. abadā. Long, and no n."*

- **The mistake:** the mīm of *al-ḥamdu* left half-open, humming into the dāl.
  **Why it happens:** speed. The lips are the slowest articulator in the mouth and the
  first to be skipped.
  **Say:** *"Close your lips completely. I want to see them meet."*

- **The mistake:** a glottal stop before every ال — *lahu ʾal-mulku*.
  **Why it happens:** same waṣl-hamzah error as 4-01, which returns whenever a new text
  puts it in an unfamiliar place.
  **Say:** *"lahul-mulk, one word. Step over the alif."*

## Homework

1. The whole kalima, three times after each ṣalāh, on the marked breath plan. Three good
   ones beat ten rushed ones.
2. *qadīr* alone, thirty times a day, thin r. This is the drill for this lesson.
3. The rāʾ set from Drill 2, daily, out loud.
4. Add today's stops to your rule card. Circle every one that is a **repeat** from 4-01 or
   4-02 — you should be able to see the unit accumulating.
5. Record yourself once and listen for the second half only. Mark every madd that came out
   shorter than it should.
