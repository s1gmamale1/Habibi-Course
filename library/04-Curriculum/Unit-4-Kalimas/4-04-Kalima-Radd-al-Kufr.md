# Lesson 4.4 — Kalima Radd al-Kufr

> **`needs-review`.** Not Qurʾān, so not corpus-verifiable and not in `examples:` — see
> [[4-01-Kalima-Tayyiba]]. This kalima has the **widest wording variation of the eight**:
> the list of things renounced differs from primer to primer in both length and order, and
> some primers end at *wa aslamt* without repeating the Ṭayyiba. **Match your student's
> printed primer exactly** and record which edition you used in [[Verification-Log]]. The
> rule analysis is given phrase by phrase so it survives a different list.

## Objectives

- Student can recite Kalima Radd al-Kufr from memory with correct tajweed on a planned
  breath scheme.
- Student can sort every **ال** in the renunciation list into sun or moon, unaided.
- Student can produce [[Izhar-Halqi]] three times in the opening without one of them
  turning into ikhfāʾ.
- Student can name a rule she gets **wrong** and say why — the list is long enough that
  self-diagnosis is now the real skill.

## Hook

This is the long one, and pretending otherwise wastes her goodwill. Show it and say:

> **"This kalima is mostly a list. Lists are where tajweed goes to die — you speed up, and
> every rule in the list goes at once. So today we are not going to recite it. We are going
> to *count* it."**

Then the actual hook, which is the reward:

> **"There are ten definite articles in this list. Every single one is a rule. You already
> know the rule. By the end of today you will hear ten rules in a sentence you have always
> heard as one long breath."**

That is a satisfying thing to discover, and it is true.

## The text

**اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ أَنْ أُشْرِكَ بِكَ شَيْئًا وَأَنَا أَعْلَمُ بِهِ، وَأَسْتَغْفِرُكَ لِمَا لَا أَعْلَمُ بِهِ، تُبْتُ عَنْهُ وَتَبَرَّأْتُ مِنَ الْكُفْرِ وَالشِّرْكِ وَالْكِذْبِ وَالْغِيبَةِ وَالْبِدْعَةِ وَالنَّمِيمَةِ وَالْفَوَاحِشِ وَالْبُهْتَانِ وَالْمَعَاصِي كُلِّهَا، وَأَسْلَمْتُ وَأَقُولُ لَا إِلٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ مُحَمَّدٌ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ**

*allāhumma innī aʿūdhu bika min an ushrika bika shayʾaw wa anā aʿlamu bih, wa astaghfiruka
limā lā aʿlamu bih, tubtu ʿanhu wa tabarraʾtu mina-l-kufri wa-sh-shirki wa-l-kidhbi
wa-l-ghībati wa-l-bidʿati wa-n-namīmati wa-l-fawāḥishi wa-l-buhtāni wa-l-maʿāṣī kullihā,
wa aslamtu wa aqūlu lā ilāha illa-llāhu muḥammadur rasūlu-llāh*

> "O Allah, I seek refuge in You from knowingly associating anything with You, and I ask
> Your forgiveness for what I do not know of it. I repent of it and I renounce disbelief,
> association, lying, backbiting, innovation, slander, indecency, false accusation and all
> acts of disobedience. I submit, and I say: there is no god except Allah; Muḥammad is the
> Messenger of Allah."

**Radd al-Kufr** (رَدُّ الْكُفْر) means *the rejection of disbelief*. It ends by returning
to the Ṭayyiba, which is the fourth time she has recited that sentence in this unit — and
it should now be automatic.

## Rules occurring in it

### 1. اللَّهُمَّ — [[Lam-Jalalah]] at the start, and [[Ghunnah]]

A new case of the lām rule, and it needs stating explicitly because the usual test — *look
at the vowel before it* — has nothing to look at.

**At the beginning of a phrase, with nothing before it, the lām of the name of Allah is
heavy.** *ALLĀHumma*, full and thick. (The default is heaviness; only a preceding kasra
makes it light, and there is no preceding anything here.)

Then **مّ** — a mīm mushaddadah → **2 counts of [[Ghunnah]]**, in the nose. She has done
this in *muḥammad* since 4-01.

### 2. إِنِّي — [[Ghunnah]] again, immediately

**نّ**, nūn mushaddadah, 2 counts of ghunnah, then a natural madd on the yāʾ.

Two doubled nasals in the first two words. Say them as a pair: *allāhum-ma in-nī*. If she
can hold both, the ghunnah rule is secure and you can stop checking it for the rest of the
unit.

### 3. أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ أَنْ أُشْرِكَ — [[Izhar-Halqi]] twice

**مِنْ أَنْ** — nūn sākinah followed by a **hamzah**, a throat letter → iẓhār, the nūn
fully and clearly pronounced.

**أَنْ أُشْرِكَ** — nūn sākinah followed by a **hamzah** again → iẓhār again.

Two in a row, three words apart. This is the densest iẓhār in the unit and it is the place
where a student who has over-learned ikhfāʾ will start hiding nūns that should be plain.
Both n's are **out loud**.

Then **أُشْرِكَ**: **شْ** sākinah (whispered, [[Hams]], no bounce), and **رِ** with a
kasra → **light** ([[Ra-Tarqiq]]).

### 4. شَيْئًا وَأَنَا — [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]]

**The new rule of this lesson.**

**شَيْئًا** ends in tanwīn fatḥ; the next word begins with **و**. Nūn sākinah or tanwīn
followed by one of **ي ن م و** → **idghām with ghunnah**: the nūn merges into the
following letter **and carries 2 counts of nasal sound with it**.

*shayʾaw wa anā* — a doubled wāw, hummed.

Now set it against what she already has, because this is the pair that decides whether she
understands idghām at all:

| | Following letter | Result |
|---|---|---|
| مُحَمَّدٌ رَسُولُ (4-01) | ر | merged, **no** ghunnah |
| أَنْ لَا (4-02) | ل | merged, **no** ghunnah |
| شَيْئًا وَأَنَا (4-04) | و | merged, **with** 2 counts of ghunnah |

Same merging, opposite nose. See [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]] and [[Idgham-Bila-Ghunnah]];
ر and ل are the only two that drop the ghunnah, and that is the whole distinction.

### 5. بِهِ — [[Madd-Silah]], flagged as optional

The pronoun-hāʾ of **بِهِ** sits between two voweled letters and is followed by a
consonant → **ṣilah ṣughrā**, 2 counts, *bihī wa…*. Same convention flagged in 4-02 §6:
this is a Qurʾānic reading rule applied to a non-Qurʾānic text. Apply it or do not, but
say which, and be consistent across all eight lessons.

### 6. وَأَسْتَغْفِرُكَ — [[Tafkhim]] and [[Ra-Tafkhim]]

**غْ** — a sākin ghayn. Ghayn is one of the seven **elevated** letters (خ ص ض ط ظ غ ق),
so it is **heavy**: the back of the tongue rises and the sound fills the mouth
([[Tafkhim]], and [[Istila]] for the underlying characteristic). Heavy *and* sākin is the
hardest combination to hold — there is no vowel to carry the weight.

**رُ** with a ḍamma → **heavy** ([[Ra-Tafkhim]]).

### 7. تُبْتُ عَنْهُ — [[Qalqalah-Sughra]] and [[Izhar-Halqi]]

**تُبْتُ** — **بْ** sākinah mid-word, and bāʾ is a qalqalah letter → the **lesser**
qalqalah, a small clean bounce, **no vowel after it**: *tub-tu*.

**عَنْهُ** — nūn sākinah followed by **ه**, a throat letter → **iẓhār**, third time in this
kalima. *ʿan-hu*, the n fully audible.

Hāʾ is the softest of the throat letters and the easiest one before which a student
smudges the nūn into a hum. Watch for it specifically.

### 8. وَتَبَرَّأْتُ — [[Ra-Tafkhim]] on a doubled rāʾ

**رّ** with a fatḥa → heavy, **and doubled**, so the weight is held for the full length of
two rāʾs. Then **أْ**, a hamzah with sukūn — a clean glottal stop, cut, not softened into
a vowel: *ta-bar-raʾ-tu*.

### 9. The renunciation list — [[Lam-Shamsiyyah]] vs [[Lam-Qamariyyah]]

**This is the pedagogical payload of the lesson.** Nine definite articles in a row, and
each one is decided by a single letter.

The rule: the **ل** of ال is silent and merged into the next letter if that letter is a
**sun** letter (ت ث د ذ ر ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ل ن); it is pronounced clearly if the next letter
is a **moon** letter.

| Phrase | Next letter | Sun or moon | Read as |
|---|---|---|---|
| الْكُفْرِ | ك | moon | *al-kufri* — l audible |
| وَالشِّرْكِ | ش | **sun** | *wa-sh-shirki* — no l |
| وَالْكِذْبِ | ك | moon | *wa-l-kidhbi* |
| وَالْغِيبَةِ | غ | moon | *wa-l-ghībati* |
| وَالْبِدْعَةِ | ب | moon | *wa-l-bidʿati* |
| وَالنَّمِيمَةِ | ن | **sun** | *wa-n-namīmati* — no l, **plus ghunnah** |
| وَالْفَوَاحِشِ | ف | moon | *wa-l-fawāḥishi* |
| وَالْبُهْتَانِ | ب | moon | *wa-l-buhtāni* |
| وَالْمَعَاصِي | م | moon | *wa-l-maʿāṣī* |

Seven moon, two sun. The two sun lāms are printed with a **shadda on the following
letter** — she does not need to remember the fourteen sun letters to read this correctly;
she needs to **look for the shadda**. Teach it that way round. The letter list is for
explaining, the shadda is for reading.

**وَالنَّمِيمَةِ carries two rules at once**: the sun lām merges into the nūn, *and* the
resulting nūn is doubled, so it takes **2 counts of [[Ghunnah]]**. *wan-namīmati*. It is
the single hardest word in the list and the one worth drilling alone.

Three more things buried in that list:

- **الْكُفْرِ** — the rāʾ is light while joining (it carries a kasra), but **if you stop on
  it**, the rāʾ goes sākin and you look at the vowel two letters back — the **ḍamma** of
  *ku* — which makes it **heavy**. Joined: *al-kufri*, light. Stopped: *al-kufr*, heavy.
  Do not stop there in this recitation, and now she knows why it matters.
- **وَالشِّرْكِ** — **رْ** sākinah preceded by a **kasra** → **light** ([[Ra-Tarqiq]]).
  Right next to the heavy one. This is the rāʾ contrast of the lesson.
- **وَالْبِدْعَةِ** — **دْ** sākinah mid-word → [[Qalqalah-Sughra]], second bounce of the
  lesson, and much easier to miss than *tubtu* because it is buried in a list.

### 10. وَأَسْلَمْتُ وَأَقُولُ — [[Izhar-Shafawi]]

**مْ** sākinah followed by **ت** → the mīm is pronounced clearly with the lips fully
closed, no hum running on: *wa aslam-tu*.

**وَأَقُولُ** — qāf is elevated → heavy; natural madd on the wāw.

### 11. The closing Ṭayyiba

**لَا إِلٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ مُحَمَّدٌ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ** — everything from 4-01, unchanged:
[[Madd-Munfasil]] at 4, heavy [[Lam-Jalalah]] twice, [[Idgham-Bila-Ghunnah]] at
*muḥammadur rasūl*, [[Madd-Arid-Lissukun]] on the final *-llāh*.

By this point in a long recitation it will be the **weakest** part of her performance, not
the strongest, even though it is the part she knows best. Expect that, and check it *at the
end of the sentence*, not in isolation — the isolated version has been correct since 4-01.

**In plain terms:** this is the longest kalima and it is built on humming. Every نّ and مّ with a shadda gets two beats through the nose — get that and most of the sentence is right.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the list, alone.** Do not recite the kalima. Recite **only the nine phrases**
of the renunciation list, slowly, and after each one say "sun" or "moon". Twice through.
This is the core work of the lesson.

**Drill 2 — hunt the shadda.** Read the list again with a pencil and mark every shadda. Two
marks. Those are your two sun lāms, and that is how you will read every unfamiliar ال for
the rest of your life — you look, you do not remember.

**Drill 3 — the hardest word.** *wan-namīmati*, twenty times. No l. Two counts of hum on
the n. Pinch your nose: it must change.

**Drill 4 — three clear n's.** *min an … an ushrika … ʿanhu.* All three fully out loud.
Then tell me the letter that follows each, and why that letter forces iẓhār.

**Drill 5 — the idghām table.** Say *muḥammadur rasūl* (no hum), *an lā* (no hum),
*shayʾaw wa anā* (hum). Pinch test on each. Then say which two letters are the exceptions.

**Drill 6 — two bounces.** *tub-tu* … *wa-l-bid-ʿati*. Find the qalqalah in each. Bounce,
no vowel.

**Drill 7 — the breath plan.** Mark it before reciting. The natural scheme is four
breaths: after *aʿlamu bih* · after the second *aʿlamu bih* · after *wa-l-maʿāṣī kullihā*
· after *wa aslamtu* · then to the end. **Never breathe inside the list** — if you cannot
reach the end of the list, the list is too fast, not too long. See [[Waqf-Types]].

**Drill 8 — the recitation pass.** Whole kalima, marked breath plan, half speed. Then full
speed. Then tell me — before I do — which rule you lost. In a sentence this long, naming
your own error is the skill being trained.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** the l audible in seven articles and gone in two, three fully spoken n's in
the opening, and a Ṭayyiba at the end that is as good as it was in isolation.

- **The mistake:** the list accelerates and every article turns into an undifferentiated
  *wal-, wal-, wal-*.
  **Why it happens:** lists have their own rhythm and the rhythm wins. She is reciting a
  shape she has heard, not reading nine separate words. This is the defining failure of
  this kalima.
  **Say:** *"Slower than feels right. Every wal- is a decision. Nine decisions."*

- **The mistake:** *wa-l-namīmati* — the l pronounced before the nūn.
  **Why it happens:** nūn is a sun letter but it does not *feel* like one; the merged form
  *wan-na* sounds to her like a mistake, so she corrects back to the wrong version.
  **Say:** *"There is a shadda on the n. The l has already gone into it. wan-namīmah — and
  hold the n."*

- **The mistake:** *mina-l-kufr* pronounced with a heavy final rāʾ while joining.
  **Why it happens:** she is anticipating the stopped form, which is heavy, and the ḍamma
  of *ku* pulls the mouth back.
  **Say:** *"You are not stopping. It has a kasra — thin it. al-kufri."*

- **The mistake:** the third n, in *ʿanhu*, hummed instead of spoken.
  **Why it happens:** hāʾ is a soft throat letter with almost no closure, so there is
  nothing to make the nūn snap off cleanly. Also, by this point in the sentence she is
  tiring.
  **Say:** *"ʿan — stop the n with your tongue — hu. Two pieces."*

- **The mistake:** no ghunnah on *shayʾaw wa anā*, merged flat.
  **Why it happens:** three iẓhārs and two ghunnah-less idghāms have preceded it; the
  default has been set to "no nose."
  **Say:** *"Wāw is one of the four. This one hums. Two counts."*

- **The mistake:** the closing Ṭayyiba rushed and flattened — *lā* back to two counts, the
  final *-llāh* clipped.
  **Why it happens:** it is the end, she is out of air, and she has said it correctly
  hundreds of times already this week — which makes it feel finished rather than pending.
  **Say:** *"Breathe before the last sentence. It is the sentence, not the ending."*

## Homework

1. The renunciation list alone — Drill 1 — twice a day. Not the whole kalima. **The list.**
2. *wan-namīmati*, twenty times a day.
3. The whole kalima, once a day, on the marked breath plan, at half speed.
4. Add today's stops to your rule card. This lesson adds more than any other; expect two
   sides.
5. Write the nine articles out and next to each write "sun" or "moon" **from memory**, then
   check against the text. Bring the sheet with your errors uncorrected — the errors are
   what I want to see.
