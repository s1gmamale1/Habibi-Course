# Lesson 4.6 — Kalima Tamjīd

> **`needs-review`.** Not Qurʾān; not corpus-verifiable; Arabic kept out of `examples:` —
> see [[4-01-Kalima-Tayyiba]]. Check the wording against your student's printed primer and
> log it in [[Verification-Log]]. This is the most stable of the eight — its wording is
> the well-known *bāqiyāt ṣāliḥāt* formula plus *lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata* — but check it
> anyway.

## Objectives

- Student can recite Kalima Tamjīd from memory with correct tajweed in **one breath**.
- Student can produce **five occurrences of the name of Allah — three heavy, two light —
  inside a single sentence**, and say what decided each.
- Student can produce [[Qalqalah-Sughra]] in *subḥān* without adding a vowel.
- Student can say what does **not** happen in *akbar*, and why.

## Hook

This kalima is shorter than the last two, and she will relax when she sees it. Do not let
her.

> **"Five times in this one sentence you say the name of Allah. Three of them are heavy
> and two of them are light. Two of them sit right next to each other. If you can do this
> sentence, the lām rule is finished — you will never have to think about it again."**

That is not a motivational exaggeration; it is accurate. **Tamjīd is the lām jalālah
assessment of Unit 4.** Everything else in it is revision.

The two that sit next to each other are the ones to point at:

> سُبْحَانَ **اللَّهِ** وَالْحَمْدُ **لِلَّهِ**

Heavy, then light, five syllables apart, both printed with the same four letters.

## The text

**سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ وَلَا إِلٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ، وَلَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ الْعَلِيِّ الْعَظِيمِ**

*subḥāna-llāhi wa-l-ḥamdu li-llāhi wa lā ilāha illa-llāhu wa-llāhu akbar, wa lā ḥawla wa
lā quwwata illā bi-llāhi-l-ʿaliyyi-l-ʿaẓīm*

> "Glory be to Allah, and praise be to Allah, and there is no god except Allah, and Allah
> is the Greatest; and there is no power and no strength except with Allah, the Most High,
> the Most Great."

**Tamjīd** (تَمْجِيد) means *glorification*, *extolling*. Note that its closing half — from
*wa lā ḥawla* to the end — is **word for word the closing of Kalima Istighfār**, which she
did yesterday. Say so. Half of this lesson is already done.

## Rules occurring in it

### 1. سُبْحَانَ — [[Qalqalah-Sughra]] and [[Madd-Tabii]]

**بْ** sākinah mid-word, and bāʾ is one of the five qalqalah letters → the **lesser**
qalqalah: a small, clean bounce with **no vowel after it**. *sub-ḥān*, not *subu-ḥān* and
not *sub-ḥān* with the bāʾ simply swallowed.

This is the clearest qalqalah in the whole unit — an isolated sākin bāʾ at the start of a
familiar word — so it is the one to use for correcting the technique. Get it right here and
*tubtu* (4-04) and *adhnabtuhū* (4-05) will follow.

Then **حَا** — natural madd, 2 counts.

### 2. سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ — [[Lam-Jalalah]] **heavy** (1 of 5)

The preceding vowel is the **fatḥa** of *-na* → **heavy**. And the alif of the name is a
waṣl hamzah, dropped: *subḥāna-llāh*, one continuous sound, no glottal stop
([[Hamzat-Wasl]]).

### 3. وَالْحَمْدُ — [[Lam-Qamariyyah]] and [[Izhar-Shafawi]]

**الْحَمْدُ** — ḥāʾ is a **moon** letter → the l of the article is **pronounced clearly**:
*wa-l-ḥamd*.

And inside it: **مْ** sākinah followed by **د** → [[Izhar-Shafawi]], lips fully closed, mīm
clear, no hum carried onto the dāl.

Both rules already met in 4-03 §2. Check, do not teach.

### 4. لِلَّهِ — [[Lam-Jalalah]] **light** (2 of 5)

The preceding vowel is the **kasra** of *li-* → **light**. Thin, forward, said with the
tongue high and the back of the mouth empty: *wa-l-ḥamdu li-llāh*.

She met the light form yesterday in *bi-llāh*. This one is harder, for one specific reason:
**it comes four syllables after a heavy one**, and the mouth does not want to reset that
fast. That is exactly the skill this kalima trains.

Say the pair as a unit, slowly, and make her hear the difference in her own voice:

> *subḥāna-**LLĀH**i · wa-l-ḥamdu **li-llāh**i*

If she cannot hear it, put your hand flat under her jaw. Heavy raises the back of the
tongue; light does not. She can feel it even when she cannot hear it.

### 5. وَلَا إِلٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ — the Ṭayyiba opening, fifth time

[[Madd-Munfasil]] on *lā* at 4 counts, [[Madd-Tabii]] at 2 on the dagger alif, silent
[[Hamzat-Wasl]], and [[Lam-Jalalah]] **heavy** after the fatḥa of *illā* (**3 of 5**).

Fifth lesson running. It should now be automatic, and if it is not, that is the honest
finding of this lesson and it goes in the log.

### 6. وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ — [[Lam-Jalalah]] **heavy** (4 of 5), and a rule that does *not* fire

**وَاللَّهُ** — the preceding vowel is the **fatḥa** of *wa-* → **heavy**. Note that this
is the article-less name with a waṣl hamzah after the wāw: *wa-llāhu*, joined, no stop.

**أَكْبَرُ** — and here is the interesting part. **كْ** is a sākin letter mid-word, and
students bounce it: *aki-bar*. **Kāf is not a qalqalah letter.** The five are ق ط ب ج د,
and kāf is not among them. Kāf is a whispered letter ([[Hams]]) — the breath escapes
through it, cleanly, with no bounce and no vowel.

Then **رَ** with a fatḥa → **heavy** ([[Ra-Tafkhim]]). At the stop the rāʾ goes sākin and
the vowel before it is still a fatḥa, so it stays **heavy**: *akbar*, full — but **not
bounced**, because rāʾ is not a qalqalah letter either.

*akbar* is therefore a word with **two sākin-adjacent letters and zero qalqalah in it**,
and almost everyone puts a bounce somewhere. Ask her to find the qalqalah in *akbar*. The
correct answer is "there isn't one." That question is worth more than the explanation.

### 7. وَلَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ — [[Leen]] and [[Tafkhim]]

**حَوْلَ** — wāw sākinah after a fatḥa → a **līn** letter: flat, soft, **short** while
joining. If she stops on it, it lengthens ([[Madd-Leen]]) — but she should not stop on it
here.

**قُوَّةَ** — **ق** is an elevated letter → **heavy** ([[Tafkhim]]). Doubled wāw, held for
two counts of length in the mouth, **no ghunnah** (only mīm and nūn nasalise, as
established in 4-03 §4).

### 8. إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ الْعَلِيِّ الْعَظِيمِ — the close, identical to 4-05

**بِاللَّهِ** — kasra before it → **light** (5 of 5), exactly as in 4-05 §11.

**الْعَلِيِّ** — ʿayn is a moon letter → l audible; doubled yāʾ, held, no hum.
**الْعَظِيمِ** — moon lām again; **ظ** elevated with iṭbāq → **heavy**; and at the stop,
madd letter + sākin final = [[Madd-Arid-Lissukun]], 2, 4 or 6 counts.

Now make her count the five herself rather than telling her: *subḥāna-llāh · li-llāh ·
illa-llāhu · wa-llāhu · bi-llāh* — **heavy, light, heavy, heavy, light**. A student who
counts the tally remembers it; a student who is handed it does not.

**In plain terms:** the name of Allah appears five times in one short sentence, heavy in three and light in two. The vowel just before it decides every one — nothing else does.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the pair.** *subḥāna-llāhi wa-l-ḥamdu li-llāh.* Only that. Thirty times. Heavy
then light. My hand stays under your jaw until you can feel the difference without it.

**Drill 2 — count them.** Read the kalima silently and put a finger on every occurrence of
the name of Allah. Write **H** or **L** above each. Then read only those five words in
order — *subḥāna-llāh, li-llāh, illa-llāhu, wa-llāhu, bi-llāh* — heavy, light, heavy,
heavy, light. That sequence is the lesson.

**Drill 3 — the rule-spotting pass.** Silent read, finger on each rule, name it out loud.
By now you should be naming most of them without hesitation; mark only the ones that stop
you.

**Drill 4 — the bounce and the non-bounce.** *sub-ḥān* (bounce on the b) … *ak-bar* (**no**
bounce on the k). Alternate, twenty times. Then tell me which letters get a qalqalah. All
five, out loud.

**Drill 5 — one breath.** This kalima fits in one breath and should be recited in one.
Breathe fully, then the whole thing. If you cannot reach the end, you are too fast at the
start, not too slow at the end — the madds are not the problem, the panic is. See
[[Waqf-Types]].

**Drill 6 — the recitation pass.** Whole kalima, one breath, half speed, then full speed.
Then say your own H/L sequence back to me from memory.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** three thick lāms and two thin ones in one breath, a bounce on *subḥān*, and
no bounce anywhere in *akbar*.

- **The mistake:** *li-llāh* said heavy, immediately after *subḥāna-llāh*.
  **Why it happens:** the mouth does not reset in four syllables, and heaviness has been
  the default for five lessons. This is a **motor** problem, not a knowledge problem — she
  will be able to state the rule correctly while producing the wrong sound.
  **Say:** *"Kasra. Thin. Smile into it."* And if that fails, separate them: say *li-llāh*
  alone five times, then join it back on.

- **The mistake:** *subḥāna-llāh* said light, having over-corrected from the drill.
  **Why it happens:** the drill itself. Alternation drills produce alternation errors, and
  that is a sign the contrast is being learned, not that it is failing.
  **Say:** *"Fatḥa before it. Fill your mouth. Then thin the next one."*

- **The mistake:** *subu-ḥān* — a vowel after the bāʾ.
  **Why it happens:** "bounce" gets interpreted as a note with the voice on. The qalqalah
  is a release of a closure, not a sound of its own.
  **Say:** *"Cut it. The b stops, and the stop is what you hear. No u."*

- **The mistake:** *aki-bar* or *ak-k-bar* — a bounce on the kāf.
  **Why it happens:** it is a sākin letter in the middle of a word, which is the shape
  qalqalah usually has, and the word is shouted so often that the extra beat is everywhere
  around her.
  **Say:** *"Kāf is not one of the five. Breathe through it. ak-bar, two pieces, no
  bounce."*

- **The mistake:** a glottal stop before every name — *subḥāna ʾAllāh*.
  **Why it happens:** the same waṣl-hamzah error from 4-01, which reappears whenever a
  sentence has many of them close together. Five in this one sentence is the most in the
  unit.
  **Say:** *"Five alifs, five silences. Slide over every one."*

- **The mistake:** the closing *al-ʿaẓīm* clipped.
  **Why it happens:** one breath for the whole kalima means she arrives at the last word
  with nothing left.
  **Say:** *"Then take two breaths — after akbar. A planned stop is better than a
  strangled ending."*

## Homework

1. Drill 1 — *subḥāna-llāhi wa-l-ḥamdu li-llāh* — thirty times a day. This is the drill of
   the whole unit, not just this lesson.
2. Drill 2's H/L sequence, written on your rule card and said aloud once a day.
3. The whole kalima, one breath, five times after each ṣalāh. This one is short enough to
   do properly every time; there is no excuse for a rushed version.
4. Go back to 4-05 and recite its closing half — *wa lā ḥawla* to the end — then this
   kalima's closing half. They are the same words. If they come out differently, one of
   them is wrong and you should tell me which.
5. Find one recording of anyone reciting *lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā bi-llāh* and listen
   only to the lām of *bi-llāh*. Bring me your verdict: heavy or light. You are now able
   to judge this, which is a new thing.
