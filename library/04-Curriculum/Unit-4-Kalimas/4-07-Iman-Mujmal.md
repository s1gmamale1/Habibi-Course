# Lesson 4.7 — Īmān Mujmal

> **`needs-review`.** Not Qurʾān; not corpus-verifiable; Arabic kept out of `examples:` —
> see [[4-01-Kalima-Tayyiba]]. Check against your student's printed primer and log it in
> [[Verification-Log]]. Watch in particular for **كَمَا هُوَ بِأَسْمَائِهِ** — some
> primers print *kamā huwa bi asmāʾihī wa ṣifātihī*, some *bi jamīʿi asmāʾihī*. Match the
> primer she holds.

## Where this sits

The six kalimas are done. **Īmān Mujmal and Īmān Mufaṣṣal are a different pair** — not
kalimas at all, but the two statements of belief, "in brief" and "in detail", that
traditionally close this memorisation set. Say that to her; the unit has a shape and she
should know she has crossed into its last part.

Tajweed-wise they are the two hardest texts in Unit 4, and they contain the two rules the
kalimas never gave you: **[[Madd-Badal]]** and **[[Madd-Muttasil]]**. Both are in this
lesson.

## Objectives

- Student can recite Īmān Mujmal from memory with correct tajweed on a planned breath
  scheme.
- Student can produce [[Madd-Muttasil]] at 4 counts in *asmāʾihī* and distinguish it from
  the [[Madd-Munfasil]] she has used since 4-01.
- Student can produce [[Iqlab]] twice at the end and finish on a clean
  [[Qalqalah-Kubra]].
- Student can name every rule in the text without prompting — this is the first text where
  that is a reasonable expectation.

## Hook

Start with a single word, before the text is even visible:

> آمَنْتُ

> **"Two rules in one word, and it is the first word. You have used neither of them yet in
> this unit."**

**آمَنْتُ** — *āmantu*, "I have believed." The madda over the alif is one rule; the nūn
sākinah before the tāʾ is another. She will have said this word thousands of times without
either.

That is the hook, and it is the same hook as the whole unit, sharpened: **the words are
familiar; the rules in them are not.**

## The text

**آمَنْتُ بِاللَّهِ كَمَا هُوَ بِأَسْمَائِهِ وَصِفَاتِهِ وَقَبِلْتُ جَمِيعَ أَحْكَامِهِ إِقْرَارٌ بِاللِّسَانِ وَتَصْدِيقٌ بِالْقَلْبِ**

*āmantu bi-llāhi kamā huwa bi asmāʾihī wa ṣifātihī wa qabiltu jamīʿa aḥkāmihī iqrārum
bi-l-lisāni wa taṣdīqum bi-l-qalb*

> "I believe in Allah as He is, with His names and His attributes, and I accept all His
> commands — affirming with the tongue and confirming with the heart."

**Mujmal** (مُجْمَل) means *in summary*, *in brief*. Its counterpart, [[4-08-Iman-Mufassal]],
says the same thing item by item.

## Rules occurring in it

### 1. آمَنْتُ — [[Madd-Badal]] and [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]]

Two rules, one word, and the word is first.

**آ** — the wavy madda sign over the alif marks **a hamzah followed by a madd letter**.
That is **madd badal**, the "substitute madd": **2 counts**, no more. Not four. Not six.

She will want to give it four, because it *looks* long and because she has spent six
lessons stretching *lā* to four. Madd badal is hamzah-**then**-madd; madd munfaṣil is
madd-**then**-hamzah, across a word boundary. **Order decides length.** That contrast is
worth writing on the card:

| | Shape | Counts |
|---|---|---|
| [[Madd-Badal]] | hamzah → madd letter | **2** |
| [[Madd-Munfasil]] | madd letter → hamzah, next word | **4** |

**نْتُ** — nūn sākinah followed by **ت**, one of the fifteen ikhfāʾ letters → the nūn is
**hidden**, with **2 counts of ghunnah**, the tongue already moving to the tāʾ.
*āmantu* — the n never fully arrives.

Same ikhfāʾ she did at *anta* in 4-05 §8. Point at it; the recognition is the reward.

### 2. بِاللَّهِ — [[Lam-Jalalah]] **light**

Kasra of *bi-* before it → **light**, thin, forward. Third light one in the unit, after
4-05 and 4-06, and by now it should need no correction at all.

Note where it sits: **the second word of the text**. That matters, because at the end of
this sentence there is nothing else to compare it against, and in 4-08 there will be.

### 3. كَمَا هُوَ — [[Madd-Tabii]]

**كَمَا** — fatḥa + alif, natural madd, **2 counts**. Then **هُوَ**, and the hāʾ here is a
throat letter pronounced cleanly.

Nothing more, and say so — after *āmantu* she will be hunting.

### 4. بِأَسْمَائِهِ — [[Madd-Muttasil]]

**The new rule of the lesson and the most audible change in the text.**

**ـَآئِ** — a madd letter (the alif) followed by a **hamzah in the same word**. That is
**madd muttaṣil**, the "connected madd", and it is obligatory: **4 counts** in the
recitation of Ḥafṣ (5 in some renderings, but be consistent with whatever Unit 3 taught).

*bi as-mā-ʾi-hī* — the *mā* is long.

Now the three-way contrast, which is the intellectual content of this lesson:

| Rule | Shape | Where in the text | Counts |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Madd-Tabii]] | madd letter, nothing after | *kamā* | 2 |
| [[Madd-Badal]] | hamzah **then** madd | *āmantu* | 2 |
| [[Madd-Muttasil]] | madd **then** hamzah, same word | *asmāʾihī* | 4 |
| [[Madd-Munfasil]] | madd **then** hamzah, next word | *lā ilāha* (4-01) | 4 |

Four madd rules, two lengths, all decided by **what is next to the madd letter and whether
it is inside the word or across the join**. If she can fill this table in herself by the
end of the lesson, 4-07 has done its job.

Also in this word: **سْ** sākinah, whispered, no bounce (sīn is not a qalqalah letter).

### 5. وَصِفَاتِهِ — [[Tafkhim]]

**ص** is an elevated letter with iṭbāq ([[Istila]], [[Itbaq]]) → **heavy**. The tongue
seals against the roof of the mouth and the vowel after it comes out dark: *ṣi-*, not
*si-*.

Then a natural madd on the alif, 2 counts.

The ṣād/sīn pair is worth thirty seconds here, because *asmāʾihī* (sīn, light) and
*ṣifātihī* (ṣād, heavy) are adjacent and the contrast is free.

### 6. وَقَبِلْتُ جَمِيعَ أَحْكَامِهِ — [[Tafkhim]], and two clean sākins

**وَقَبِلْتُ** — **ق** is elevated → **heavy**. The **لْ** is sākin and clean, no bounce
(lām is not a qalqalah letter). Note also that the **ب** here has a kasra, so there is **no
qalqalah** in *qabiltu* — a student hunting for bounces will find one anyway.

**جَمِيعَ** — natural madd on the yāʾ, 2 counts; **ع** is a throat letter, cut cleanly, not
softened into a vowel.

**أَحْكَامِهِ** — **حْ** sākinah, whispered, no bounce; natural madd on the alif.

### 7. إِقْرَارٌ — [[Qalqalah-Sughra]] and [[Ra-Tafkhim]]

**قْ** sākinah mid-word, and qāf **is** one of the five qalqalah letters → the **lesser**
qalqalah: a bounce, no vowel. *iq-rār*.

This is the qalqalah of the lesson, and it is harder than *subḥān* in 4-06 because qāf is
also **heavy** — she has to bounce it *and* keep it dark, and one usually costs the other.

**رَا** — rāʾ with a fatḥa → **heavy**, plus a natural madd. And the final **رٌ** carries a
ḍamma → **heavy** as well. Two heavy rāʾs in one word.

### 8. إِقْرَارٌ بِاللِّسَانِ — [[Iqlab]]

Tanwīn ḍamm followed by **ب** → **iqlāb**: the n becomes an m, hidden, with **2 counts of
ghunnah**, lips closed.

*iqrārum bi-l-lisān* — there is no n in it.

Second iqlāb of the unit, after *dhambin* in 4-05 §3, and this one is at a word boundary
rather than inside a word, which makes it easier to miss and easier to fix.

### 9. بِاللِّسَانِ — [[Lam-Shamsiyyah]]

The article's lām before **ل** — lām is a **sun** letter, so the article's lām merges into
it: *bi-l-lisān*, a doubled lām, held.

Same shape she met at *alladhī* in 4-05 §7. The tell is the shadda, as always.

Then a natural madd on the alif, 2 counts.

### 10. وَتَصْدِيقٌ بِالْقَلْبِ — the last three words

[[Tafkhim]], [[Iqlab]], [[Lam-Qamariyyah]], and the rule that ends the text.

**وَتَصْدِيقٌ** — **صْ** sākinah, elevated *and* sealed → **heavy while silent**, which is
the hardest heaviness to hold because there is no vowel carrying it. Natural madd on the
yāʾ. The **ق** is heavy.

**وَتَصْدِيقٌ بِالْقَلْبِ** — tanwīn ḍamm before **ب** → **iqlāb** again, second time in
four words. Lips closed, 2 counts of hum: *taṣdīqum bi-l-qalb*.

**بِالْقَلْبِ** — **ق** is a **moon** letter → the article's lām is **pronounced clearly**:
*bi-l-qalb*, l audible. And the qāf is heavy. The **لْ** of *qalb* is sākin and clean.

**And the final بِ** — when you stop, it goes sākin, and bāʾ is a qalqalah letter, so the
whole text ends on a **[[Qalqalah-Kubra]]**: a full, strong bounce, stronger than any
mid-word one. *bi-l-qalb*, with the b released hard and clean.

**Ending a text on a qalqalah kubrā is a gift** — it is the single most satisfying sound in
the unit and the easiest thing in this lesson to get audibly right. Use it. End every
recitation deliberately on it.

**In plain terms:** the آ at the very start is two beats, not four. The hamzah comes *before* it, not after, and that is the whole difference.

## Drills

**Drill 1 — the madd table.** Say these four in order and give me the count for each:
> *kamā* (2) · *āmantu* (2) · *asmāʾihī* (4) · *lā ilāha* (4)

Then say **why** each is what it is. Hamzah before or hamzah after; same word or next word.
This is the drill for this lesson.

**Drill 2 — the first word.** *āmantu*, thirty times. Two counts on the ā — count them on
your fingers where I can see. Then the hidden n. Both, every time.

**Drill 3 — the rule-spotting pass.** Silent read, finger on each rule, name it aloud. You
should name almost all of them today without help. Mark only what stops you.

**Drill 4 — heavy and light neighbours.** *bi asmāʾihī* (light s) … *wa ṣifātihī* (heavy
ṣ). Alternate ten times. Then *bi-llāh* (light lām) in the same breath — three decisions in
one phrase.

**Drill 5 — bounce and keep it dark.** *iq-rār*, twenty times. The q bounces **and** stays
heavy. If it bounces light, it has turned into a k.

**Drill 6 — lips closed twice.** *iqrārum bi-l-lisān* … *taṣdīqum bi-l-qalb*. Both iqlābs,
in a mirror. Your lips must meet both times.

**Drill 7 — the ending.** *bi-l-qalb*, twenty times, stopping properly each time. Full
bounce on the b. Nothing after it — no vowel, no breath sound, just the bounce and then
silence.

**Drill 8 — the breath plan.** Mark it first. The natural scheme is three breaths: after
*wa ṣifātihī* · after *jamīʿa aḥkāmihī* · then to the end. Do not breathe between *iqrārum*
and *bi-l-lisān* — that would destroy the iqlāb. See [[Waqf-Types]].

**Drill 9 — the recitation pass.** Whole text, marked plan, half speed, then full speed.
Then name your own weakest rule before I do.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** two counts on *āmantu*, four on *asmāʾihī*, lips closed on both iqlābs, and
a hard clean bounce at the very end.

- **The mistake:** *āāāmantu* — madd badal stretched to four or six.
  **Why it happens:** the madda sign looks emphatic, and six lessons of madd munfaṣil at
  four counts have set a default. She is applying a rule she knows, in the wrong place.
  **Say:** *"Hamzah first, then the madd. That order is two counts. Two."*

- **The mistake:** *asmāʾihī* clipped to two counts.
  **Why it happens:** the hamzah is written small and sits above the line; she does not see
  it as a letter that triggers anything.
  **Say:** *"There is a hamzah after the alif, inside the word. Four counts. Same length as
  lā ilāha, and for the same reason — but this one is inside."*

- **The mistake:** *iqrārun bi-l-lisān* — the n kept.
  **Why it happens:** iqlāb across a word boundary is much less visible than iqlāb inside a
  word; nothing in the printing of *iqrārun* suggests a change.
  **Say:** *"Look at what starts the next word. A b. So the n becomes an m. Close your
  lips."*

- **The mistake:** *iq-rār* with the qāf bounced light — turning into *ik-rār*.
  **Why it happens:** the qalqalah bounce is produced at the front of the mouth by default,
  and qāf's makhraj is at the very back. Bouncing and staying back are competing motor
  demands.
  **Say:** *"Bounce it from the back. Deep. If it sounds like a k, it has come forward."*

- **The mistake:** *bi-l-qalbi* — the final kasra pronounced, no stop, no qalqalah.
  **Why it happens:** she reads what is written, and the kasra is written. Stopping rules
  are the one place the page is not the last word, as in *abadā* in 4-03.
  **Say:** *"You're stopping. Drop the vowel and bounce the b. That's the end of it."*

- **The mistake:** the ṣād in *taṣdīq* gone light while sākin.
  **Why it happens:** heaviness lives in the vowel for most students; a sākin heavy letter
  has no vowel to carry it and quietly thins out.
  **Say:** *"Seal your tongue against the roof and hold it there through the silence. Then
  the d."*

## Homework

1. Drill 1 — the madd table, out loud, twice a day, with the counts and the reasons.
2. *āmantu* thirty times a day. First word, two rules; if it is wrong, everything after it
   starts wrong.
3. The whole text once a day on the marked breath plan.
4. Add the four-madd table to your rule card. It is the most useful thing on the card and
   it applies to every page of the muṣḥaf, not just to this text.
5. Read [[4-08-Iman-Mufassal]]'s text once, silently, before the next lesson — do not
   recite it, just look. It is the longest text in the unit and you should not meet it cold.
