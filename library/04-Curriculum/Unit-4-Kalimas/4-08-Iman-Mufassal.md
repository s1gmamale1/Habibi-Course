# Lesson 4.8 — Īmān Mufaṣṣal

> **`needs-review`.** Not Qurʾān; not corpus-verifiable; Arabic kept out of `examples:` —
> see [[4-01-Kalima-Tayyiba]]. Of the eight this is the one to check most carefully: the
> six articles are always the same six, but primers differ on **وَالْبَعْثِ بَعْدَ
> الْمَوْتِ** (some print *wa-l-baʿthu baʿda-l-mawt*, nominative, as a separate clause) and
> on whether **تَعَالَىٰ** is present. Match the primer she holds and log it in
> [[Verification-Log]].

## Objectives

- Student can recite Īmān Mufaṣṣal from memory with correct tajweed on a planned breath
  scheme, without a rule collapsing in the second half.
- Student can produce the **light and heavy lām of the name of Allah within one sentence**
  — *bi-llāh* and *mina-llāh* — on demand.
- Student can produce three rāʾs in six words, all light, without rolling any of them.
- Student can walk the whole text naming every rule, unaided. **This is the exit
  assessment of Unit 4.**

## Where this sits

This is the last lesson of the unit and the longest text in it. Two things follow from
that, and both should shape how you run the session.

**First: give it more time than any other lesson in Unit 4.** Two sittings is normal. There
is no new *rule* here that she has not already met — every single rule in this text has
appeared somewhere in 4-01 to 4-07 — but there are more of them, closer together, than
anywhere else, and the failure mode of Unit 4 has always been **duration**, not knowledge.

**Second: this is where the unit gets assessed.** Not by a test, but by the rule-spotting
pass in Drill 1. If she can walk this text naming rules, she has the unit. If she cannot,
the gap she shows you is the real curriculum for the next few sessions — and it will
almost certainly be one specific rule, not a general weakness.

## Hook

Show her the six articles of faith in English first, before any Arabic:

> Allah · His angels · His books · His messengers · the Last Day · the decree, its good
> and its ill — and resurrection after death.

> **"Six things, one sentence. You have believed all six of these since before you could
> read. Today is the last time in this course that we work on something you already know
> by heart — and it is the longest one."**

Then the tajweed hook, which is the honest one:

> **"There is nothing new in this sentence. Every rule in it, you have already done. The
> only new thing is that there are thirty of them and they are close together."**

## The text

**آمَنْتُ بِاللَّهِ وَمَلَائِكَتِهِ وَكُتُبِهِ وَرُسُلِهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ وَالْقَدَرِ خَيْرِهِ وَشَرِّهِ مِنَ اللَّهِ تَعَالَىٰ وَالْبَعْثِ بَعْدَ الْمَوْتِ**

*āmantu bi-llāhi wa malāʾikatihī wa kutubihī wa rusulihī wa-l-yawmi-l-ākhiri wa-l-qadari
khayrihī wa sharrihī mina-llāhi taʿālā wa-l-baʿthi baʿda-l-mawt*

> "I believe in Allah, and in His angels, and in His books, and in His messengers, and in
> the Last Day, and in the decree — its good and its ill — from Allah the Exalted, and in
> the resurrection after death."

**Mufaṣṣal** (مُفَصَّل) means *in detail*, *spelled out* — the same belief as
[[4-07-Iman-Mujmal]], itemised.

## Rules occurring in it

### 1. آمَنْتُ — [[Madd-Badal]] and [[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]]

Identical to 4-07 §1, word for word. **2 counts** on the madda (hamzah *then* madd letter —
the short one), and the nūn sākinah **hidden** before the tāʾ with 2 counts of ghunnah.

Check it in five seconds. If it has decayed since yesterday, that is worth knowing before
you spend an hour on a longer text.

### 2. بِاللَّهِ — [[Lam-Jalalah]] **light**

Kasra of *bi-* → **light**. Second word of the text.

**Mark this one.** In §7 the same name appears again with the opposite weight, and the two
of them inside one sentence are the assessment item of the unit.

### 3. وَمَلَائِكَتِهِ — [[Madd-Muttasil]]

**ـَلَآئِ** — a madd letter followed by a **hamzah in the same word** → madd muttaṣil,
**4 counts**, obligatory.

Same rule and the same shape as *asmāʾihī* in 4-07 §4, and it comes just as early in the
sentence. If she gave four counts yesterday and two today, the rule is not learned — it was
remembered for one lesson.

### 4. وَكُتُبِهِ وَرُسُلِهِ — [[Madd-Silah]] and [[Ra-Tafkhim]]

**وَكُتُبِهِ** — the pronoun-hāʾ sits between two voweled letters and is followed by a
consonant (the wāw of *wa*) → **ṣilah ṣughrā**, **2 counts**: *kutubihī wa…*. Flagged as
optional in 4-02 §6; whatever you decided there, do the same here.

Every one of the four pronoun-hāʾs in this text — *malāʾikatihī, kutubihī, rusulihī,
khayrihī, sharrihī* (five, in fact) — takes the same treatment. That is the most repeated
decision in the text, so decide it once.

**وَرُسُلِهِ** — **رُ** with a ḍamma → **heavy** ([[Ra-Tafkhim]]).

**This is the only heavy rāʾ in the entire text.** Every other rāʾ here is light. Mark it,
because §6 depends on the contrast.

### 5. وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ — four rules in two words

[[Lam-Qamariyyah]] twice, [[Leen]], [[Madd-Badal]] and [[Ra-Tarqiq]].

**وَالْيَوْمِ** — yāʾ is a **moon** letter → the article's lām is **pronounced clearly**:
*wa-l-yawm*. And **يَوْ** is a **līn** letter (wāw sākinah after a fatḥa): flat and short
while joining ([[Leen]]).

**الْآخِرِ** — hamzah is a **moon** letter → l audible. Then **آ** — a hamzah followed by a
madd letter → **[[Madd-Badal]]**, **2 counts**, the same short one as *āmantu*. Two madd
badals in one sentence, both two counts.

**خ** is elevated → **heavy** ([[Tafkhim]]). And the final **رِ** carries a kasra →
**light** ([[Ra-Tarqiq]]) — heavy letter and light letter inside the same word, which is
exactly the mixture she found hard in *qadīr* back in 4-03.

### 6. وَالْقَدَرِ خَيْرِهِ وَشَرِّهِ — three light rāʾs

**The hardest six words in Unit 4, and the reason this lesson needs its own sitting.**

**وَالْقَدَرِ** — qāf is a **moon** letter → l audible; qāf itself is **heavy**; and the
final **رِ** has a kasra → **light**.

**خَيْرِهِ** — **خَيْ** is a **līn**; the **رِ** has a kasra → **light**. And the khāʾ
before it is **heavy**.

**وَشَرِّهِ** — a **doubled rāʾ with a kasra** → **light**, held for two rāʾs' length,
**and still thin**.

Three rāʾs, three kasras, three light. And each one is preceded by a heavy or a
back-of-the-mouth sound — qāf, khāʾ, and the doubling itself — pulling the mouth the wrong
way.

Now put §4 next to §6:

| Word | Vowel on the rāʾ | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| وَرُسُلِهِ | ḍamma | **heavy** |
| وَالْقَدَرِ | kasra | **light** |
| خَيْرِهِ | kasra | **light** |
| وَشَرِّهِ | kasra (doubled) | **light** |

One rule, four applications, in one sentence. If she can do this table by ear, [[Ra-Tafkhim]]
and [[Ra-Tarqiq]] are finished for good.

**وَشَرِّهِ is the single hardest word in the unit.** A doubled rāʾ is where every student
reaches for a roll, and a roll is not what heaviness or doubling means — the rāʾ is
*held*, not *trilled* (see [[Takrir]], the characteristic that must be suppressed, not
performed).

### 7. مِنَ اللَّهِ — [[Lam-Jalalah]] **heavy**

**The assessment item of Unit 4.**

The preceding vowel is the **fatḥa** of *-na* → **heavy**. Full, thick, back of the mouth:
*mina-LLĀH*.

And it is in **the same sentence** as the light *bi-llāhi* from §2, about twelve words
earlier. Say them back to back, out of context, and then in context:

> *bi-llāhi* … *mina-llāhi*

Light, then heavy. Same four letters, printed identically, decided entirely by the vowel
in front. That is the rule that has run through all eight lessons of this unit, and this is
the last and hardest place it appears.

### 8. تَعَالَىٰ — [[Madd-Tabii]]

**ع** is a throat letter, cut cleanly. Then **ـَىٰ**, a dagger alif → natural madd, **2
counts**, not four.

Its function here is to be a **rest**: one easy word between the three light rāʾs and the
final clause. Use it as one — slow down on it deliberately.

### 9. وَالْبَعْثِ بَعْدَ — [[Lam-Qamariyyah]], and two sākin ʿayns

**وَالْبَعْثِ** — bāʾ is a **moon** letter → l audible: *wa-l-baʿth*. Note that the **ب**
here carries a fatḥa, so there is **no qalqalah** in it — a student hunting for bounces
will put one in.

**عْ** sākinah — a throat letter, stopped cleanly with no vowel after it and no bounce.
Then again in **بَعْدَ**: *baʿ-da*.

A sākin ʿayn is one of the most commonly mangled sounds in recitation: it either grows a
vowel (*baʿa-tha*) or disappears entirely (*bath*). Two of them in three words makes this
the second drill of the lesson.

### 10. الْمَوْتِ — [[Lam-Qamariyyah]], [[Madd-Leen]], and the stop

**الْمَوْتِ** — mīm is a **moon** letter → l audible: *al-mawt*.

**مَوْ** is a **līn** letter. While joining it stays short — but **you are stopping here**,
because this is the end of the text. A līn letter followed by a letter made sākin by
stopping lengthens to **2, 4 or 6 counts**: that is [[Madd-Leen]].

*al-mawwwt* — and then the tāʾ, clean, no bounce (tāʾ is not a qalqalah letter).

Compare with how 4-07 ended, on a hard [[Qalqalah-Kubra]] at *bi-l-qalb*. This text ends on
a **long soft līn** instead. Two texts, two completely different endings, and she should be
able to say why each is what it is. That comparison is the last thing you teach in Unit 4.

## Drills

**This lesson gets the most drill time in the unit.** Plan two sittings. Sitting one: Drills
1 to 5, no full recitation at all. Sitting two: Drills 6 to 9.

**Drill 1 — the rule-spotting pass. This is the exit assessment.** Do not recite. Read
silently, finger on each rule, name it out loud. There are around thirty stops. I write down
every one you cannot name and say nothing while you work. That list — not your recitation —
is what tells us both whether Unit 4 has landed.

**Drill 2 — the two names.** *bi-llāhi* … *mina-llāhi*. Twenty times, alternating. Light,
heavy. Then tell me the vowel before each. If you can do this pair cold, you have the lām
rule for life.

**Drill 3 — the rāʾ table.** *wa rusulihī* (heavy) · *wa-l-qadari* (light) · *khayrihī*
(light) · *wa sharrihī* (light). In that order, ten times. Then in a random order that I
call out, and you tell me the weight before you say the word.

**Drill 4 — the hardest word.** *wa sharrihī*, thirty times. Doubled. **Thin.** Not rolled.
Put the tip of your tongue where it belongs and hold it — do not let it flap. Smile while
you say it if that helps; it forces the tongue forward.

**Drill 5 — the two ʿayns.** *wa-l-baʿthi baʿda*. Twenty times. Both ʿayns sākin, both cut
clean, no vowel after either, neither one dropped. This is the second-hardest thing in the
text and nobody warns students about it.

**Drill 6 — the two endings.** Recite the last three words of 4-07 — *bi-l-qalb*, hard
bounce — then the last two of this one — *al-mawt*, long and soft. Alternate five times.
Then tell me the rule for each ending.

**Drill 7 — the breath plan.** Mark it before you recite. The natural scheme is three
breaths: after *wa rusulihī* · after *wa-l-yawmi-l-ākhir* · after *mina-llāhi taʿālā* ·
then to the end. **Do not breathe inside *khayrihī wa sharrihī*** — those two belong
together and splitting them wastes the contrast you just drilled. See [[Waqf-Types]].

**Drill 8 — half speed, whole text.** On the plan. Every madd counted on your fingers.
If you cannot count and recite at once, you are too fast.

**Drill 9 — full speed, and the honest report.** Recite it properly, once. Then, before I
say anything, tell me **which rule broke**. In a text this long one always does, and by now
you should know which one before I do. That self-report is the last skill of Unit 4 and the
one that carries into everything after it.

## Listen-for

**Listen for:** *bi-llāh* thin and *mina-llāh* thick in the same breath, three light rāʾs
in a row, two clean sākin ʿayns, and a long soft ending.

- **The mistake:** *wa sharrihī* rolled, or heavy, or both.
  **Why it happens:** doubling invites a trill, and every rāʾ she has heard doubled in
  ordinary speech is trilled. This is not a tajweed error being made — it is ordinary Arabic
  phonetics being applied to a text where tajweed asks for something narrower.
  **Say:** *"Hold it, don't flap it. One long r, thin, tongue forward."*

- **The mistake:** *mina-llāh* said light, in a sentence that opened with *bi-llāh*.
  **Why it happens:** the light form was set at the start of the sentence and has been
  running for twelve words. Sentence-level inertia, the same thing that made *li-llāh* hard
  in 4-06.
  **Say:** *"Fatḥa before it now. Fill your mouth. mina-LLĀH."*

- **The mistake:** *bi-llāh* said heavy, having over-corrected after Drill 2.
  **Why it happens:** the drill. Alternation drills produce alternation errors, and that is
  a sign the contrast is being learned rather than failing.
  **Say:** *"Kasra. Thin it. Then the other one thick."*

- **The mistake:** *malāʾikatihī* clipped to two counts.
  **Why it happens:** the hamzah is small, high above the line, and does not look like a
  letter that changes anything. Exactly the *asmāʾihī* error from 4-07.
  **Say:** *"Hamzah inside the word after the madd. Four counts. Same as yesterday."*

- **The mistake:** *baʿatha* or *bath* — the sākin ʿayn given a vowel or dropped.
  **Why it happens:** the ʿayn's makhraj is deep in the throat and hard to stop cleanly
  without either releasing into a vowel or letting go of it entirely. It is a genuinely
  difficult articulation, not carelessness.
  **Say:** *"Squeeze it and stop. Nothing after it. baʿ — then d."*

- **The mistake:** *al-mawti* — the final kasra pronounced, no lengthening.
  **Why it happens:** she reads what is written, and the stop rules are the one place where
  the page is not the last word. Third time this pattern has appeared in the unit, after
  *abadā* (4-03) and *bi-l-qalb* (4-07).
  **Say:** *"You are stopping. Drop the vowel, and stretch the aw. al-mawwwt."*

- **The mistake:** everything correct in the first half, madds and weights collapsing after
  *wa-l-yawmi-l-ākhir*.
  **Why it happens:** duration. This has been the failure mode of every long text in the
  unit, and it is the thing this lesson exists to fix.
  **Say:** *"Breathe at the plan. Halve the speed of the second half. The rules cost
  attention and you have run out — buy some back with time."*

## Homework

1. Drill 2 — the two names — twenty times a day. If you do one thing from this unit for
   the rest of your life, do this one.
2. Drill 4 — *wa sharrihī* — thirty times a day, thin.
3. The whole text once a day on the marked breath plan, at half speed. Once, properly, is
   worth more than five rushed.
4. Complete your rule card. Go back through all eight lessons and tick every rule you can
   now name and produce. Bring the card. **The unticked ones are what we work on next**,
   and they will be the same three or four rules for everybody.
5. Recite all eight — the six kalimas, Mujmal, Mufaṣṣal — back to back, once, this week.
   Not for accuracy; for endurance. Tell me where it fell apart.

## Closing the unit

When she can do Drill 1 unaided, say this, and mean it:

> **"You have just applied every rule in this course to text that is not the Qurʾān. That
> was the point. Nothing you did here was tested by the four things this course promises to
> teach you — and everything you did here makes those four things easier. Now take the same
> eyes to a page of the muṣḥaf."**

The unit was always optional (see [[4-01-Kalima-Tayyiba]] for why it is in the course at
all). What it produces is not a set of memorised formulae — she had those before she
started — but **the habit of looking at familiar Arabic and seeing rules in it**. That habit
is the transferable thing, and it belongs to end goal 1: reading an unseen page cleanly,
unaided. See [[Assessment]].
