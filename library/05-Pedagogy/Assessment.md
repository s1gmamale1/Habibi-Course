# Assessment

How this course scores recitation, what the checkpoints test, and what a failed
checkpoint actually costs the student. The scoring axis is the classical error
taxonomy; the mechanics come from a live qiraat competition rulebook and a
teacher-facing error-marking guide, both of which converge on the same numbers.

## 1. The scoring model — Jali / Khafi

Classical tajweed splits errors into two tiers, and every serious rubric in the
field is built on that split.

| Error | What it is | Deduction |
|---|---|---|
| **Laḥn Jali** (major, clear) | Wrong makhraj, or a wrong *ṣifah lāzimah* — a permanent, defining characteristic. Changes meaning or grammar. Obvious to any listener, scholar or not. | **−2** |
| **Laḥn Khafi** (minor, hidden) | Wrong *ṣifah ʿāriḍah* — a situational characteristic: ghunnah duration, madd length, degree of tafkhīm. Does not change meaning; degrades quality. Detectable only by a trained ear. | **−1** |
| Non-fluency | Hesitant, not flowing | **−0.5** |
| **Self-correction after prompt** | Student catches their own error once alerted | **−1** |
| **Examiner has to correct** | Student cannot self-correct | **−2** |

The self-correction line is the most informative number on the table. A student who
fixes their own error on a prompt has a working internal monitor and needs practice;
a student who cannot fix it even when told something is wrong has not yet formed the
category and needs re-teaching. Those are different problems with different
remedies, so they carry different costs.

Substituting one letter for another — [[Qaf]] flattened into [[Kaf]], [[Dad]]
collapsed toward [[Zay]], [[Ha-throat]] merged with [[Ha-soft]] — is always Jali.
Holding a madd for three counts instead of four is Khafi. The practical rule for
the teacher: *if a native listener with no tajweed training would notice, it is
Jali.*

Marking convention, taken from the teacher guides: Jali in red, Khafi in yellow.
Mark **all Jali errors first**, because they affect meaning; assign targeted Khafi
practice second.

## 2. Errors are tracked per letter and per rule — never only per passage

This is the single most important operational decision in this document.

A score of "−7 on this page" is unactionable. A record of "−2 [[Ha-throat]],
−2 [[Ha-throat]], −1 [[Madd-Munfasil]], −2 [[Ayn]]" says: the student has a
throat-letter problem, revise Unit 3.1 and the [[Common-Mistakes]] track for
ح/خ/ه, and leave madd alone.

So every deduction is logged against **the specific letter or the specific rule that
caused it**, and the running record is kept across sessions, not reset each time.
Two consequences:

- A checkpoint result can name the units to revise. "Revise 3-19 iẓhār halqī and
  the ح/خ/ه contrast" — not "retake Checkpoint 2."
- Recurring weak letters become visible before they fossilise. A letter that costs
  a deduction in three consecutive sessions is a fossilising habit, and it is far
  cheaper to break now than in six months.

The diagnostic-first principle applies at every checkpoint: the *first* purpose of a
checkpoint reading is to catalogue this student's specific errors, and only the
second is to produce a pass/fail.

## 3. The three checkpoints

**Checkpoint 1 — Letters & Sounds.** All 29 letters, all three positions, read
aloud. Per letter, two binary judgements: makhraj correct (Jali if wrong) and ṣifāt
correct (Khafi if off). Plus any voweled syllable read on sight.
*Pass:* zero Jali on isolated letters. A small number of Khafi (≤3) is
**pass-with-homework-flag**, not a fail.

**Checkpoint 2 — Reading Mechanics.** The biggest gate in the course. Unseen,
fully-voweled words and then sentences, read unaided, with no prompting.
Scored with the full point table in §1.
*Pass:* the hard rule is **no Jali error survives an examiner correction**. Every
deduction is logged with its causing letter or rule, so revision targets named
Unit 2 lessons rather than the whole unit.

**Final Checkpoint — four tests, one per end goal.** Detailed in §4.

## 4. The four final tests

**Test 1 — Unseen muṣḥaf page.** Plain Uthmani, no colour. A full page, not a line:
sustaining precision across extended recitation is itself a competency, and it is
what the ijāzah exam actually tests.
*Pass:* zero uncorrected Jali. Khafi ≤5 clean · 6–10 pass-with-flag · >10 revise
and retest.

**Test 2 — Hifz recitation.** al-Fātiḥa, then 114 → 105 unaided. Scored with the
same Jali/Khafi table, **plus a separate memorisation-fidelity axis** recording
omitted, added, or transposed words. The two axes are kept apart deliberately: a
student with solid memorisation and rough tajweed needs recitation practice, a
student with clean tajweed who cannot recall the text needs more repetitions. One
combined number hides which. (Note that word omission or addition is itself
meaning-changing, so it scores as Jali on its own axis.)
*Fail granularity:* more than 3 examiner corrections in one surah fails **that
surah alone**, not the test. See [[Hifz-Method]].

**Test 3 — Oral rule quiz.** Binary correct/incorrect per rule named and explained;
covers every family. Knowledge, not performance, so the Jali/Khafi scale does not
apply — a rule is either stated correctly or it is not.
*Pass:* **≥90%.**

**Test 4 — Colour-muṣḥaf reading.** Identify what each coloured span is and what it
demands.
*Pass:* ≥90% of colour-to-rule identifications correct, **and** zero Jali on the
coloured spans themselves — knowing the rule and executing it are scored separately.
Recommended item: al-Fātiḥa 1:7, which carries [[Izhar-Halqi]], [[Madd-Munfasil]],
[[Madd-Lazim]] and [[Lam-Shamsiyyah]] in a single line.

## 5. Outcomes — three, and none of them is a restart

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pass** | Advance. |
| **Pass-with-homework-flag** | Advance, with named drills attached to the next block of sessions. The flag is carried forward and cleared explicitly, not forgotten. |
| **Revise-and-retest** | Repeat **only the named units**, then retest **only the failed component**. |

**A failed checkpoint never triggers a restart.** This is a design commitment, not
a kindness. The error record from §2 already identifies exactly which letters and
rules failed; re-teaching material the student demonstrably holds wastes sessions
and, for an adult learner, is the most reliable way to lose them. The calendar is a
suggestion; the checkpoints decide advancement — but they decide it at the
granularity of a unit, never of the course.

## 6. What the score is and is not evidence of

Tests 3 and 4's identification half are evidence about knowledge, and they stand on
their own. Tests 1, 2 and 4's execution half are evidence about **production**, and
production evidence is only as good as the ear that scored it. In this course a
qualified teacher is present, so those scores mean something. In any asynchronous or
self-scored version of the same rubric they do not, and must not be reported as if
they did. See [[Methodology]] §4 for the scope claim this course is allowed to make.

No result here is an ijāzah, and a completion record must say so in as many words.
