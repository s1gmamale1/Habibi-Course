# Methodology

Why this course is sequenced the way it is. Read this before writing any lesson —
the order is load-bearing, and several of its choices deliberately depart from the
books they are built on.

## 1. The spine: al-Jazariyyah, drilled with Tuhfah

The rule order follows **[[Muqaddimah-Jazariyyah]]** (Ibn al-Jazari, d. 833 AH,
~107 verses). Its organising principle is a single clean split:

- **Ḥaqq al-ḥarf** — what a letter intrinsically *is*: its makhraj, its [[Sifat]],
  its heaviness, the special cases of rāʾ and lām. Taught first.
- **Mustaḥaqq al-ḥarf** — what a letter *becomes in context*: [[Ghunnah]], idghām,
  ikhfāʾ, madd. Taught second.

Intrinsic before contextual. That is not an aesthetic preference: the contextual
rules literally cannot be applied without the intrinsic ones. A student who cannot
tell [[Ha-throat]] from [[Kha]] from [[Ha-soft]] cannot apply [[Izhar-Halqi]],
because iẓhār *is* the rule "when the following letter is one of these six throat
letters." The prerequisite is phonetic, not pedagogical.

**[[Tuhfat-al-Atfal]]** (al-Jamzuri, 1746, 61 verses) is the standard beginner matn
and supplies the drill content — the noon rules, the mīm rules, the lām rules, the
madd taxonomy, all with worked word lists. But it is deliberately narrow: it has
**no makharij, no sifat, no rāʾ, no tafkhīm, no waqf**. It cannot be the skeleton.
So: Jazariyyah gives the skeleton, Tuhfah fills the flesh.

### Why this beats both video courses we studied

[[Arabic101]] is Tuhfah's content reordered by frequency of use, with Jazariyyah's
first two chapters pulled out as a parallel track. It is excellent material and its
*rhythm* is worth copying (see §3), but its "Common Mistakes" and "Advanced"
playlists are unordered standalone topics, not a syllabus. A student cannot walk it
front to back and arrive somewhere.

[[Muallimi-Soniy]] is not matn-driven at all. It is organised around progressively
reading a muṣḥaf page, which is why orthography and stopping marks fill its first
33 lessons. That produces a student who can *read* early — genuinely valuable, and
the reason we introduce practical stopping early too — but the rules arrive as a
list of things the page happens to contain, with no principle telling you why one
comes before another.

A matn spine gives us the thing neither has: **explicit prerequisites**. Every
lesson can name what must already be solid, and a failed checkpoint can name the
exact unit to revise rather than sending the student back to the start.

## 2. Two places where we correct the classical order

The classical texts are authorities on content, not on teaching order. Two of their
orderings are pedagogically backwards, and we fix both — openly, so nobody later
mistakes the change for an error.

### 2.1 Ghunnah before the noon rules that need it

Tuhfah puts the four rulings of nūn sākinah and tanwīn in chapter 1, and the
obligatory ghunnah of the doubled nūn and mīm in chapter 2. But
[[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]] is *defined by* ghunnah. So is [[Iqlab]]. So is
[[Ikhfa-Haqiqi]]. Teaching them before the student has ever held a two-count nasal
sound means teaching four rules whose defining feature is undefined.

Most teachers silently reorder this. We do it explicitly: **[[Ghunnah]] is lesson
3-15, the whole of stage 3.3, before any of the noon-sākinah stage 3.4.** By the
time [[Idgham-Maal-Ghunnah]] arrives, the student has already produced ghunnah in
the doubled letters, in [[Ikhfa-Shafawi]] and in [[Idgham-Shafawi]], and the new
rule is a new *trigger* for a sound they already own.

### 2.2 Practical stopping in Unit 2, formal waqf in Unit 3.7

[[Madd-Arid-Lissukun]] — the madd that appears only because you stopped — cannot be
taught to someone who does not stop. Jazariyyah puts madd in chapter 11 and [[Waqf-Types|waqf]] in
chapter 12; the earlier syllabus in this repo also deferred waqf to the end.

But the real constraint arrives much earlier than either: **the moment a student
reads a full sentence, they must breathe somewhere.** That is Unit 2, lessons
2-12 to 2-14, long before any tajweed rule.

So waqf is taught **twice**:

1. **Practical stopping — Unit 2.12–2.14.** Just the mechanics: drop the final
   ḥarakah, tāʾ marbūṭah becomes hāʾ, pick a place that does not break the sense.
   Enough to read a page out loud without suffocating.
2. **Formal waqf — Unit 3.7, lessons 3-34 to 3-36.** [[Waqf-Signs]] and
   [[Waqf-Types]] as a system, plus what else changes when you stop.

[[Madd-Arid-Lissukun]] lands at 3-31, between the two — after the student can
stop, before the sign system is formalised. That is the only window where the rule
is teachable.

## 3. The lesson rhythm

Four commitments, all of them adopted from what works in [[Arabic101]] and confirmed
by the assessment research:

**One rule per lesson, with explicit prerequisites.** Not "the noon rules" in a
lesson — [[Izhar-Halqi]] in a lesson, [[Iqlab]] in a lesson. A lesson that teaches
two rules cannot be failed precisely, and a checkpoint that cannot name the failing
rule cannot prescribe revision.

**Corrective, myth-busting framing.** Adults arrive with prior recitation habits
and half-remembered rules from childhood maktab. The most efficient teaching move
is usually not "here is a new fact" but "here is what you were probably doing, here
is why it feels right, here is what it should be." This is why
[[Common-Mistakes]] is a full parallel track rather than a footnote in each lesson.

**Every stage closes with revision + Q&A.** Lessons 3-09, 3-14, 3-19, 3-26, 3-33
teach nothing new. They exist because interleaved retrieval is what moves a rule
from recognised to owned, and because a live student accumulates questions that
have no home in a lesson plan. The hifz strand keeps moving through these lessons;
only new *rules* pause.

**Scholarly disagreement is named, not hidden.** Where the sources genuinely
differ — the length of [[Madd-Munfasil|madd munfaṣil]], the degrees of [[Qalqalah-sifah|qalqalah]], the number of [[Sakt|sakt]]
in Ḥafṣ, the levels of [[Tafkhim|tafkhīm]] — teach the jumhūr position and name the alternative
once. Presenting a contested length as settled fact is the kind of error that
destroys a teacher's credibility the first time the student meets another reciter.

## 4. What this course honestly cannot do

Tajweed is transmitted by **talaqqī** — direct oral reception, mouth to ear, from a
qualified reciter who holds a connected chain. That is not a tradition wrapped
around the knowledge; for the parts of tajweed that are gradient and performed
rather than described, it *is* the knowledge. The exact hue of ikhfāʾ, the real
duration of a ḥarakah, the degree of heaviness in a given tafkhīm — these exist
because they were heard, and no description substitutes for hearing them from
someone who was corrected by someone who was corrected.

There are two further reasons a book or a screen is structurally insufficient:

- **You cannot hear your own error.** An adult's perception is filtered through
  their first language. A student whose L1 merged [[Tha]] into [[Seen]] hears their
  own س as a correct ث. The error is invisible from the inside. It needs an
  external ear.
- **Certification is transmission.** An ijāzah certifies a chain. By definition no
  course can confer one.

So state the scope plainly, in the product and to the student:

> This course teaches you the rules and trains your ear. It cannot hear you.
> Tajweed has always been received face to face from a qualified teacher, and no
> app or book replaces that. What this does is make your time with a teacher
> shorter and sharper: you arrive knowing the rules, able to hear the difference,
> and already knowing which sounds your own language will trip you on.

What a course of this shape *can* deliver, and should be measured on:

- Declarative knowledge — name, identify, explain, and predict every rule.
- **Recognition in the muṣḥaf** — see where a rule fires and say why.
- **Auditory discrimination** — hear [[Dad]] against [[Za-heavy]], two counts
  against four, ikhfāʾ against iẓhār.
- Vocabulary and framework to *use a teacher efficiently*.
- Self-diagnosis habits — record, compare, notice, and know which unit to revisit.

This course is live-taught to one adult student three times a week, so it has a
teacher in the loop by design. But the same honesty applies to every artefact it
produces: a checkpoint result is evidence about knowledge and perception, and about
production **only** insofar as a qualified ear was present when it was scored. See
[[Assessment]] for how that line is drawn in the rubric, and [[Hifz-Method]] for
the memorisation strand that runs alongside all of this.
